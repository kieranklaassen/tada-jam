# Licence notes

The repo's own licence (`LICENSE`) covers the pack's tooling and the text the pack writes itself: descriptions, glosses and design notes.

Official text from a third party stays its publisher's. That covers the wording of a standard, foundation or core goal, its official code, and the title of the document it comes from. Recording it here does not change who owns it or the terms it is published under.

Reuse terms differ per publisher, so they are recorded per source, not once for the pack. Each official document has one source record, `education/sources/<slug>.md`. Its `reuse_policy` field holds the policy, its `terms_quote` and `terms_url` fields hold the publisher's own words on reuse and where they are published, and the paragraph of its body that starts with `Reuse:` says how the policy follows from them. The tables below repeat what those records state and add nothing to it.

## What the two policies mean

`verbatim`: the law or the publisher's terms allow the wording to be copied, so the official wording is committed. It sits in each record under `## Official wording`, and that section ends with a `Source:` line that names the publisher and the document. Every Dutch source is `verbatim`.

`description-only`: the source's terms do not grant this repo the right to reproduce its wording, and the repo is public, so the wording is not committed. A record of such a source holds its official code, its locator, the id of its source record and the hash of its wording (`wording_sha256`), and under `## Summary` a description in the pack's own words. Where a faithful summary could not differ from the official sentence, the record holds the line that says so and no summary. The wording itself is kept in a store outside the repo. Every California source is `description-only`, and the validator rejects a California record marked `verbatim`.

Two kinds of fragment of description-only wording are committed all the same, because the tools that read the sources need them:

- A locator file (`education/locators/us-ca/`) holds the bounding words of each span it locates, at most four words an anchor, and the two sides of each correction of the extracted text, at most four words a side. The two anchors of a span together hold at most half of the words they locate. A located text of three words or fewer cannot be bounded with less, so that limit does not apply to it and its anchors may hold it whole. Two spans are of that kind, each a table cell of two words that says the earlier age range has no foundation: in `us-ca/preschool-tk/mathematics` under Strand 3.0, code 3.3, and in `us-ca/preschool-tk/reading-language` under Strand 4.0, code 4.2.
- `education/manifest/us-ca-additions.ts` holds the two sides of each export correction, at most four words a side.

The extraction and the California importer enforce these limits (`WORD_CAP`, `ANCHOR_SHARE_ONE_IN` and `ANCHOR_SHARE_FROM` in `education/tools/extract.ts`), and the extraction names each span that is too short for the limit of half every time it runs. Nothing else in the repo may hold description-only wording, whole or in part. A code, the printed name of a document, a domain, a strand or a foundation, and the label of an age range are not wording in this sense, and records, frames and locators hold them as printed.

No permission covers these fragments: the department's website copyright statement asks permission for any part of the site's material. Whether the repo keeps them is the owner's decision, together with the question in the next section.

The design notes and the English gloss are the pack's own text under either policy.

## Permission has not been asked

No publisher has been asked for permission to commit California wording. Whether to ask the California Department of Education, and the California Department of Social Services for the infant and toddler foundations, is an open question for the owner. Until there is permission, every California source stays `description-only`, and `npm run education:overlap` and `npm run education:reuse-history` are run before every push.
