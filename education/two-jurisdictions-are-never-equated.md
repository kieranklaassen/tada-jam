---
title: A record from California is never presented as equal to, or a translation of, a record from the Netherlands, and a game for both names records from each
applies_when:
  - designing a learning game for children in California and in the Netherlands
  - planning a game with a learning goal that is played in English and in Dutch
  - writing a game's ART.md or PR text that names standards from two countries
  - reviewing a game that claims to follow a curriculum in more than one country
tags: [learning-games, curriculum, california, netherlands, jurisdictions]
---

The pack records each jurisdiction's statements as they are published and states no relation between them. The lookup never puts the two in one list. Two statements about the same skill can differ in the age they are for, in what they bound, and in standing.

Do:

- A game for both jurisdictions names `us-ca` records and `nl` records under separate headings, each with its own standing and check state.
- A difference between the two sets is written as a difference, and the plan says which one the game follows at that point.
- Where one jurisdiction has no record for the skill at that age, the plan says so and names nothing in its place.
- A claim about what Dutch schools work towards rests on `nl` records, and a claim about California rests on `us-ca` records, whatever language the game is played in.
- A lookup by `--code` without `--jurisdiction` can return matches in both jurisdictions. They are printed in separate lists and are unrelated.

Do not:

- No "equivalent to", "the Dutch version of", "corresponds to" or "maps to" between a `us-ca` record and an `nl` record, and no table that pairs them.
- The English gloss of a Dutch record is the pack's gloss. It is not an official translation and is never cited as a California statement.
- No record of one jurisdiction is used to fill a gap in the other.

Reason: the two frameworks are written by different authorities for different school systems, and the pack has checked each record only against its own source. An equivalence would be a claim nobody has checked.
