## Official wording

ontdekken en benoemen van duidelijke uiterlijke kenmerken van mensen en overeenkomsten of verschillen met andere mensen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering and naming clear outward features of people and similarities or differences with other people

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks in a mirror next to another child and says what they see: curly hair, brown eyes, tall, small. The child lays a hand next to an adult's hand and says whose is bigger, or notices that two children both have a braid. A child of 2 points at one feature such as hair; a child of 4 says "we both have ..." or "you have ..., I have ...".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The features are clear outward ones, so features that can be seen; the statement gives no examples and no list. It speaks of similarities or differences with other people. It does not mention character or behaviour.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age notice one striking feature and treat it as the whole person: every man with a beard is "grandpa". They name differences more easily than similarities. They say a difference aloud without knowing that the remark can hurt.
