## Official wording

ervaren dat het uiterlijk van planten en bomen verandert met de seizoenen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing that the appearance of plants and trees changes with the seasons

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child visits the same tree several times in a year: kicks through its fallen leaves, touches the bare branches, later finds buds and blossom. The child gathers chestnuts and coloured leaves in autumn and picks daisies in spring. A child of 2 throws leaves in the air and says "leaves"; a child of 4 says that the tree had leaves before and has none now.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The verb is experiencing; the statement asks for no explanation and no names of seasons. It is about how plants and trees look. Animals are not in this statement, and the influence of the seasons on people is in `Weer, klimaat en hemellichamen / 2`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often think that a bare tree is dead or broken. They do not connect the tree in winter with the same tree in summer: the visits are months apart, and without a photo they do not remember how it looked.
