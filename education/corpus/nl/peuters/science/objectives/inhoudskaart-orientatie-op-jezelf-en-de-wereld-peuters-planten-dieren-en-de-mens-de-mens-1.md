## Official wording

aanwijzen en benoemen van onderdelen van het eigen lichaam

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

pointing to and naming parts of one's own body

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child touches their own nose, ear, belly or knee when an adult names it, and says the word when the adult points. The child does this while washing, while dressing, or in a song with movements. A child of 2 points to a few large parts such as nose and foot; a child of 4 also names smaller parts such as elbow, chin and heel.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement is about the child's own body, and about two actions: pointing and naming. It names no body parts and sets no number of them. It does not mention what a part is for.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age point correctly well before they can say the word, so pointing and naming do not come at the same time. They mix up parts that are close together or alike: elbow and knee, wrist and ankle, chin and cheek. Parts they cannot see on themselves, such as the back or the neck, come later.
