## Official wording

praten over weersverschijnselen zoals wind, regen, storm en sneeuw en de relatie met de seizoenen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

talking about weather phenomena such as wind, rain, storm and snow and the relation with the seasons

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child stands at the window or outside and says what the weather is doing: "rain", "wind", "cold". The child holds out a hand to catch rain or snow, watches leaves blow, and talks with an adult about what goes with winter or summer, such as snow with a coat and a hat. A child of 2 names one thing with a single word while feeling it; a child of 4 says a short sentence and links snow to winter.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The activity is talking; the statement does not ask for an explanation of how weather comes about. Wind, rain, storm and snow are given as examples (*zoals*). The statement names the relation with the seasons, but not the names or the order of the seasons, and it sets no number of weather words.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age take the weather of today for the season: a cold day in summer is called winter. They tie a season to one event of their own, such as a birthday or a holiday, and not to the weather. Many use one word for several things, such as "rain" for snow and hail.
