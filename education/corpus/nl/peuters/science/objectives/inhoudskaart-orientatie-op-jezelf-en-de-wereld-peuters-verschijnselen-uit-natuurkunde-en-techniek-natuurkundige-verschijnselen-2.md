## Official wording

ontdekken en verwonderen over: licht (licht en donker), geluid (volume, toonhoogte), temperatuur (warmte), kracht (van water, van magneten) en elektriciteit (lamp)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering and wondering about: light (light and dark), sound (volume, pitch), temperature (warmth), force (of water, of magnets) and electricity (lamp)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child switches a torch on and off in a dark tent, hits a drum softly and hard, and feels a warm cup and a cold one. The child holds a hand in the stream of water from a tap, picks up paper clips with a magnet, and presses a switch so that a lamp goes on. A child of 2 repeats the action and looks surprised; a child of 4 says what happens, such as "the magnet takes it" or "now it is dark".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The verbs are discovering and wondering about; the statement asks for no explanation and no measuring. The brackets name light and dark for light, volume and pitch for sound, warmth for temperature, water and magnets for force, and a lamp for electricity. The statement states no safety condition for electricity or warmth.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age think the switch or the torch itself is the light, and do not link the lamp to electricity. They expect a magnet to pull everything, or everything that looks like metal. They use "loud" and "high" for the same thing and cannot yet tell volume from pitch.
