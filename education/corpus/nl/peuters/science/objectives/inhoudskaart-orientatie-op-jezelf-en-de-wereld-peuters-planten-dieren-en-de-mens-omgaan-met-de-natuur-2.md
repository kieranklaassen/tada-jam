## Official wording

ervaren van schoonheid en esthetische waarde van natuur (met planten en dieren)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing the beauty and aesthetic value of nature (with plants and animals)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child stops at a flower, a butterfly or a spider web with drops on it and keeps looking for some time. The child smells blossom, strokes soft moss, picks out the stone or leaf they find the most beautiful, and shows it. A child of 2 looks, touches and says "oh"; a child of 4 says "beautiful" and tells what they like about it.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The verb is experiencing: the statement asks for no words, no judgement and no product such as a drawing. The brackets name plants and animals. The statement names no place, no season and no kind of nature.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the stopping: they walk on, or they grab at once and pick or squeeze what they find beautiful, so that it breaks. What a child finds beautiful is often not what the adult points at.
