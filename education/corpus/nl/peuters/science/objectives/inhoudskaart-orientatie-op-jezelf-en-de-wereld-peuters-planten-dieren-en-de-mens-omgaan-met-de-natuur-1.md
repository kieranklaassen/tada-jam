## Official wording

ontmoeten, verwonderen en beseffen van de grote diversiteit aan planten, dieren en schimmels (paddenstoelen)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

encountering, wondering at and realising the great diversity of plants, animals and fungi (mushrooms)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child squats by a tree stump and finds a beetle, a snail, moss and a mushroom, and looks closely at each. The child lifts a stone to see what lives under it, collects different leaves in a basket, and shows an adult what was found. A child of 2 points, says "look" and wants to touch; a child of 4 says that there are many different ones and names a few.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The three verbs are encountering, wondering at and realising; none asks a child to name, sort or count kinds. The brackets name mushrooms (*paddenstoelen*) after the word fungi (*schimmels*). The statement sets no number of kinds and names no place where the child meets them.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often call everything small that crawls by one word ("a bug") and everything green "plant" or "grass", so the differences go unnoticed unless an adult looks with them. They take a mushroom for a plant or a flower. Some children are afraid to touch or to look closely.
