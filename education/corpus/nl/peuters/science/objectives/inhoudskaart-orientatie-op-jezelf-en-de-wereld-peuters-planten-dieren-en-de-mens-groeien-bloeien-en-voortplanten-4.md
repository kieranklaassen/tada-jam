## Official wording

ervaren dat mensen en dieren groeien en ouder worden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing that people and animals grow and get older

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child stands against the wall to be measured and sees that the mark is higher than last time, or finds that last year's shoes no longer fit. The child lays a photo of themselves as a baby next to a new one, and sees a kitten or a puppy that is bigger at each visit. A child of 2 says "big" and stretches up; a child of 4 says "I was a baby, now I am big".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The verb is experiencing; the statement asks for no words and no explanation. It names people and animals; plants are in `Groeien, bloeien en voortplanten / 1`. It does not mention ages in numbers, names of life stages, or death.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often take size for age: the tallest person is the oldest, and a small adult is taken for a child. They think that adults no longer get older, and that they can pass an older brother or sister in age by growing.
