## Official wording

verkennen van uiterlijke kenmerken van planten en dieren zoals vorm, kleur, bouw en beharing

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring outward features of plants and animals such as shape, colour, build and hair covering

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child strokes a dog's coat and a hairy leaf, feels a smooth shell and a prickly chestnut husk, and looks at the colours of a ladybird. The child lays two leaves side by side and sees that one is round and one is pointed. A child of 2 touches and says one word such as "soft" or "red"; a child of 4 says how two animals or two leaves differ.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The verb is exploring; the statement does not ask for naming or comparing. Shape, colour, build and hair covering (*beharing*) are given as examples (*zoals*). The statement sets no list of plants or animals.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age attend to one feature at a time, mostly colour or size, and overlook build. They group animals by one feature: everything with four legs and fur is "dog", everything that flies is "bird". They use "big" and "small" for every difference in shape.
