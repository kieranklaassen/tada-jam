## Official wording

verkennen en benoemen van dingen als resultaat van: ik zie, ik hoor, ik voel, ik ruik, ik proef

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring and naming things as a result of: I see, I hear, I feel, I smell, I taste

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child feels in a bag without looking and says what is in it, smells a jar and says "orange", hears a bell behind a screen and says "bell", and tastes a piece of fruit with eyes closed. The child says "I hear ..." or "I smell ..." after the adult's example. A child of 2 names the thing with one word; a child of 4 also says how they know: "I hear a car".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement lists five "I" sentences: I see, I hear, I feel, I smell, I taste. It asks for exploring and naming things. It does not mention the names of the sense organs, and it sets no list of things or words.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age say "I see" for everything they notice, also for a sound or a smell. They cannot yet tell what a thing is by one sense alone and want to look: they open their eyes or pull the object out of the bag. They have few words for taste and smell, mostly "nice" and "yuck".
