## Official wording

ervaren dat planten groeien en bloeien

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing that plants grow and flower

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child pushes a bean or some cress seed into wet soil or cotton wool, waters it, and looks again each day. The child holds a finger next to the stem to see that it is taller, and finds the bud that has opened into a flower. A child of 2 pours the water and points at the green; a child of 4 says that the plant was small before and is bigger now.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The verb is experiencing: the statement asks for no words, no explanation and no names of plant parts. It names growing and flowering only. What a plant needs in order to grow, and seeds or fruits, are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the waiting: growth is too slow to see, so they expect a change within minutes, dig the seed up to look, or lose interest before the plant flowers. Many do not see a plant as alive, because it does not move.
