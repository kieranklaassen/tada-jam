## Official wording

bespreken dat jonge dieren geboren worden of uit een ei komen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discussing that young animals are born or come out of an egg

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks at a hen with chicks, at a nest or at a picture book, and talks with an adult about where the young came from. The child holds an empty egg shell, or puts toy animals with their young: the lamb with the sheep, the chick with the hen. A child of 2 says "egg" or "baby sheep"; a child of 4 says that the chick came out of an egg and the lamb did not.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The activity is discussing. The statement names two ways, being born or coming out of an egg, and names no animals. The text sets no level of detail on mating or birth. It does not ask a child to say which animals lay eggs.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age think eggs come from hens only and are for eating; they do not link the egg on their plate to a chick. They assume that all young animals come in the same way, mostly the way of the animal they know best. Many do not see the young as the same kind of animal when it looks different, such as a tadpole and a frog.
