## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child stacks blocks into a tower until it falls, lays a plank across two blocks to make a bridge, and pushes large click bricks together and pulls them apart. The child tries which block stays on top and builds again after a collapse. A child of 2 stacks a few blocks and knocks them over on purpose; a child of 4 builds something they have planned, such as a garage, and puts wide blocks at the bottom.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement names construction materials, and no kinds, sizes or tools; it sets nothing to build. It stands under the sub-heading on technical principles and systems (*Technische principes en systemen*), and names no principle such as balance or joining.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age put a block half over the edge of the one below and do not see why the tower falls. They build upwards on a base that is too narrow. They press too hard or not hard enough to join two parts, and then stop.
