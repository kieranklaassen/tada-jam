## Official wording

waarnemen van herkenbare onderdelen van de plant en van het dier

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

observing recognisable parts of the plant and of the animal

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks at a plant and touches its leaf, stem and flower, and pulls up a weed to see the root. With an animal or a picture of one, the child finds the legs, tail, ears, beak or wings. A child of 2 points at a leaf or a tail when an adult names it; a child of 4 finds several parts and says some of their names.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The verb is observing; the statement does not ask for naming. "Recognisable" bounds it to parts a young child can make out; the statement itself lists no parts, so leaf, stem, legs or tail are not named in it. It does not mention what a part is for.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age use the words for their own body for every animal: a bird has "hands", a fish has "legs". They call every coloured part of a plant "flower" and every green part "leaf". They do not count the root as part of the plant, because they cannot see it.
