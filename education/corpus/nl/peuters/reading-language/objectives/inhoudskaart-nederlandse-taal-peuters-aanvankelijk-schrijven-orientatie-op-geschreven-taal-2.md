## Official wording

ontdekken dat tekens gebruikt worden om iets te vertellen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering that signs (*tekens*, written marks or characters) are used to tell something

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees an adult look at a label and then say whose cup it is. The child asks what the marks on a box mean. The child makes marks on paper and says "this says mama". A child of 2 notices that adults look at marks and then say or do something; a child of 4 makes marks of their own and tells an adult what they say.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says discovering that signs tell something, not reading what they tell. *Tekens* is a wide word that covers letters, numerals and other marks; the wording does not say which. The statement does not mention drawing; drawing is named in `Oriëntatie op geschreven taal / 3`.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that the writing next to a picture says the name of the picture. Thinking that the same marks say something different each time. Expecting a big thing to have a long written word and a small thing a short one.
