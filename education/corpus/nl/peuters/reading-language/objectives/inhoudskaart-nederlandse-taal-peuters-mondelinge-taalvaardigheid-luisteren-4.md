## Official wording

luisteren naar en begrijpen van een verhaal (eenvoudige chronologische structuur met korte zinnen) (bijv. tijdens of na het voorlezen passende prenten aanwijzen)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

listening to and understanding a story (simple chronological structure with short sentences) (e.g. pointing to fitting pictures during or after the reading aloud)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to a short picture book in which the events follow each other in order. When the adult asks "where is the bear sleeping?", the child points to the picture that fits. A child of 2 points to a figure or thing in the picture when it is named; a child of 4 points to the picture that fits an event and says what happened after it.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The story has a simple chronological structure and short sentences; that is stated in brackets without "e.g.". Pointing to fitting pictures during or after the reading aloud is given as an example of what the child does. The statement sets no length for the story. Retelling is not mentioned here; it is the example in `Luisteren / 8`.

### Common mistakes

Pointing to the picture they like best or the one with the most to see, not the one that fits. Understanding each page separately without linking it to the page before. Not knowing who "he" or "it" refers to in a later sentence.
