## Official wording

verschil ontdekken tussen 'schrijven' en 'lezen'

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering the difference between 'writing' and 'reading'

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees one adult write a note and another adult read it aloud later. The child makes scribbles on a list and then "reads" the list back; the child holds a book and says "I am reading", and holds a pencil and says "I am writing". A child of 2 copies both acts, looking in a book and moving a pencil over paper, without naming them; a child of 4 says "I am writing" while making marks and "you read it" when handing the paper over.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says discovering the difference; it does not ask the child to read or write. The source puts both words in quotation marks and does not explain why.

### Common mistakes

Using the words "read" and "write" for each other. Calling drawing "writing", or writing "drawing". Thinking that reading is telling about the pictures or saying a text from memory.
