## Official wording

herkennen van verschillende vormen van schriftelijke communicatie uit de eigen leefwereld (bijv. sturen en ontvangen van berichten zoals met Whatsapp)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising different forms of written communication from one's own everyday world (e.g. sending and receiving messages such as with Whatsapp)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child watches a parent type a message and hears that it is for grandma. The child sees a postcard arrive, a shopping list being written and a note on the door, and plays at these: tapping on a toy phone, putting a drawing in an envelope and handing it over. A child of 2 copies the tapping and hands over a "letter"; a child of 4 says that a card, a list and a phone message are each a way of telling someone something.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording is about forms of written communication that occur in the child's own everyday world. Sending and receiving messages is given as an example, and Whatsapp as an example of that. The wording says recognising: it does not say that the child reads or writes messages or handles a device.

### Common mistakes

Not telling writing apart from drawing. Calling every piece of paper with writing on it by one name, such as "a letter". Thinking that a written message says whatever the child wants it to say.
