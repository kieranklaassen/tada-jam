## Official wording

uitspreken van alle klinkers en medeklinkers in een woord zoals ook onbeklemtoonde lettergrepen en alle achter elkaar staande medeklinkers (bijv. 'banaan' i.p.v. 'naan', 'trap' i.p.v. 'tap')

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

pronouncing all vowels and consonants in a word, including unstressed syllables and all consonants that stand one after another (e.g. '*banaan*' (banana) instead of '*naan*', '*trap*' (stairs) instead of '*tap*')

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says the names of things while playing and eating, and says a word again after an adult has repeated it in full. The child sings songs that hold words with several consonants in a row. A child of 2 often says a long word without its unstressed syllable and says one consonant where two stand together; a child of 4 says most words with all their syllables and still shortens some groups of consonants.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording names all vowels and consonants in a word, and after *zoals ook* ("such as also", glossed as "including") it names unstressed syllables and consonants that stand one after another. The two word pairs are examples. The statement does not say which sounds; forming the sounds of Dutch is the subject of `Spreken / vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing / 2`. It sets no age within these years.

### Common mistakes

Leaving out an unstressed syllable, as in *naan* for *banaan*. Saying one consonant where two or three stand together, as in *tap* for *trap*. Leaving off the last consonant of a word.
