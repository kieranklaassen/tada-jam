## Official wording

verkennen van schrijven (krabbels) (bijv. eigen naam, bij tekeningen of eigengemaakte boodschappenlijstjes) - vooral via tekenen, krabbelen, letterachtige vormen, letter-/tekenreeksen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring writing (scribbles) (e.g. own name, with drawings or self-made shopping lists) - mainly through drawing, scribbling, letter-like shapes, strings of letters or signs

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child makes wavy lines under a drawing and says that it is their name. In pretend play the child makes a shopping list of zigzag lines, and makes rows of circles and sticks on paper. A child of 2 makes scribbles and dots and may say afterwards that it is writing; a child of 4 makes separate letter-like shapes in a row, often with letters from their own name, and "reads" them back.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says exploring, with "scribbles" in brackets. The examples are the child's own name, writing with drawings, and self-made shopping lists. The sub-point after the dash says this happens mainly (*vooral*) through drawing, scribbling, letter-like shapes and strings of letters or signs. The statement does not mention correct spelling or how the child holds the pencil.

### Common mistakes

Writing letters mirrored and in any direction across the page. Using the same string of letters, usually those of their own name, for every word. Making the writing for a big thing longer than for a small thing.
