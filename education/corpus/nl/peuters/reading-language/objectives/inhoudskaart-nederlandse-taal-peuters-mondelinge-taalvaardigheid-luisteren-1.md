## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

developing story comprehension

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to a short picture book, points to the characters when they are named and says what happened. The child plays the story with dolls or objects, and with a familiar book says what comes on the next page. A child of 2 points at characters and things that are named; a child of 4 says who was in the story, what happened and how it ended.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording names story comprehension and nothing more: no kind of story, no length and no way of showing understanding. The statement `Luisteren / 4` names a story with a simple chronological structure and short sentences.

### Common mistakes

Remembering separate events or pictures without their order. Telling about something that happened to themselves, which the story reminded them of, in place of the story. Not knowing why a character does something.
