## Official wording

bewust worden van klanken in woorden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

becoming aware of sounds in words

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child claps or stamps once for each part of a name while saying it, and listens while an adult says a word very slowly. The child hears an adult say two words that start the same, one after the other, and repeats them. A child of 2 enjoys the stretched and repeated sounds and copies them; a child of 4 says that two words begin with the same sound, or that one word takes longer to say than another.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says becoming aware. It does not ask the child to name sounds, to split a word into sounds or to join sounds into a word, and it does not say which sounds or which place in the word. The statement does not mention letters.

### Common mistakes

Attending to what a word means and not to how it sounds, for example saying that "train" is a long word because a train is long. Hearing the parts of a word (syllables) or its rhyming end long before hearing one single sound in it.
