## Official wording

beantwoorden van verschillende type vragen met korte taaluitingen en eventueel ondersteunt door gebaren (vraagtypen zoals aanwijsvragen; luistervragen; wie-wat-waar-vragen; keuzevragen (tussen twee dingen kiezen); voorspelvragen)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

answering different types of questions with short utterances, possibly supported by gestures (question types such as pointing questions (*aanwijsvragen*); listening questions (*luistervragen*); who-what-where questions; choice questions (choosing between two things); prediction questions)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Asked "where is the cat?", the child points to it in the book. Asked "do you want apple or pear?", the child says "pear"; asked "who is that?", the child says "grandpa"; asked "what will happen now?", the child says "fall!". A child of 2 answers pointing questions and choice questions with a point or one word; a child of 4 answers who-what-where questions and prediction questions in a short sentence.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The answers are short utterances, and gestures may support them. The five question types follow "such as", so they are examples; the choice question is between two things. The example list holds no why-questions or how-questions. The card does not explain *luistervragen*. The source prints *ondersteunt* where *ondersteund* (supported) is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Choosing the option that was named last in a choice question, whatever they want. Saying "yes" to a question they have not understood. Answering a "who" or "where" question as if it asked "what".
