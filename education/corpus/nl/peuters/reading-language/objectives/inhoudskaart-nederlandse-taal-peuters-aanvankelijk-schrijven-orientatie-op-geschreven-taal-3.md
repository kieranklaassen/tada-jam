## Official wording

ervaren dat tekenen en tekens mogelijkheden bieden tot communicatie

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing that drawing and signs offer possibilities for communication

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child makes a drawing for someone and gives it to them; an adult asks what it shows and writes the child's words underneath. The child puts a drawing or a mark on a list of things to buy, and holds up a picture card to show what they want. A child of 2 hands a scribble to an adult and names it afterwards; a child of 4 draws something on purpose to tell or ask something, and adds marks as "writing".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says experiencing, and it names both drawing and signs (*tekens*, written marks or characters). It does not ask for letters. It does not say with whom the child communicates.

### Common mistakes

Expecting the other person to know what the drawing means without being told. Giving a different meaning to the same drawing or marks from one moment to the next.
