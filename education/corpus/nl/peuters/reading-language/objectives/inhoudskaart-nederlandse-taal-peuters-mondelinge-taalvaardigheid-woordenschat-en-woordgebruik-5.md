## Official wording

(nieuwe) woorden vormen (morfologie)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

forming (new) words (morphology)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While playing, the child says plurals and diminutives, such as *blokken* (blocks) and *autootje* (little car). The child tells what happened with past forms of verbs, and makes up a compound word for a thing whose name they do not know. A child of 2 begins to use plurals and diminutives; a child of 4 forms past tenses and makes up compound words of their own.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. "New" stands in brackets, so the wording covers both forms of existing words and newly made words. The brackets after it give the term morphology. The statement names no word forms: plurals, diminutives and verb forms are not listed.

### Common mistakes

Applying the regular rule to an irregular verb, as in *loopte* for *liep* (walked) or *gevalt* for *gevallen* (fallen). Giving a word the wrong plural ending, as in *koes* for *koeien* (cows).
