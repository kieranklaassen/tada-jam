## Official wording

stellen van vragen om ergens meer over te weten te komen (bijv. wie, wat, waar, waarom) of hulp te krijgen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

asking questions to find out more about something (e.g. who, what, where, why) or to get help

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child points at an object and asks "what is that?", or asks "where is mama?" and "why?". The child holds out their coat to an adult and asks for help with the zip. A child of 2 asks with a point, a rising tone and one word such as "that?"; a child of 4 asks why-questions and asks for help in a sentence.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording names two purposes: finding out more about something, and getting help. Who, what, where and why are given as examples. The statement does not say that the questions are correctly formed.

### Common mistakes

Pulling at the adult or crying instead of asking. Asking "why?" again and again without listening to the answer. Leaving out the question word or the verb, so that the question sounds like a statement.
