## Official wording

ontdekken van relaties tussen klanken en letters in een speelse context (alfabetisch principe)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering relations between sounds and letters in a playful context (alphabetic principle)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds the first letter of their own name on their coat hook, their cup or a drawing and points to it. The child hears an adult say a sound while pointing to a letter on a block or a stamp, and says the sound too. A child of 2 treats the letter as a mark that belongs to them; a child of 4 links a few letters to a sound or to a person, for example "the m of mama".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says discovering, and it says in a playful context. It names sounds and letters and gives the term alphabetic principle in brackets. It does not say which letters or how many, and it does not mention reading words or writing letters.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that the first letter of their name is their whole name, or that it belongs to them alone. Thinking that a letter stands for a whole word or a thing and not for one sound. Giving the name of a letter where its sound is meant.
