## Official wording

gesprekjes voeren met de leidster en/of een ander kind

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having short conversations with the childcare worker (*leidster*, the woman who leads the group) and/or another child

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While eating fruit or having a nappy changed, the child tells the adult what they see, answers a question and adds something. In the sandpit the child talks with another child about who has the spade. A child of 2 joins in with words and gestures and soon turns back to play; a child of 4 keeps to one subject for longer and also talks with another child without an adult's help.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The Dutch uses the diminutive *gesprekjes*, glossed here as "short conversations". The wording names the childcare worker and one other child as partners; a conversation with a whole group is not mentioned. The statement sets no subject and no number of turns.

### Common mistakes

Not responding to what the other said and starting on a subject of their own. Talking at the same time as the other. Assuming that the listener knows who or what they mean, for example saying "he did it" without saying who.
