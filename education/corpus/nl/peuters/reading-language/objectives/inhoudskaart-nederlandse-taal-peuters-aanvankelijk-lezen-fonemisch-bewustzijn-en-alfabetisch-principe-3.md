## Official wording

spelen van spelletjes rond taal (woord- en klankspelletjes)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: Ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

playing games around language (word and sound games)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says the last word of a line when an adult stops in a verse they both know. The child corrects an adult who says a familiar word wrongly on purpose, and feels an object in a bag and says its name. A child of 2 fills in a missing word in a familiar song; a child of 4 makes up nonsense words that sound like a real word and repeats them to another child.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The brackets name word games and sound games. The wording does not say which games, how long they last or who leads them. The statement does not mention letters or written words.

### Common mistakes

Answering a sound game by meaning: asked for a word that sounds like *kat* (cat), the child says "dog". Forgetting the rule of the game after one or two turns and going back to naming things.
