## Official wording

benoemen wat wel en niet leuk is (en daarmee omgaan)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming what is and is not fun (and dealing with that)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says "I like painting" and "I don't like the swing", or pushes away the plate with cucumber and says "no". When the group does something it does not like, such as putting on coats, it says so and then joins in, with or without an adult's help. A child of 2 says "no" or "again" and shows the rest with its body; a child of 4 says in a sentence what it likes and does not like, and more often accepts that the disliked thing happens anyway.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement has two parts: naming what is and is not fun, and, in brackets, dealing with that. The Dutch *leuk* covers fun, nice and liked. It does not say what "dealing with that" looks like, and it does not say whether activities, things or people are meant.

### Common mistakes

A child says "no" to everything during a period in which it practises refusing, including things it likes. A child can name what it does not like and cannot yet bear that it happens anyway. A child says it does not like something it has never tried.
