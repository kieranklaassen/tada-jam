## Official wording

reageren (verbaal en non-verbaal) op signalen van andere mensen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reacting (verbally and non-verbally) to signals from other people

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child waves back when someone waves, comes when an adult beckons, and stops when another child holds up a hand or says "stop". It hands back a toy when the other child reaches for it and cries, and asks "what's wrong?". A child of 2 reacts to clear signals such as an outstretched hand, a call or crying; a child of 4 also reacts to a look or a tone of voice and answers in words.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The brackets name both ways of reacting: verbal and non-verbal. The signals come from other people, so adults are included as well as children. The sub-heading is about being open to another person's emotions; the statement does not say which signals.

### Common mistakes

A child notices a signal and goes on with what it was doing. A child misses a quiet signal, such as turning away, and reacts only to crying or shouting. A child copies the signal, for example by crying along, instead of answering it.
