## Official wording

omgaan met verhoudingen in een groep

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with the relations (*verhoudingen*, how members stand towards one another) in a group

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child plays in a group in which one child decides what happens and others follow, and finds its own place: it joins, follows, proposes something, or walks away. It notices that the adult decides some things and that older children can do more. A child of 2 mostly stays near the adult and one familiar child; a child of 4 has preferred playmates and sometimes leads a small play scene.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording is general: it names relations in a group and no kind of relation, no group size and no behaviour. The Dutch *verhoudingen* covers how members stand towards one another, including who leads and who follows.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is the other side of a relation: a child who always leads does not notice that the others stop following, and a child who always follows does not say what it wants itself.
