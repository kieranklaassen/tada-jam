## Official wording

belangstelling en sympathie tonen voor andere kinderen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

showing interest in and liking (*sympathie*, a warm feeling towards someone) for other children

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child walks up to a child who comes in, smiles, and shows the car it holds. It asks "what are you doing?", sits down next to a child it likes, or keeps a seat free for that child. A child of 2 shows interest by coming close, looking and touching; a child of 4 uses the other child's name, asks a question and has clear favourites.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording names showing interest and showing liking; the Dutch *sympathie* means a warm feeling towards someone, not pity. It does not say towards how many children or in what way. Comforting is a separate statement, `Ervaren wat het vraagt om in een groep deel te nemen / 5`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is checking whether the other child wants the attention: a child shows liking by hugging, grabbing, or taking the other child's toy to look at it, and does not see that the other child pulls back.
