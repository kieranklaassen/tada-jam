## Official wording

kinderen spelenderwijs worden uitgedaagd in de ontwikkeling van hun motorische vaardigheden, cognitieve vaardigheden, taalvaardigheden en creatieve vaardigheden, teneinde kinderen in staat te stellen steeds zelfstandiger te functioneren in een veranderende omgeving;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kwaliteit kinderopvang, artikel 2, onderdeel b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

children are challenged through play in the development of their motor skills, cognitive skills, language skills and creative skills, in order to enable children to function more and more independently in a changing environment;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 2 to 4 in childcare climbs, rides a tricycle and threads beads; fits puzzle pieces and sorts blocks; listens to a story and talks about it; paints, builds and sings. The child puts on their own shoes and pours their own drink, and gets a task that is a little harder than the last one. A child of 2 tries a task with an adult close by; a child of 4 chooses a harder puzzle and finishes it alone.

### Limits

This is a legal requirement on the childcare provider, not on the child: it says what the provider sees to, and sets nothing a child must be able to do. It is one lettered part of an article; the article's opening words, which are in this record's locator and not in its wording, say that the holder (*houder*) or the childminder (*gastouder*) sees to this in any case, taking account of the stage of development the children are in. The part names four kinds of skills: motor, cognitive, language and creative. The way is through play (*spelenderwijs*), and the aim is that children can function more and more independently in a changing environment. It names no ages, no content within a kind of skill and no level to reach.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often stop when a task is too hard and go back to what they can already do. Many refuse help ("I do it myself") and then cannot go on. They choose the same favourite activity each day and leave the other kinds out unless an adult offers them.
