## Official wording

naast elkaar spelen in eenzelfde situatie (hoek, spel)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

playing next to each other in the same situation (corner, *spel*, meaning game or play)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children sit in the same play corner, each with its own pan and spoon, each cooking by itself. They look at what the other does and sometimes copy it, without making one plan together. A child of 2 plays beside another child and hardly speaks to it; a child of 4 moves between playing beside and playing with the other child, for example by handing it a plate.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording is playing next to each other, not playing together: it asks for no shared plan and no talk. The brackets name a corner (a play area of the room) and *spel*, a word that means both a game and play. It sets no number of children and no length of time.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is attention for the other child's space and things: a child takes material from the neighbour's pile or sits on the spot the other child was using, without seeing that this disturbs the other child.
