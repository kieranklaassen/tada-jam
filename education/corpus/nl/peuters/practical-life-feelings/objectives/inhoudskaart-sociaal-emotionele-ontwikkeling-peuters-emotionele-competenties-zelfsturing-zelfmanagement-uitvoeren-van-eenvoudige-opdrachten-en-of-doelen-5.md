## Official wording

grotendeels zichzelf kunnen redden (maar heeft wel hulp nodig bij het aan- en uitkleden, vooral bij ritsen en knopen openmaken)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

being able to manage largely by oneself (but does need help with dressing and undressing, especially with undoing zips and buttons)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child eats with a spoon or fork, drinks from an open cup, washes and dries its hands, and fetches its own coat and shoes. It pulls its trousers up and down and puts its arms in the sleeves, and asks an adult to undo the zip or the buttons. A child of 2 does parts of these actions and an adult finishes them; a child of 4 does most of them alone and still asks for help with fastenings.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says largely: the child manages most things by itself. The brackets state where help is still needed: dressing and undressing, especially undoing zips and buttons. The statement lists no other self-care actions; eating, washing hands and using the toilet are not named.

### Common mistakes

A child puts a shoe on the wrong foot or a coat on back to front. A child pulls at a zip or button with force instead of using two hands for the two sides. A child insists on doing it alone and does not ask for help when it cannot go on, or asks for help at once with something it can do.
