## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring what the limits are in conflicts

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a quarrel over a doll a child pulls, shouts "mine" or pushes, and finds out what happens next: the other child cries, pushes back or lets go, and the adult steps in at hitting or biting. The child tries saying "stop" or holding up a hand and sees whether that works. A child of 2 uses its body first; a child of 4 more often uses words and looks to the adult before going further.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The verb is exploring: the statement is about finding out where the limits lie, not about keeping to them. It does not say which limits or who sets them: the other child, the adult or the rules of the group. It names no kind of conflict.

### Common mistakes

A child takes the limit to depend on who is watching, and goes further when no adult is near. A child does not read the other child's signals (crying, turning away) as a limit. A child holds to its own limit ("mine") and sees none on the other child's side.
