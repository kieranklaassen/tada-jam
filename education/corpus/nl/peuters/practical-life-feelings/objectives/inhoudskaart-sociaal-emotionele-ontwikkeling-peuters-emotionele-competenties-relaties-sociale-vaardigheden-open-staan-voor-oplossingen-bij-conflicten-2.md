## Official wording

proberen mee te denken over oplossingen bij conflicten

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

trying to think along about solutions in conflicts

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a quarrel over one bucket, the adult asks what could be done, and a child answers "another bucket" or "me first, then you", or fetches a second bucket. The child nods or shakes its head at a proposal from the adult or the other child. A child of 2 accepts or refuses a solution an adult offers; a child of 4 proposes one itself, most often taking turns or a second object.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says trying and thinking along: the child takes part in looking for a solution, and nothing says that the child finds one or carries it out alone. The sub-heading is about being open to solutions in conflicts. It names no kind of conflict and no kind of solution.

### Common mistakes

A child proposes only the solution in which it gets the thing itself. A child agrees to a solution and does not keep to it a minute later. A child cannot think along while it is still crying or angry; the thinking comes after it has calmed down.
