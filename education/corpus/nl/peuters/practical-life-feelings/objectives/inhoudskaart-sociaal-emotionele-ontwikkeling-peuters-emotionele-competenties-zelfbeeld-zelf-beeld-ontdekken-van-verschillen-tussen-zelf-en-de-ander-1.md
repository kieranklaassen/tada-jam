## Official wording

benoemen van zichzelf als 'ik'

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming oneself as 'I'

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says "I do it" and "I want milk" instead of using its own name. It points to itself in a photograph or a mirror and says "that's me". A child of 2 often still says its own name ("Mila do it") or "me"; a child of 4 uses "I" and "you" correctly in conversation.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement is about one word, "I" (*ik*), used by the child for itself. It stands under a sub-heading about discovering differences between oneself and the other person. Other pronouns are not named here; the word "we" is in `Deel uitmaken van een groep / 1`.

### Common mistakes

A child refers to itself by its name. A child says "you" for itself, because that is what it hears others call it. A child uses "me" or "mine" in the place of "I".
