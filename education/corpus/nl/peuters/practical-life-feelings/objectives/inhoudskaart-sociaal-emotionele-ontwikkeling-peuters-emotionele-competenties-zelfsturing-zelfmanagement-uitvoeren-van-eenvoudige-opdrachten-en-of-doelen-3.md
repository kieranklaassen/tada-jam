## Official wording

(zelfstandig) starten met een eenvoudige opdracht of bekende taak

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

starting (independently) on a simple assignment or familiar task

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child hears "fetch your cup", walks to the tray, takes its own cup and brings it to the table. When fruit time begins it climbs onto its chair, and after painting it goes to the tap to wash its hands, without waiting for an adult to lead it. A child of 2 starts when the task is one step and the adult points; a child of 4 starts a familiar routine by itself and begins a short assignment after hearing it once.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement is about starting, not about finishing. The assignment is simple or the task is familiar; "independently" stands in brackets. It gives no number of steps and no examples.

### Common mistakes

A child waits for the adult to repeat the request to it personally, after it was said to the whole group. A child starts walking and forgets on the way what it was going to do. A child starts with the part it likes and skips the first step.
