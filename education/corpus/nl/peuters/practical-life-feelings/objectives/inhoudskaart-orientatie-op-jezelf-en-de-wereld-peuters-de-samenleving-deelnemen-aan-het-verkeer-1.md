behaving safely in the street (walking, playing)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child walks on the pavement holding an adult's hand, stops at the kerb when the adult stops, and looks both ways together with the adult before crossing. While playing outside, the child stays on the pavement or in the playground and leaves a ball that rolls onto the road for the adult. A child of 2 stops only when held or called; a child of 4 stops at the kerb alone most of the time and says "wait, a car".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The brackets name walking and playing. The statement does not mention cycling, traffic rules, signs or traffic lights, and it does not say that a child does this without an adult.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age run after a ball or towards a person without looking: they act on the impulse although they know the rule. They think a driver sees them whenever they see the car. They cannot judge how fast a car is coming or from which side a sound comes.
