## Official wording

kunnen troosten of 'goed-maken'

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

being able to comfort or 'make up' (*goed-maken*, putting right what went wrong)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child walks to a child who cries, strokes its arm, or brings its cuddly toy. After knocking over another child's tower, it helps to rebuild the tower, gives back the block it took, or holds out a hand. A child of 2 comforts by offering what comforts itself, such as its own cuddly toy; a child of 4 asks or looks at what the other child wants.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says comforting or making up, joined by "or"; it does not ask for both. It prescribes no form: saying sorry is not mentioned. It does not say whether an adult prompts the child.

### Common mistakes

A child offers its own comfort object, which the other child may not want. A child says "sorry" as a word without doing anything to repair the harm. A child comforts too roughly, for example by hugging a child who wants to be left alone.
