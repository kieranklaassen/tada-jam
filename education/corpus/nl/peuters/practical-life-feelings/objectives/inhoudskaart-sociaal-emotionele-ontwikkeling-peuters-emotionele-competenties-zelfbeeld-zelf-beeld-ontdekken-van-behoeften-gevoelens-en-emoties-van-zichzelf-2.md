## Official wording

tonen van eigen gevoelens (non-verbale wijze)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

showing one's own feelings (non-verbal way)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child laughs, claps and jumps when it is glad. It frowns, turns away, stamps or cries when something goes wrong, and hides behind an adult's leg when it is afraid. A child of 2 shows each feeling at once and with the whole body; a child of 4 also shows smaller signs, such as a pout or crossed arms, and sometimes holds a feeling back.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The brackets limit this statement to the non-verbal way: face, body, sound and gesture. Words for feelings are not asked here. It does not say which feelings.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is a signal others can read: a child becomes quiet or withdraws when it is sad or afraid, and nobody notices. A child's body can also show a feeling more strongly than the cause explains, because tiredness or hunger adds to it.
