## Official wording

uiten van eigen wil

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

expressing one's own will

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says "no", "I want that" or "do it myself", pushes away the spoon an adult holds out, and pulls the adult by the hand to the thing it wants. It picks its own cup and refuses the other one. A child of 2 expresses its will with "no", with its body and sometimes with a tantrum; a child of 4 says what it wants in a sentence and sometimes gives a reason.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording names only expressing one's own will. It does not say that the will is followed, and it sets no form in which the child expresses it. The sub-heading is about getting a grip on one's own feelings.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is saying what they do want: a child says "no" and screams, and the adult has to guess the wish. A child also goes on refusing after it has been given what it asked for.
