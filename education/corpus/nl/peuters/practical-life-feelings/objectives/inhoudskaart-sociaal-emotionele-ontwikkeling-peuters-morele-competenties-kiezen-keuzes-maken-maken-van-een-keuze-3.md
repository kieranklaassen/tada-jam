## Official wording

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making the choice clear verbally

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says "that one", "the red one" or "I want the bike" instead of only pointing or grabbing. Asked where it wants to play, it says "blocks" or "with Noor". A child of 2 says one word together with pointing; a child of 4 says its choice in a short sentence that someone can understand without seeing where it points.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement is about making the choice clear in words. It sets no sentence length and no vocabulary. `Maken van een keuze / 1`, about choosing between two options, does not say how the choice is shown.

### Common mistakes

A child says "that one" while nothing shows which one it means. A child answers "yes" to a question that offered two options. A child says one thing and takes the other.
