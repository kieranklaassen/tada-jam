## Official wording

plezier beleven in het leren van nieuwe dingen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking pleasure in learning new things

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child tries the new balance beam, falls off, laughs and climbs on again. It asks "what is that?" about an unfamiliar tool, wants to pour from the jug again after the first success, and shows the adult what it has just learned to do. A child of 2 repeats a new action many times with visible delight; a child of 4 asks for something harder and says "look what I can do".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. This is a statement about pleasure, not about performance: it names taking pleasure in learning new things and no particular things. It stands under the sub-heading about carrying out simple assignments and goals. It sets no pace and no result.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is a second attempt: when a new thing fails at first, a child of this age may turn away from it, and the pleasure returns once it succeeds at a smaller step. A child also avoids a new thing when a familiar one is available.
