## Official wording

weten dat 'nee' zeggen een reactie kan opleveren van de ander

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing that saying 'no' can bring a reaction from the other person

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says "no" when another child asks for its doll and sees that the other child walks away, cries or gets angry. It says "no" to the adult at tidying time and hears what the adult says back. A child of 2 says "no" and is surprised by what follows; a child of 4 looks at the other's face while it says "no" and sometimes adds "later" or "you can have this one".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The verb is knowing, and what is known is that saying "no" can bring a reaction from the other person. It does not say which reaction, and it does not tell the child to say "no" less often. The statement stands under the sub-heading about getting a grip on one's own feelings.

### Common mistakes

A child does not link the other child's crying or anger to its own "no". A child expects that its "no" ends the matter and is upset when the other insists. A child stops saying "no" altogether to a child who reacted strongly.
