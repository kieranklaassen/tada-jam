## Official wording

Voor de voorschoolse educatie wordt een programma gebruikt waarin op gestructureerde en samenhangende wijze de ontwikkeling wordt gestimuleerd op het gebied van taal, rekenen, motoriek en de sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit basisvoorwaarden kwaliteit voorschoolse educatie, artikel 5.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

For pre-school education (*voorschoolse educatie*, an early-years programme before primary school) a programme is used in which development is stimulated in a structured and coherent way in the area of language, arithmetic, motor skills and social-emotional development.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of two and a half to 4 in such a group takes part in a planned day: a picture book read aloud in a small group, a counting song with fingers, climbing and cutting, and play with other children in the home corner. One theme, such as the shop, comes back in the book, the song and the play corner for several weeks. A child of two and a half joins in for a few minutes and mostly watches and copies; a child of 4 answers questions about the book, counts a few objects and plays a part with others.

### Limits

This is a legal requirement on the provider of pre-school education, not on the child: it says that a programme is used and what that programme covers, and sets nothing a child must be able to do. The article names four areas: language, arithmetic, motor skills and social-emotional development. It asks that the programme stimulates development in a structured and coherent way. It names no programme, no content within an area, no ages and no level to reach. The starting age is in artikel 2, first paragraph (*eerste lid*), of the same decree: the offer is arranged so that a child can receive pre-school education from the day the child turns two and a half.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. In a planned group activity, children of this age most often stop paying attention after a few minutes, or watch without joining in. They join in the action and leave out the language: they make the movements of a counting song and do not say the number words.
