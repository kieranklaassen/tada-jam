## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing basic emotions (angry, happy, sad, afraid)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks at four faces, on cards or made by an adult, and points to the angry one, the happy one, the sad one and the frightened one. It makes each face itself in a mirror and says which is which. In a picture book it says that the bear is afraid and the rabbit is happy. A child of 2 tells happy from not happy; a child of 4 tells all four apart and names them.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The brackets name four basic emotions: angry, happy, sad and afraid. Other emotions, such as surprise, disgust, pride or jealousy, are not named. The statement stands under a sub-heading about the child's own needs, feelings and emotions; it does not say whether the emotions are told apart in the child itself, in others or in pictures.

### Common mistakes

A child tells happy from the other three first, and mixes up the unpleasant ones: sad and angry, or angry and afraid. A child reads the feeling from what happens in a picture and not from the face. A child sorts faces into "good" and "not good" and stops there.
