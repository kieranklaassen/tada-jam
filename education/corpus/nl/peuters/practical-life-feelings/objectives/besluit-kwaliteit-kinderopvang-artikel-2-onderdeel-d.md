## Official wording

kinderen worden gestimuleerd om op een open manier kennis te maken met de algemeen aanvaarde waarden en normen in de samenleving met het oog op een respectvolle omgang met anderen en een actieve participatie in de maatschappij.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kwaliteit kinderopvang, artikel 2, onderdeel d.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

children are stimulated to get to know, in an open way, the generally accepted values and norms in society with a view to respectful dealings with others and active participation in society.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 2 to 4 in childcare greets others on arrival, says "thank you", waits for a turn, and helps to set the table or tidy up. The child hears from the adult why something is done ("we wait, so that everyone gets a turn") and takes part in shared moments such as a birthday in the group. A child of 2 copies the greeting and the tidying; a child of 4 says the rule, asks why, and reminds other children of it.

### Limits

This is a legal requirement on the childcare provider, not on the child: it says what the provider sees to, and sets nothing a child must be able to do. It is one lettered part of an article; the article's opening words, which are in this record's locator and not in its wording, say that the holder (*houder*) or the childminder (*gastouder*) sees to this in any case, taking account of the stage of development the children are in. The part says that children get to know these values and norms "in an open way"; it does not list them and does not say what "generally accepted" (*algemeen aanvaarde*) covers. It gives two purposes: respectful dealings with others and active participation in society. It names no ages.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age follow a rule while the adult is watching and stop following it when the adult leaves. They think a rule holds only in the place where they learned it. They say a polite word as a routine, without a link to the other person.
