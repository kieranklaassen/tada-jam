## Official wording

herkennen van eigen gevoelens

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising one's own feelings

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child feels its face go hot and its fists tighten when a toy is taken, and says "angry" or stamps. It laughs and jumps when a parent arrives, and says "happy" when an adult asks. In front of a mirror it makes the face that fits how it feels. A child of 2 shows the feeling with its body and repeats the word an adult gives; a child of 4 says by itself "I am sad".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The verb is recognising and the object is the child's own feelings. It names no feelings; `Ontdekken van behoeften, gevoelens en emoties van zichzelf / 3` names four basic emotions. Showing feelings is a separate statement, `Ontdekken van behoeften, gevoelens en emoties van zichzelf / 2`.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up a bodily state and a feeling: it is tired or hungry and acts angry. A child uses one word, often "angry" or "sad", for every unpleasant feeling. A child agrees with the feeling it is asked about ("are you sad?", "yes") without checking it against how it feels.
