## Official wording

grip krijgen op het uiten van primaire gevoelens (reguleren van gevoelens)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting a grip on the expressing of primary feelings (regulating feelings)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child that is angry stamps or says "angry!" instead of biting. A child that is sad goes to the adult for a hug or fetches its cuddly toy, and after a while goes back to play. A child that is very glad jumps and shouts, and lowers its voice when the adult asks. A child of 2 calms down with an adult who holds it and names the feeling; a child of 4 sometimes calms itself, for example by walking away or taking a breath.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording is about the expressing of primary feelings, and the brackets call this regulating feelings. It does not list the primary feelings; `Ontdekken van behoeften, gevoelens en emoties van zichzelf / 3` names four basic emotions. It says getting a grip, which is a process, and it names no way of calming down and no adult help.

### Common mistakes

A child is overwhelmed by a strong feeling and cannot use what it knows in a calm moment. A child takes "calm down" to mean that the feeling is not allowed, and hides it. A child calms down and becomes angry or sad again at the first reminder of the cause.
