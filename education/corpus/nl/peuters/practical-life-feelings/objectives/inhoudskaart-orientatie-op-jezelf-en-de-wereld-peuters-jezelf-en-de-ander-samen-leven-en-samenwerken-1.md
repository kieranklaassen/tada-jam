## Official wording

zorgen voor een plek voor jezelf binnen de eigen groep

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

securing a place for yourself within your own group

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child walks up to children who are playing and joins in, says "I want to play too", and sits down on a free spot in the circle. The child asks for a turn and holds on to a toy they are using when another child pulls at it. A child of 2 stands close and watches, or plays alongside; a child of 4 asks to join, proposes a part for themselves in the play, and says no when pushed aside.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement does not say what "a place" (*een plek*) means: it can be read as a spot to sit or play, or as a position among the other children, and the Dutch leaves this open. "Your own group" (*de eigen groep*) is not defined further, and no group size is named.

### Common mistakes

Some children of this age take a place by force: they push, grab or sit on top of another child. Others wait at the edge and do not ask. They enter a play by changing it and are then refused by the others.
