## Official wording

op gemak voelen in de eigen groep

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

feeling at ease in one's own group

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child walks into the room after saying goodbye to its parent, hangs up its bag and goes to play. It moves freely through the room, asks the adult for help, eats and sleeps there, and laughs with other children. A child of 2 needs a fixed goodbye routine and stays near a familiar adult at first; a child of 4 goes straight to its friends or to a chosen activity.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. This is a statement about a feeling: being at ease, in the child's own group. It names no behaviour by which this shows and no time the child needs to settle. Other groups and unfamiliar places are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out when they are not at ease is play and speech: the child stands and watches, keeps its coat or cuddly toy with it, and does not ask for what it needs. Being quiet is not always a sign of unease; some children who are at ease also watch for a long time.
