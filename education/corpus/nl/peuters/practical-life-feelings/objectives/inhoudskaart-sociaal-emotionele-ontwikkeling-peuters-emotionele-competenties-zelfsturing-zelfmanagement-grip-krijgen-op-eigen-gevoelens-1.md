## Official wording

reageren (non-verbaal en verbaal) op anderen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reacting (non-verbally and verbally) to others

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child smiles back at a child who smiles, turns its head away from a child who comes too close, and says "no" or "mine" when someone takes its toy. It answers "yes" when asked to play and holds out its hand. A child of 2 reacts mostly with face, body and single words; a child of 4 reacts mostly with sentences, and with less pushing and crying.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The brackets name both ways of reacting: non-verbal and verbal. The statement stands under the sub-heading about getting a grip on one's own feelings. It does not say to whom the child reacts or in which situations. `Open staan voor de emoties van een ander / 1` is a statement about reacting to signals from other people.

### Common mistakes

A child reacts with its body (pushing, biting, screaming) where it lacks the words. A child gives no visible reaction and the other child takes this as agreement. A child reacts to the last thing that happened and not to what led to it.
