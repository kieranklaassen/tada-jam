## Official wording

ontdekken dat er algemeen aanvaarde waarden en normen zijn (b)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering that there are generally accepted values and norms (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says "thank you" and "sorry" after an adult's example, waits for a turn, and hears the same rule in different places, such as "we do not hit". The child tells another child "that is not allowed" and looks at the adult to check. A child of 2 follows a rule while the adult is near and reminds them; a child of 4 can say a few rules and points out when one is broken.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The card marks this as an aspect of citizenship (b). The verb is discovering that such values and norms exist. The statement names none of them and does not say what "generally accepted" (*algemeen aanvaarde*) covers. It does not ask a child to follow or explain them.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age think a rule belongs to the adult who says it, and that it does not hold when that adult is away. They apply a rule strictly to other children and not to themselves. They think a rule of one place holds everywhere, or that a general rule holds in one place only.
