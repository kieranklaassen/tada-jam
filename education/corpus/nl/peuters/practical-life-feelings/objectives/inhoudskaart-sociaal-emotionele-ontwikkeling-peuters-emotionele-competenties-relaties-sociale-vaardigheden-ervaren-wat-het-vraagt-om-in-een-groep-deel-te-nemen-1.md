## Official wording

vertellen wat wel en niet mag in de groep

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

telling what is and is not allowed in the group

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says a rule of the group aloud, such as that sand stays in the sandpit or that children walk indoors. It tells another child or a doll "that is not allowed" when it sees something against a rule, and points to where the coats go. A child of 2 says "no, not allowed" or shakes its head; a child of 4 says the rule in a short sentence and sometimes adds what is allowed.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording covers both what is allowed and what is not allowed, and only in the group. It does not say how many rules, which rules, or who made them. Keeping to the rules is a separate statement, `Ervaren wat het vraagt om in een groep deel te nemen / 2`.

### Common mistakes

A child can say a rule and break it a moment later; saying and doing are separate at this age. A child names only what is forbidden and leaves out what is allowed. A child applies a rule to others and not to itself.
