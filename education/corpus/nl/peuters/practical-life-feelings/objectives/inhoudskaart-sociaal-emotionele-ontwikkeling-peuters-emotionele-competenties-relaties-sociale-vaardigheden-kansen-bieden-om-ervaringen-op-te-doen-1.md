## Official wording

met andere kinderen 'iets' delen bijv. ruimte of speelgoed

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

sharing 'something' with other children, e.g. space or toys

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child moves over on the bench so that another child can sit, plays at the same sand table, or hands over one of the two cars it holds. It lets another child put blocks on the same tower. A child of 2 shares space more easily than an object it holds, and gives something up mostly when an adult asks; a child of 4 sometimes offers a toy by itself or agrees to use it after the other child.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement stands under a sub-heading about offering chances to gain experience, so it is about the child having the occasion to share. Space and toys are given as examples; "something" is left open. It sets no amount, no duration and no rule for fair sharing.

### Common mistakes

A child treats a toy it has just put down as still its own and protests when another child takes it. A child understands sharing as giving the thing away permanently and refuses for that reason. A child shares the thing it does not want and keeps the one it wants.
