## Official wording

proberen grenzen te verleggen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

trying to shift limits (*grenzen verleggen*, of what one can or may do)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child climbs one rung higher on the climbing frame than last time, jumps from the second step instead of the first, or carries a fuller jug. It also tries how far a rule goes, for example by walking a little further away than agreed and looking back at the adult. A child of 2 tries more than it can manage and looks to the adult; a child of 4 judges better what it can do and takes a next step that is a little harder.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says trying. The Dutch *grenzen verleggen* means shifting limits; the statement does not say whether these are the limits of what the child can do or of what the child is allowed to do. The sub-heading is about wanting to do things oneself. The statement states no safety condition.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is judging the risk: a child climbs up and cannot get down, or lifts something that is too heavy. A cautious child leaves out the trying itself and stays with what it can already do.
