## Official wording

op zijn/haar beurt wachten (van tijd tot tijd)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

waiting for his/her turn (from time to time)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child stands in the line for the slide and goes when the child in front has gone. At the table it waits until the fruit bowl reaches it, and when a ball is rolled back and forth it rolls the ball away and waits for it to return. A child of 2 waits a few seconds with an adult beside it who says whose turn it is; a child of 4 waits while two or three other children have their turn.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The brackets say "from time to time": the statement does not expect waiting every time. It names no length of waiting and no number of children. It does not say whether an adult helps.

### Common mistakes

A child grabs the object when its wish is strong, even though it knows whose turn it is. A child thinks its turn is lost when another child goes first, and cries or walks away. A child takes a turn and does not hand the object on afterwards.
