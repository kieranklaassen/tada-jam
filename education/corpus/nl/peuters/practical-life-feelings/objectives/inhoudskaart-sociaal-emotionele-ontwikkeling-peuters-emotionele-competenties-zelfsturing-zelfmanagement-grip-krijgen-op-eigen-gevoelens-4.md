## Official wording

grip krijgen op plezierige en onplezierige gevoelens van zichzelf

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - pré-fase (peuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting a grip on one's own pleasant and unpleasant feelings

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child that is excited about a birthday runs in circles and then sits down for the song when the adult gives a sign. A child that is disappointed because outdoor play ends cries briefly, takes the adult's hand and walks inside. A child of 2 stays in the feeling until an adult helps; a child of 4 recovers faster and sometimes says what would help, such as "I want to sit with you".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement names both pleasant and unpleasant feelings, and only the child's own. It says getting a grip, which is a process; it names no feelings and no way of handling them. `Grip krijgen op eigen gevoelens / 3` is about the expressing of primary feelings.

### Common mistakes

A child's pleasant excitement grows until it turns into wild behaviour or crying. A child handles a small disappointment well at one moment and not at all when it is tired. A child tries to end an unpleasant feeling by getting what it wants, not by calming down.
