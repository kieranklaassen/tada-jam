herkennen van eigen gevoelens

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising one's own feelings

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child points at the drawn face that looks the way they feel, or says "I am angry" or "I am sad" when an adult asks. The child goes to an adult for comfort when hurt and says "scared" at a loud noise. A child of 2 shows the feeling with the whole body and says a word after the adult has named it; a child of 4 says the word alone and sometimes says why.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement is about the child's own feelings; feelings of others are in `Gevoelens, wensen en opvattingen / 3`. It names no feelings and no number of them. It does not mention naming or explaining a feeling.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age use "angry" or "sad" for every unpleasant feeling, also for tired, hungry or scared. They feel something in the body, such as a tummy ache, without knowing which feeling it is. They can say what they felt only after it is over, not while it lasts.
