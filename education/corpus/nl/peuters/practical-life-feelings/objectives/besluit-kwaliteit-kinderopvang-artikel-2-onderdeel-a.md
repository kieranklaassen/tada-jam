## Official wording

op een sensitieve en responsieve manier met kinderen wordt omgegaan, respect voor de autonomie van kinderen wordt getoond en grenzen worden gesteld aan en structuur wordt geboden voor het gedrag van kinderen, zodat kinderen zich emotioneel veilig en geborgen kunnen voelen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kwaliteit kinderopvang, artikel 2, onderdeel a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

children are treated in a sensitive and responsive way, respect is shown for children's autonomy and limits are set to, and structure is offered for, children's behaviour, so that children can feel emotionally safe and secure;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 2 to 4 in childcare goes to a familiar adult when upset and is picked up or spoken to. The child chooses what to play with, says "I do it myself" and gets time to try. The child hears the same limits each day, such as "we do not hit", and knows the order of the day: play, fruit, outside, sleep. A child of 2 holds on to the parent at arrival and settles after comfort; a child of 4 says goodbye, starts to play, and comes for help when needed.

### Limits

This is a legal requirement on the childcare provider, not on the child: it says how adults treat children, and sets nothing a child must be able to do. It is one lettered part of an article; the article's opening words, which are in this record's locator and not in its wording, say that the holder (*houder*) or the childminder (*gastouder*) sees to this in any case, taking account of the stage of development the children are in. The part names what is done (treating children in a sensitive and responsive way, showing respect for their autonomy, setting limits and offering structure for their behaviour) and one aim: that children can feel emotionally safe and secure (*geborgen*). It names no ages and no methods.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often test a limit again and again to find out whether it holds, and cry or get angry at a new limit. In a new group or with a new adult they stay quiet and do not yet show what they need.
