## Official wording

aandacht hebben en zorg dragen voor de eigen leefomgeving

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

paying attention to and taking care of one's own living environment

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child puts the blocks back in their box after play, hangs their coat on their own hook, and throws a wrapper in the bin. The child helps to wipe the table, sweeps sand back into the sandpit, and tells an adult when something is broken or spilled. A child of 2 puts a few things away together with an adult; a child of 4 tidies a corner alone when asked and notices litter on the ground.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. "One's own living environment" (*de eigen leefomgeving*) is not defined further: the statement does not say whether it means the group room, the home, the street or all of these. The sub-heading prints the word "safe" in brackets: *(Veilige) leefomgeving*. The statement names no tasks.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often do not see a mess as a mess: for them the toys on the floor are still in use. They start to tidy and begin to play with what they pick up. They take care of their own things and not of shared ones.
