## Official wording

opzeggen van stukjes telrij (akoestisch tellen) (bijv. door het imiteren van anderen zoals het tellen bij verstoppertje)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reciting pieces of the counting sequence (acoustic counting, *akoestisch tellen*) (e.g. by imitating others, such as the counting in hide-and-seek)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child covers their eyes against the wall and says "one, two, three" aloud as older children do in hide-and-seek, then calls that they are coming. The child counts aloud before jumping off a step: "one, two, three, go". A child of 2 says two or three number words in a row; a child of 4 recites a longer piece in a fixed order, sometimes skipping a word.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording says "pieces" (*stukjes*) of the counting sequence, not the whole sequence, and sets no number range. Acoustic counting is saying the number words in order, without objects. Imitating others, as in hide-and-seek, is given as an example.

### Common mistakes

The child leaves out a number word, often the same one each time, or changes the order after the first few. The child says the words as one unbroken sound and cannot start from a number other than one. The child ends the count with a number word they like, not the one that comes next.
