## Official wording

vergelijken van kleine hoeveelheden 'op het oog' of door gelijke rijtjes te maken; en vergelijken van grotere hoeveelheden met groot verschil in aantal 'op het oog'

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing small quantities 'by eye' or by making equal rows; and comparing larger quantities with a large difference in number 'by eye'

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at two plates with two and four grapes and points at the plate with more. With two small groups of blocks the child lays two rows, one block opposite one block, and sees which row has blocks without a partner. A child of 2 picks the bigger heap when the difference is large; a child of 4 lines up two small groups to check which has more, or sees that they are equal.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. Two cases are named: small quantities, compared by eye or by rows, and larger quantities, compared by eye where the difference in number is large. No numbers are given for "small", "larger" or "large difference". The statement does not mention counting as a way to compare.

### Common mistakes

The child calls the group that takes up more space the larger one, also when its objects are only spread wider. The child calls the group with the bigger objects "more". When making rows the child does not place the objects one opposite one, so the longer row is not the row with more.
