## Official wording

'tillen' en 'wegen' van verschillende voorwerpen en vergelijken wat zwaarder is of het zwaarst en wat lichter is of het lichtst en deze begrippen leren herkennen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

'lifting' and 'weighing' different objects and comparing which is heavier or the heaviest and which is lighter or the lightest, and learning to recognise these concepts

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a stone in one hand and a pine cone in the other, moves both hands up and down and says which is heavier. With three bags, one of sand, one of beans and one of cotton wool, the child lifts each and puts the heaviest at one end. A child of 2 lifts things one by one and reacts to a heavy one; a child of 4 compares two things in the hands and names the heavier, and with help finds the heaviest of three.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The inverted commas around 'lifting' and 'weighing' are in the wording. The comparison words are heavier, heaviest, lighter and lightest; the wording speaks of learning to recognise them and does not mention saying them. No units and no numbers are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child picks the bigger object as the heavier without lifting it. The child understands "heavier" but also points at the heavy one when asked which is lighter. With three objects the child compares two and forgets the third.
