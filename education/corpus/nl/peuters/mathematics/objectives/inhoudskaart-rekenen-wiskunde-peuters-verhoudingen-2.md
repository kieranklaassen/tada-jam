## Official wording

gesprekjes voeren over verhoudingen (bijv. m.b.v. vragen als: Wat is groter, een muis of een olifant? En hoe zit dat bij een grote knuffel van een muis en een klein speelgoed olifantje?)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having short conversations about proportions (*verhoudingen*, how sizes or amounts relate to each other) (e.g. with the help of questions such as: Which is bigger, a mouse or an elephant? And how is that with a big cuddly toy of a mouse and a small toy elephant?)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a big cuddly mouse and a small toy elephant and is asked which is bigger. The child says that this toy mouse is bigger, and that a real elephant is much bigger than a real mouse. A child of 2 points at the bigger toy in their hands; a child of 4 keeps the size of the toy and the size of the real animal apart and says so.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The two questions are an example of how such a conversation is started: the first compares the real animals, the second compares a big toy of the small animal with a small toy of the big animal. *Verhoudingen* covers how sizes relate and also ratios between amounts; the example questions are about sizes. The statement is about conversation; no measuring and no numbers are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child answers the second question from what they know of real animals and says the elephant is bigger though the toy elephant is smaller. The child answers only about the toys in sight and does not think of the real animals. The child thinks a toy is the same size as the real thing.
