## Official wording

omgaan met begrippen rond hoeveelheden zoals meer/minder, meeste/minste, evenveel, weinig/veel, alles/niets

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with concepts around quantities such as more/fewer, most/fewest, the same number (*evenveel*), few/many, all/nothing

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears and uses these words at the fruit table and in the sandpit: "I want more", "you have the most", "I have nothing", "we have the same number". The child points at the bowl with few beads and the bowl with many when asked. A child of 2 uses "more" and "nothing"; a child of 4 also uses "fewer", "the most" and "the same number" when looking at two or three groups.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The words after "such as" are examples: *meer/minder*, *meeste/minste*, *evenveel*, *weinig/veel*, *alles/niets*. The statement is about words in use and sets no number range. In Dutch *meer*, *veel* and *weinig* are used both for things one counts and for amounts such as sand or water.

### Common mistakes

The child understands "more" long before "fewer", and answers "which has fewer?" by pointing at the group with more. The child takes "the same number" to mean "the same things". The child says "many" for any group above two or three.
