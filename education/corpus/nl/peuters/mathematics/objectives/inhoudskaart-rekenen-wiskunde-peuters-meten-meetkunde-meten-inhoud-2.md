## Official wording

ervaring opdoen met inhouden door vullen, overgieten en leegmaken (bijv. vullen/legen van flessen, bekers en emmers met zand en water; vullen van dozen met blokken)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with capacities (*inhouden*, how much things hold) by filling, pouring from one into another and emptying (e.g. filling/emptying bottles, cups and buckets with sand and water; filling boxes with blocks)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the sandpit the child scoops sand into a bucket until it is full, pats it down and tips it out. At the water table the child fills a bottle through a funnel, pours it into a cup and sees the cup overflow. When tidying up, the child puts blocks into a box until no more fit. A child of 2 fills and empties many times in a row; a child of 4 says beforehand that the water from the bottle will not all fit into the small cup.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The statement names three actions: filling, pouring from one container into another and emptying. The examples name bottles, cups and buckets with sand and water, and boxes filled with blocks. No measures and no counting of scoops are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child keeps pouring when the container is full and does not link the overflow to "full". The child expects all the water from a big container to fit into a small one. The child packs blocks loosely and says the box is full while gaps remain.
