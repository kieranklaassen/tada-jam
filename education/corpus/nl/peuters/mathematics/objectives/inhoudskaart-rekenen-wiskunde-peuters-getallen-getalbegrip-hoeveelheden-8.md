## Official wording

nadenken over begrippen als (heel) veel en (heel) weinig

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking about concepts such as (very) many and (very) few

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a full box of beads and at two beads in a dish and says which is "very many" and which is "few". With an adult the child talks about whether five peas on a plate are many and whether five children on the slide are many. A child of 2 says "many" for a big heap; a child of 4 starts to notice that the same number is called many in one situation and few in another.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The words named are *veel* and *weinig*, each with *heel* (very) in brackets. In Dutch these words cover both things one counts (many, few) and amounts (much, little). The statement gives no numbers for what is many or few.

### Common mistakes

The child takes "many" as a fixed number, often any group above three. The child calls a group "many" because the objects are big or take up much space. The child does not yet see that "many" depends on what is being compared.
