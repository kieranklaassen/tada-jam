## Official wording

gesprekjes voeren waarin begrippen voorkomen die te maken hebben met tijdsbeleving en tijdsbesef zoals nu, straks, toen, vroeg/laat, lang/kort, even, langzaam/snel

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having short conversations in which concepts occur that have to do with the experience of time and the awareness of time, such as now, in a while (*straks*), back then (*toen*), early/late, long/short, for a moment (*even*), slow/fast

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears and answers remarks such as "we are going outside in a while", "wait a moment" and "that took a long time". The child walks slowly like a snail and runs fast like a hare and says which is which. A child of 2 understands "now" and waits briefly at "in a while"; a child of 4 uses "in a while", "back then", "long" and "fast" in their own sentences.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The words after "such as" are examples: *nu*, *straks*, *toen*, *vroeg/laat*, *lang/kort*, *even*, *langzaam/snel*. The statement is about these words occurring in conversation; it names no clock times, no days and no lengths of time in minutes.

### Common mistakes

The child uses one word for all time that is not now, for example "yesterday" for everything past. The child takes "in a while" to mean at once. The child judges how long something took by how much happened, not by the time.
