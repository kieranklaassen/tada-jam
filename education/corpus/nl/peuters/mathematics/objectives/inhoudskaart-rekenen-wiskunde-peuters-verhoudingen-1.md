## Official wording

experimenteren met verhoudingen (bijv. een pop aankleden met passende kleren)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experimenting with proportions (*verhoudingen*, how sizes relate to each other) (e.g. dressing a doll in clothes that fit)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tries a small jumper on a large doll, finds that the arm will not go in, and fetches a bigger jumper. The child puts the big doll in the big bed and the small doll in the small bed, or picks a spoon that suits the doll's bowl. A child of 2 tries clothes on by pulling and pushing; a child of 4 looks at the doll and the clothes first and picks ones that will fit.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. Dressing a doll in clothes that fit is the one example. *Verhoudingen* can also be read as ratios; the example given is about sizes that fit together. The statement names no numbers, no fractions and no words such as half or twice.

### Common mistakes

The child picks clothes by colour or liking and not by size. The child keeps pushing when a garment is too small and does not look for another. The child judges size by one dimension, such as length, and overlooks width.
