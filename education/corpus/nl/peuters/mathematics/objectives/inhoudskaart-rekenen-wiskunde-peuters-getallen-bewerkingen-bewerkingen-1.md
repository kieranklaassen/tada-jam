## Official wording

ervaring opdoen met optellen en aftrekken door steeds een voorwerp (of persoon) erbij of eraf halen en beseffen dat het eentje meer of minder wordt (bijv. in een versje als tien kleine kikkertjes)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with adding and subtracting by each time adding or taking away an object (or person) and realising that it becomes one more or one fewer (e.g. in a rhyme such as ten little frogs, *tien kleine kikkertjes*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts one more block on a row, or takes one toy animal off the table, and looks at the group again. In a counting rhyme one child sits down or one finger is folded away at each verse, and the group says the new number together. A child of 2 says "one more" or "gone"; a child of 4 says how many there are after one is added or taken away, for a small group of things.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The change is always one object or one person at a time. The rhyme about ten little frogs is given as an example; the ten in its title is not stated as a number range, and the statement sets none. The statement does not mention written sums or the signs + and −.

### Common mistakes

After one is taken away the child counts the group again from one and does not expect the number to be one lower. The child recites the numbers of the rhyme as a fixed text without linking each number to the group that is left. The child says the same number again after one has been added.
