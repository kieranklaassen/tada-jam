## Official wording

vergelijken en ontdekken van tegenstellingen en omgaan met begrippen daarbij (bijv. groot/klein, grootste/kleinste, groter/kleiner)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and discovering opposites and dealing with the concepts that go with them (e.g. big/small, biggest/smallest, bigger/smaller)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts a big bear and a small bear side by side and says which is big and which is small. With three bears the child points at the biggest and the smallest, and gives the bigger chair to the bigger bear. A child of 2 picks the big one of two; a child of 4 says "bigger" and "smaller" when comparing two things and finds the biggest of three.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The card prints this statement under the sub-heading Lengte, omtrek en oppervlakte (length, perimeter and area). The three word pairs in brackets are examples: *groot/klein*, *grootste/kleinste*, *groter/kleiner*. No numbers or units are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child treats "big" as fixed to a thing and does not accept that the same bear is big next to one bear and small next to another. The child says "the biggest" when only two things are compared, or "bigger" for the biggest of three. The child understands "bigger" before "smaller" and points at the bigger one for both.
