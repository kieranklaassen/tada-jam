## Official wording

gesprekjes voeren over concrete voorwerpen en situaties die niet direct zichtbaar zijn (=voorstellingsvermogen) (bijv. over speelgoed thuis of voorwerpen in de speelzaal)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having short conversations about concrete objects and situations that are not directly visible (= the ability to picture things in the mind, *voorstellingsvermogen*) (e.g. about toys at home or objects in the *speelzaal*: the playgroup, or a hall for movement play)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At the table an adult asks what toys the child has at home, and the child tells which, where they are kept and what they look like. The child says where the big ball is in the room next door without going to look. A child of 2 names a thing that is out of sight, such as their bed or their cat; a child of 4 describes it and its place in a few sentences.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The objects and situations are concrete ones that cannot be seen at that moment. The wording itself equates this with *voorstellingsvermogen*, the ability to picture something in the mind. Toys at home and objects in the *speelzaal* are examples; *speelzaal* can mean the playgroup itself or a hall for movement play, and the wording does not say which. The statement does not mention drawings, maps or building from memory.

### Common mistakes

The child talks about what is in sight at that moment instead of the thing asked about. The child assumes the listener knows their home and leaves out what the listener needs to know. The child mixes what is really there with what they would like to be there.
