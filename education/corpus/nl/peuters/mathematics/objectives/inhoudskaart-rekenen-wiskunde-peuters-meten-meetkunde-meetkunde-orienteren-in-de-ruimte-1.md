## Official wording

ervaring opdoen met meetkundige begrippen zoals voor, achter, naast, op, onder, in, boven, dichtbij, ver in relatie tot jezelf en t.o.v. jezelf (bijv. a.d.h.v. voorleesverhalen en prentenboeken)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with geometric concepts such as in front of, behind, next to, on, under, in, above, close by, far, in relation to yourself and with respect to yourself (e.g. by means of read-aloud stories and picture books)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child crawls under the table, climbs on the bench, sits in a box and stands behind the curtain, and hears and says the word for each place. With a picture book the adult asks where the cat is, and the child points and says "under the bed". A child of 2 follows "in", "on" and "under" with their own body; a child of 4 also uses "behind", "in front of", "next to" and "far" in speech.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The nine words after "such as" are examples. The positions are taken from the child's own body: the wording says both "in relation to yourself" and "with respect to yourself" (*in relatie tot jezelf en t.o.v. jezelf*), two phrases with nearly the same meaning, and the record's wording has it that way. Left and right are not named. Read-aloud stories and picture books are given as an example.

### Common mistakes

The child mixes up "in front of" and "behind" for things that have no front of their own, such as a ball. The child uses one word, such as "next to", for any place near something. The child understands a word while moving their own body but not yet in a picture.
