## Official wording

onderscheiden en benoemen van meetkundige vormen zoals cirkel (rondje), vierkant, driehoek

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing and naming geometric shapes such as circle (*rondje*, the everyday word for a round shape), square, triangle

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child pushes shapes through the holes of a shape sorter and turns a piece until it fits. The child picks all the round pieces from a box of flat shapes and says "a round one", or points at a triangle when asked. A child of 2 fits and matches shapes by trying; a child of 4 names circle, square and triangle and finds them in things nearby, such as a plate or a window.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. Circle, square and triangle follow "such as", so they are examples; for the circle the wording adds the everyday word *rondje* in brackets. No other shapes are named. The statement is about telling shapes apart and naming them; it does not mention properties such as the number of sides or corners.

### Common mistakes

The child does not recognise a triangle that is long and thin or that stands on its point. The child gives a square another name when it stands on a corner. The child calls every shape with corners a square, or every oval a circle.
