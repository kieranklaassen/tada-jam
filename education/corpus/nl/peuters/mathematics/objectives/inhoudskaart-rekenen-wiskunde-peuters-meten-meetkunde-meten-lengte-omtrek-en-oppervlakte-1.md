## Official wording

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing by length (e.g. 'by eye', by holding side by side, by going to stand side by side)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds two sticks next to each other with one end of each on the table and sees which reaches higher. Two children stand back to back and an adult lays a hand on their heads to show who is taller. A child of 2 sees which of two very different sticks is the long one; a child of 4 puts two things side by side to check which is longer.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The three ways in brackets are examples: by eye, holding things side by side, and standing side by side. The comparison is direct: no measuring tool, steps or units are mentioned. The sub-heading also names perimeter and area; this statement is about length only.

### Common mistakes

The child does not line up the ends, and calls the object whose far end reaches further the longer one. The child calls a thing shorter when it is bent or lies crooked. The child mixes up tall, long and big.
