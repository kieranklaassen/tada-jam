## Official wording

verkennen van de indeling en het verloop van de dag (dagritme) en de namen voor de verschillende momenten: ochtend, middag, avond, nacht

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring how the day is divided and how it passes (daily rhythm, *dagritme*) and the names for the different moments: morning, afternoon, evening, night

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child goes through the fixed order of the day in the group: coming in, fruit, playing outside, lunch, sleeping, going home. With an adult the child says what belongs to the morning and what to the night: breakfast, or lying in bed in the dark. A child of 2 knows what comes after a familiar moment, such as coats after the tidy-up song; a child of 4 uses "morning", "afternoon", "evening" and "night" and names something that happens in each.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. Four names follow the colon: morning, afternoon, evening and night. The statement names no clock times, no days of the week and no seasons.

### Common mistakes

The child mixes up "afternoon" and "evening". The child calls every sleep "night", also a nap in the daytime. The child knows the order of events but cannot attach the names to them.
