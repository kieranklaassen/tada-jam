## Official wording

onderzoeken van eigenschappen van voorwerpen (bijv. lengte, oppervlakte, gewicht, inhoud, kleur) en op basis van een eigenschap sorteren (= classificeren)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating properties of objects (e.g. length, area, weight, *inhoud* (capacity or volume), colour) and sorting on the basis of a property (= classifying)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child handles a box of loose things, feels which are heavy, sees which are long, and says their colours. The child then puts all the red buttons in one dish and the others in another, or puts all the long sticks together. A child of 2 puts together two things that are the same; a child of 4 sorts a small collection by one property and says what the things in a group share.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The five properties in brackets are examples: length, area, weight, *inhoud* and colour. *Inhoud* can be read as capacity (how much something holds) or as volume, and the wording does not choose. The wording speaks of sorting on the basis of a property; sorting by two properties at once is not mentioned. The statement sets no number of objects or of groups.

### Common mistakes

The child changes the property partway: starts with red things and goes on with round things. The child makes a picture or a row with the objects instead of groups. The child sorts correctly but cannot say why the things belong together.
