## Official wording

experimenteren met spiegels en spiegelen (bijv. je eigen lichaam of voorwerpen bekijken in de spiegel)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experimenting with mirrors and mirroring (e.g. looking at your own body or at objects in the mirror)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands at a low mirror, waves, pulls faces and touches their nose while watching the mirror. The child holds a toy against the mirror and looks at the toy and its image, or looks behind the mirror for the other toy. A child of 2 watches their own movements and reaches for the image; a child of 4 places a block against the mirror on purpose to see two blocks and notices that the image moves when they move.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. Looking at your own body or at objects in the mirror is given as an example. The statement does not mention symmetry, a line of symmetry or drawing a mirror image. It does not say what kind of mirror.

### Common mistakes

The child treats the image as another child or a second object and looks behind the mirror for it. The child reaches towards the mirror to take the thing seen in it. The child expects the image to raise the opposite hand, as a person facing them would.
