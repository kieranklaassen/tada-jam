## Official wording

synchroon tellen (bijv. een telwoord noemen bij elk voorwerp of bij elke stap op een traptrede)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

synchronous counting (*synchroon tellen*) (e.g. saying a number word for each object or for each step onto a stair tread)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child climbs the stairs and says one number word as each foot lands on a tread. The child moves buttons one at a time from one bowl to another and says one number word per button. A child of 2 says some number words while stepping, not one per step; a child of 4 keeps word and movement together for a short count.

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. Synchronous counting means that one number word goes with one object or one movement. The two examples are objects and steps on a staircase. The statement sets no number range and does not mention saying the total.

### Common mistakes

The child's voice goes faster than the hand, so more words than objects are said. A number word of two syllables, such as *ze-ven*, is spread over two objects. The child points at an object twice when the objects lie in a heap and not in a row.
