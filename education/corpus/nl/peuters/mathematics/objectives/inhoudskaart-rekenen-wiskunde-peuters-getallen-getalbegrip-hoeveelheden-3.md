## Official wording

leggen van eén-één relaties door voorwerpen aan elkaar te koppelen, bij elkaar te leggen of te verbinden (bijv. bij elk kopje een schoteltje neerzetten)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making one-to-one relations by coupling objects to each other, laying them together or connecting them (e.g. putting a saucer by each cup)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sets the table in the home corner: one saucer by each cup, one spoon on each plate. The child puts one doll in each bed, or one lid on each pot, and sees whether a bed or a lid is left. A child of 2 pairs two or three things with help; a child of 4 pairs a row of things and says "one too few" or "one left".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The wording names three ways: coupling, laying together and connecting; the cup and saucer are an example. The statement mentions no counting and no number words, and sets no number of objects. The stored wording prints the term as "eén-één", with the accent on the second letter of the first word; one-to-one (*één-één*) is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

The child puts two things by one object and none by the next. The child stops when the things in their hand are gone and does not check whether every cup has a saucer. The child lays the things in two separate piles instead of in pairs.
