## Official wording

ervaring opdoen met begrippen rond inhoud zoals vol/voller, leeg/leger, veel/meer/meest(e), weinig/minder/minst(e), evenveel, genoeg

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - peuters (pre-fase), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with concepts around capacity (*inhoud*, how much something holds) such as full/fuller, empty/emptier, much/more/most, little/less/least, the same amount (*evenveel*), enough

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At drinks time the child holds out a cup and says "full" or "more", and looks at two cups to see who has more. The child pours water into two equal cups until the adult and the child agree that they hold the same amount. A child of 2 uses "full", "empty" and "more"; a child of 4 also uses "fuller", "less", "the most", "the same amount" and "enough".

### Limits

This statement describes what is offered to children of about 2 to 4 before they start school, not what a child must be able to do. The words after "such as" are examples: *vol/voller*, *leeg/leger*, *veel/meer/meest(e)*, *weinig/minder/minst(e)*, *evenveel*, *genoeg*. The statement is about meeting these words in use; no measures are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child calls a cup "full" as soon as there is something in it. The child understands "more" but not "less", and points at the fuller cup for both. The child says two cups hold the same amount only when the cups look alike.
