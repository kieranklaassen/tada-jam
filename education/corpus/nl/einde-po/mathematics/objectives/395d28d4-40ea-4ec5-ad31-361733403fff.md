## Official wording

De leerling meet, redeneert en rekent met grootheden en bijpassende eenheden.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 11 A.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil measures, reasons and calculates with quantities (*grootheden*, things that can be measured) and the units that go with them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 measures the corridor with a tape measure in metres, weighs a school bag in kilograms, reads a thermometer in degrees Celsius and times a run with a stopwatch. Then the child calculates with the results: how many cups of 25 centilitres can be filled from a bottle of 1.5 litres. The child says why an hour and a half is 90 minutes and not 150.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 11, which is about insight when working with quantities and units; it is the only goal sentence of that goal. Its items 11 A a to 11 A e list the quantities and say what is done with them. The sentence speaks of "the units that go with them" and names none. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child starts measuring at the end of the ruler or at the 1 instead of at the 0. A child treats time as a decimal number and writes 1 hour 30 minutes as 1.30 hours. A child changes area units with the factor for lengths and says 1 square metre is 100 square centimetres.
