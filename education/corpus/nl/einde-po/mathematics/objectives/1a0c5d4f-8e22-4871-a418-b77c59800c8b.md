## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads "Anna walks 400 metres to the bakery, then 250 metres on to school, and takes the same way back" and draws a line with three marks, labels them home, bakery and school, and writes 400 and 250 at the two parts. From the sketch the child works out 650 metres one way and 1300 metres there and back. For a problem about shares the child draws a strip and divides it into parts.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 15 B, which says that the pupil makes and uses mathematical models; 15 B sits under core goal 15. The item does not say what kind of scheme is meant (a sketch, a strip, a number line, a table and an arrow diagram are not named) or what kind of situation. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child draws a detailed picture of the people and things and leaves out the numbers and how they belong together. A child draws lengths with no regard for their size, so the shorter distance is the longer line, and then reads the wrong thing from the sketch. A child sees drawing as extra work and writes a sum at once, also when the situation has several steps.
