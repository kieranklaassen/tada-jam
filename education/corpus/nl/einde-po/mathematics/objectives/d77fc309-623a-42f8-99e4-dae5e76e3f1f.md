## Official wording

kwalitatieve en kwantitatieve verhoudingen, procenten, schaal en samengestelde grootheden;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 10 C a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

qualitative and quantitative ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion), percentages, scale and compound quantities (*samengestelde grootheden*, quantities made from two other quantities);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says that the head in a drawing is too large for the body (a qualitative ratio: said without numbers), and that paint is mixed 2 tins of blue to 3 tins of yellow (a quantitative ratio). The child measures 4 centimetres on a map with scale 1 : 25 000 and works out 1 kilometre on the ground. The child works out 15% of 40 euros, and compares two prices per kilogram or two speeds in kilometres per hour.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 10 C, which says that the pupil reasons and calculates with ratios; 10 C sits under core goal 10. The item lists what the goal sentence covers. It does not say which percentages, which scales or which compound quantities are meant; speed, one compound quantity, is named among the quantities in item 11 A a. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

With a scale a child multiplies and then leaves out the change of unit: 4 centimetres at 1 : 25 000 is given as 100 000 kilometres, or left as 100 000 centimetres. A child gives 15% of 40 as 15. With a compound quantity a child divides in the wrong direction (kilograms per euro where euros per kilogram is asked) and does not notice.
