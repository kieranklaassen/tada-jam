## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(standard) measures of area (*oppervlaktematen*) km², m², dm², cm²

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 names the four units and their notations at once: square kilometre (km²), square metre (m²), square decimetre (dm²) and square centimetre (cm²). The child shows each as a square: 1 cm by 1 cm drawn in an exercise book, 1 dm by 1 dm cut from paper, 1 m by 1 m marked on the floor with tape, and 1 km by 1 km pointed out on a map of the town.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names four units of area with their notations: km², m², dm² and cm². It names no others: the square millimetre is not in it, and the are and the hectare are a separate statement under the same heading. The word "standard" is printed in brackets before the term. The statement prints no relation between the four units and asks for no conversion. The fragment has no verb; under this heading, knowing the units and their notations by heart is presumably meant. The decree prints this as a statement of its own, apart from the other statements on measures under the same heading.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the raised 2 as "times two", or does not read it at all and treats m² as m. A child gives an area in a unit of length. A child assumes that the step between two neighbouring units of area is 10, as it is for lengths.
