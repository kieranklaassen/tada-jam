## Official wording

procenten als decimale getallen (honderdsten)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

percentages as decimal numbers (hundredths)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says and writes at once: 35% is 0.35, 7% is 0.07, 50% is 0.50 or 0.5, and 0.12 is 12%. The child reads 35% as 35 hundredths, the same words as for 0.35.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading B (relating to one another). The statement is a fragment without a verb: read with its kind, it concerns having ready that a percentage can be written as a decimal number. The brackets name hundredths. The statement names no particular percentage and sets no number range. A percentage with a decimal part, such as 12.5%, is 0.125 as a decimal number, which has thousandths; the statement does not say whether such percentages are meant. The meaning of percentages above 100 is a separate statement of this level (sub-heading C, *weten waarom*).

### Common mistakes

A child writes 7% as 0.7. A child reads 0.5 as 5%. A child keeps the percent sign and writes 35% as 0.35%.
