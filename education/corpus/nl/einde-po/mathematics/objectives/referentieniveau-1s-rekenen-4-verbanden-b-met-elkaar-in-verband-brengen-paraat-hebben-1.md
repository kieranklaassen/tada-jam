## Official wording

eenvoudige tabellen en diagrammen opstellen op basis van een beschrijving in woorden

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

drawing up simple tables and diagrams on the basis of a description in words

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads: "In our class 8 children come by bicycle, 5 on foot and 3 by car." The child draws a table with two columns and three rows and fills it in, then draws a bar chart with three bars of heights 8, 5 and 3. The child writes a title, the headings of the columns and what each axis stands for.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for making tables and diagrams that are simple, starting from a description in words. It names no kind of diagram. The Dutch *diagram* is the word used for charts such as the bar chart (*staafdiagram*). It stands under knowing by heart, having ready: a skill the pupil carries out readily. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child leaves out the title, the column headings or the labels of the axes. A child numbers the axis with unequal steps. A child draws bars whose heights do not match the numbers in the description.
