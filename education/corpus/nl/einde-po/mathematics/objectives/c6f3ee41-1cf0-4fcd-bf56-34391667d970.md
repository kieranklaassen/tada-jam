## Official wording

schatten en controleren met referentiematen en meetreferenties;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 11 A d.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

estimating and checking with reference measures (*referentiematen*, sizes a child knows from familiar things) and measurement references (*meetreferenties*);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 estimates the height of the classroom from a door ("a door is about 2 metres, the ceiling is half a door higher, so about 3 metres"). The child estimates a weight by holding the object in one hand and a 1-kilogram pack of sugar in the other, and a volume by thinking of a 1-litre carton of milk. Then the child measures to check, or checks a calculated answer against what is known: "a bicycle cannot weigh 150 kilograms".

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 11 A, which says that the pupil measures, reasons and calculates with quantities and the units that go with them; 11 A sits under core goal 11. The law defines neither term, does not say how *meetreferenties* differ from *referentiematen*, and lists no reference measures. The item names two uses: estimating and checking. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child has no reference for units that are seldom handled, such as a kilometre, a tonne or a millilitre, and gives an estimate that is far too large or too small. A child estimates and does not check, or changes the measurement afterwards to match the estimate. A child thinks an estimate has to be exact and answers "2.13 metres".
