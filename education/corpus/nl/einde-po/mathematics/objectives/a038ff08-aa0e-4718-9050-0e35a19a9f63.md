## Official wording

De leerling ontwikkelt een wiskundige attitude.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 17.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil develops a mathematical attitude.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school asks "how do they know that?" at a number in a news report, tries a second way when the first does not work, and wants to know why a rule works and not only that it works. The child explains a method to a classmate, listens to the classmate's way, and says which of the two is shorter.

### Limits

This is the sentence of core goal 17 itself. One goal sentence, 17 A, and its items 17 A a to 17 A c say what it covers. The sentence of the goal is about the pupil; 17 A is addressed to the school and says what the school does: showing the usefulness and the power of mathematics, stimulating an inquiring and critical attitude, and having pupils reflect. Core goal 17 is one of the two core goals of the domain *wiskunde en de wereld* (mathematics and the world); the other is core goal 18. The law does not define "mathematical attitude" and names no behaviour by which it is seen. This goal names no mathematical content and states no number range.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is asking whether a number or an answer can be right. Many children also take being good at mathematics to mean being fast, and stop when an answer does not come at once.
