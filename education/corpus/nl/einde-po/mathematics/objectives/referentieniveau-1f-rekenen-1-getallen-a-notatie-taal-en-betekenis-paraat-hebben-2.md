## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once which of two numbers is greater: 4100 or 4099, 0.8 or 0.75. The child says the relation in both directions ("7 is greater than 5, so 5 is less than 7") and lays three price tags in order from low to high.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names the two relations and nothing else. It does not print the symbols < and >, and does not say which kinds of number are compared. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child calls the decimal number with more digits the greater one, so 0.75 is called greater than 0.8. A child compares two fractions by the denominators alone and calls ¼ greater than ½. A child who uses the symbols < and > writes them the wrong way round.
