critically judging a representation;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at a drawing a classmate made of 2/5, a rectangle in 5 unequal parts with 2 coloured, and says that it does not show 2/5 because the parts are not equal. The child looks at a bar chart with an axis that starts at 50 and says that the difference between the bars looks larger than it is. The child says what is clear in a representation, what is missing, and what would make it better.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 16 A, which says that the pupil uses mathematical language and mathematical representations; 16 A sits under core goal 16. It speaks of one representation and does not say whose it is (the pupil's own or someone else's) or by what standard it is judged. Judging conclusions presented with graphical representations is in item 12 A d, under core goal 12. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child judges how neat or colourful a representation is and not whether it is correct. A child accepts a printed representation as right because it is printed. A child says "it is wrong" and cannot point to what is wrong.
