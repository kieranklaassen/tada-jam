## Official wording

gebruiken van wiskundige instrumenten bij meten en andere praktische handelingen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 18 A b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using mathematical instruments when measuring and in other practical actions;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 weighs 250 grams of flour on kitchen scales and measures 300 millilitres of milk in a measuring jug for a recipe. The child measures a window with a tape measure to cut a sheet of paper that fits it, times a 60-metre run with a stopwatch, and uses a calculator to add up the costs of a class party.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 18 A, which says that the pupil recognises and uses mathematics in everyday situations and in situations in society; 18 A sits under core goal 18. It names measuring and "other practical actions" and does not say which actions those are. It names no instrument. Instruments are also the subject of goal sentence 16 B and its items, under core goal 16. This item states no precision and no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child does not set the scales to zero with the bowl on them and weighs the bowl as well. A child reads a measuring jug from above and not with the eye level with the mark. A child reads the wrong scale on an instrument that has two, such as millilitres and grams on a measuring jug.
