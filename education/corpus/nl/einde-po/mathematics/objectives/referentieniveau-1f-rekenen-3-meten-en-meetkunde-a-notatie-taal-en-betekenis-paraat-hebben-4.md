## Official wording

veelgebruikte meetkundige begrippen (zoals rond, recht, vierkant, midden, horizontaal etc.)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Paraat hebben / 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

much-used geometric concepts (such as round, *recht* [straight; also "right" as in right angle], square, middle, horizontal etc.)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 uses these words without having to think about them: says that the table top is round and the edge of the book is straight, puts a counter in the middle of a square sheet, and holds a ruler horizontal when asked. The child understands the same words in an instruction such as "draw a straight line through the middle of the circle".

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The brackets give five words after "such as": round, *recht*, square, middle and horizontal. They are examples of the level, "etc." leaves the list open, and the statement names no other concept. *Recht* means straight, and it is also the word in *rechte hoek* (right angle); the statement gives the bare word. Vertical, parallel, diagonal and the word for angle are not printed in this statement, and it does not say whether they count as much-used concepts.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up horizontal and vertical. A child uses "straight" only for lines that run level or upright and calls a slanting straight line "not straight". A child takes "the middle" to be any place between the ends and not the point halfway.
