## Official wording

afmetingen bepalen met behulp van afpassen, schaal, rekenen

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Functioneel gebruiken / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

determining dimensions by means of measuring off (*afpassen*, laying a unit end to end), scale, calculating

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 finds the width of the playground by laying a rope of 5 m end to end 6 times and says it is 30 m. On a floor plan on which 1 cm stands for 1 m, the child measures a room as 4 cm by 6 cm and says it is 4 m by 6 m. The child works out the length of a row of 8 tiles of 30 cm each as 240 cm without measuring the row.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names three ways of determining a dimension: measuring off (*afpassen*: laying or stepping the same unit along something again and again), scale, and calculating. It names no units, no particular scale (such as 1 : 100) and no number range, and it does not say which dimensions are meant or of what.

### Common mistakes

When measuring off, a child leaves gaps or overlaps between the placements. With a scale, a child gives the length measured on the drawing as the real length, or divides where multiplying is needed. A child mixes centimetres on the drawing with metres in reality and gives the result with the wrong unit.
