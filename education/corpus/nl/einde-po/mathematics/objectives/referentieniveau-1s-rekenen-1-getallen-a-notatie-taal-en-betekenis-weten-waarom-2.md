## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 explains that the 0 in 507 keeps the 5 in the hundreds place and that without it the number would be 57. The child shows what 0 does in calculations: adding 0 changes nothing, multiplying by 0 gives 0, and writing a 0 behind a whole number makes it ten times as large (35 becomes 350). The child explains why 3.05 and 3.5 are different numbers while 2.50 and 2.5 are equal.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading A (notation, language and meaning). The statement is a fragment without a verb: read with its kind, it concerns understanding why the number 0 matters. It does not say in which respect (as a digit that holds a place, as a number in calculations, or as a point on the number line); the wording leaves this open. It gives no example and sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child leaves out a zero that holds a place and writes three thousand and five as 305. A child reads 3.05 as 3.5, or calls 2.50 larger than 2.5. A child answers 6 × 0 = 6.
