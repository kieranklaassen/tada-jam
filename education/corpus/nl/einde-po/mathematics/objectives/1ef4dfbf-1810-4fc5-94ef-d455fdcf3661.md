## Official wording

De leerlingen leren handig optellen, aftrekken, vermenigvuldigen en delen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 29.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to add, subtract, multiply and divide (*delen*, which also means sharing) in a handy way (*handig*, by a short way that fits the numbers).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at the numbers before starting. For 99 + 46 the child takes 100 + 46 and goes back 1, to 145; for 1000 − 998 the child counts on from 998, which gives 2. For 6 × 49 the child takes 6 × 50 and subtracts 6, to 294; for 240 ÷ 5 the child doubles both numbers, to 480 ÷ 10 = 48. The child says why that way fits these numbers.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is the fourth of six goals under the heading *Getallen en bewerkingen* (numbers and operations). It names the four operations. The goal does not define *handig*; in Dutch arithmetic teaching the word usually means choosing a short way that fits the numbers, by using properties of numbers and operations. It does not say whether the work is done in the head or on paper. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child adjusts in the wrong direction: for 83 − 29 the child takes 83 − 30 and then subtracts 1 more, which gives 52 instead of 54. A child uses a property where it does not hold, for example by swapping the numbers in a subtraction or a division. A child uses the written column procedure for every sum, also for 1000 − 998.
