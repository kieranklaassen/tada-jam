## Official wording

eigen referentiematen ontwikkelen («in 1 kg appels zitten ongeveer 5 appels»)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Weten waarom / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

developing reference measures of one's own ("in 1 kg of apples there are about 5 apples")

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 weighs out 1 kg of apples, counts about 5, and keeps that as a measure of the child's own. The child finds other measures of this kind by measuring: the width of a hand span, the weight of a full school bag, the length of a bicycle. Later the child uses one of them to judge another amount and explains why it fits, for example that a bag of 10 such apples will weigh about 2 kg.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The brackets give one example of a reference measure of one's own: about 5 apples in 1 kg of apples. It is an example of the level, not a limit; the statement names no other measure and no unit apart from the kilogram in the example. The statement does not say how many such measures a pupil develops or for which quantities, and it names no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child treats a reference measure as exact and expects every kilogram of apples to hold exactly 5. A child carries the measure over to things of a very different size, for example uses the apple count for plums. A child remembers a number without the unit or without the thing it belongs to.
