## Official wording

relatieve grootte: de helft van iets kan minder zijn dan een kwart van iets anders

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Weten waarom / 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

relative size: half of something can be less than a quarter of something else

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 compares half of a small bar of 40 grams (20 grams) with a quarter of a large bar of 200 grams (50 grams) and sees that the quarter is more here. The child explains why: a fraction says how large a part is compared with its own whole, so the two wholes have to be known before the parts can be compared. The child makes up another pair in which the half is the larger amount.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The sentence after the colon is the statement's own illustration of relative size, with a half and a quarter; it is an example of the level, not a limit. The statement names no amounts, and other fractions and percentages are not named. Relative comparison is a separate statement of this level (sub-heading A, *weten waarom*).

### Common mistakes

A child holds that a half is always more than a quarter, whatever the wholes are. A child compares fractions or percentages of different wholes as if they were amounts, for example half of one child's pocket money with a quarter of another's. A child carries this over to numbers without a whole and doubts that ½ is larger than ¼ on the number line.
