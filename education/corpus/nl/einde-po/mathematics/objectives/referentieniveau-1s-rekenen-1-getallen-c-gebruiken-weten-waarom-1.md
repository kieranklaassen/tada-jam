## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing that there are procedures that always work and why

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 explains that adding in columns gives the right answer for any two numbers, because ones are added to ones and tens to tens, and ten of one unit are exchanged for one of the next. The child sets this beside a shortcut that suits only some numbers: 1998 + 3567 is quick by way of 2000, and 2746 + 3567 is not. The child shows the reason with place-value material or a worked example.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement does not name the procedures, the operations or the kinds of numbers. "Always" is the statement's own word; it does not say for which numbers. It asks for knowing that such procedures exist and why they work; carrying procedures out stands in statements under *paraat hebben*. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child gives the steps as the reason ("because you carry the 1") without saying what the carried 1 stands for. A child thinks a shortcut that worked once always works, such as writing a zero at the end to multiply by 10, which fails for 3.5 × 10. A child holds that a procedure is right because it was taught and has no way to test it.
