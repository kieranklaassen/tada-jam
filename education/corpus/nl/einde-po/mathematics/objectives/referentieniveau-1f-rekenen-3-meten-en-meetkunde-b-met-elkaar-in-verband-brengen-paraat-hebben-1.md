## Official wording

1 dm³ = 1 liter = 1000 ml

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

1 dm³ = 1 litre = 1000 ml

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once that a hollow cube of 1 dm by 1 dm by 1 dm holds 1 litre, and that 1 litre is 1000 ml. The child pours a carton of 1 litre of water into such a cube and sees that it is full, and reads the mark of 1000 ml on a measuring jug as the same amount.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is one chain of equalities with three units: the cubic decimetre (dm³), the litre and the millilitre (ml). It names no other unit and no other relation: the cubic centimetre, the cubic metre, the decilitre and the centilitre are not in it. Standing under knowing by heart, it asks for the fact itself; it does not ask for an explanation of why the three are equal.

### Common mistakes

A child says that 1 litre is 100 ml, using the step that belongs to metres and centimetres. A child reads dm³ as dm or as 3 dm and does not link it to a cube of 1 dm by 1 dm by 1 dm. A child thinks that litres are only for liquids and that dm³ is something else, and so does not accept that a box of 1 dm³ holds 1 litre.
