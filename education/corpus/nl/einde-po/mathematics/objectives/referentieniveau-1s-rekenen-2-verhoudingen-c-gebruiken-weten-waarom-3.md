## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 explains that 150% of an amount is the whole amount and half of it again, so 150% of 80 euros is 120 euros, and that 200% is twice the amount. The child tells apart cases where more than 100% makes sense (a price that is now 120% of last year's price) from cases where it cannot occur (more than 100% of the class being present).

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement is a fragment without a verb: read with its kind, it concerns understanding what a percentage above 100 means. "Above 100" is open upwards; the statement names no upper limit and no example. Calculating with such percentages is not named in this statement.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks a percentage can never be more than 100, because 100% is everything. A child mixes up "rose by 150%" and "rose to 150%". A child reads "200% more" as twice as much, where it is three times as much.
