## Official wording

weergeven van een situatie in wiskundetaal;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 15 B b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

representing a situation in mathematical language (*wiskundetaal*);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 hears "there are 4 boxes with 6 eggs each, and 5 eggs are broken" and writes 4 × 6 − 5 = 19. The child writes "a quarter of the 28 pupils" as 28 ÷ 4 = 7, and "the price went from 20 to 25 euros" as 25 − 20 = 5. The child also says aloud, with words such as "times", "difference" and "a quarter of", what the written line stands for.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 15 B, which says that the pupil makes and uses mathematical models; 15 B sits under core goal 15. The item does not say which parts of mathematical language are meant; item 16 A a, under core goal 16, names symbols, notations and *begrippen*. The item names no particular notation; the number sentences above are the pack's examples. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child writes the numbers in the order in which the text gives them, so "5 eggs fewer than 24" becomes 5 − 24. A child reads the equals sign as "and then" and writes a chain such as 4 × 6 = 24 − 5 = 19. A child chooses the operation from one word in the text ("more" means adding) and not from the situation.
