## Official wording

geheel getal (deel van nemen):
1/3 deel van 150 euro

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

whole number (taking a part of):
1/3 part of 150 euros

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says that a 1/3 part of 150 euros is 50 euros, by dividing 150 by 3. With play money the child lays 150 euros in three equal piles and takes one pile.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The wording is a fragment in the form of an index entry: the thing first ("whole number") and the action in brackets. The pack reads it as taking a part of a whole number; the source prints it this way. The example, a 1/3 part of 150 euros, is an example of the level, not a limit; the statement does not say which fractions are meant or whether the numerator can be more than 1. The fraction is printed with a slash (1/3) and is kept so. Another statement of this row under the same kind names multiplying a fraction by a whole number in a meaningful situation.

### Common mistakes

A child multiplies by 3 instead of dividing and says 450 euros. A child subtracts 3 and says 147 euros. A child takes 1/3 as 0.3 or 30% and says 45 euros.
