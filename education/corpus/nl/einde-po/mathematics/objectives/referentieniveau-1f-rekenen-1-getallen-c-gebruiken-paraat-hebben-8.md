## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out 35 × 67 in four parts, 30 × 60 = 1800, 30 × 7 = 210, 5 × 60 = 300 and 5 × 7 = 35, and adds them to 2345, or uses a shorter written procedure. Beforehand the child says that the result has to lie between 30 × 60 = 1800 and 40 × 70 = 2800.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The stated range is a number of two digits times a number of two digits. The statement names no way of working. The example, printed with an open equals sign, is an example of the level, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

A child multiplies only tens by tens and ones by ones: 30 × 60 + 5 × 7 = 1835. A child writes the partial product of the tens without its zero, 210 instead of 2100 for 35 × 60. A child makes an error in adding the partial products.
