## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 builds a cube of 1 m by 1 m by 1 m from metre sticks and says that this is what "cubic metre" means. The child stacks cubes of 1 cm by 1 cm by 1 cm into a block, counts them to give its size in cubic centimetres, and explains that "cubic" says the unit is a cube whose length, width and height are each 1 of the length unit.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The one example is *kubieke*, the word in *kubieke meter* (cubic metre); it comes after "such as" and is an example of the level, not a limit. The statement names no other prefix and no unit. The source calls *kubieke* a prefix (*voorvoegsel*) although it is written as a separate word. Which other words it counts as prefixes is not said; *vierkante* (square) and kilo, centi and milli would all fit the word.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks a cubic metre is three times a metre, or reads the raised 3 in m³ as "times 3". A child thinks that a cubic metre must have the shape of a cube.
