## Official wording

De leerling lost wiskundige problemen en toepassings-problemen op.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 15 A.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil solves mathematical problems and application problems (*toepassingsproblemen*, problems in which mathematics is applied).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works on a problem inside mathematics, such as finding every number between 10 and 50 that leaves a remainder of 1 when it is divided by 2, by 3 and by 4 (13, 25, 37 and 49). The same child works on a problem from a situation: 28 pupils go to a museum, a ticket costs 4.50 euros and the bus costs 98 euros in all, so the trip costs 224 euros, which is 8 euros for each pupil. In both, the child decides how to start, carries the plan out and checks the result.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 15, which is about using mathematical ways of thinking and working. Its items 15 A a to 15 A d name devising and carrying out an approach for a problem that cannot be solved in a routine way, using heuristics, working the outcomes of calculations into a solution, and reflecting. The sentence names two kinds of problem, mathematical problems and application problems, and defines neither. The source prints the second word with a hyphen inside it (*toepassings-problemen*); the gloss gives what is plainly meant. This goal states no number range and no level of difficulty for the problems.

### Common mistakes

A child looks for a signal word ("in all", "left") and picks the operation from it without picturing the situation. A child stops when no known method is recognised at once. A child does not check whether the answer is possible in the situation.
