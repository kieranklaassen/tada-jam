## Official wording

gebruiken van een instrument en de bijbehorende wiskundetaal;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 16 B c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using an instrument and the mathematical language (*wiskundetaal*) that goes with it;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays a ruler along a pencil with the 0 at one end, reads 14 centimetres and 3 millimetres, and writes 14.3 cm. The child reads a thermometer as "minus 2 degrees Celsius" and kitchen scales as "350 grams", and uses words such as "scale", "mark", "unit" and "to the nearest millimetre" when telling a classmate how the measuring was done. On a calculator the child presses the keys for the operation and reads the decimal sign in the display.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 16 B, which says that the pupil uses measuring instruments and other mathematical instruments; 16 B sits under core goal 16. It joins two things: using the instrument, and using the language that belongs to it. It names no instrument and no terms. This item states no precision and no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the number and leaves out the unit, or gives a unit that does not belong to the scale that was read. A child writes 14 centimetres and 3 millimetres as 14.03 cm or as 17. A child reads a calculator display such as 4.5 as "4 remainder 5".
