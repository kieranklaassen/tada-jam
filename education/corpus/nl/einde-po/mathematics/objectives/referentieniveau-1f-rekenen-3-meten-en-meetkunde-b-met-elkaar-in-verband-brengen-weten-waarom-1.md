## Official wording

(lengte)maten en geld in verband brengen met decimale getallen:
1,65 m is 1 meter en 65 centimeter
€ 1,65 is 1 euro en 65 eurocent

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Weten waarom / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

relating (length) measures and money to decimal numbers:
1.65 m is 1 metre and 65 centimetres
€ 1.65 is 1 euro and 65 euro cents

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays out a coin of 1 euro and 65 cents in small coins and writes € 1.65, and marks 1 metre and then 65 cm more on a wall and writes 1.65 m. The child explains why both are written in the same way: there are 100 cents in a euro and 100 centimetres in a metre, so the two digits after the decimal point count hundredths of the whole unit.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement gives two examples: 1.65 m read as 1 metre and 65 centimetres, and € 1.65 read as 1 euro and 65 euro cents. They are examples of the level, not a limit. The brackets in "(length) measures" leave open whether other measures are meant as well; the only units printed are the metre, the centimetre, the euro and the euro cent, and the statement names no others. The record holds each example on its own line; neither ends with a punctuation mark. It prints a decimal comma (1,65), which the gloss writes as a point. The statement names no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads 1.5 m as 1 metre and 5 centimetres instead of 1 metre and 50 centimetres. A child writes 1 metre and 5 centimetres as 1.5 m instead of 1.05 m, and 1 euro and 5 cents as € 1.5. A child carries the two decimals over to a unit where the step is not 100 and reads 1.65 kg as 1 kilogram and 65 grams.
