## Official wording

1 km² = 1 000 000 m² = 100 ha

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Paraat hebben / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

1 km² = 1 000 000 m² = 100 ha

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once that 1 km² is 1 000 000 m² and that it is 100 ha. Reading that a nature reserve measures 3 km², the child says 300 ha. The child also works in the other direction: 50 ha is half a square kilometre.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement is one chain of equalities with three units: the square kilometre (km²), the square metre (m²) and ha, the abbreviation of hectare. It names no other unit and prints no other relation; the are is not in it. That 1 ha is 10 000 m² follows from the chain but is not printed. Standing under knowing by heart, it asks for the fact itself; it does not ask for an explanation.

### Common mistakes

A child says that 1 km² is 1000 m², using the step from kilometres to metres. A child miscounts the zeros of 1 000 000. A child does not know what ha stands for.
