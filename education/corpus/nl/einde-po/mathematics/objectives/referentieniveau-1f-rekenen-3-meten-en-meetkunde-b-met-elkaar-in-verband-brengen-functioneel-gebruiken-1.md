## Official wording

in betekenisvolle situaties samenhang tussen enkele (standaard)maten
• km → m
• m → dm, cm, mm
• l → dl, cl, ml
• kg → g, mg

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Functioneel gebruiken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

in meaningful situations, relationship between some (standard) measures
• km → m
• m → dm, cm, mm
• l → dl, cl, ml
• kg → g, mg

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads on a signpost that the swimming pool is 2.5 km away and says that this is 2500 m. The child measures a shelf of 1.2 m and tells the shop it is 120 cm, pours a carton of 1 l into glasses of 2 dl and fills 5 glasses, and weighs out 250 g of flour four times from a bag of 1 kg.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement lists four lines of relations between units: kilometre to metre; metre to decimetre, centimetre and millimetre; litre to decilitre, centilitre and millilitre; kilogram to gram and milligram. It names no other units: hectometre, decametre, hectolitre, tonne and the units of area and of volume are not in the list. Each arrow points from the larger unit to the smaller ones, and the statement does not say whether the other direction is meant too. The condition "in meaningful situations" is part of the statement. It prints no conversion factors and no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child moves the decimal point the wrong way and makes 2.5 km into 0.0025 m. A child uses the same step for every pair of units and says that 1 km is 100 m or that 1 kg is 100 g. A child mixes up the prefixes and takes a decilitre to be larger than a litre, or a centilitre and a millilitre to be the same.
