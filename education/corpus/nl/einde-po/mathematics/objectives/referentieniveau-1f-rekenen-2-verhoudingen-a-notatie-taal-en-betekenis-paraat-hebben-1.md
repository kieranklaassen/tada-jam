## Official wording

een vijfde deel van alle Nederlanders korter schrijven als «1/5 deel van...»

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing a fifth part of all Dutch people more briefly as "1/5 part of..."

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads "a fifth part of all Dutch people" in a text and writes it down as "1/5 part of all Dutch people". The child also does it the other way round and reads 1/5 aloud as "a fifth" or "one fifth part".

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verhoudingen* (ratios), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is one example of the level, not a limit: one fraction with numerator 1, written out in words and then in fraction notation. It asks for the shorter way of writing and for no calculation of how many people that is. The fraction is printed with a slash (1/5) and is kept so. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child writes 5/1, 1.5 or 5% for a fifth. A child takes "a fifth" for the fifth one in a row, not for one of five equal parts. A child writes 1/5 correctly but cannot say what the 5 stands for.
