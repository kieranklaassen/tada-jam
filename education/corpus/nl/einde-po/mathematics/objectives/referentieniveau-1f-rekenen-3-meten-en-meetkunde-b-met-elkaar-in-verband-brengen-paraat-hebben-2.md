## Official wording

een 2D representatie van een 3D object zoals foto, plattegrond, landkaart (inclusief legenda), patroontekening

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Paraat hebben / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

a 2D representation of a 3D object such as photo, *plattegrond* (floor plan, or street plan), map (including legend), pattern drawing (*patroontekening*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once that a photo of the school, a floor plan of it and a map of the town are flat pictures of things that have length, width and height. The child points out on the photo which wall of the real building is shown, finds a classroom on the floor plan, and looks up in the legend of the map what a colour or symbol stands for. With a pattern drawing, for example a sewing pattern, the child says which flat piece becomes which part of the finished object.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement gives four examples after "such as": photo, *plattegrond*, map (with its legend) and pattern drawing. They are examples of the level, not a limit, and it names no other kind of representation. *Plattegrond* means a floor plan of a building and also a street plan of a town; the statement does not say which. *Patroontekening* is literally a pattern drawing; the word is commonly used for a drawn pattern for cutting out, such as a sewing pattern, and the statement does not explain it. The fragment has no verb: under knowing by heart, recognising these as flat representations of things with three dimensions is presumably meant. The statement does not mention scale.

### Common mistakes

A child reads a map or a floor plan as a picture seen from the side and looks for the height of things in it. A child does not use the legend and guesses what a colour or symbol means. A child cannot match the plan to the direction the child is facing and points to the wrong side.
