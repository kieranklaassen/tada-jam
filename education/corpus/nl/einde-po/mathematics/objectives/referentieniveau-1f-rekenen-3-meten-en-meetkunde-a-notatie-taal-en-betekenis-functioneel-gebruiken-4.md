## Official wording

eenvoudige routebeschrijving (linksaf, rechtsaf)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Functioneel gebruiken / 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

simple route description (turn left, turn right)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 walks from the classroom to the gym while following spoken steps such as "go out of the door, turn left, then take the second corridor on the right". The child also tells a visitor the way to the bicycle shed in the same kind of steps, or traces a described route with a finger on a street plan.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The brackets name two terms: turn left (*linksaf*) and turn right (*rechtsaf*). The fragment has no verb, so it does not say whether following a route description, giving one or both is meant. Compass directions and distances are not named in this statement. Routes on a map with a grid are a separate statement in part C, under *paraat hebben*.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up left and right when the route on a map runs towards the child, because left and right are then the other way round from the child's own view. A child leaves out a turn or the starting direction when giving a route.
