## Official wording

De leerling redeneert en rekent met gehele en decimale getallen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 10 A.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil reasons and calculates with whole numbers and decimal numbers.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 adds the prices on a till receipt, measures a jump in metres to two decimal places, and puts a card with 2.75 between 2.7 and 2.8 on a number line. (Dutch writes the decimal sign as a comma: 2,75.) The child estimates before calculating, and says why half of 8, written as 0.5 × 8, is 4.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 10, which is about numbers and ratios. Its items 10 A a to 10 A e name the operations, what is memorised, and the forms and ways of calculating. This goal states no number range and no number of decimal places. The Dutch *gehele getallen* can also be read as "integers"; the sentence does not say whether negative numbers are meant.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the decimal number with more digits as the larger one, so 0.125 is called larger than 0.5. In a written addition such as 3.5 + 0.25 a child lines the digits up from the right and not by the decimal sign. Many children expect multiplying to make a number larger, so they distrust 0.5 × 8 = 4.
