## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

enlargement as an application of ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 explains why an enlarged picture keeps its shape: every length is multiplied by the same number, so width and height keep the same ratio (10 by 15 cm and 20 by 30 cm are both 2 : 3). The child shows what goes wrong when the same number of centimetres is added to both sides: 20 by 25 cm is 4 : 5, a different shape.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement is a fragment without a verb: read with its kind, it concerns understanding why enlarging is a matter of ratios. It names no enlargement factor and no numbers. Making smaller and the effect on area are not named.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks enlarging means adding the same length to every side. A child thinks a picture that is twice as wide and twice as high has twice the area, where it has four times the area. A child calls every larger picture an enlargement, also one that has been stretched in one direction.
