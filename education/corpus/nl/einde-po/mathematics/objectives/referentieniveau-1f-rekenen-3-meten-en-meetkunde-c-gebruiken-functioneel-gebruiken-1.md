## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 changes a measure into another unit when a practical task needs it. A recipe asks for 0.5 kg of flour and the scales show grams, so the child weighs 500 g. A plank of 2.4 m has to fit a space measured as 235 cm, so the child writes 240 cm and sees that the plank is 5 cm too long. The child turns a walk of 1500 m into 1.5 km to compare it with a distance on a signpost.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names no units. "Common" is not defined, and the statement sets no number range and no direction of conversion. The unit pairs listed in the statement on the relationship between measures (part B, under *functioneel gebruiken*) are that statement's own and are not repeated here.

### Common mistakes

A child multiplies where dividing is needed and makes 1500 m into 1,500,000 km. A child uses the wrong step, such as 100 grams in a kilogram. A child converts the number but writes the old unit behind it.
