## Official wording

meetkundige patronen voortzetten (hoe weet je wat het volgende figuur uit de rij moet zijn)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Weten waarom / 5.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

continuing geometric patterns (how do you know what the next figure in the row must be)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at a row of figures laid with matches: one square, two squares side by side, three squares side by side. The child gives the rule in words before laying the next figure: each figure has one more square, and each new square needs 3 more matches, so the figures have 4, 7, 10 and then 13 matches. The child lays the fourth figure and counts to check.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names continuing geometric patterns, and the brackets hold a question: how the pupil knows what the next figure must be. It names no figures and no patterns. It stands under understanding why: the reasoning is asked for, and not only the next figure. Recognising and continuing simple patterns in rows of numbers and figures is a separate statement of domain *Verbanden*, part B, under *paraat hebben*.

### Common mistakes

A child lays a next figure that looks similar and cannot give the rule. A child follows one feature of the pattern, such as the number of pieces, and ignores another, such as the position or the turn. A child counts shared sides twice and says that the row of squares needs 4, 8 and 12 matches.
