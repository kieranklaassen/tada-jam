## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

ten-structure (*tienstructuur*, the grouping of numbers in tens)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 knows without working it out that ten ones make a ten, ten tens a hundred and ten hundreds a thousand. The child says at once that 340 is 34 tens, and counts a heap of coins by making piles of ten.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating to one another); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is one word, with no verb and no example. It does not say up to which place the structure goes, or whether tenths and hundredths are included. A statement of this row under *weten waarom* names the structure of the decimal system. This statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child sees a number as digits that stand next to each other, so 34 is "a 3 and a 4" and not 3 tens and 4 ones. Asked how many tens there are in 340, a child answers 4. A child does not use the tens when counting a large amount and counts everything by ones.
