## Official wording

taal van verhoudingen (per, op, van de)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Functioneel gebruiken / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

language of ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) (*per*, *op*, *van de*: "per", "in" or "to", "of the")

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads "€2.40 per kilo" on a market stall, "1 in 5 children comes by bike" in a newspaper and "scale 1 to 100" on a floor plan, and says for each what is compared with what. The child uses the same words when telling about a recipe: "2 eggs per 250 grams of flour."

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verhoudingen* (ratios), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The brackets name three Dutch words: *per* (per), *op* (as in "1 op de 4", 1 in 4, and "1 op 100", 1 to 100) and *van de* (as in "3 van de 10", 3 of the 10). The statement prints no "such as", so the pack claims neither that the list is complete nor that it is only an illustration. It has no verb; under this kind it is about using these words in practical situations. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child takes "€2.40 per kilo" as the price of the whole bag, whatever it weighs. A child mixes up "1 in 4" (one of every four, a quarter) and "1 to 4" (one against four, a fifth of the total). A child reads the two numbers of a ratio as two separate amounts and does not compare them.
