## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

unit fractions (*stambreuken*, fractions with numerator 1) [image: (1/3)], proper fractions [image: (2/5)], mixed numbers [image: (1 1/2)] and improper fractions [image: (12/4)];

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 folds a paper strip into four equal parts and names one part 1/4 (a unit fraction) and three parts 3/4 (a proper fraction). The child then lays seven quarter pieces in a row and names them both ways: 7/4 (an improper fraction) and 1 3/4 (a mixed number). The child says which of these amounts are less than one whole and which are more.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 10 B, which says that the pupil reasons and calculates with fractions as a number, a ratio and a division; 10 B sits under core goal 10. The item lists the four kinds of fraction that are meant. After each kind the law gives one example in round brackets: 1/3 for a unit fraction, 2/5 for a proper fraction, 1 1/2 for a mixed number and 12/4 for an improper fraction. The decree prints each example, with its round brackets, as an image; the wording holds the pack's transcription of each image in square brackets after the word "afbeelding", and the gloss writes "image" there. The images show each fraction with a horizontal bar; the transcription writes it on one line with a slash. The four are examples, not a limit. The example 12/4 equals the whole number 3. In a proper fraction (*echte breuk*) the numerator is smaller than the denominator; in an improper fraction (*onechte breuk*) it is not; a mixed number (*gemengd getal*) is a whole number with a fraction. This item states no range of denominators.

### Common mistakes

A child writes the mixed number 1 3/4 as 13/4, or reads it as 1 times 3/4. A child believes a fraction is always less than one whole and says 7/4 cannot exist, because 7 parts cannot be taken out of 4. When changing 7/4 into a mixed number a child keeps the 7 and writes 7 1/4.
