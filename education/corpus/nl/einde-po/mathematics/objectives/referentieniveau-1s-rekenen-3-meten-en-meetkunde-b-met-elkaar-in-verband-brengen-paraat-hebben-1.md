## Official wording

1 m³ = 1000 liter

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

1 m³ = 1000 litres

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once that 1 m³ is 1000 litres. Reading a water meter that shows 3 m³ used, the child says 3000 litres. The child also works in the other direction: a tank of 500 litres is half a cubic metre.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement is one equality with two units: the cubic metre (m³) and the litre. It names no other unit and no other relation. Standing under knowing by heart, it asks for the fact itself; it does not ask for an explanation of why the two are equal.

### Common mistakes

A child says that 1 m³ is 100 litres, or gives the relation that belongs to the cubic decimetre and says that 1 m³ is 1 litre. A child pictures 1 m³ as far smaller than it is and guesses that it holds a few buckets of water. A child reads m³ as m or as m².
