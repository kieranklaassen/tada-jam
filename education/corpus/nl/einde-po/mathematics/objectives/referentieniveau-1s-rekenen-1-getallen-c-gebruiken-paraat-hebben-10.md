## Official wording

een geheel getal vermenigvuldigen met een breuk of omgekeerd

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 10.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

multiplying a whole number by a fraction or the other way round

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out 6 × ¾ as six times three quarters, 18/4, which is 4½, and ¾ × 6 as three quarters of 6, also 4½. The child takes ⅔ of 12 marbles by dividing by 3 and multiplying by 2 and gets 8 marbles. The child knows that 5 bottles of ½ litre hold 2½ litres together.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement names two cases: a whole number times a fraction, and the other way round (*of omgekeerd*). It prints no example, names no denominators and sets no number range. Mixed numbers are not named in this statement.

### Common mistakes

A child multiplies both the numerator and the denominator by the whole number: 6 × ¾ = 18/24, which is ¾ again. A child expects multiplying to make a number larger and rejects ¾ × 6 = 4½ because it is less than 6. A child does not see that ¾ × 6 and "¾ of 6" are the same calculation.
