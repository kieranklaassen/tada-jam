## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising a trend in data

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at the temperatures measured at noon on each day of two weeks and says that they go up over the period, although two days were colder. The child points along the row of numbers or along the line of a graph to show the direction. The child says the trend in one sentence: "it is getting warmer", "it stays about the same" or "it goes down".

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for recognising a trend in data, in practical situations. It does not name the form in which the data are given, a kind of trend, or a calculation. Making predictions about a future situation on the basis of a graph or diagram is a separate statement in part C under *weten waarom*.

### Common mistakes

A child reads off single values and does not say in which direction the whole set moves. A child takes one high or low value for the trend. A child compares only the first and the last value.
