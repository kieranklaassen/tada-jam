## Official wording

uit het hoofd optellen, aftrekken,
vermenigvuldigen en delen met «nullen», ook met eenvoudige decimale getallen:
30 + 50
1200 – 800
65 × 10
3600 : 100
1000 × 2,5
0,25 × 100

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing (*delen*, which also means sharing) in the head with "zeros", also with simple decimal numbers:
30 + 50
1200 – 800
65 × 10
3600 : 100
1000 × 2.5
0.25 × 100

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once: 30 + 50 = 80, 1200 − 800 = 400, 65 × 10 = 650, 3600 ÷ 100 = 36, 1000 × 2.5 = 2500 and 0.25 × 100 = 25. The child says how: 12 hundreds minus 8 hundreds is 4 hundreds; times 100 moves every digit two places to the left.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement puts "zeros" in quotation marks and does not define the word; the examples show round numbers that end in zeros, and multiplying and dividing by 10, 100 and 1000. The six examples are examples of the level, not a limit; the record holds each on its own line. The decree's table breaks the statement itself over two rows, after "aftrekken,", and the wording keeps that break as a line break; the gloss reads the two lines as one sentence. "Simple" decimal numbers are not defined. The statement sets no number range. The decree prints the Dutch decimal comma (2,5) and writes division with a colon; the gloss writes the English decimal point and keeps the colon.

### Common mistakes

A child puts zeros behind a decimal number and writes 0.25 × 100 = 0.2500. A child counts the zeros wrongly and says 3600 ÷ 100 = 360. A child multiplies the two parts of 2.5 apart from each other and writes 1000 × 2.5 = 2000.5.
