## Official wording

een geheel getal delen door een breuk of gemengd getal:
10 : 2 1/2

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dividing (*delen*, which also means sharing) a whole number by a fraction or mixed number:
10 : 2 1/2

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads 10 ÷ 2½ as "how many times does 2½ fit into 10?" and answers 4, because 4 × 2½ = 10. The child works out how many pieces of ½ metre can be cut from 6 metres of ribbon: 6 ÷ ½ = 12. The child finds 4 ÷ ⅔ = 6 by checking that 6 × ⅔ = 4.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). 10 : 2 1/2 (ten divided by two and a half) is an example of the level, not a limit. The number divided is a whole number in this statement; dividing a fraction or a mixed number by a fraction is a separate statement of this level under the same sub-heading and kind. The statement names no denominators and sets no number range. The colon is the division sign of Dutch arithmetic, and the source prints the mixed number with full-size digits and a space (2 1/2); it means 2½.

### Common mistakes

A child expects dividing to make a number smaller, halves 6 where 6 ÷ ½ is asked and answers 3. A child divides the other way round, 2½ ÷ 10. A child reads 2 1/2 as 2 × ½ or as 21/2.
