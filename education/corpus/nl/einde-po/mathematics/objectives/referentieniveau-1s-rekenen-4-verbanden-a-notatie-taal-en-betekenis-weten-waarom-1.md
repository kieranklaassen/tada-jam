## Official wording

grafiek in de betekenis van «grafische voorstelling»

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1S, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Weten waarom / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

graph (*grafiek*) in the meaning of "graphic representation"

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays a line graph, a bar chart and a pie chart side by side and explains that each is a *grafiek* in this wide sense: a drawing that represents data. The child says what the drawing makes visible that a list of numbers does not, such as which value is the largest.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement is about the meaning of a word: *grafiek* understood as any graphic representation (*grafische voorstelling*, which the source prints between « »). It names no kind of graph. It has no verb; under this heading, understanding the word in this wide sense is presumably meant.

### Common mistakes

A child uses the word graph only for a line graph and does not count other drawings of data. A child reads a graph as a picture of the scene, for instance a rising line as a hill. A child looks at the shape of a graph without reading what its axes or parts stand for.
