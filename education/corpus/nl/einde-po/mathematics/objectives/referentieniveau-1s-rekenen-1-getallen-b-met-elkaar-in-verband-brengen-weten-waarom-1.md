## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 explains that each place is worth ten times the place to its right: ten ones make a ten, ten tens make a hundred, and after the decimal point ten hundredths make a tenth. The child splits 4306.25 into 4 thousands, 3 hundreds, 0 tens, 6 ones, 2 tenths and 5 hundredths. The child explains why multiplying by 10 moves every digit one place to the left, so that 36.5 becomes 365.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading B (relating to one another). The statement is a fragment without a verb: read with its kind, it concerns understanding how the place-value system is built and why it works. "Decimal" here names the base of ten of the system. The statement does not say how far the places run to the left or to the right of the decimal point, and it sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the digits after the decimal point as a second whole number and calls 0.125 larger than 0.5. A child expects the places after the point to mirror those before it and looks for a "oneths" place. A child says that multiplying by 10 means writing a zero at the end and gives 3.5 × 10 = 3.50.
