## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

structure of the decimal system (*tientallig stelsel*, the base-ten number system)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 explains why the 3 in 3405 is worth 3000 and the 3 in 0.3 is worth three tenths: each place is worth ten times the place to its right. The child explains why the 0 in 3405 cannot be left out. The child explains why 65 × 10 = 650 and 2.5 × 10 = 25: every digit moves one place to the left, so the rule "put a zero behind it" does not work for 2.5.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in row B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating to one another); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is a noun phrase with no verb; under this kind it is about understanding why the system works as it does. It does not say whether decimal places are included, and sets no number range. A statement of this row under *paraat hebben* names the ten-structure (*tienstructuur*).

### Common mistakes

A child uses "put a zero behind it" as the rule for multiplying by 10 and writes 2.5 × 10 = 2.50. A child treats zero as nothing and leaves it out of a number. A child can name the places but cannot say that a hundred is ten tens.
