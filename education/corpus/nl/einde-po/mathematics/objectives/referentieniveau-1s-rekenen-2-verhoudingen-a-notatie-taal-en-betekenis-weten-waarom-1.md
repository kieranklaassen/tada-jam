## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 compares two results by the share and not by the amounts alone: 8 goals from 10 shots is better shooting than 12 goals from 20 shots, although 12 is more than 8, because 8 of 10 is 80% and 12 of 20 is 60%. The child explains in the child's own words why the totals have to be taken into account. The child does not need the name for this kind of comparing.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading A (notation, language and meaning). The brackets say "not the term" (*term niet*): the pupil does not have to know the term "relative comparison"; the statement is about the idea. The statement is a fragment without a verb: read with its kind, it concerns understanding what comparing relatively is and why it is done. It names no numbers and no form (fractions, percentages or ratios).

### Common mistakes

A child compares the amounts alone and calls 12 of 20 better than 8 of 10 because 12 is more. A child compares what is missing and calls 3 of 5 and 5 of 7 equally good because each is 2 short.
