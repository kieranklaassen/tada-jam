## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about fractions, for example: is there a smallest fraction?

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 argues that there is no smallest fraction above 0: take any small fraction, such as 1/1000, halve it, and 1/2000 is smaller again. The child folds a strip in half again and again, names the parts ½, ¼, ⅛ and 1/16, and says why the halving never has to stop. The child gives the reasoning in words or a drawing, not only a yes or a no.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading B (relating to one another). The question "is there a smallest fraction?" follows "for example" (*bijvoorbeeld*): it is an example of the reasoning meant, not the only question and not a limit. The statement prints no answer to the question. It names no denominators and sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child names a fraction with a large denominator, such as 1/100 or 1/1,000,000, as the smallest. A child thinks a larger denominator makes a larger fraction and so looks for the smallest fraction among the small denominators. A child answers yes or no without giving a reason.
