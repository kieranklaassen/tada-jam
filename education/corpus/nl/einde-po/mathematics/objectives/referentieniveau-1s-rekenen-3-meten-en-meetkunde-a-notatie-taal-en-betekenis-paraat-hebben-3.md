## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once that milli- means a thousandth, centi- a hundredth and kilo- a thousand. The child applies this to a unit: a millimetre is a thousandth of a metre, a centilitre is a hundredth of a litre, a kilogram is a thousand grams. Meeting the same prefix on an unfamiliar unit, the child still says what it means.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for the meaning of the prefixes of units. "Such as" (*zoals*) introduces examples: milli-, centi- and kilo- are examples of the level, not a limit, and the statement names no other prefix. It names no units to which the prefixes are attached. The decree prints this as a statement of its own, apart from the other statements on measures under the same heading. Standing under knowing by heart, it asks for the meanings as ready knowledge.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up milli- and centi-. A child links milli- to a million and thinks that a metre holds a million millimetres. A child knows kilometre and kilogram as whole words and does not see that kilo- means a thousand in both.
