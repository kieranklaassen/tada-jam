## Official wording

verhoudingen en breuken met een rekenmachine omzetten in een (afgerond) kommagetal

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Functioneel gebruiken / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

converting ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) and fractions with a calculator into a (rounded) decimal number (*kommagetal*, literally "comma number")

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 keys 5 ÷ 8 into a calculator for the fraction ⅝ and reads 0.625. For 7 goals from 9 penalty kicks the child keys 7 ÷ 9, sees 0.7777777 on the display and writes 0.78. The child decides how many decimals to keep for the situation.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading B (relating to one another). The calculator is part of this statement. The word "rounded" stands in brackets: the result may be rounded, and the statement does not say to how many decimals. It names both ratios and fractions, gives no example and sets no number range. *Kommagetal* is the everyday Dutch word for a decimal number, which Dutch writes with a comma (0,625).

### Common mistakes

A child keys the numbers in the wrong order: 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6. A child copies every digit on the display, or cuts off and writes 0.77. For a ratio such as 2 : 3 a child does not know which division is meant: one part by the other (2 ÷ 3) or one part by the whole (2 ÷ 5).
