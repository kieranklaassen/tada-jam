## Official wording

delingen uit de tafels (tot en met 10) uit het hoofd kennen

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing by heart the divisions from the multiplication tables (up to and including 10)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 answers at once, without counting or working out: 56 ÷ 8 = 7, 63 ÷ 9 = 7, 42 ÷ 6 = 7 and 90 ÷ 10 = 9. The child uses a multiplication fact in both directions: 7 × 8 = 56, so 56 ÷ 8 = 7 and 56 ÷ 7 = 8.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The brackets give the range: up to and including 10 (*tot en met*, so 10 itself is included). They stand after "the tables", so the range reads most directly as the tables of up to 10; the statement does not say how far each table runs. Dividing with a remainder is a separate statement of this level under the same sub-heading and kind.

### Common mistakes

A child works out each division by repeated subtraction or by reciting the table, and does not know it by heart. A child mixes up neighbouring facts and answers 54 ÷ 9 = 7 or 56 ÷ 8 = 6. A child knows 7 × 8 = 56 and does not link it to 56 ÷ 7.
