## Official wording

memoriseren van getalrelaties, splitsingen van getallen tot 20 en de tafels van vermenigvuldiging, en deze kennis vlot en wendbaar toepassen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 10 A b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

memorising number relations (*getalrelaties*, known links between numbers), the splits of numbers up to 20 (*splitsingen*, the ways to split a number into two parts) and the multiplication tables, and applying this knowledge fluently and flexibly;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says without counting that 13 splits into 8 and 5 or into 6 and 7, and that 7 × 8 is 56. The child uses one known fact to reach another: 6 × 8 is 48, so 7 × 8 is one more 8; 100 is 4 × 25, so 12 × 25 is 3 × 100. The child also uses a fact in the other direction and says that 56 divided by 7 is 8.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 10 A, which says that the pupil reasons and calculates with whole and decimal numbers; 10 A sits under core goal 10. The range "up to 20" is stated for the splits of numbers. The Dutch *tot 20* does not say outright whether 20 itself is included; in Dutch school use it normally is. The item does not say which multiplication tables are meant, and it gives no range and no list for the number relations. It asks for two things: knowing these facts from memory, and using them quickly and in more than one way.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up table facts that lie close together, such as 7 × 8 = 54 or 6 × 7 with 6 × 8. A child knows a table only as a row to recite, has to start at 1 × 7 to reach 6 × 7, and cannot go back from 42 to 6 × 7. A child still counts on fingers for a split such as 13 into 8 and 5.
