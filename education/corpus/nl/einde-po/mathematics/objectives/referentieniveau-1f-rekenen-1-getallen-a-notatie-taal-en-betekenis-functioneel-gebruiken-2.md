## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

number names such as three quarters (*driekwart*), one and a half (*anderhalf*) and million

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 hears "one and a half hours" and sets a timer to 90 minutes. The child reads "three quarters of a litre" in a recipe and fills a measuring jug to 750 ml. The child reads "2 million visitors" in a newspaper and writes it as a 2 with six zeros.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. "Zoals" (such as) introduces the three names as examples; the statement gives no complete list. Dutch has a single word for each of the three (*driekwart*, *anderhalf*, *miljoen*). The statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child takes one and a half hours as 1.30 and calculates with it as a decimal number, where it is 1.5 hours or 90 minutes. A child reads three quarters as "3 and a quarter" or as 3.4. A child writes a million with the wrong number of zeros, for example with five.
