## Official wording

een breuk met een breuk vermenigvuldigen of een deel van een deel nemen, met name in situaties:
¹/₂ deel van ¹/₂ liter
³/₄ × ⁵/₈

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 pours half of a half-litre carton into a glass and says the glass holds a quarter of a litre: ½ × ½ = ¼. For ¾ × ⅝ the child multiplies the numerators and the denominators and gets 15/32. The child shows why on a rectangle divided into 4 rows of 8 boxes: 3 rows of 5 boxes are 15 of the 32 boxes.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement says "particularly in situations" (*met name in situaties*): it stresses situations and does not exclude calculations without one; its second example, ³/₄ × ⁵/₈, is a calculation without a situation. Both examples are examples of the level, not limits. The statement names no denominators and sets no number range, and mixed numbers are not named. The record holds each example on its own line.

### Common mistakes

A child takes "half of a half" as ½ + ½ and answers 1 litre. A child makes the denominators equal first, as for adding, and then multiplies only the numerators: 6/8 × 5/8 = 30/8. A child expects multiplying to make a number larger and distrusts an answer that is smaller than both fractions.
