## Official wording

relatie tussen ruimtelijke figuren en bijbehorende bouwplaten

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Weten waarom / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

relation between three-dimensional figures (*ruimtelijke figuren*) and the corresponding nets (*bouwplaten*, flat patterns that fold into the figure)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 cuts an empty cardboard box open along its edges, lays it flat, and says which flat piece was which face. Looking at a drawn net, the child says which figure it folds into and which faces will lie opposite each other, then cuts it out and folds it to check. The child explains why six squares in one straight row do not fold into a cube.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The fragment names the link between three-dimensional figures and their nets. It names no figures. It has no verb; under this heading, understanding and explaining how a figure and its net belong together is presumably meant.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks that any six joined squares fold into a cube. A child cannot say which edges of a net meet when it is folded. A child draws a net with a face missing, or with two faces that fold onto the same place.
