## Official wording

3,5 is 3 en 5/10

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Paraat hebben / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

3,5 is 3 and 5/10

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once that 3,5 is three wholes and five tenths, writes 3,5 as 3 5/10 and writes 3 5/10 as 3,5. On a ruler the child shows 3,5 cm as 3 centimetres and 5 millimetres.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verhoudingen* (ratios), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The gloss and these notes keep the Dutch decimal comma, because the statement is about notation; English writes 3.5. The statement is a bare example of the level, not a limit: a number with one decimal place, written as a whole number and tenths. It does not simplify 5/10 to ½. The fraction is printed with a slash and is kept so. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads 3,5 as 3 and 1/5, or as the fraction 3/5. A child takes 3,5 hours for 3 hours and 5 minutes. A child reads the comma as a separator between two whole numbers, "3 and 5".
