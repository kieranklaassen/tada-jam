## Official wording

De leerling interpreteert data.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil interprets data.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school looks at a table of how classmates travel to school, or at a graph of the temperature over a week, and says what it shows: which way of travelling is most common, on which day it was warmest, what changed during the week. The child also says what cannot be read from it.

### Limits

This is the sentence of core goal 12 itself. One goal sentence, 12 A, adds representing data, and its items 12 A a to 12 A e name tables, the average, graphical representations and judging conclusions. The sentence does not say what kind of data or how much. This goal states no number range. The reference levels 1F and 1S, which are other records of this lane (domain *Verbanden*, relationships), are worded in more detail.

### Common mistakes

A child reads single values ("on Tuesday it was 14 degrees") and does not say what the whole table or graph shows. A child draws a conclusion that goes beyond the data: from one class the child concludes that most children in the country cycle to school.
