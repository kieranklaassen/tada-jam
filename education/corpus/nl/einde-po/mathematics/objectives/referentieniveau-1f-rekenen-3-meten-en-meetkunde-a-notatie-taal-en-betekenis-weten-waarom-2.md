## Official wording

een vierkante meter hoeft geen vierkant te zijn

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Weten waarom / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

a square metre does not have to be a square

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 marks out a square of 1 m by 1 m on the floor with tape, and next to it a strip of 2 m by 0.5 m. The child cuts a paper square of 1 m² into four strips, lays them end to end or in an L shape, and explains that the amount of paper is still 1 m² although the shape is no longer a square.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is a single insight: an area of one square metre can have a shape that is not a square. It names the square metre only; it names no other unit, no figure other than the square and no calculation. It does not say that the same holds for other square units or for cubic units.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks an area can be given in square metres only if the surface is a square. A child reads "4 square metres" as a square with sides of 4 metres, which is 16 m². A child thinks that cutting a surface up and laying the pieces differently changes its area.
