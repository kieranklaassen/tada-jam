## Official wording

breuken omzetten in een kommagetal, eindig of oneindig aantal decimalen

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Weten waarom / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

converting fractions into a decimal number (*kommagetal*, literally "comma number"), finite or infinite number of decimals

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 divides 1 by 4 and stops at 0.25 with nothing left, then divides 1 by 3 and sees the same remainder come back at every step, so the 3s never stop: 0.333... The child explains why: when the remainder becomes 0 the decimals end, and when a remainder comes back the digits repeat without end. The child tries ⅛ (0.125, which ends) and ⅙ (0.1666..., which does not).

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading B (relating to one another). Read with its kind, the statement concerns understanding that converting a fraction gives a decimal number that either ends or goes on without end, and why. It does not say which fractions end, gives no rule about denominators, names no notation or term for repeating decimals and does not mention a calculator. It sets no number range. *Kommagetal* is the everyday Dutch word for a decimal number, which Dutch writes with a comma (0,25).

### Common mistakes

A child takes the calculator display for the whole number and holds that ⅓ is exactly 0.3333333. A child thinks a decimal number that never ends is a very large number. A child holds that ⅔ is exactly 0.67.
