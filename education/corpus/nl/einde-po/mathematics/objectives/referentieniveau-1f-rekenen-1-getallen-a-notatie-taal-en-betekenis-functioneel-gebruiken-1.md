## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

pronunciation and written form of whole numbers, fractions and decimal numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a price tag of €12.95 aloud and reads "1.5 litres" on a bottle as one and a half litres. On hearing "three thousand and forty" the child writes 3040. The child reads ¾ in a recipe as three quarters, and writes "two and a half" as 2½ or 2.5.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names three kinds of number and has no verb; under this kind it is about saying and writing numbers that are met in practical situations. It sets no number range, and names no denominators and no number of decimal places. Dutch writes the decimal sign as a comma (12,95).

### Common mistakes

Dutch says the ones before the tens (45 is *vijfenveertig*, "five-and-forty"), so a child who writes a number from hearing swaps the digits and writes 54. A child writes a number with an empty place as it sounds: "three thousand and forty" becomes 300040 or 340. A child writes two euros and five cents as 2.5.
