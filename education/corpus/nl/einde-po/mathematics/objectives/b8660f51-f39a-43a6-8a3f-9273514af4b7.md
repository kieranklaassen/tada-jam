## Official wording

rekenvormen: hoofdrekenen, schattend rekenen, schriftelijk rekenen en rekenen met de rekenmachine;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 10 A d.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

forms of calculation (*rekenvormen*): mental calculation, calculation by estimating, written calculation and calculation with the calculator;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out 6 × 25 mentally, rounds 398 × 21 to 400 × 20 to say about how much it is, sets out 3476 + 2859 on paper, and keys a long division into a calculator and reads the display. The child can say afterwards which of the four was used.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 10 A, which says that the pupil reasons and calculates with whole and decimal numbers; 10 A sits under core goal 10. It explains the term *rekenvorm* used in item 10 A c and lists four forms; the calculator is one of them. It does not say when each form is to be used: item 10 A c asks the child to choose. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks an estimate is a wrong answer, works out the exact answer first and then rounds it. A child keys a number wrongly into the calculator (a missed decimal sign, two digits swapped) and does not notice that the result cannot be right. A child reads a calculator result such as 4.5 as "4 remainder 5".
