## Official wording

formele schrijfwijze 1 : 100 («staat tot») herkennen en gebruiken

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Paraat hebben / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and using the formal notation 1 : 100 ("is to", *staat tot*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads 1 : 100 aloud as "one is to one hundred" (*één staat tot honderd*) and knows at once that it gives a ratio, not a division that has to be worked out. The child writes a ratio in this form: 1 part syrup to 7 parts water as 1 : 7, and a floor plan on which 1 cm stands for 100 cm as 1 : 100.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading A (notation, language and meaning). 1 : 100 is the printed example of the notation, not a limit on the ratios. The statement has two verbs: recognising and using. In Dutch arithmetic the colon is also the division sign; the statement gives the reading "is to" (*staat tot*) for this notation. "Formal" is not explained; the word implies less formal ways of writing a ratio, which this statement does not name.

### Common mistakes

A child reads 1 : 100 as the division 1 ÷ 100 and answers 0.01 where a ratio is meant. A child takes 1 : 7 for syrup and water to mean that 1/7 of the drink is syrup, where it is ⅛. A child turns the order round and writes 100 : 1 for a drawing that is smaller than the real thing.
