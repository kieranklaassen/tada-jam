## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering *maten* (units of measure, or measured sizes)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lines up three parcels by weight after reading their labels: 750 g, 1.2 kg and 2 kg. The child decides which of two bottles holds more, one of 1.5 l and one of 75 cl, and puts the heights of four children in order when some are given in metres and some in centimetres.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names no quantity, no units and no number range. *Maten* can mean units of measure (a centimetre is smaller than a metre) or measured sizes (1.2 kg is more than 750 g); the statement does not say which, and both can be compared and ordered. The unit pairs listed in the statement on the relationship between measures, under the same part and kind, are that statement's own and are not repeated here.

### Common mistakes

A child compares the numbers and ignores the units, so 750 g counts as more than 1.2 kg. A child reads the number with more digits as the larger measure and puts 1.25 m above 1.5 m. A child orders from large to small when small to large is asked.
