## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

choosing the right unit of measurement for a situation or calculation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who measures a classroom for a new floor decides to measure the sides in metres and to give the area in m². Before adding a plank of 2.5 m and a plank of 40 cm, the child writes both in one unit: 250 cm and 40 cm make 290 cm, or 2.5 m and 0.4 m make 2.9 m. The child writes the unit beside the answer.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for choosing a unit of measurement that is right for a situation or for a calculation, in practical use. It names no units and no situations. Reasoning about which measure fits in which context is a separate statement in part A under *weten waarom*.

### Common mistakes

A child calculates with numbers in different units without converting, and adds 2.5 m and 40 cm to 42.5. A child gives the answer without a unit. A child chooses a unit that gives an awkward number, such as the length of a street in millimetres.
