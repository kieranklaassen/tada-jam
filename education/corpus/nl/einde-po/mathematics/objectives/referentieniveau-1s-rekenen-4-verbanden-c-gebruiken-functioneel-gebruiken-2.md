## Official wording

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Functioneel gebruiken / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing global (*globale*, giving only the general course) graphs, for example: who is first at the finish?

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at two lines in one sketch, one for each runner in a race, with time along the bottom and distance up the side. The child finds the height that stands for the finish and says whose line reaches it first, further to the left. The child also says who was ahead halfway and where one runner passed the other.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for comparing global graphs in practical situations. *Globaal* means rough or overall: such a graph gives the general course of something; the statement does not define the word further. "For example" (*bijvoorbeeld*) introduces one example, the question who reaches the finish first; it is an example of the level, not a limit. The statement names no kind of graph and no quantities for the axes.

### Common mistakes

A child picks the line that ends highest or is longest. A child picks the line that starts steepest and does not follow both lines to the finish. A child reads the sketch as a picture of the track and takes the crossing of two lines for a place where two paths cross.
