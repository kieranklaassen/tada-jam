## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads 1½ litres on a bottle and 2¼ hours on a timetable and says them as "one and a half" and "two and a quarter". Measuring a plank of two whole metres and three quarters of a metre, the child writes 2¾ metres. Following a recipe that asks for 1½ teaspoons, the child fills one whole spoon and then half a spoon.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading A (notation, language and meaning). The statement is a single term without a verb: read with its sub-heading and kind, it concerns using the notation, the word and the meaning of a mixed number in practical situations. A mixed number is a whole number written with a fraction, such as 2¾; this example is the pack's, the statement prints none. The statement names no denominators and sets no number range. Adding and subtracting mixed numbers and writing a fraction as a mixed number are separate statements of this level (sub-heading C, *paraat hebben*).

### Common mistakes

A child reads 2¾ as 2 times ¾, or as the fraction 23/4. A child does not see that 2¾ lies between 2 and 3 and places it before 2 or after 3 on a number line.
