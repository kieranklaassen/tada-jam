## Official wording

optellen en aftrekken van breuken en gemengde getallen zoals 6¾, ook via standaardprocedures

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 9.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding and subtracting fractions and mixed numbers such as 6¾, also by way of standard procedures

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 gives ½ + ¼ = ¾ at once, and for unlike denominators makes them equal first: ⅔ + ¾ = 8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12 = 1 5/12. With mixed numbers the child deals with the wholes and the fractions: 6¾ + 2½ = 9¼. For 6¾ − 2⅞ the child exchanges one whole for eight eighths (5 14/8 − 2⅞) and gets 3⅞.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). 6¾ follows "such as" (*zoals*): it is an example of a mixed number, not a limit. The statement names no denominators and does not say whether they are like or unlike. "Also by way of standard procedures" adds procedures to other ways of calculating; the statement names neither. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child adds numerators and denominators: ½ + ⅓ = 2/5. In a subtraction with mixed numbers a child takes the smaller fraction from the larger whichever number it belongs to: 6¼ − 2¾ = 4½ (it is 3½). A child leaves a result as 8 5/4, or drops the extra whole and writes 6¾ + 2½ = 8¼.
