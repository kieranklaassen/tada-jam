## Official wording

De leerlingen leren aanpakken bij het oplossen van reken-wiskundeproblemen te onderbouwen en leren oplossingen te beoordelen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 25.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to give reasons for approaches in solving problems in arithmetic and mathematics, and learn to judge solutions.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says why 16 × 25 was done by halving and doubling: "8 × 50 is easier, and it is 400." The child compares this way with another child's way and says which was shorter. The child checks a solution against an estimate (20 × 25 = 500, so 400 is possible) and against the question (a number of buses has to be a whole number).

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is the third of three goals under the heading *Wiskundig inzicht en handelen* (mathematical insight and acting). The goal names two things: giving reasons for an approach, and judging solutions. It does not say whose solutions are judged, nor by what standard. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

Asked for a reason, a child repeats the steps taken and does not say why this approach fits the problem. A child judges a solution by whether it looks neat, not by whether the answer can be right. A child holds that only one approach is correct and calls another correct way wrong.
