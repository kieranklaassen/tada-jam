## Official wording

rekenen met percentages, ook met moeilijkere getallen en minder «mooie» percentages (eventueel met de rekenmachine)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating with percentages, also with more difficult numbers and less "nice" percentages (possibly with the calculator)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out 15% of 240 as 10% plus 5%: 24 + 12 = 36, and 35% of 80 as 28. For 17% of 365 euros the child takes 1% (3.65 euros) 17 times, or keys 0.17 × 365 into a calculator, and gets 62.05 euros. The child also finds what percentage 45 is of 60: 75%.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). "More difficult numbers" and "less nice percentages" are comparisons that the statement does not define; it names no numbers and no percentages, and the source sets "nice" in quotation marks. The word "also" (*ook*) shows that these are in addition to something the statement does not name. The calculator is optional: *eventueel* means "possibly" or "if need be". The statement does not say which kinds of percentage calculation are meant.

### Common mistakes

A child divides by the percentage: 15% of 240 becomes 240 ÷ 15 = 16. A child keys 17 × 365 into the calculator and does not divide by 100. A child works out the discount and gives it as the new price.
