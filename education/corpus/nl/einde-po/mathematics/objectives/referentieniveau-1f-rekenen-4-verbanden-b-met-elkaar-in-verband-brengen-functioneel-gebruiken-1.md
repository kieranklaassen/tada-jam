## Official wording

eenvoudige patronen (vanuit situatie) beschrijven in woorden, bijvoorbeeld: Vogels vliegen in V-vorm. «Er komen er steeds 2 bij.»

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1F, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Functioneel gebruiken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing simple patterns (from a situation) in words, for example: Birds fly in a V shape. "Each time 2 more are added."

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at drawings of birds flying in a V: first 1 bird, then 3, then 5, then 7. The child says in the child's own words what happens: "each time 2 birds are added, one on each side". The child does the same for a row of tables with chairs around it or a growing stack of cans, and says how the number changes from one step to the next.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement gives one example after "for example": birds that fly in a V shape, described as "each time 2 more are added". It is an example of the level, not a limit; the statement names no other pattern. The description is in words: no formula, table or number range is named. The pattern comes "from a situation", and the statement does not say how the situation is given (seen, drawn or told).

### Common mistakes

A child describes what the picture looks like ("it is a V") and not how the number changes. A child gives the numbers of one step but no rule that holds for every step. A child counts the bird at the point of the V with both arms and gets the count wrong.
