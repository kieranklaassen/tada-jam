## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading off information from common tables such as transport timetable (*dienstregeling*), lesson timetable (*lesrooster*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 finds in a bus timetable the first bus after 8:15 from a given stop and the time it arrives in town. In the lesson timetable of the class the child runs a finger along the row for Thursday and the column for the third lesson and says which subject is given then. The child does this as a routine, without help.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names two tables after "such as": a transport timetable and a lesson timetable. They are examples of the level, not a limit; it names no other table and does not define "common". It asks for reading off information. Using information from tables for calculations and conclusions is a separate statement in part C.

### Common mistakes

A child reads along the wrong row or column and gives the time at the next stop or the lesson of the next day. A child gives the departure time where the arrival time is asked. A child does not read the headings or the notes with a table, for example that a bus does not run on Saturdays.
