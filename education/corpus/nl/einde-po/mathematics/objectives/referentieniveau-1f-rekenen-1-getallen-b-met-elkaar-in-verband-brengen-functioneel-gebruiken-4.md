## Official wording

splitsen en samenstellen van getallen op basis van het tientallig stelsel

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Functioneel gebruiken / 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

splitting and composing numbers on the basis of the decimal system (*tientallig stelsel*, the base-ten number system)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 splits 3405 into 3 thousands, 4 hundreds, 0 tens and 5 ones. The child pays 347 euros with 3 notes of a hundred, 4 notes of ten and 7 coins of one euro, and also with 34 notes of ten and 7 coins. The child composes "6 hundreds, 12 tens and 3 ones" into 723.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in row B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating to one another); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The splitting named here follows the places of the number (ones, tens, hundreds); a split of another kind, 12 = 7 + 5, is an example in a statement of row C. The statement does not say whether decimal numbers are included. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child leaves out the zero for an empty place and writes 3 thousands, 4 hundreds and 5 ones as 345. A child writes three hundred and five as 3005. A child does not see that 12 tens are 120, because each place is thought to hold at most 9.
