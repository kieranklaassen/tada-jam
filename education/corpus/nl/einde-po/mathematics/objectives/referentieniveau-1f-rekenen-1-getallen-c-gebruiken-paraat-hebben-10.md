## Official wording

vergelijken en ordenen van de grootte van eenvoudige breuken en deze in betekenisvolle situaties op de getallenlijn plaatsen:
¼ liter is minder dan ½ liter

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 10.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering the size of simple fractions and placing them on the number line in meaningful situations:
¼ litre is less than ½ litre

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 fills one measuring jug to the mark of ¼ litre and another to ½ litre and says which holds less. The child knows without working it out that ¼ is less than ½ and ½ less than ¾, and points to the three on the scale of a jug of 1 litre, which is a number line from 0 to 1.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement does not define "simple fractions"; the example uses a quarter and a half. Placing fractions on the number line is tied to meaningful situations. The example is an example of the level, not a limit. The fractions ¼ and ½ are kept as the decree prints them, with raised and lowered digits.

### Common mistakes

A child calls ¼ more than ½ because 4 is more than 2. A child places a fraction on the line by its denominator, so ¼ is put at 4. A child compares only the numerators and calls ¾ and 3/8 equal.
