## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

markings on a compass rose (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW; in Dutch N, NO, O, ZO, Z, ZW, W, NW)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at the compass rose on a map and says that the lake lies south-west (ZW) of the village. The child turns the map until the N of the compass rose points north, then points in the direction NO. The child follows a route description such as "go east to the bridge, then north-west".

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The brackets name eight directions by their Dutch abbreviations: N (*noord*, north), NO (*noordoost*, north-east), O (*oost*, east), ZO (*zuidoost*, south-east), Z (*zuid*, south), ZW (*zuidwest*, south-west), W (west) and NW (*noordwest*, north-west). The statement names no finer directions and no degrees. The fragment has no verb; under this heading, using the markings in practical situations is presumably meant.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up east and west, O and W. A child takes the top of every map, or the direction the child is facing, to be north without looking at the compass rose. A child mixes up the directions between the four main ones and reads NO as the direction between north and west.
