## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at the legend beside a diagram or a map to find out what each colour, line style or symbol stands for, for example that the blue bars are for boys and the red bars for girls. The child then uses this to answer a practical question about what the diagram or map shows.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The fragment is two words and has no verb. It follows the statement on reading and interpreting simple graphs and diagrams under the same part and kind, so reading and using a simple legend is presumably meant. It does not say what kind of graph, diagram or map the legend belongs to, and it does not define "simple".

### Common mistakes

A child does not look at the legend and guesses what a colour or symbol means. A child swaps two entries of the legend, mostly when the colours or patterns look alike. A child reads a symbol that stands for several units, such as one figure for 10 people, as 1.
