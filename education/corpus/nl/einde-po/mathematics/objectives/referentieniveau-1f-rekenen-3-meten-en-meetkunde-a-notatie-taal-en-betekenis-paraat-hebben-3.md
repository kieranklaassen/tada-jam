## Official wording

namen van enkele vlakke en ruimtelijke figuren, zoals rechthoek, vierkant, cirkel, kubus, bol

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Paraat hebben / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

names of some plane and solid figures, such as rectangle, square, circle, cube, sphere

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 names shapes straight away when seeing or holding them: a door is a rectangle, a floor tile a square, the rim of a cup a circle, a die a cube, a football a sphere. The child picks the cube and the sphere out of a box of solids when asked for them by name.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement gives five names after "such as": rectangle, square and circle, which are plane figures, and cube and sphere, which are solid figures. They are examples of the level, not a closed list; "some" is not specified, and the statement names no other figure. It asks for the names: properties of the figures and calculation are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

A child uses the name of the flat figure for the solid one and calls a cube a square or a sphere a circle. A child does not accept a square as a square when it stands on a corner. A child does not see that a square is also a rectangle.
