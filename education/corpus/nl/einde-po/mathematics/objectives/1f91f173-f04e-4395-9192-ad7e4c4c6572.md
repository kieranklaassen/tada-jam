## Official wording

relaties leggen tussen verhoudingen, procenten en breuken;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 10 C b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making connections between ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion), percentages and fractions;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads "1 in 5 children wears glasses" and says that this is 1/5 of the children and 20%. For a class of 30 the child counts out 6 children. To compare "3 out of 4" in one group with 70% in another, the child changes 3 out of 4 into 75%.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 10 C, which says that the pupil reasons and calculates with ratios; 10 C sits under core goal 10. Item 10 B b, under the goal sentence on fractions, names a similar link and adds decimal numbers; this item does not name decimal numbers. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the ratio 1 to 4 (one part against four other parts) as the fraction 1/4, where it is 1/5 of the whole. A child says "1 in 5" is 5%. A child thinks equal percentages mean equal numbers: 20% of a small class and 20% of a large school are taken to be the same number of children.
