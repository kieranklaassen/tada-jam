## Official wording

op basis van een grafiek of diagram voorspellingen doen over een toekomstige situatie

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Weten waarom / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making predictions about a future situation on the basis of a graph or diagram

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at a line graph of the height of a bean plant measured each week: 4 cm, 6 cm, 8 cm, 10 cm. The child extends the line and predicts about 12 cm for the next week, with the reason that the plant grew 2 cm each week. The child adds that the prediction is uncertain and that the plant will not keep growing at that rate.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for predictions about a future situation, made from a graph or a diagram. These two are named; the table is not named in this statement. The Dutch *diagram* is the word used for charts such as the bar chart (*staafdiagram*). It names no method for predicting and does not say how far ahead. It stands under understanding why: the child says on what the prediction rests. Recognising a trend in data is a separate statement in part A under *functioneel gebruiken*.

### Common mistakes

A child states the prediction as a certainty. A child uses only the last two values and ignores the rest of the graph. A child extends a pattern without limit, for instance a plant that keeps growing 2 cm every week for years.
