## Official wording

optellen en aftrekken van veel voorkomende gelijknamige en ongelijknamige breuken binnen een betekenisvolle situatie:
⅛ + ⅛ ; ½ + ¾

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding and subtracting frequently occurring fractions with the same denominator (*gelijknamig*) and with different denominators (*ongelijknamig*) within a meaningful situation:
⅛ + ⅛ ; ½ + ¾

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 puts two pizza slices of ⅛ together and says that this is 2/8, a quarter of the pizza. Pouring ½ litre and ¾ litre into one jug, the child says "half is two quarters, and three quarters more makes five quarters, so 1¼ litres".

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The adding and subtracting is tied to a meaningful situation and to fractions that occur often; the statement does not list them. The two examples are both additions, one with the same denominator and one with different denominators; they are examples of the level, not a limit. No subtraction is printed as an example. The fractions ⅛, ½ and ¾ are kept as the decree prints them.

### Common mistakes

A child adds numerators and denominators: ½ + ¾ becomes 4/6, and ⅛ + ⅛ becomes 2/16. A child does not see that ½ is the same as 2/4 and so cannot start on ½ + ¾. A child does not check the result against the situation: 4/6 litre is less than the ¾ litre that was poured in.
