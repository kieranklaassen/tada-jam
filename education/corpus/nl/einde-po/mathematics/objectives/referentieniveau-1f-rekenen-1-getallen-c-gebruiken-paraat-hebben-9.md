## Official wording

getallen met maximaal drie cijfers delen door een getal met maximaal 2 cijfers, al dan niet met een rest:
132 : 16 =

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 9.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dividing (*delen*, which also means sharing) numbers with at most three digits by a number with at most 2 digits, with or without a remainder:
132 : 16 =

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out 132 ÷ 16 by taking away groups of 16: 5 × 16 = 80 leaves 52, and 3 × 16 = 48 leaves 4. The child writes 8 remainder 4, and checks with 8 × 16 + 4 = 132.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The stated range is a number of at most three digits divided by a number of at most two digits, with or without a remainder. The statement does not say how the remainder is written (as a remainder, a fraction or a decimal number) and names no way of working. The source prints "drie" as a word and "2" as a digit. The example, printed with an open equals sign, is an example of the level, not a limit. Dutch writes division with a colon (132 : 16); the gloss keeps it.

### Common mistakes

A child stops while the remainder is still larger than the divisor and writes 7 remainder 20. A child writes the remainder behind a decimal sign, 8.4, where 132 ÷ 16 is 8.25. A child leaves out a zero in the result, so 612 ÷ 6 becomes 12 instead of 102.
