## Official wording

rekenen met eenvoudige schaal

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Functioneel gebruiken / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating with a simple scale (*schaal*, as on a map or scale drawing)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 measures 6 cm between two villages on a map with scale 1 : 50,000 and works out 6 × 50,000 = 300,000 cm, which is 3 km. For a floor plan with scale 1 : 100 the child draws a wall of 7.5 metres as a line of 7.5 cm.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). "Simple" is not defined: the statement names no scales, no units and no number range. It does not say in which direction the calculating goes (from the drawing to the real size or back). The formal notation 1 : 100 is a separate statement of this level (sub-heading A, *paraat hebben*).

### Common mistakes

A child multiplies 6 by 50,000 and calls the result 300,000 metres or 300 kilometres without converting the centimetres. A child multiplies where dividing is needed when going from the real size to the map. A child loses or adds a zero when converting centimetres to kilometres.
