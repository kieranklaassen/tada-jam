## Official wording

De leerling gebruikt wiskundetaal en wiskundig gereedschap.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 16.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil uses mathematical language (*wiskundetaal*) and mathematical tools (*wiskundig gereedschap*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school writes a calculation with the right signs and layout, says it in the words of mathematics ("the product", "a quarter of", "the perimeter"), and shows it in a table or a drawing. The same child takes a ruler, kitchen scales or a calculator, estimates first, uses it, and says the result with its unit.

### Limits

This is the sentence of core goal 16 itself. Two goal sentences say what it covers, each with its own items: 16 A (mathematical language and mathematical representations) and 16 B (measuring instruments and other mathematical instruments). Core goal 16 is one of the two core goals of the domain *wiskundige denk-werkwijzen* (mathematical ways of thinking and working); the other is core goal 15. This sentence says *gereedschap* (tools) and 16 B says *instrumenten* (instruments); the law does not say whether the two differ. The sentence names no symbol, no kind of representation and no instrument. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child uses an everyday word where the mathematical term is more exact: "the outside" for the perimeter, or "bigger" for both longer and heavier. A child reads a number from an instrument and gives it without a unit.
