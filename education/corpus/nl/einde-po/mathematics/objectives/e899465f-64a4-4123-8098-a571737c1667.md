## Official wording

meten met passende meetinstrumenten;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 11 A b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

measuring with suitable measuring instruments;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes a ruler for a pencil, a tape measure for a long jump, a measuring jug for water, kitchen scales for flour, a thermometer for the temperature outside and a stopwatch for a run. The child sets the instrument up properly (the zero at the start, the jug on a flat table, the eye level with the mark), reads the scale, also at the marks between the numbers, and says the result with its unit.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 11 A, which says that the pupil measures, reasons and calculates with quantities and the units that go with them; 11 A sits under core goal 11. The item names no instruments: choosing one that suits is part of what it asks. The quantities are listed in item 11 A a. The item states no precision and no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child starts at the edge of the ruler or at the 1 instead of at the 0. A child reads the marks without a number wrongly and counts each as 1 where it stands for 2, 5 or 50. A child picks an instrument that is too short, moves it along several times and loses count or leaves gaps.
