## Official wording

delingen uit de tafels (tot en met 10) uitrekenen:
45 : 5
32 : 8

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

working out divisions from the tables (up to and including 10):
45 : 5
32 : 8

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out 45 ÷ 5 = 9 and 32 ÷ 8 = 4 by thinking of the table: "how many times 8 is 32?" The child gives the answer within a few seconds and names the product that goes with it, 4 × 8 = 32.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The verb is working out (*uitrekenen*); the statement on products, of the same row and kind, says knowing by heart. The brackets say "up to and including 10" and do not spell out what the 10 bounds. The two divisions are examples of the level, not a limit. Dutch writes division with a colon (45 : 5); the gloss keeps it.

### Common mistakes

A child does not link the division to the table and counts up in eights on the fingers. A child gives the answer of a neighbouring product, for example 8 for 54 ÷ 6 (6 × 8 is 48). A child reads the division the wrong way round, as 8 ÷ 32.
