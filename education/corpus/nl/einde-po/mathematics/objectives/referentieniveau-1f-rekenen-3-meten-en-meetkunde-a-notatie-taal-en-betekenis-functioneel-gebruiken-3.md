## Official wording

aantal standaard referentiematen gebruiken («een grote stap is ongeveer een meter», in een standaard melkpak zit 1 liter)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Functioneel gebruiken / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using a number of standard reference measures ("a big step is about a metre", a standard milk carton holds 1 litre)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 paces the length of the classroom in big steps, counts 9 steps and says the room is about 9 metres long. The child looks at a bucket and judges how many milk cartons of 1 litre would fill it, and gives the answer as about that many litres.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The brackets name two reference measures: a big step for about a metre, and a standard milk carton for 1 litre. They are examples of the level, not a limit: the statement speaks of "a number of" such measures and does not list the rest, and it names no others. The source prints quotation marks around the first example only. The statement names no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child takes ordinary small steps and still counts each as a metre, so the estimate is too high. A child uses a reference measure for the wrong quantity, for example gives what a bottle holds in metres. A child treats the estimate as exact and leaves out "about".
