## Official wording

uit het hoofd splitsen, optellen en aftrekken onder 100, ook met eenvoudige decimale getallen:
12 = 7 + 5
67 – 30
1 – 0,25
0,8 + 0,7

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

splitting, adding and subtracting in the head under 100, also with simple decimal numbers:
12 = 7 + 5
67 – 30
1 – 0.25
0.8 + 0.7

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once that 12 is 7 and 5, that 67 − 30 is 37, that 1 − 0.25 is 0.75 and that 0.8 + 0.7 is 1.5. The child does the same with 45 + 38 = 83 and splits 60 into 25 and 35, without writing anything down.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. "Onder 100" (under 100) is the stated range; the text does not say whether it bounds the numbers in the sum, the result, or both. The four examples are examples of the level, not a limit; the record holds each on its own line. "Simple" decimal numbers are not defined: the examples have one or two decimal places. The decree prints the Dutch decimal comma (0,25); the gloss writes the English decimal point.

### Common mistakes

A child adds the decimals as whole numbers and writes 0.8 + 0.7 = 0.15. A child writes 1 − 0.25 = 0.85. In 52 − 38 a child takes the smaller digit from the larger in both places and says 26.
