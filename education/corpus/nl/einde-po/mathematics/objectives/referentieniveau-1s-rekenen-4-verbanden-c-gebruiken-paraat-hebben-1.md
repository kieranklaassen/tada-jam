## Official wording

berekeningen uitvoeren op basis van informatie uit tabellen, grafieken en diagrammen

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

carrying out calculations on the basis of information from tables, graphs and diagrams

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads in a table that a museum had 120 visitors on Monday, 95 on Tuesday and 140 on Wednesday. The child adds them to 355 visitors and says that Wednesday had 45 more than Tuesday. From a bar chart the child reads two heights and works out the difference in the same way.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for calculations with information taken from three named sources: tables, graphs and diagrams. The Dutch *diagram* is the word used for charts such as the bar chart (*staafdiagram*). It names no kind of calculation and sets no number range. It stands under knowing by heart, having ready: a skill the pupil carries out readily. Drawing conclusions about a situation on the basis of a graph or diagram is a separate statement in this part under *weten waarom*.

### Common mistakes

A child reads a value from the wrong row or column, or reads a bar or point against the wrong mark on the axis. A child overlooks a note with the numbers, such as "× 1000" in a heading. A child reads the right values and then chooses the wrong calculation, adding where a difference is asked.
