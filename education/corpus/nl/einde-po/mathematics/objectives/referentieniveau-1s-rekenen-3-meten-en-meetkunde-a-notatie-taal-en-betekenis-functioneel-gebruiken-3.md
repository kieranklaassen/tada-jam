## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising everyday language ("a *kuub* of sand"; *kuub* is the everyday word for a cubic metre)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads on a delivery note or hears at a builder's yard "a *kuub* of sand" and says that this is 1 m³ of sand. The child shows with metre sticks about how large a block of 1 m by 1 m by 1 m is, and writes "2 *kuub*" as 2 m³.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement is about recognising an everyday expression for a measure when it is met in a practical situation. It gives one example between quotation marks (the source prints « »): *kuub*, the everyday Dutch word for a cubic metre. The example is an example of the level, not a limit; the statement names no other everyday expression. It asks for recognising, not for calculating.

### Common mistakes

A child does not link *kuub* to m³ and takes it for a measure of weight, because sand is heavy. A child takes *kuub* for a cube (*kubus*) of any size. A child pictures 1 m³ as much smaller than it is.
