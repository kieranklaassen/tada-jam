## Official wording

laten zien hoe verschillende leergebieden wiskundetaal en wiskundige representaties gebruiken;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 18 B b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

showing how different learning areas (*leergebieden*) use mathematical language (*wiskundetaal*) and mathematical representations;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 meets a climate graph in geography, a timeline in history and a table of measurements in a science lesson, and says what each has in common with the graphs, number lines and tables of the mathematics lesson. The child notices that a word such as "average" or "scale" means the same in both lessons, and reads the axis of the climate graph in the way an axis is read in mathematics.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 18 B, which says that the school supports the use of mathematics in different learning areas; 18 B sits under core goal 18. Like 18 B, it says what the school does and is not a requirement on the pupil. The verb is showing. It names two things that the learning areas use: mathematical language and mathematical representations; goal sentence 16 A, under core goal 16, is about the pupil using these two. It names no learning area and no representation. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

This item is about what the school does, so it has no typical error of a child. Children most often treat a graph or a table in another subject as a picture that belongs to that subject and do not read it with what they know from mathematics: a child reads a timeline as a row of pictures and ignores the distances between the years.
