## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 picks the instrument that fits the task and uses it: draws a line of 7.5 cm with a ruler, measures the width of a door with a tape measure, weighs 300 g of sugar on kitchen scales, and measures 250 ml of milk in a measuring jug.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names one instrument, the ruler; the "other common measuring instruments" are not listed, and it names no others. A statement in part A, under *functioneel gebruiken*, names measuring jug, scales and thermometer beside the ruler, as that statement's own. This statement names no units, no precision and no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child lays the end of the ruler, not its 0 mark, at the start of what is measured. A child does not hold the instrument straight or level: the tape measure sags, or the jug is read while it is tilted. When drawing a line of a given length, a child counts the marks instead of the spaces between them and draws the line too short.
