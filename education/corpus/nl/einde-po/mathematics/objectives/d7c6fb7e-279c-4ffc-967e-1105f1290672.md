## Official wording

De leerling maakt en gebruikt wiskundige modellen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 15 B.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil makes and uses mathematical models.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 turns a situation into a drawing, a table or a sum that keeps only what matters for the question. For "3 kilograms of apples cost 7.50 euros, what do 5 kilograms cost" the child draws a ratio table with a row for kilograms and a row for euros, fills in 2.50 euros for 1 kilogram and reads 12.50 euros for 5 kilograms. The child uses the same table for a new question about 8 kilograms.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 15, which is about using mathematical ways of thinking and working. Its items 15 B a to 15 B d name representing a situation schematically, representing it in mathematical language, selecting the relevant features, and using abstract models. The sentence names both making and using. The law does not define "mathematical model". Item 15 B d names abstract models; nothing in core goal 15 names a particular model, and the ratio table above is the pack's example. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child draws a picture of the story (the apples, the shop) and no model of the quantities. A child fills in a ratio table by adding the same number in both rows: from 3 to 5 kilograms is 2 more, so 7.50 euros becomes 9.50 euros. A child can use a model that is given and cannot set one up.
