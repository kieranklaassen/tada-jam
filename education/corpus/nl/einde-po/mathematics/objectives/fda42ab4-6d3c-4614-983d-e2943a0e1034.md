## Official wording

De leerling gebruikt wiskundige denk-werkwijzen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 15.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil uses mathematical ways of thinking and working (*denk-werkwijzen*, one compound word in Dutch).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school meets a question for which no method has been given, such as how many tables the school needs for a party of 130 people when 6 people sit at one table. The child draws the situation, writes 130 ÷ 6 = 21 remainder 4, and says that 22 tables are needed. The child writes the steps down so that a classmate can follow them, and reads the question again to see whether the answer fits it.

### Limits

This is the sentence of core goal 15 itself. Three goal sentences say what it covers, each with its own items: 15 A (solving mathematical problems and application problems), 15 B (making and using mathematical models) and 15 C (devising and describing algorithms). Core goal 15 is one of the two core goals of the domain *wiskundige denk-werkwijzen* (mathematical ways of thinking and working); the other is core goal 16. The sentence names no kind of problem and no mathematical content. This goal states no number range, and the sentence sets no level of difficulty.

### Common mistakes

A child takes all the numbers in a problem and carries out the operation practised most recently, without asking what the situation is. A child stops at the result of the calculation (21 remainder 4) and does not turn it into an answer to the question.
