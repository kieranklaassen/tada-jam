## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at a row of dot patterns: 1 dot, then a square of 2 by 2 dots, then 3 by 3, then 4 by 4. The child says the numbers of dots, 1, 4, 9 and 16, lays the next pattern with counters as 5 by 5, and says that it has 25 dots. The child sees the number in the arrangement, as rows of equal length, without counting the dots one by one.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement is one word. It names no particular dot pattern and has no verb. The decree prints it as a statement of its own directly after the statement on recognising and continuing simple patterns in rows of numbers and figures, in the same part and under the same kind; this statement itself does not say what the pupil does with dot patterns. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child counts the dots one by one and miscounts, and does not use the rows and columns. A child continues 1, 4, 9 with 14 by repeating the last step of 5. A child sees the numbers grow and does not link them to the shape of the pattern.
