## Official wording

uit het hoofd optellen, aftrekken, vermenigvuldigen en delen met «nullen»; ook met complexere getallen en decimale getallen:
18 : 100
1,8 × 1000

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing (*delen*, which also means sharing) mentally with "zeros"; also with more complex numbers and decimal numbers:
18 : 100
1.8 × 1000

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 gives these answers mentally: 18 ÷ 100 = 0.18 and 1.8 × 1000 = 1800, and also 12,000 − 500 = 11,500, 30 × 600 = 18,000, 2400 ÷ 60 = 40 and 0.3 × 200 = 60. The child reasons with place value (when dividing by 100 every digit moves two places to the right) and does not rely on striking off zeros.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The source sets "zeros" in quotation marks; the pack reads it as calculations with round numbers such as tens, hundreds and thousands. The two calculations 18 : 100 and 1.8 × 1000 are examples of the level, not limits. "More complex numbers" is not defined and the statement sets no number range. The colon is the division sign of Dutch arithmetic (18 : 100 means 18 divided by 100). The record holds each example on its own line. The source prints a decimal comma (1,8); the gloss uses English notation.

### Common mistakes

A child carries "add zeros" or "strike off zeros" over to decimals and writes 1.8 × 1000 = 1.8000. A child miscounts the zeros and answers 30 × 600 = 1800. A child moves the digits the wrong way and gives 18 ÷ 100 = 1800.
