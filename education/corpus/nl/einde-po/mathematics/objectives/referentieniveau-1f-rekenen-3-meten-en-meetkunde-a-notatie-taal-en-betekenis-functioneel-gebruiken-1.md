## Official wording

meetinstrumenten aflezen en uitkomst noteren; liniaal, maatbeker, weegschaal, thermometer etc.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Functioneel gebruiken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading measuring instruments and noting the result; ruler, measuring jug, scales, thermometer etc.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays a ruler along a pencil and writes down 14.5 cm. The child pours water into a measuring jug, reads the level at 350 ml and writes it down, reads 1.2 kg on kitchen scales with a bag of apples on them, and reads 18 °C on a thermometer by the window. Each time the child writes the number with its unit.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names four instruments: ruler, measuring jug, scales and thermometer. The word "etc." leaves the list open, and the statement names no others. It names no units, no number range and no precision of reading, so it does not say whether a reading between two marks is expected.

### Common mistakes

A child starts measuring from the end of the ruler or from the 1 instead of from the 0. A child counts every mark on a scale as 1 without checking what one step is worth, for example on a jug marked in steps of 50 ml. A child writes the number down without the unit.
