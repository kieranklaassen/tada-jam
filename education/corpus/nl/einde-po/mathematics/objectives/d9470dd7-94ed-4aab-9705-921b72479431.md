## Official wording

gebruiken van abstracte modellen om rekenaanpakken te laten zien, situaties te interpreteren en problemen op te lossen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 15 B d.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using abstract models to show approaches to calculating (*rekenaanpakken*), to interpret situations and to solve problems.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 draws an empty number line and shows 1000 − 387 as a jump of 13 from 387 to 400 and a jump of 600 from 400 to 1000, together 613. The child draws a rectangle of 12 by 15, splits it into 10 by 15 and 2 by 15, and shows why 12 × 15 is 150 + 30 = 180. For "60% of the 250 pupils come by bicycle" the child draws a strip for 250, marks 10% as 25 and reads 150 for 60%.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 15 B, which says that the pupil makes and uses mathematical models; 15 B sits under core goal 15. It names three uses of abstract models: showing approaches to calculating, interpreting situations and solving problems. It names no model: the number line, the rectangle and the strip are not in the text. The item does not say what makes a model abstract. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child copies the model as a drawing that has to be made and cannot say what the jumps or the parts stand for. On a number line a child counts the marks and not the distances between them, and miscounts by one. A child can use a model only in the kind of sum in which it was learned.
