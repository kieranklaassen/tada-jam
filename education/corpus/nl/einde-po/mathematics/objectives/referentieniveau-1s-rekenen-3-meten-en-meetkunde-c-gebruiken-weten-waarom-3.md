## Official wording

verschillende omtrek mogelijk bij gelijkblijvende oppervlakte

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Weten waarom / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

different perimeter (*omtrek*) possible with the area (*oppervlakte*) staying the same

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes 12 square tiles and lays a rectangle of 3 by 4, then one of 2 by 6, then one of 1 by 12. The child counts the lengths of the sides around each: 14, 16 and 26. The child says that the area stayed 12 tiles each time while the perimeter changed, and explains why the long thin rectangle has the longest edge.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The fragment states one insight: figures with the same area can have different perimeters. It names no figures and no numbers. It does not state the reverse case, the same perimeter with a different area. It has no verb; under this heading, understanding and explaining the insight is presumably meant.

### Common mistakes

A child believes that figures with the same area must have the same perimeter. A child believes that a longer perimeter always means a larger area. A child counts the tiles along the edge and not the lengths of the sides, and so counts a corner tile once where it has two outer sides.
