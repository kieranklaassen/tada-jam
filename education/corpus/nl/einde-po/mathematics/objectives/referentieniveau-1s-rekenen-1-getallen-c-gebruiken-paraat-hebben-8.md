## Official wording

omzetten van breuken in decimale getallen (eventueel met de rekenmachine)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 8.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

converting fractions into decimal numbers (possibly with the calculator)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 gives the common ones at once: ½ = 0.5, ¼ = 0.25, ¾ = 0.75, ⅕ = 0.2, 1/10 = 0.1 and ⅛ = 0.125. For a fraction that is not known by heart, such as 7/16, the child divides the numerator by the denominator, on a calculator if needed, and reads 0.4375.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement names no fractions and no number of decimals, and it sets no number range. The calculator is optional: the Dutch *eventueel* means "possibly" or "if need be", so the statement neither requires nor excludes it. The direction named is from fractions to decimal numbers; the reverse is not named in this statement. Statements of domain Verhoudingen at this level (sub-heading B) also deal with fractions as decimal numbers.

### Common mistakes

A child builds the decimal from the digits of the fraction: ⅕ becomes 0.5 or 1.5, and ⅛ becomes 0.8. A child keys the division the wrong way round, 16 ÷ 7 for 7/16. A child copies the digits on the calculator display for ⅓ and treats 0.3333333 as exact.
