## Official wording

interpreteren van een uitkomst «met rest» bij gebruik van een rekenmachine

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Weten waarom / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

interpreting a result "with a remainder" when using a calculator

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 keys in 50 ÷ 16, reads 3.125 and explains that the digits after the decimal sign are not the remainder: 3 times 16 is 48, so the remainder is 2, and 2 of 16 is 0.125. For 30 ÷ 4 the child reads 7.5 and explains what it means: €7.50 each when 30 euros are shared by 4, and 7 groups with 2 children over when 30 children form groups of 4.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is about a division that leaves a remainder and is keyed into a calculator, which shows decimals and no remainder. Under this kind it asks for understanding how the two belong together. A statement of this row under *functioneel gebruiken* is about the remainder in contexts. This statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the decimals as the remainder: 3.125 becomes "3 remainder 125" and 7.5 becomes "7 remainder 5". A child rounds what the display shows up or down without looking at the situation. A child cannot get from 3.125 back to the remainder of 2.
