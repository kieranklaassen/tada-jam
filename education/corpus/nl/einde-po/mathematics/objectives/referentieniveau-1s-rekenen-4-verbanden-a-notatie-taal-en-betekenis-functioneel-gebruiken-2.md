bar chart (*staafdiagram*), pie chart (*cirkeldiagram*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a bar chart of how the children of a class travel to school: the child reads the height of each bar off the axis and says which way of travelling is the most common. In a pie chart of the same class the child says that the sector covering half the circle stands for half the class. In a class of 28, the child says that a sector of a quarter of the circle stands for 7 children.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names two kinds of diagram, the bar chart and the pie chart, and no others. The fragment has no verb; under this heading, reading and using these two in practical situations is presumably meant. Drawing up simple tables and diagrams on the basis of a description in words is a separate statement in part B under *paraat hebben*.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the height of a bar wrongly when one step on the axis stands for more than 1. A child reads a sector of a pie chart as a number of things and not as a share of the whole. A child compares sectors of two pie charts that stand for different totals as if equal sectors meant equal numbers.
