## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

estimating measurement results and outcomes beforehand;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before measuring, a child of 11 or 12 says "the table is about 1 metre 20 long" and "this bag weighs about 2 kilograms", writes the estimate down, and then measures. Before keying 398 × 21 into a calculator, the child says "about 400 × 20, so about 8000", and then compares the display, 8358, with it.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 16 B, which says that the pupil uses measuring instruments and other mathematical instruments; 16 B sits under core goal 16. It covers two things, measurement results and outcomes, and places the estimate before the measuring or calculating. It does not say how close an estimate has to be. Estimating is also named in item 11 A d, under core goal 11, and in item 10 A d, under core goal 10. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child measures or calculates first and writes down an "estimate" afterwards that matches. A child thinks an estimate has to be exact and counts a near estimate as wrong. A child has no reference for a unit that is seldom handled, such as a millilitre or a tonne, and gives an estimate that is far too large or too small.
