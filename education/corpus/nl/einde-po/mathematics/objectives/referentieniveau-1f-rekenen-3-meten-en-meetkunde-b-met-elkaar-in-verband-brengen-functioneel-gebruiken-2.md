## Official wording

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

time (months, weeks, days in a year, hours, minutes, seconds)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out from a calendar that a holiday of 6 weeks is 42 days, and that a training session of 90 minutes lasts 1 hour and 30 minutes. The child says how many months are in a year and about how many weeks, and counts on from a start time of 14:45 and a film of 100 minutes to an end time of 16:25.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The brackets name months, weeks, days in a year, hours, minutes and seconds. The fragment has no verb; it stands in the part on relating things to each other, after the statement on the relationship between measures, so the relations between these units of time are presumably meant. The words "in a year" can be read with days only or with months, weeks and days together; the statement leaves this open. It prints none of the numbers themselves (such as 12, 365 or 60) and does not mention leap years or the lengths of the separate months.

### Common mistakes

A child calculates with time as if an hour had 100 minutes and writes 1 hour and 30 minutes as 1.3 hours. A child takes every month as 4 weeks and so counts 48 weeks in a year. A child goes wrong when adding minutes past the full hour and makes 14:45 plus 30 minutes into 14:75.
