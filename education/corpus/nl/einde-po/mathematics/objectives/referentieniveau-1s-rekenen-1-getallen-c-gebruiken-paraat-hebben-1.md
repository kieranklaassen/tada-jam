## Official wording

standaardprocedures gebruiken ook met getallen boven de 1000 en met complexere decimale getallen in complexere situaties

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using standard procedures also with numbers above 1000 and with more complex decimal numbers in more complex situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 sets out a written calculation with large numbers or decimals and carries it through without hesitation: 3476 + 12,859 = 16,335, 4005 − 1738 = 2267, 24 × 365 = 8760 and 12.75 × 8 = 102. The child keeps the digits in their places and the decimal points under one another. For a trip with stages of 1284.5 km and 937.75 km the child adds them to 2222.25 km.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement gives one bound: numbers above 1000, with no upper limit. "More complex decimal numbers" and "more complex situations" are comparisons that the statement does not define; it names no number of decimals. The word "also" (*ook*) shows that these are in addition to something the statement does not name. The statement does not say which procedures or which operations are meant.

### Common mistakes

A child loses a carried or exchanged digit, most often across zeros, as in 4005 − 1738. A child lines up decimal numbers by their last digit, so that in 12.5 + 3.75 the 5 stands under the 5. In long multiplication a child forgets the zero that belongs to the row for the tens.
