scale (*schaal*, as on a map or scale drawing)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 finds the note 1 : 50,000 on a walking map and says what it means: 1 centimetre on the map is 50,000 centimetres outside, which is 500 metres. On a floor plan with scale 1 : 100 the child reads that a room drawn 4 cm wide is 4 metres wide.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading A (notation, language and meaning). The statement is a single word without a verb: read with its sub-heading and kind, it concerns using the notation, the word and the meaning of scale in practical situations. It names no scales and no kind of drawing, map or model, and it sets no number range. The formal notation 1 : 100 (sub-heading A, *paraat hebben*) and calculating with a simple scale (sub-heading C, *functioneel gebruiken*) are separate statements of this level.

### Common mistakes

A child keeps the wrong unit and reads 1 : 50,000 as 1 centimetre for 50,000 metres. A child thinks a larger number after the colon means a larger, more detailed drawing. A child multiplies 4 cm by 100, gets 400 and calls it 400 metres.
