## Official wording

verschil tussen cijfer en getal

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Weten waarom / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

difference between digit (*cijfer*) and number (*getal*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 explains that there are ten digits, 0 to 9, and that every number is written with one or more of them. The child points out that the number 474 has three digits, that its two 4s are the same digit with different values (400 and 4), and that 7 can be a digit in a number and also a number itself.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading A (notation, language and meaning). The statement is a fragment without a verb: read with its kind, it concerns understanding why a digit and a number are not the same thing. It gives no example and sets no number range. In everyday Dutch *cijfer* is also used loosely for a number and for a school mark; set beside *getal* (number) here, it means digit.

### Common mistakes

A child calls every number a digit, as everyday speech does ("the digit 25"). Asked how many digits 3050 has, a child counts only the 3 and the 5 and answers two. A child gives a digit its face value whatever its place and says the 4 in 474 is worth 4 both times.
