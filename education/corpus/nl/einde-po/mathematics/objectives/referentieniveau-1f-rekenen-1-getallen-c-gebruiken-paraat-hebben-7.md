5 uur werken voor € 5,75 per uur

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 7.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

multiplying a number with one digit by a number with two or three digits:
7 × 165 =
5 hours of work for €5.75 per hour

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out 7 × 165 as 700 + 420 + 35 = 1155, in the head with the part results written down, or with a written procedure. For 5 hours of work at €5.75 per hour the child takes 5 × 5 = 25 and 5 × 0.75 = 3.75 and says €28.75.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The stated range is a number of one digit times a number of two or three digits. The statement names no way of working (in the head or on paper). The decree prints two examples, a bare product with an open equals sign and a situation about pay; the record holds each on its own line. In the second example the number of three digits is an amount of money with two decimal places. They are examples of the level, not a limit. The decree prints the Dutch decimal comma (€ 5,75); the gloss writes the English decimal point.

### Common mistakes

A child forgets the value of a place and adds 7 × 6 = 42 where it is 7 × 60 = 420. A child forgets a digit that was carried. With the amount of money a child multiplies the euros and the cents apart from each other and writes 5 × 5.75 = 25.375.
