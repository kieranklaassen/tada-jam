## Official wording

informatie op veel verschillende manieren kan worden geordend en weergegeven

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1F, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Weten waarom / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

that information can be ordered and represented in many different ways

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes the results of a class survey on favourite fruit and puts them first in a tally list, then in a table, then in a bar chart. The child explains that all three hold the same information, and says what each one shows well: the table gives the exact numbers, and the bar chart shows at once which fruit was chosen most.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is a single insight and names no way of ordering or representing: table, graph and diagram are not printed in it. The source prints a clause in the word order that follows *dat* (that) without printing that word; knowing that information can be ordered and represented in many different ways is plainly meant, and the gloss supplies "that". The statement names no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks that a different form means different information and does not see that a table and a bar chart of the same survey say the same thing. A child believes there is one right form for each set of data. A child chooses a form by habit and cannot say what it shows well or badly.
