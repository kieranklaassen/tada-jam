## Official wording

veelvoorkomende omzettingen van percentages in breuken en omgekeerd

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Paraat hebben / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

common conversions of percentages into fractions and the other way round

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once: 50% is ½, 25% is ¼, 75% is ¾, 10% is 1/10, 20% is ⅕ and 1% is 1/100, and gives the percentage as quickly when the fraction is named. The child uses these without calculating: 25% of 80 is a quarter of 80, which is 20.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading B (relating to one another). The statement says "common" (*veelvoorkomende*, occurring often) and does not list which conversions are meant; the ones above are the pack's choice. Both directions are named. The statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child uses the denominator as the percentage: ⅕ becomes 5% and ¼ becomes 4%. A child mixes up 20% and 25% and gives 20% as ¼. A child gives ⅓ as 30%, where it is about 33%.
