## Official wording

De leerlingen leren structuur en samenhang van aantallen, gehele getallen, kommagetallen, breuken, procenten en verhoudingen op hoofdlijnen te doorzien en er in praktische situaties mee te rekenen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 26.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to see in broad outline the structure and coherence of amounts (*aantallen*, how many things there are), whole numbers, decimal numbers (*kommagetallen*, literally "comma numbers"), fractions, percentages and ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion), and to calculate with them in practical situations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school puts the cards ¼, 0.25, 25% and "1 in 4" together because they are the same amount. The child splits 3400 into 3 thousands and 4 hundreds, and places 0.5, ¾ and 1.2 on a number line. In a shop the child works out 25% off a jumper of 40 euros: 10 euros off, so 30 euros.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is the first of six goals under the heading *Getallen en bewerkingen* (numbers and operations). It names six kinds of number. The words "op hoofdlijnen" (in broad outline) bound the insight that is asked, and the calculating is tied to practical situations. This goal states no number range. The reference levels 1F and 1S, which are other records of this lane (domains *Getallen*, numbers, and *Verhoudingen*, ratios), are worded in more detail.

### Common mistakes

Many children treat decimal numbers, fractions and percentages as separate subjects and do not see that 0.5, ½ and 50% are the same amount. A child reads the decimal number with more digits as the larger one, so 0.125 is called larger than 0.5. In a ratio situation a child adds the same number to both amounts where both have to be multiplied.
