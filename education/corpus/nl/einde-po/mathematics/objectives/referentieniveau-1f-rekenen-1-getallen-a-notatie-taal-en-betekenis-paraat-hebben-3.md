## Official wording

0,45 is vijfenveertig honderdsten

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Paraat hebben / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

0,45 is forty-five hundredths

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads 0,45 aloud as "forty-five hundredths" and, on hearing those words, writes 0,45. The child also says it as 4 tenths and 5 hundredths, colours 45 of the 100 squares of a hundred-square, and reads 0,45 m on a tape measure as 45 cm.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The gloss and these notes keep the Dutch decimal comma, because the statement is about notation; English writes 0.45. The statement is a bare example of the level, not a limit: one number with two decimal places. It does not say how many decimal places a pupil has to be able to name. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child calls 0,45 greater than 0,5 because 45 is greater than 5. A child reads 0,45 as "forty-five tenths", or writes forty-five hundredths as 0,045. A child reads the comma as a separator between two whole numbers, "0 and 45".
