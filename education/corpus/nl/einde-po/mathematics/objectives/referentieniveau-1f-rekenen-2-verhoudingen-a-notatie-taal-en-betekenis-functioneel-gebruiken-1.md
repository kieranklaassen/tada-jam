## Official wording

notatie van breuken (horizontale breukstreep), decimale getallen (kommagetal) en procenten (%) herkennen

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Functioneel gebruiken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising the notation of fractions (horizontal fraction bar), decimal numbers (*kommagetal*, a number written with a decimal comma) and percentages (%)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks through a shop leaflet and the labels on packages and points out "25% off", "0.75 l" and "½ price". The child says which is a fraction, which a decimal number and which a percentage, and reads each aloud.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verhoudingen* (ratios), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The verb is recognising. Each notation is named in brackets: the horizontal bar, the decimal comma and the per cent sign. Converting fractions and ratios into percentages is named in statements of row B, not here. This statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the comma in a Dutch decimal number as a separator between two whole numbers, so 1,5 is "1 and 5". A child takes 25% for the number 25. A child does not see ½ written with a slash (1/2) as the same fraction.
