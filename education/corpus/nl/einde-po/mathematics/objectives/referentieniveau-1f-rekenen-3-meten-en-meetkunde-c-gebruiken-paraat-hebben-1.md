## Official wording

schattingen maken over afmetingen en hoeveelheden

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making estimates about dimensions and quantities

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at a door and says it is about 2 m high, looks at a bucket and says it holds about 10 litres, and looks at a jar and says there are about 50 marbles in it. The child gives the estimate straight away, with a unit where one belongs, and then checks it by measuring or counting.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names dimensions and quantities in general. It names no units, no objects, no number range and no margin within which an estimate counts as good. Reference measures are not named in this statement; they are in statements of part A.

### Common mistakes

A child gives a number without a unit, or with a unit that does not fit, such as the height of a door in kilometres. A child gives an estimate that is not possible, such as a door of 10 m, because the child has no known size to compare with. A child measures or counts exactly when an estimate is asked, and calls an estimate wrong when it is not exact.
