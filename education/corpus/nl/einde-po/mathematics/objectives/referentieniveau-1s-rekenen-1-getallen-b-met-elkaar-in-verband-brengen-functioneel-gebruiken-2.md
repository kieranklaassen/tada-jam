## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

rounding a decimal number to a whole number

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads 3.7 kilometres in a route description and says "about 4 kilometres", and rounds a bill of 12.35 euros to 12 euros for an estimate. The child looks at the first digit after the decimal point: 7.49 becomes 7 and 7.5 becomes 8.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading B (relating to one another). The statement bounds the result: a whole number. It does not say how many decimals the starting number has, it names no rounding rule and it sets no number range. Rounding to tenths or hundredths is not mentioned. Rounding within a given situation is a separate statement of this level under the same sub-heading and kind.

### Common mistakes

A child rounds in two steps: 7.46 first to 7.5 and then to 8, where 7.46 rounds to 7. A child cuts off the decimals, so 7.8 becomes 7. A child looks at the last digit and not at the first digit after the point, and rounds 12.38 to 13.
