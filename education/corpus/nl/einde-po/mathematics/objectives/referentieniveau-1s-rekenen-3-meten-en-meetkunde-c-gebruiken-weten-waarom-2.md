## Official wording

beredeneren welke vergrotingsfactor nodig is om de ene (eenvoudige) figuur uit de andere te vormen

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Weten waarom / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning out which enlargement factor (*vergrotingsfactor*) is needed to form one (simple) figure from the other

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 compares two rectangles on squared paper, one of 2 by 3 squares and one of 6 by 9, and measures the matching sides: each side of the second is 3 times as long, so the factor is 3. The child checks every pair of sides and not only one. For rectangles of 4 by 6 and 6 by 9 the child divides 6 by 4 and 9 by 6, finds 1.5 both times, and says that the factor is 1.5.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for reasoning out the enlargement factor between two figures. The word "simple" is printed in brackets before "figure"; the statement names no figures. It does not say whether the factor is a whole number, and it says nothing about what enlarging does to the area. It sets no number range. It stands under understanding why: the child gives the reasoning, not only the factor.

### Common mistakes

A child looks for a number to add and not a number to multiply by, and says that 2 by 3 becomes 6 by 9 by adding 4 to the first side and 6 to the second. A child checks one pair of sides only. A child thinks that a factor of 2 makes the area 2 times as large.
