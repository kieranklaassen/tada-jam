## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

simplifying and complicating (*compliceren*: rewriting with a larger numerator and denominator) fractions, and writing fractions as a mixed number:
⁶/₈ = ³/₄
¹/₅ = ²⁰/₁₀₀
²⁵/₄ = 6¹/₄

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 rewrites fractions without long working. The child divides the numerator and denominator of 6/8 by 2 and gets 3/4, multiplies both of 1/5 by 20 and gets 20/100, and writes 25/4 as 6¼ because 24 quarters make 6 wholes and one quarter is left. In the same way the child writes 12/18 as 2/3 and 17/5 as 3⅖.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement prints three examples, one for each action it names: simplifying (⁶/₈ = ³/₄), complicating (¹/₅ = ²⁰/₁₀₀) and writing a fraction as a mixed number (²⁵/₄ = 6¹/₄). They are examples of the level, not limits; the statement names no denominators and sets no number range. Writing a mixed number as a fraction is not named. The record holds each example on its own line.

### Common mistakes

A child changes only the numerator or only the denominator, or adds the same number to both: ¾ becomes 4/5. A child thinks that 12/18 and 6/9 are different amounts because the numbers differ. A child writes 25/4 as "6 remainder 1" or as 6.1 and not as 6¼.
