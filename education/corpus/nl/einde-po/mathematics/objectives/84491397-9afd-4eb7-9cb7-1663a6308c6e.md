## Official wording

relaties leggen tussen verschillende representaties van een wiskundig concept.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 16 A e.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making connections between different representations of a mathematical concept.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays side by side a strip with 3 of its 4 parts coloured, the fraction 3/4, the decimal number 0.75, 75%, and a mark three quarters of the way from 0 to 1 on a number line, and says that they stand for the same amount. The child points to where the 3 and the 4 can be seen in the strip and on the number line. With a taxi fare of 2 euros for each kilometre, the child shows the 2 in the description, in the steps of the table and in the rise of the graph.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 16 A, which says that the pupil uses mathematical language and mathematical representations; 16 A sits under core goal 16. It asks for connections between representations of one concept; it names no concept and no kind of representation. Converting between a description, a table and a graph is in item 13 A c, under core goal 13. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child sees 3/4, 0.75 and 75% as three different numbers. A child can change one form into another by a rule and cannot say where the same thing is visible in both. A child matches representations by the digits that appear in them and pairs 1/4 with 0.4.
