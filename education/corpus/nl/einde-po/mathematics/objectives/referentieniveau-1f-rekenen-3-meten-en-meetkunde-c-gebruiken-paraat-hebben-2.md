## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

approximating area (*oppervlakte*) by means of a grid

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays a transparent sheet with squares of 1 cm by 1 cm over a drawing of a leaf, counts the whole squares inside the outline, and then puts half squares and part squares together into whole ones. The child says the leaf covers about 23 squares, so about 23 cm², and does the same for a pond on a map with a square grid.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names one method, a grid, and one quantity, area. "Approximating" means that the result is about right, not exact. The statement names no shapes, no units, no size of grid square, no rule for part squares and no number range. Calculating the area of rectangular figures is a separate statement under the same part and kind.

### Common mistakes

A child counts only the whole squares and leaves all part squares out, or counts every part square as a whole one. A child counts the squares along the outline instead of the squares inside it, which mixes up perimeter and area. A child counts some squares twice or skips some because the counted ones are not marked.
