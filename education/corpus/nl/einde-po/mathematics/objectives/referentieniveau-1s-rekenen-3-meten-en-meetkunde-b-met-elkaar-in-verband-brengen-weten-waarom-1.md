## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays a metre stick beside a measuring tape and shows that 1 m is 10 dm, 1 dm is 10 cm and 1 cm is 10 mm. The child explains that each step is a step of 10, the same as between the places of a decimal number, so 2.35 m is 2 m, 3 dm and 5 cm. The child uses this to explain why converting to the next unit moves the digits one place.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The fragment names the decimal structure of the metric system and nothing else: no units, no prefixes and no relations. It has no verb; under this heading, understanding and explaining that structure is presumably meant. The decree prints "structure and coherence of the metric system" as a separate statement directly after this one, under the same heading.

### Common mistakes

A child moves the decimal point the wrong way and writes 2.35 m as 0.0235 cm, where it is 235 cm. A child applies the steps of 10 to time and reads 1.5 hours as 1 hour and 50 minutes. A child uses a step of 10 between units of area or of volume.
