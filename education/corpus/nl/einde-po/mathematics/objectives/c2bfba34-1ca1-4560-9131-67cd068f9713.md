## Official wording

De leerlingen leren de basisbewerkingen met gehele getallen in elk geval tot 100 snel uit het hoofd uitvoeren, waarbij optellen en aftrekken tot 20 en de tafels van buiten gekend zijn.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 27.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to carry out the basic operations with whole numbers quickly in the head, in any case up to 100, whereby adding and subtracting up to 20 and the tables (*de tafels*, the multiplication tables) are known by heart.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 answers 8 + 7 = 15, 13 − 6 = 7 and 6 × 7 = 42 without counting or reciting. The child works out 46 + 38 = 84, 72 − 25 = 47, 4 × 18 = 72 and 96 ÷ 8 = 12 in the head within a few seconds, and says how.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is the second of six goals under the heading *Getallen en bewerkingen* (numbers and operations). "In elk geval tot 100" (in any case up to 100) sets a minimum, not an upper limit. The Dutch "tot" leaves open whether 100 and 20 themselves are included, and the text does not say whether the bound is on the numbers in the sum or on the result. Two things are to be known by heart: adding and subtracting up to 20, and the tables; the goal does not say which tables. The basic operations are not listed here; goal 29 names adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing.

### Common mistakes

A child still counts on by ones for sums under 20, for example 8 + 7 on the fingers. A child knows a table only as a row to recite and has to start at 1 × 7 to reach 6 × 7. A child mixes up products that lie close together, such as 7 × 8 = 56 and 6 × 9 = 54.
