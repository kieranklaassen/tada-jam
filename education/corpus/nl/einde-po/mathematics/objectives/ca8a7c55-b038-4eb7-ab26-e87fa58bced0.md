## Official wording

De leerlingen leren wiskundetaal gebruiken.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 23.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to use mathematical language (*wiskundetaal*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school reads "¾ × 20" aloud as "three quarters of twenty" and writes "the difference between 45 and 28" as 45 − 28. The child uses words such as sum, difference, times as many, per, perimeter and area when telling how a problem was solved. The child reads a table or a graph and says in a sentence what it shows.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is the first of three goals under the heading *Wiskundig inzicht en handelen* (mathematical insight and acting). The goal does not say which words, symbols or forms of notation belong to mathematical language. This goal states no number range. The reference levels 1F and 1S, which are other records of this lane, are worded in more detail.

### Common mistakes

A child gives a mathematical word its everyday meaning: "difference" is taken as what is unlike about two numbers, not as the result of subtracting. A child reads the equals sign as "now comes the answer" and writes a chain that is not true, such as 3 + 4 = 7 + 5 = 12. A child uses perimeter and area as if they were the same word.
