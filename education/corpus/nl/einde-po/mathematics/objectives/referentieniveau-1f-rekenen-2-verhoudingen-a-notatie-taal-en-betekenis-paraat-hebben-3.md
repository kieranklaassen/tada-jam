## Official wording

«1 op de 4» is 25% of «een kwart van»

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Paraat hebben / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

"1 in 4" is 25% or "a quarter of"

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 knows without working it out that "1 in 4", 25% and "a quarter of" say the same. On hearing that 1 in 4 children has a pet, the child says "a quarter of the children, 25%", and for a class of 28 says that this is 7 children.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verhoudingen* (ratios), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is one example of the level, not a limit: one ratio said in three ways. Other such sets (for a half, or for a tenth) are not printed here. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child makes a percentage out of the digits and calls 1 in 4 "14%" or "4%". A child mixes up "1 in 4" (a quarter) and "1 to 4" (a fifth of the total). A child thinks "1 in 4" is only about a group of exactly four.
