Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Functioneel gebruiken / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

in contexts, interpreting or processing the "remainder" (when dividing with a remainder; *delen*, dividing, also means sharing)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 packs 30 eggs in boxes of 12 and says: 2 full boxes and 6 eggs over, so 3 boxes are needed to pack them all. For 50 children and buses with 16 seats the child says 4 buses, not "3 remainder 2". Four children who share 25 euros get €6.25 each, because the euro that is left over is shared as well.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names two things to do with a remainder: interpreting it, or processing it. It does not say how; the situation decides whether the result is rounded up, the remainder is dropped, or the remainder is divided further. A statement of this row under *weten waarom* is about a result with a remainder on a calculator. This statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child gives "3 remainder 2" as the number of buses. A child always rounds up or always rounds down, whatever the situation. A child writes the remainder behind a decimal sign, so 50 ÷ 16 becomes 3.2.
