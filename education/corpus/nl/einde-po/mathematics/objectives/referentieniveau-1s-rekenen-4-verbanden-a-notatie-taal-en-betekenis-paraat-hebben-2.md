coordinate system (*assenstelsel*, literally a system of axes)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 knows the word and points out its parts in a graph: the horizontal axis, the vertical axis and the point where they meet. The child reads beside each axis what it stands for and which numbers are marked along it.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement is one term and nothing else: it prints no names for the axes and no word for the point where they meet. It has no verb; under this heading, knowing the term and what it refers to is presumably meant. Placing points in a coordinate system and reading off coordinates is a separate statement in part C under *functioneel gebruiken*.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up the horizontal and the vertical axis. A child does not look at what each axis stands for or at the size of one step along it. A child writes the numbers in the spaces between the marks and not at the marks.
