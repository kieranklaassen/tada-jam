## Official wording

omtrek en oppervlakte bepalen/berekenen van figuren (ook niet rechthoekige) via (globaal) rekenen

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

determining/calculating perimeter (*omtrek*) and area (*oppervlakte*) of figures (also non-rectangular ones) by (approximate, *globaal*) calculation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays a transparent sheet of centimetre squares over the drawing of a leaf, counts the whole squares, puts part squares together into whole ones, and says that the area is about 23 cm². The child lays a string along the edge of the leaf and measures the string to find the perimeter. For an L-shaped room the child splits the floor into a rectangle of 6 m by 4 m and one of 3 m by 2 m, and adds 24 m² and 6 m² to 30 m².

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names two quantities, perimeter and area, and it names figures in general, with figures that are not rectangular added in brackets. It names no figure by name and no formula. The word *globaal* (rough, approximate) stands in brackets before "calculation": the calculation may be exact or approximate. *Omtrek* is the length around any flat figure. The statement does not name *inhoud*. It stands under knowing by heart, having ready: a skill the pupil carries out readily. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child counts only the whole squares and leaves the part squares out. A child finds the perimeter by counting the squares along the edge and not the lengths of the sides. A child gives the perimeter where the area is asked, or the area where the perimeter is asked.
