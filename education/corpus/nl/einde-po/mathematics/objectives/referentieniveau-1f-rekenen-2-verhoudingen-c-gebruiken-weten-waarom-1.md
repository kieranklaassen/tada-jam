## Official wording

eenvoudige verhoudingen met elkaar vergelijken: 1 op de 3 kinderen gaat deze vakantie naar het buitenland. Is dat meer of minder dan de helft?

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Weten waarom / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing simple ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) with one another: 1 in 3 children is going abroad this holiday. Is that more or less than half?

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 explains why 1 in 3 is less than half: "half is 1 in 2; of 6 children, 1 in 3 is 2 children and half is 3 children." The child shows the same with 30 children: 10 against 15.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verhoudingen* (ratios), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The example, a ratio of 1 in 3 compared with a half, is an example of the level, not a limit. The example does not say how many children there are. The statement does not define "simple" and sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child says that 1 in 3 is more than half, because 3 is more than 2. A child compares only the first numbers and calls 1 in 3 and 1 in 2 the same. A child says that the question cannot be answered without knowing how many children there are.
