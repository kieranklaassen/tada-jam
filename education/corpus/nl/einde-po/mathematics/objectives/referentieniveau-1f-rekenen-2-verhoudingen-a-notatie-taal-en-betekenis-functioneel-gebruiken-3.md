## Official wording

verhoudingen herkennen in verschillende dagelijkse situaties (recepten, snelheid, vergroten/verkleinen, schaal enz.)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Functioneel gebruiken / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) in various everyday situations (recipes, speed, enlarging/reducing, scale etc.)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a recipe for 4 people and says that each amount is set against the number of people, which makes it a ratio. Hearing "80 km per hour", the child says that a distance is set against a time. The child points to the scale on a map and says that it sets a length on the map against a length in reality, and says of a photo and its enlargement that the width and the height stand in the same ratio in both.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verhoudingen* (ratios), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The verb is recognising. The brackets end with "enz." (etc.), so the four situations are examples and the list is open. The statement names no numbers and sets no number range. Solving ratio problems is named in statements of row C.

### Common mistakes

A child adds the same amount to every ingredient instead of multiplying every amount by the same number. A child enlarges a drawing in one direction only. A child does not see speed as a ratio between distance and time.
