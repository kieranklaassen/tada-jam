## Official wording

kennis over bewerkingen:
3 + 5 = 5 + 3, maar 3 – 5 ≠ 5 – 3

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Weten waarom / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowledge about operations:
3 + 5 = 5 + 3, but 3 – 5 ≠ 5 – 3

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 shows with counters that 3 + 5 and 5 + 3 both make 8, because joining two groups in either order gives the same pile. The child explains that 5 − 3 is 2 and that 3 − 5 is not 2: on a number line it ends 2 below 0. The child tests other operations in the same way: 4 × 6 = 6 × 4, and 12 ÷ 4 is not equal to 4 ÷ 12.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The general phrase is "knowledge about operations"; the printed example shows one piece of such knowledge: the two numbers may change places in an addition, not in a subtraction. The example, with the numbers 3 and 5, is an example of the level, not a limit. The statement does not give the result of 3 – 5, does not use a term for this property, and prints no example with multiplication or division. The sign ≠ means "is not equal to".

### Common mistakes

A child turns the numbers round when the first is smaller and writes 3 − 5 = 2. A child does the same inside a written subtraction: in 53 − 28 the child takes 3 from 8 and gets 35 (it is 25). A child assumes that the numbers in a division may change places as in a multiplication.
