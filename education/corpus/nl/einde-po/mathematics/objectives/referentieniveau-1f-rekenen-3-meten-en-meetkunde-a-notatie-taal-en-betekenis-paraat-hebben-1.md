## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads € 12.50 aloud as "twelve euros fifty" and writes down an amount that is said aloud (Dutch notation uses a decimal comma: € 12,50). The child reads the same time from a clock with hands and from a digital clock showing 15:40, and says and writes it. The child writes a date in figures in the order day, month, year, and reads aloud and writes measures such as 3.5 km, 25 m², 2 litres, 750 g and 21 °C without hesitation.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement lists six things of which the pronunciation (how it is said aloud) and the notation are meant: amounts of money, with "euro" in brackets; time, analogue and digital; calendar and date; measures of length, of area and of *inhoud*; weight; and temperature. *Inhoud* is the Dutch word for both volume and capacity. The one printed example is the date 23-11-2007, in the order day, month, year; it is an example of the level, not a limit. The statement names no units, no symbols and no number range, and it does not say which measures of length, area or *inhoud* are meant.

### Common mistakes

A child reads € 3.05 as "three euros fifty", or writes three euros and five cents as € 3.5. A child gets the hour wrong at half past: in Dutch *half vier* (literally "half four") is 3:30, and a child writes 4:30. A child leaves out the raised 2 or 3 and writes m where m² or m³ is meant.
