## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 sees an offer in a shop, 6 packs for 18 euros, and wants 5 packs. The child first works out one pack, 18 ÷ 6 = 3 euros, and then 5 × 3 = 15 euros. Another child takes the price of one pack off the 18 euros and also reaches 15 euros.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verhoudingen* (ratios), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The example, which ends in dots where the answer goes, is an example of the level, not a limit. The statement does not explain "not directly"; in the example 5 is not a multiple or a simple part of 6, so a step in between is needed. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reasons by adding: one pack fewer, so one euro less, 17 euros. A child stops at the price of one pack and gives 3 euros as the answer. A child divides the wrong way round, 6 ÷ 18.
