## Official wording

op basis van een grafiek of diagram conclusies trekken over een situatie

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Weten waarom / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

drawing conclusions about a situation on the basis of a graph or diagram

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at a bar chart of the rainfall in each month of a year and concludes that the summer months were drier than the autumn months in that place. The child points to the bars that support the conclusion. The child also says what the chart does not tell, such as why it rained less.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for conclusions about a situation, drawn from a graph or a diagram. These two are named; the table is not named in this statement. The Dutch *diagram* is the word used for charts such as the bar chart (*staafdiagram*). It names no kind of graph or diagram and no kind of situation. It stands under understanding why: the child says on what the conclusion rests. Making predictions about a future situation is the separate statement directly after this one, under the same heading.

### Common mistakes

A child concludes more than the graph shows and gives a cause as if the graph proved it. A child bases the conclusion on one value and not on the whole. A child reads the shape of the graph as a picture of the scene.
