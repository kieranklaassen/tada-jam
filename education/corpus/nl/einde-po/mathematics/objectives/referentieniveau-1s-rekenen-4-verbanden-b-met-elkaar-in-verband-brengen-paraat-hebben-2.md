## Official wording

globale grafiek tekenen op basis van een beschrijving in woorden, bijvoorbeeld: tijd-afstand-grafiek

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Paraat hebben / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

drawing a global (*globale*, giving only the general course) graph on the basis of a description in words, for example: time-distance graph

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads: "Sam walks to school, waits a while at the crossing, and runs the last part." The child draws two axes, time along the bottom and distance from home up the side, and sketches a line that rises, stays level, and then rises more steeply. The child puts no numbers on the axes and says which part of the line belongs to which part of the story.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for a global graph. *Globaal* means rough or overall: such a graph gives the general course of something; the statement does not define the word further. "For example" (*bijvoorbeeld*) introduces one example, the time-distance graph; it is an example of the level, not a limit, and the statement names no other kind of graph. It stands under knowing by heart, having ready: a skill the pupil carries out readily.

### Common mistakes

A child lets the line go down, or stops it, where the person stands still. A child draws the route as on a map and not a graph. A child draws the slower part steeper than the faster part.
