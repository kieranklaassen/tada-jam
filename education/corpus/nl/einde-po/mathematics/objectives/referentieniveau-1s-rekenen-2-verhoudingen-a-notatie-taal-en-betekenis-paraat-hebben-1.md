## Official wording

schrijfwijze 1/4 × 260 of 260/4

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

notation 1/4 × 260 or 260/4

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 sees ¼ × 260 and 260/4 and reads both at once as the same thing: a quarter of 260, or 260 divided by 4, which is 65. The child writes "a quarter of the 260 pupils" in either form and changes from one to the other without calculating again.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading A (notation, language and meaning). The statement is a fragment: a way of writing (*schrijfwijze*), shown in two forms with the numbers 1/4 and 260. These numbers are the example, not a limit. The statement prints no result and does not say what is done with the notation; read with its sub-heading and kind, it concerns having ready that a fraction of an amount can be written as a multiplication by the fraction or as a division written as a fraction. The source prints the fractions with full-size digits and a slash.

### Common mistakes

A child reads 260/4 as a fraction that stays as it is and does not see a division with the result 65. A child works out ¼ × 260 as 260 × 4 = 1040. A child does not see that the two forms mean the same and works each out separately.
