## Official wording

laten reflecteren op eigen en andermans rekenwijze en overig wiskundig handelen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 17 A c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having pupils reflect on their own and other people's way of calculating (*rekenwijze*) and other mathematical activity (*wiskundig handelen*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out 5 × 198 as 5 × 200 − 10 = 990, and a classmate does it in columns on paper. The two tell each other what they did; the child says which way was shorter for these numbers and whether it would be as short for 5 × 137, and tries the classmate's way once. After a measuring task the child says what went well and what to do differently.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 17 A, which says that the school stimulates the development of a mathematical attitude in pupils; 17 A sits under core goal 17. Like 17 A, it says what the school does and is not a requirement on the pupil. The Dutch *laten reflecteren* ("to have reflect") names no one; the pupils are understood, and the gloss adds them. The reflection is on a pupil's own and on another person's way of calculating and other mathematical activity. The term *rekenwijze* is also used in items 10 A c and 10 A e, under core goal 10. The item does not say in what form the reflection takes place. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

This item is about what the school does, so it has no typical error of a child. Asked to reflect, children most often repeat the steps they took and give no judgement of them, or call another child's way wrong because it differs from their own.
