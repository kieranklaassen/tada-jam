## Official wording

relatie tussen breuken, verhoudingen en percentages

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Weten waarom / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

relationship between fractions, ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) and percentages

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 describes one situation in three ways and explains why they agree: 3 of every 4 pupils have a bicycle, that is ¾ of the pupils, and that is 75 of every 100, or 75%. The child explains that a percentage is a fraction with denominator 100. For syrup and water mixed 1 : 4, the child reasons that the syrup is ⅕ of the drink, which is 20%.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading B (relating to one another). The statement is a fragment without a verb: read with its kind, it concerns understanding how fractions, ratios and percentages are connected and why. It names no numbers and gives no example. Decimal numbers are not named in this statement. Converting between these forms stands in statements under the other two kinds.

### Common mistakes

A child turns the ratio 1 : 4 straight into ¼ or 25%. A child sees fractions, ratios and percentages as three separate subjects and cannot say why 3 of 4 is 75%. A child takes a percentage for an amount and reads 75% as 75 pupils.
