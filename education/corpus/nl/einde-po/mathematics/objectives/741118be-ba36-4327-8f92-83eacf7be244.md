## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using mathematical symbols, notations and *begrippen* (which can be read as concepts or as the terms for them);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 writes and reads signs such as +, −, ×, =, <, > and %, the fraction bar, and the sign for dividing (Dutch schools write a colon, 12 : 4). The child writes amounts and measures in the usual notation, such as € 3.50, 1.5 l, 3/4 and 14:30 (Dutch writes the decimal sign as a comma: € 3,50). When explaining, the child uses words such as "sum", "product", "numerator", "perimeter", "average" and "ratio", and can say what each means.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 16 A, which says that the pupil uses mathematical language and mathematical representations; 16 A sits under core goal 16. It names three things and lists no symbol, notation or term. The Dutch *begrippen* can mean concepts or the words for them; the gloss keeps both readings. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads < and > the wrong way round. A child reads the equals sign as "gives" and accepts 4 + 3 = 7 + 2 = 9. A child mixes up terms that belong together, such as numerator and denominator, or perimeter and area.
