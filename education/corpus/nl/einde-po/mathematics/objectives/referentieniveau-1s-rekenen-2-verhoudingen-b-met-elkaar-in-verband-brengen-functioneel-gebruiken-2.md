approximating fractions as finite decimal numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 divides 2 metres of ribbon into 3 equal pieces and writes each piece as about 0.67 m, knowing that ⅔ does not come out exactly. Sharing 10 euros among 3 people, the child gives each 3.33 euros and has 1 cent left. The child chooses how many decimals the situation needs: centimetres for the ribbon, cents for the money.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading B (relating to one another). The verb is approximating: the result is a decimal number with an end that lies near the fraction and need not equal it. The statement names no fractions and no number of decimals, does not mention a calculator, and sets no number range. Converting with a calculator (sub-heading B, *functioneel gebruiken*) and fractions with a finite or infinite number of decimals (sub-heading B, *weten waarom*) are separate statements of this level.

### Common mistakes

A child treats the approximation as exact and writes ⅓ = 0.33, then is surprised that 3 × 0.33 is 0.99 and not 1. A child cuts off and does not round: ⅔ becomes 0.66. A child rounds early in a calculation of several steps, so that the end result is wrong.
