## Official wording

efficiënt rekenen (+, –, ×, :) gebruik makend van de eigenschappen van getallen en bewerkingen, met eenvoudige getallen

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 5.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating efficiently (+, –, ×, :) making use of the properties of numbers and operations, with simple numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has short ways ready: 98 + 47 as 100 + 45 = 145, 301 − 298 by counting on from 298, which gives 3, 5 × 68 as 10 × 34 = 340, and 96 ÷ 4 as half of 48, which is 24. For 25 × 7 × 4 the child first takes 25 × 4 = 100 and then says 700.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The brackets name the four operations; the colon is the Dutch sign for division. The statement does not name the properties and does not define "simple numbers". It does not say whether the work is done in the head or on paper. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child swaps the numbers in a subtraction or a division, as is allowed in an addition or a multiplication. A child adjusts in the wrong direction: 83 − 29 becomes 83 − 30 − 1 = 52 instead of 54. A child splits only half of a product: 4 × 26 becomes 4 × 20 + 6 = 86 instead of 104.
