## Official wording

laten gebruiken van wiskundige modellen, wiskundige instrumenten en algoritmes in verschillende leergebieden.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 18 B d.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having pupils use mathematical models, mathematical instruments and algorithms in different learning areas (*leergebieden*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 uses a ratio table in a cooking lesson to change a recipe for 4 people into one for 10, and a thermometer and a rain gauge for a week of weather measurements in a science lesson. The child follows a fixed set of steps to put the measurements in order and to find their average. In a physical education lesson the child times runs with a stopwatch and records them in a table.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 18 B, which says that the school supports the use of mathematics in different learning areas; 18 B sits under core goal 18. Like 18 B, it says what the school does and is not a requirement on the pupil. The Dutch *laten gebruiken* ("to have use") names no one; the pupils are understood, and the gloss adds them. It names three things to be used: mathematical models, mathematical instruments and algorithms, which are the subjects of goal sentences 15 B, 16 B and 15 C. It names no learning area. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

This item is about what the school does, so it has no typical error of a child. Children most often do not think of a model or an instrument from the mathematics lesson when they are in another lesson, and guess or copy a number where they could measure or calculate.
