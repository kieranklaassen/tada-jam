## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 sees 0.75 litre on a bottle and says it is three quarters of a litre, and reads a price of 2.50 euros as two and a half euros. When a recipe asks for ¼ kilogram and the kitchen scale shows decimals, the child adds flour until the display shows 0.250. The child uses whichever form fits the situation.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading A (notation, language and meaning). The statement is a fragment without a verb: read with its sub-heading and kind, it concerns using the link between a fraction and a decimal number in practical situations. It names no fractions, no number of decimal places and no direction of converting, and it sets no number range. Converting fractions into decimal numbers is a separate statement of this level (sub-heading C, *paraat hebben*).

### Common mistakes

A child builds the decimal from the digits of the fraction: ¼ becomes 1.4 or 0.4, and ⅛ becomes 0.8. A child calls 0.25 larger than 0.5 because 25 is larger than 5. A child treats ½ and 0.5 as two different amounts because they are written differently.
