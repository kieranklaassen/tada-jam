## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reflecting (*spiegelen*, mirroring) in 2D and 3D

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 draws the mirror image of a figure on squared paper on the other side of a line, and then checks it with a small mirror held upright on the line. The child explains that every point of the image lies as far from the line as the point it comes from, on the opposite side. With blocks the child builds the mirror image of a small building on the other side of an upright mirror, and says which sides have changed places.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The fragment names reflecting in two dimensions and in three dimensions and nothing else: no figures, no objects, and no number or position of mirror lines. It stands under understanding why: understanding and explaining what reflecting does to a figure is presumably meant.

### Common mistakes

A child shifts the figure to the other side of the line without turning it over. With a slanted mirror line, a child measures the distances horizontally or vertically and not at right angles to the line. In 3D a child builds a copy that is turned round and not mirrored, so that left and right are not swapped.
