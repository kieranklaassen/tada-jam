## Official wording

redeneren met en over eigenschappen van meetkundige figuren en begrippen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 14 A a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning with and about properties of geometric figures and concepts;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 holds a cube and a pyramid and counts the faces, edges and corners of each. The child hears "four equal sides and four right angles" and picks the shape, and argues whether a rectangle with four equal sides is a square. The child sorts a set of shapes by a property, such as having parallel sides, and explains the sorting.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 14 A, which says that the pupil reasons about geometric figures and determining position and carries out geometric transformations; 14 A sits under core goal 14. The item lists neither the figures nor the concepts: it does not say which shapes, or whether angles, parallel lines or symmetry are meant. Flat and solid figures are not told apart here; item 14 A d names both. The item states no sizes or number range.

### Common mistakes

A child names a shape by its general look and its position and not by its properties: a square standing on a corner is called a diamond, and a long thin triangle is not accepted as a triangle. A child does not accept that a square is a rectangle. A child uses the name of a flat shape for a solid and calls a cube a square.
