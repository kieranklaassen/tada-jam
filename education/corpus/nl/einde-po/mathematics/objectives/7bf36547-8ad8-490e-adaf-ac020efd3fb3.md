## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who does not see how to start a problem makes a drawing or a table, tries a smaller case first, or works backwards from the end. For "how many handshakes are there when 8 children each shake hands once with every other child", the child tries 2, 3 and 4 children (1, 3 and 6 handshakes), sees that each new child adds one handshake for every child already there, and goes on to 10, 15, 21 and 28.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 15 A, which says that the pupil solves mathematical problems and application problems; 15 A sits under core goal 15. The item is one verb and one noun: it names no heuristic and does not say how many a pupil uses. The law does not define the term. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child knows one way of starting, mostly trying numbers, and uses it for every problem. A child makes a drawing that shows the story (the children, the room) and not the quantities. A child finds the pattern in the small cases and extends it wrongly, for example by doubling the 6 handshakes of 4 children to get 12 for 8 children.
