## Official wording

De leerlingen leren meten en leren te rekenen met eenheden en maten, zoals bij tijd, geld, lengte, omtrek, oppervlakte, inhoud, gewicht, snelheid en temperatuur.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 33.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to measure and learn to calculate with units and measures, such as with time, money, length, perimeter, area, volume (*inhoud*, which also covers capacity), weight, speed and temperature.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 measures a table with a tape measure as 125 cm and writes it as 1.25 m. For a room of 4 m by 3 m the child works out a perimeter of 14 m and an area of 12 m². The child works out that 14:45 to 16:10 is 1 hour and 25 minutes, counts the change from 10 euros for €6.35 as €3.65, and reads a thermometer and a measuring jug.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is the second of two goals under the heading *Meten en meetkunde* (measuring and geometry); goal 32 is about geometric problems. "Zoals" (such as) introduces the nine quantities as examples, not as a closed list. The goal names no particular unit; the units above are the pack's examples. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up perimeter and area. A child converts with the wrong factor: 1 m² is taken as 100 cm², where it is 10,000 cm². A child calculates with time in tens and hundreds, so 1 hour 45 minutes plus 30 minutes becomes 1 hour 75 minutes.
