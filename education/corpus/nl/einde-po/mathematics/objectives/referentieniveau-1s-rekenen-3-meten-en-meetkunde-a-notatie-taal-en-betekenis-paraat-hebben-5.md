## Official wording

(standaard)inhoudsmaten m³, dm³, cm³

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Paraat hebben / 5.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(standard) measures of *inhoud* (volume or capacity) m³, dm³, cm³

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 names the three units and their notations at once: cubic metre (m³), cubic decimetre (dm³) and cubic centimetre (cm³). The child shows each as a cube: a block of 1 cm by 1 cm by 1 cm held between two fingers, a cube of 1 dm by 1 dm by 1 dm held in two hands, and a cube of 1 m by 1 m by 1 m set out with metre sticks.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names three units with their notations: m³, dm³ and cm³. It names no others: the cubic millimetre is not in it, and neither are the litre and the units made from the litre. *Inhoud* covers both volume (the space a solid takes up) and capacity (what a container holds). The word "standard" is printed in brackets before the term. The statement prints no relation between the three units and asks for no conversion. The fragment has no verb; under this heading, knowing the units and their notations by heart is presumably meant. The decree prints this as a statement of its own, apart from the other statements on measures under the same heading.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the raised 3 as "times three". A child mixes up m² and m³ and gives the *inhoud* of a box in m². A child thinks that these units are only for solid things and that an amount of liquid can only be given in litres.
