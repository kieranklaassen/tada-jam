## Official wording

bewerken van de uitkomsten van berekeningen tot een oplossing van een probleem;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 15 A c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

working the outcomes of calculations into a solution to a problem;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has worked out 130 ÷ 6 = 21 remainder 4 for the question how many tables of 6 are needed for 130 people, and says "22 tables", because the last 4 people need a table too. When 250 euros are shared among 8 people and the calculator shows 31.25, the child says "31 euros and 25 cents each". For "how many buses with 50 seats for 120 children" the child changes 2.4 into 3 buses and writes the answer as a sentence with its unit.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 15 A, which says that the pupil solves mathematical problems and application problems; 15 A sits under core goal 15. It is about the step after the calculating: from the outcome of a calculation to a solution to the problem. The verb *bewerken* means working on something until it becomes something else; the item does not say what that work consists of, and rounding, adding a unit and combining several outcomes are not named. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child gives the result of the calculation as the answer: "21 remainder 4 tables" or "2.4 buses". A child rounds by the rule (2.4 becomes 2) where the situation asks for rounding up. A child leaves out the unit, or answers another question than the one asked (the number of people at the last table, not the number of tables).
