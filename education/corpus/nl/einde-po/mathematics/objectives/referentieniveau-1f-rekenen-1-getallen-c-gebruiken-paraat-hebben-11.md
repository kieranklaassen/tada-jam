## Official wording

omzetten van eenvoudige breuken in decimale getallen:
½ = 0,5; 0,01 = [afbeelding: 1/100]

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

converting simple fractions into decimal numbers:
½ = 0.5; 0.01 = [image: 1/100]

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once that ½ is 0.5, ¼ is 0.25, ¾ is 0.75 and 1/10 is 0.1. Reading 0.5 litre on a bottle, the child says "half a litre"; reading 0.01, the child says "one hundredth" and writes 1/100.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row C, *Gebruiken* (using); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement does not define "simple fractions". It prints two examples on one line, with a semicolon between them: ½ = 0.5 and 0.01 = 1/100. The decree prints the fraction 1/100 as an image, with a horizontal bar; the wording holds the pack's transcription of it in square brackets after the word "afbeelding", written on one line with a slash, and the gloss writes "image" there. The second example starts from the decimal number, the other direction from the one the statement names. The examples are examples of the level, not a limit. The decree prints the Dutch decimal comma (0,5); the gloss writes the English decimal point and keeps ½ as printed.

### Common mistakes

A child makes a decimal number out of the two digits of the fraction and writes ¼ = 0.4 or 1.4. A child writes 1/5 = 0.5, where it is 0.2. A child writes one hundredth as 0.100 or as 0.1.
