## Official wording

De leerling gebruikt meetinstrumenten en andere wiskundige instrumenten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 16 B.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil uses measuring instruments and other mathematical instruments.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes a tape measure for the length of the corridor, kitchen scales for flour and a stopwatch for a run, estimates first, measures and reads the result with its unit. The child uses a calculator for 3476 × 28, compares the display, 97,328, with the estimate 3500 × 30 = 105,000, and says whether the result can be right.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 16, which is about using mathematical language and mathematical tools. Its items 16 B a to 16 B d name choosing, with reasons, to use an instrument on the basis of the possibilities, limitations and measurement accuracy (*meetnauwkeurigheid*); estimating beforehand; using the instrument with the mathematical language that goes with it; and determining, interpreting and judging the result. Neither the sentence nor its items name a particular instrument, and the sentence does not say what the "other mathematical instruments" are; the tape measure, kitchen scales, stopwatch and calculator above are the pack's examples. Measuring with suitable measuring instruments is also item 11 A b, under core goal 11. This goal sets no level of accuracy or precision and states no number range; item 16 B a names measurement accuracy as one ground for choosing to use an instrument, and gives no level for it.

### Common mistakes

A child starts measuring at the edge of the ruler or at the 1 and not at the 0. A child reads the marks between the numbers wrongly and counts each as 1 where it stands for 2, 5 or 50. A child accepts what an instrument or a calculator shows without asking whether it can be right.
