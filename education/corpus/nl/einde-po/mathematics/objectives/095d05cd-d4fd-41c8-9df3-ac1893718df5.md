## Official wording

leesbaar weergeven van berekeningen en probleemaanpakken;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 16 A b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

representing calculations and approaches to problems readably (*leesbaar*, legible or easy to follow);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 writes the work for a problem so that a classmate can follow it without asking: one step on each line, the digits lined up by place value, a word or a unit beside each number ("28 × 4.50 = 126 euros for tickets"), and the answer as a sentence at the end. The child and a classmate read each other's work, and each says where it could not be followed.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 16 A, which says that the pupil uses mathematical language and mathematical representations; 16 A sits under core goal 16. It covers two things: calculations and approaches to problems. *Leesbaar* can mean legible handwriting or work that a reader can follow; the law does not say which. The item does not say for whom the work has to be readable and prescribes no layout. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child writes only the answer, or loose numbers spread over the page in no order. A child joins steps with equals signs that are not true: 28 × 4.50 = 126 + 98 = 224. A child lines digits up from the left, so that tens stand under hundreds.
