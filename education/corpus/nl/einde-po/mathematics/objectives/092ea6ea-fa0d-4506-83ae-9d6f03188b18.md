## Official wording

bepalen van omtrek, oppervlakte en inhoud van rechthoekige figuren;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 11 A c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

determining the perimeter, area and volume of rectangular figures;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 measures the four sides of a rectangular sandpit and adds them (perimeter), covers a rectangle with square tiles and counts the rows and the tiles in a row (area), and fills a box with cubes, layer by layer (volume). For a terrace of 3 by 4 metres the child works out 14 metres of edging and 12 square metres of tiles.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 11 A, which says that the pupil measures, reasons and calculates with quantities and the units that go with them; 11 A sits under core goal 11. The stated limit is the shape: rectangular figures. Triangles, circles and other shapes are not mentioned in this item. Because volume is named, box-shaped solids are presumably meant as well; the text says only "rectangular figures". The verb "determining" leaves the method open (counting, measuring or calculating) and no formula is named. The item states no units and no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up perimeter and area: adds the sides when the area is asked, or multiplies them when the perimeter is asked. For the perimeter a child adds only the two sides that have a number written beside them. For the volume of a block building a child counts only the cubes that can be seen.
