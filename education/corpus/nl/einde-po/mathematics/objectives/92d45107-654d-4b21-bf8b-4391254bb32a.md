## Official wording

herkennen van verhoudingen in concrete situaties;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 10 C c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) in concrete situations;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 notices that a recipe, a price label ("3 for 2 euros"), a map with a scale bar, and a shadow twice as long as its stick each hold a fixed relation between two amounts. The child puts it in words: "for every 2 of these there are 3 of those". The child also says when a situation does not work this way: the price of apples by weight does, a child's height by age does not.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 10 C, which says that the pupil reasons and calculates with ratios; 10 C sits under core goal 10. It asks for recognising only: comparing is item 10 C d and solving is item 10 C e. The stated condition is "concrete situations"; the item names no particular situations. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child treats every situation with two growing numbers as a ratio: "she was 1 metre tall at age 4, so 2 metres at age 8". A child looks at the difference and not at the ratio, and describes 2 to 3 and 4 to 6 as "1 more" and "2 more" without seeing they are the same ratio.
