## Official wording

delen met rest of (afgerond) decimaal getal:
122 : 5

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 6.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dividing (*delen*, which also means sharing) with a remainder or a (rounded) decimal number:
122 : 5

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out 122 ÷ 5 in both forms: 24 remainder 2, and 24.4. The child chooses the form that fits: 122 children in groups of 5 gives 24 groups and 2 children left over, and 122 euros shared among 5 people gives 24.40 euros each. For 100 ÷ 3 the child gives 33 remainder 1, or the rounded 33.3.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). 122 : 5 is an example of the level, not a limit; it comes out exactly as 24.4. The word "rounded" stands in brackets: the statement does not say when to round or to how many decimals. It names no size for the numbers and sets no number range. The colon is the division sign of Dutch arithmetic (122 : 5 means 122 divided by 5).

### Common mistakes

A child writes the remainder after a decimal point: 122 ÷ 5 = 24.2. A child stops too early and gives a remainder that is larger than the divisor: 23 remainder 7. A child stops at the remainder when a decimal number is asked and cannot continue the division past the decimal point.
