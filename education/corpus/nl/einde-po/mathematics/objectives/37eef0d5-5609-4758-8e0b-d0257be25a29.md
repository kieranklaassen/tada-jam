## Official wording

De leerling bedenkt en beschrijft algoritmes.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 15 C.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil devises and describes algorithms.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 writes numbered steps that someone else can follow without asking anything: "1. Take a number. 2. Double it. 3. Add 6. 4. Halve the result. 5. Take away the number you started with." A classmate follows the steps with 7 (14, 20, 10, 3) and with 12 (24, 30, 15, 3) and ends on 3 both times. The child also writes the steps for putting five number cards in order from small to large.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 15, which is about using mathematical ways of thinking and working. Its items 15 C a to 15 C d name algorithms with a limited number of steps, describing how an algorithm leads to a fixed result, judging the result of an algorithm that has been run through, and devising an algorithm. The law does not define "algorithm". The sentence names no particular form in which an algorithm is described; the numbered steps above are the pack's example. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child leaves out a step that seems obvious to the writer, so the reader cannot go on. A child writes steps that work for the one example tried and not for other numbers. A child writes a step that can be read in two ways, such as "add them".
