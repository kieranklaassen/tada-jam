## Official wording

eenvoudige patronen in rijen getallen en figuren herkennen en voortzetten:
1 – 3 – 5 – 7 – ....
100 – 93 – 86 – 79 – ....

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Paraat hebben / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and continuing simple patterns in rows of numbers and figures:
1 – 3 – 5 – 7 – ....
100 – 93 – 86 – 79 – ....

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at 1, 3, 5, 7, says that each number is 2 more than the one before, and continues with 9, 11, 13. For 100, 93, 86, 79 the child says that each number is 7 less and continues with 72, 65, 58. With a row of figures the child says what changes from one figure to the next and draws the next one.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for two actions, recognising and continuing, on patterns that are simple, in rows of numbers and in rows of figures. It prints two example rows, each on its own line in the record and each ending in four dots: one that goes up by 2 and one that goes down by 7. Both examples have a constant step; they are examples of the level, not limits. The statement gives no example of a row of figures and does not say what makes a pattern simple. It stands under knowing by heart, having ready: a skill the pupil carries out readily.

### Common mistakes

A child looks only at the first two numbers and does not check the step on the rest of the row. A child makes a subtraction error where a step crosses a ten, and writes 71 or 73 after 79 where 72 is right. A child continues the row and cannot say the rule.
