## Official wording

samenhang tussen (standaard)maten ook door terugrekenen, in complexere situaties en ook met decimale getallen «Is 1750 g meer of minder dan 1,7 kg?»

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Functioneel gebruiken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

relation between (standard) measures, also by calculating back (*terugrekenen*), in more complex situations and also with decimal numbers "Is 1750 g more or less than 1.7 kg?"

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 answers the question by writing both weights in one unit: 1.7 kg is 1700 g, so 1750 g is more, by 50 g. The child also goes back from the smaller unit to the larger: 1750 g is 1.75 kg, and 1.75 kg is more than 1.7 kg. The child does this with real things, such as a bag weighed at 1750 g on kitchen scales and a pack labelled 1.7 kg.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement is about the relations between measures, used in practical situations. The word "standard" is printed in brackets before "measures". The statement adds three things with "also" (*ook*): calculating back, more complex situations, and decimal numbers; it does not say what they are added to, and it does not say what makes a situation more complex. It gives one example question between quotation marks (the source prints « »), which compares 1750 g with 1.7 kg. The example names the gram and the kilogram; it is an example of the level, not a limit, and the statement names no other units. The fragment has no verb.

### Common mistakes

A child reads 1.7 kg as 1 kg and 7 g. A child moves the decimal point the wrong way when calculating back and writes 1750 g as 17.5 kg. A child compares the two numbers without looking at the units.
