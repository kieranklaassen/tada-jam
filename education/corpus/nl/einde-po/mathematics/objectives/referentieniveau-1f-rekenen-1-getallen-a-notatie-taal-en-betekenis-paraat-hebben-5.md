## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

numerator, denominator, fraction bar

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 points at the parts of ¾ and names them: the 3 is the numerator (*teller*, "counter"), the 4 is the denominator (*noemer*, "namer") and the line between them is the fraction bar (*breukstreep*). The child uses the words when explaining a sum: "the denominators are the same, so I add the numerators."

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement lists three terms and has no verb; under this kind it is about knowing the words and what each one points to. It asks for no calculation and sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child swaps the two words and calls the lower number the numerator. A child can name the parts but cannot say that the denominator tells into how many equal parts the whole is divided. A child takes the fraction with the larger denominator for the larger fraction.
