## Official wording

beredeneerd kiezen voor gebruik van een instrument op basis van de mogelijkheden, beperkingen en meetnauwkeurigheid;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 16 B a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

choosing, with reasons, to use an instrument on the basis of the possibilities, limitations and measurement accuracy;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has to measure the thickness of a book, the length of the school yard and 5 millilitres of syrup. The child says that a ruler with millimetre marks suits the book, that the same ruler is too short for the yard and a long tape measure suits it, and that a measuring jug with a mark at every 100 millilitres cannot show 5 millilitres. The child also says when an instrument is not needed, as with a calculator for 6 × 25.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 16 B, which says that the pupil uses measuring instruments and other mathematical instruments; 16 B sits under core goal 16. It names three grounds for the choice: possibilities, limitations and measurement accuracy. The Dutch *kiezen voor gebruik van een instrument* can be read as choosing which instrument to use or as deciding whether to use one; the text does not say whose possibilities and limitations are meant, and those of the instrument are the plain reading. It names no instrument and states no degree of accuracy. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child takes the instrument that is nearest or most familiar, mostly the ruler, for every length. A child thinks that the instrument with the smallest marks is the best one for every task. A child gives a result more exactly than the instrument can show, such as 12.37 centimetres from a ruler with millimetre marks.
