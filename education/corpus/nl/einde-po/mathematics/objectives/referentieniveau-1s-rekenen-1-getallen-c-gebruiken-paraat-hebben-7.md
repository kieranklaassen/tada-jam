## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering fractions by size, also by way of standard procedures, and placing them on the number line in meaningful situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 puts ⅔, ¾ and ⅝ in order by making the denominators equal: 16/24, 18/24 and 15/24, so ⅝ is the smallest and ¾ the largest. For easier pairs the child reasons without a procedure: ⅞ is more than ⅚ because it is a smaller piece short of 1. When three jugs hold ⅔, ¾ and ⅝ litre, the child marks the three amounts on a number line from 0 to 1 to show which jug holds most.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement names no denominators and sets no number range. "Also by way of standard procedures" adds procedures to other ways of comparing; the statement names neither the procedures nor the other ways (making the denominators equal, above, is the pack's example). The condition "in meaningful situations" is attached to placing fractions on the number line, not to comparing and ordering. The source prints "standaard-procedures" with a hyphen; the gloss reads it as the word "standard procedures" used in other statements. The decree's table breaks the statement over four rows, and the wording keeps each break as a line break; the gloss reads the four lines as one sentence.

### Common mistakes

A child judges by the size of the digits and calls ⅝ larger than ¾ because 5 and 8 are larger than 3 and 4. A child thinks ⅛ is larger than ⅙ because 8 is larger than 6. On a number line from 0 to 2 a child places ½ in the middle of the line, at 1.
