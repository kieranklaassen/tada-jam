## Official wording

ontbrekende afmeting bepalen van een foto die vergroot wordt

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Functioneel gebruiken / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

determining the missing dimension of a photo that is being enlarged

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has a photo of 10 cm by 15 cm that is enlarged so that the short side becomes 30 cm. The child sees that every length becomes 3 times as long and gives 45 cm for the long side. When the long side has to become 24 cm, the child uses a ratio table (15 to 24 is times 1.6) and finds 16 cm for the short side.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement names one situation: a photo that is enlarged, of which one dimension is missing. It is printed as a statement of its own, not marked as an example, and it does not say whether other enlargements (a drawing, a map) are meant as well. It names no numbers, no enlargement factor and no method. Making a photo smaller is not named.

### Common mistakes

A child adds the same amount to both sides where multiplying is needed: 10 by 15 becomes 30 by 35. A child applies the factor to one side only. A child pairs the wrong sides and compares the short side of one photo with the long side of the other.
