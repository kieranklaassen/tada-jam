## Official wording

5 is gelijk aan (evenveel als) 2 en 3

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

5 is equal to (as many as) 2 and 3

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says without counting that 5 is as many as 2 and 3 together, and reads the equals sign as "is as many as". The child fills the empty place in 5 = 2 + … and in … = 2 + 3 with the same ease as in 2 + 3 = ….

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is a bare example and states no rule; it shows the words "is equal to" and "as many as". The numbers 5, 2 and 3 are an example of the level, not a limit. The statement writes "2 en 3" (2 and 3) with the word "and", not with a plus sign. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the equals sign as "now write the answer", so a sum with the total on the left, 5 = 2 + 3, is called wrong. In … + 3 = 5 a child adds the two numbers in view and writes 8. A child writes a chain that is not true, such as 2 + 3 = 5 + 4 = 9.
