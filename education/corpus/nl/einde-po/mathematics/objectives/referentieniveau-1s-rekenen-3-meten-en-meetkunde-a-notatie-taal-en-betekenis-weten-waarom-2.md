## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says that the distance between two towns is given in kilometres and the width of a door in centimetres, and gives a reason: the number is then neither very large nor very small. The child says that the floor of a room is given in m² because it is an area, and the water in a bath in litres because it is an amount that a container holds. The child corrects a sentence such as "the pencil is 15 m long" and explains what is wrong with it.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for reasoning about the fit between a measure and a context. It names no measures and no contexts. The Dutch *maat* can mean a unit of measurement or a size; the gloss keeps "measure". It stands under understanding why: the child gives reasons for a choice. Choosing the right unit of measurement for a situation or calculation is a separate statement in part B under *functioneel gebruiken*.

### Common mistakes

A child chooses a unit for the wrong quantity, such as metres for the area of a floor. A child uses the unit the child knows best, such as centimetres, for everything. A child picks a suitable unit and cannot give a reason for it.
