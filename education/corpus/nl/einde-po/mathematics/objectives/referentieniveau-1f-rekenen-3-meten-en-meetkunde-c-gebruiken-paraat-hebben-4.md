## Official wording

routes beschrijven en lezen op een kaart met behulp van een rooster

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing and reading routes on a map with the help of a grid

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at a town map with a grid of lettered columns and numbered rows, finds the school in square B3 and the swimming pool in square E5, and tells the way from one to the other, naming the squares the route passes through. The child also follows a route that someone else describes with the grid and puts a finger on the place where it ends.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names a map and a grid, and two actions: describing routes and reading them. It does not say how the grid is labelled (letters and numbers for the squares, or numbers along two axes). Compass directions, scale and distances are not named. Simple route descriptions with "turn left" and "turn right" are a separate statement in part A.

### Common mistakes

A child finds the right column but the wrong row, or swaps the two parts of a grid reference and looks for the letter along the edge that carries the numbers. A child names the square where a place lies but cannot say how the route runs between two squares. A child counts the starting square as the first step and stops one square before the end.
