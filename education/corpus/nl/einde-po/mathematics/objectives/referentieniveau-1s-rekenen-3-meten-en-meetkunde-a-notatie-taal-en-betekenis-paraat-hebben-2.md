## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says at once that a *ton* is 1000 kg. Reading a sign at a bridge that allows vehicles up to 3 *ton*, the child says 3000 kg. The child also works in the other direction and says that 500 kg is half a *ton*.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names one unit, the *ton* (the metric ton, written "tonne" in British English), and prints one relation in brackets: 1000 kg. It names no other unit of weight and no other relation. The decree prints this as a statement of its own, apart from the other statements on measures under the same heading. Standing under knowing by heart, it asks for the fact itself.

### Common mistakes

A child says that a *ton* is 100 kg. A child confuses the weight with the other meanings of the Dutch word: *ton* is also said for 100 000 euros, and it is the word for a barrel. A child cannot go back from kilograms to *ton* and writes 2500 kg as 25 *ton*, where it is 2.5 *ton*.
