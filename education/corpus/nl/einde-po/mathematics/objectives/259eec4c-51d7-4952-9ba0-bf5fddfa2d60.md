## Official wording

beredeneerd vergelijken van verhoudingen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 10 C d.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion), with reasons;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 decides which of two drinks is stronger, 2 cups of syrup to 5 of water or 3 cups to 8, by making the syrup equal: 6 to 15 against 6 to 16, so the first is stronger. The child decides which offer is cheaper, 3 bottles for 4 euros or 5 bottles for 7 euros, by working out 15 bottles in both: 20 euros against 21 euros. Each time the child says why.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 10 C, which says that the pupil reasons and calculates with ratios; 10 C sits under core goal 10. The word *beredeneerd* asks for a reason together with the answer. The item prescribes no method (a ratio table, an amount for one, percentages). This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child looks at one number only and calls 3 to 8 the stronger drink because it has more syrup. A child compares the differences (5 − 2 = 3 against 8 − 3 = 5) instead of the ratios. With two offers a child chooses the lower total price or the larger number of items without relating the two.
