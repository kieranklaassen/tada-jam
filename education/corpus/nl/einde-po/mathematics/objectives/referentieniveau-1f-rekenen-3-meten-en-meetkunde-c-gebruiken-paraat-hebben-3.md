## Official wording

omtrek en oppervlakte berekenen van rechthoekige figuren

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1F, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating perimeter (*omtrek*) and area (*oppervlakte*) of rectangular figures

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out for a rectangular garden of 6 m by 4 m that the fence around it is 20 m long (6 + 4 + 6 + 4) and that the grass covers 24 m² (6 × 4). The child does this fluently for a table top, a room floor or a sheet of paper once the length and the width are known.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The stated limit is the shape: rectangular figures (*rechthoekige figuren*). The statement does not say whether this means rectangles only or also figures made up of rectangles, such as an L shape. It names perimeter and area; *inhoud* (volume or capacity) is not in it. It names no formula, no units and no number range, and it names no other shape: triangles and circles are not in it.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up the two: adds the sides when the area is asked, or multiplies length and width when the perimeter is asked. For the perimeter a child adds only the two sides that have a number written beside them and gets 10 m instead of 20 m. A child gives the area in metres instead of square metres.
