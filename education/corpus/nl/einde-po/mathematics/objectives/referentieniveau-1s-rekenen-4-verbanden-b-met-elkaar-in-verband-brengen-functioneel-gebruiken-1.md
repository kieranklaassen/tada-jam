## Official wording

conclusies trekken door gegevens uit verschillende informatiebronnen met elkaar in verband te brengen (alleen in eenvoudige gevallen)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Functioneel gebruiken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

drawing conclusions by relating data from different information sources to each other (only in simple cases)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays a bus timetable beside a notice with the opening times of a swimming pool. The bus arrives at 9:40 and the pool opens at 10:00, so the child concludes that taking this bus means waiting 20 minutes at the door. The child says which fact came from which source.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for drawing conclusions from data that come from more than one source, in practical situations. The brackets limit it to simple cases; it does not say what makes a case simple. It names no kinds of source and does not say how many sources.

### Common mistakes

A child reads each source separately and reports two facts without a conclusion. A child combines data that do not belong together, such as numbers for different days or in different units. A child takes a value from the wrong row or column of one source.
