## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 knows the word and says what it is: the box beside a chart or map that tells what each colour, line or symbol stands for. In a bar chart with bars in two colours the child looks at the legend first and then says which bars belong to which group.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement is one term and nothing else. It has no verb; under this heading, knowing the term and what a legend is for is presumably meant. It does not say with which kinds of representation the legend is met.

### Common mistakes

A child skips the legend and guesses what the colours stand for. A child mixes up the legend with the title or with the labels on the axes. A child confuses the Dutch words *legenda* (key) and *legende* (a story).
