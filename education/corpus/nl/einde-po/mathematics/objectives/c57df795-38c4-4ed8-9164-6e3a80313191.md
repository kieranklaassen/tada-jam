## Official wording

aanbieden van wiskundige concepten en denk-werkwijzen in onderlinge samenhang;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 18 B a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

offering mathematical concepts and ways of thinking and working (*denk-werkwijzen*) in connection with one another;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works on a task for which several parts of mathematics are needed together: for a plan of a school garden the child measures the plot, draws it to scale, works out the area of the beds and the cost of the plants, and sets the plan out so that others can read it. The child uses what he or she knows about ratios, measuring and money in one piece of work.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 18 B, which says that the school supports the use of mathematics in different learning areas; 18 B sits under core goal 18. Like 18 B, it says what the school does and is not a requirement on the pupil. The verb is offering. "Mathematical concepts" and "mathematical ways of thinking and working" are the names of two domains of these core goals (core goals 10 to 14, and core goals 15 and 16). The words *in onderlinge samenhang* (in connection with one another) do not say whether the connection is among the concepts, between the concepts and the ways of thinking and working, or both. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

This item is about what the school does, so it has no typical error of a child. Children most often keep subjects of mathematics apart: a child who can work with ratios in a lesson on ratios does not use them for a scale drawing.
