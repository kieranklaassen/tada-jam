## Official wording

eenvoudige globale grafieken en diagrammen (beschrijving van een situatie) lezen en interpreteren

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1F, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Functioneel gebruiken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading and interpreting simple global (*globale*, giving only the general course) graphs and diagrams (description of a situation)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at a line that shows the water level in a bath over time and tells what happened: the bath fills, someone gets in, the level stays the same for a while, then the plug is pulled. The child points to where a line rises fastest or where a bar is tallest, and says in words what that means in the situation.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names graphs and diagrams in general and names no kind of either: line graph, bar chart and pie chart are not printed in it. It sets two conditions: simple, and *globaal*. *Globaal* means rough or overall: such a graph gives the general course of something; the statement does not define the word further. The words in brackets, "description of a situation", say what the graph or diagram does. The statement asks for reading and interpreting; making a bar chart is a separate statement in part C. It names no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads a graph as a picture of the situation: a line that goes up is taken for a hill, or the shape of the line for the shape of a path. A child reads off single points but does not describe the course (rising, falling, staying level). A child ignores what the two axes stand for and tells a story that does not fit the graph.
