## Official wording

een breuk of gemengd getal delen door een breuk, vooral binnen een situatie:
1 1/2 : 1/4 ; hoeveel pakjes van 1/4 liter moet je kopen als je 1 1/2 liter slagroom nodig hebt?

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 1 Getallen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Paraat hebben / 14.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dividing (*delen*, which also means sharing) a fraction or mixed number by a fraction, mainly within a situation:
1 1/2 : 1/4 ; how many packs of 1/4 litre must you buy if you need 1 1/2 litres of whipping cream?

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 draws 1½ litres as six quarter litres and answers that 6 packs of ¼ litre are needed: 1½ ÷ ¼ = 6. In the same way the child finds that ⅛ fits 6 times into ¾, so ¾ ÷ ⅛ = 6, and that 2½ ÷ ½ = 5. The child checks by multiplying back: 6 × ¼ = 1½.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement says "mainly within a situation" (*vooral binnen een situatie*): it stresses situations and does not exclude calculations without one. The calculation 1 1/2 : 1/4 and the question about whipping cream are one example of the level, not a limit. The divisor named is a fraction; dividing by a mixed number is not named in this statement. It names no denominators and sets no number range. The colon is the division sign of Dutch arithmetic, and the source prints the fractions with full-size digits (1 1/2 means 1½) and a semicolon between the calculation and the question.

### Common mistakes

A child multiplies where dividing is asked: 1½ × ¼ = ⅜. A child expects an answer smaller than 1½ because dividing is thought to make a number smaller. A child turns the wrong fraction upside down and works out ⅔ × ¼ = 1/6.
