## Official wording

lengte, oppervlakte, inhoud, gewicht (massa), snelheid, tijd, geld, temperatuur en geheugenomvang;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 11 A a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

length, area, volume (*inhoud*, which also covers capacity), weight (mass), speed, time, money, temperature and memory size (*geheugenomvang*, the storage size of digital data);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 measures a classmate's height in centimetres, works out the floor area of the classroom, pours a litre of water into cups, weighs ingredients in grams and reads a speed in kilometres per hour. The child reads a timetable, pays and counts the change in euros and cents, and reads a thermometer. The child compares the size of a photo file with the free space on a memory stick.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 11 A, which says that the pupil measures, reasons and calculates with quantities and the units that go with them; 11 A sits under core goal 11. The item lists nine quantities. The law itself adds "(massa)" after weight. The item names no particular unit for any of them; the units above are the pack's examples. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up area with perimeter, and volume with weight (the larger object is taken to be the heavier one). A child calculates with time in tens and hundreds, so 1 hour 45 minutes plus 30 minutes becomes 1 hour 75 minutes or 1.75 hours. A child does not know the order of the units for memory size and calls a megabyte larger than a gigabyte.
