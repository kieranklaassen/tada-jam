## Official wording

oppervlakte- en inhoudsmaten relateren aan bijbehorende lengtematen

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, A Notatie, taal en betekenis / Weten waarom / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

relating measures of area (*oppervlakte*) and of *inhoud* (volume or capacity) to the corresponding measures of length

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 draws a square of 1 dm by 1 dm, divides it into squares of 1 cm by 1 cm, and counts 10 rows of 10: 100 cm² in 1 dm². The child explains that the step is 100 because the square is 10 times as long and 10 times as wide. With small cubes the child builds one layer of 10 by 10 and says that 10 such layers fill a cube of 1 dm by 1 dm by 1 dm, so 1 dm³ is 1000 cm³.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in part A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for the link between a unit of area or of *inhoud* and the unit of length that belongs to it. It names no units. It stands under understanding why: the child explains the link and does not only recite it. *Inhoud* covers both volume and capacity.

### Common mistakes

A child uses the step for lengths, 10, for areas and volumes too, and says that 1 m² is 10 dm² or that 1 m³ is 10 dm³. A child takes 1 m² for a line of 1 m, or for a square with a perimeter of 1 m. A child knows that the step is 100 or 1000 and cannot say where it comes from.
