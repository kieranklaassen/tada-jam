## Official wording

samengestelde grootheden gebruiken en interpreteren, zoals km/u

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Functioneel gebruiken / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using and interpreting compound quantities, such as km/h (*km/u*, kilometres per hour)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a road sign with 30 and says that a car at 30 km/h covers 30 km in one hour and 15 km in half an hour. The child rides 18 km by bicycle in one hour and says that the speed was 18 km/h. The child compares two speeds and says which rider is faster.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for using and interpreting compound quantities in practical situations. A compound quantity (*samengestelde grootheid*) is made from two other quantities; in km/u these are distance and time, and the Dutch u stands for *uur* (hour). "Such as" (*zoals*) introduces an example: km/u is the only compound quantity named, and it is an example of the level, not a limit. The statement names no other compound quantity and sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads 30 km/h as a distance of 30 km. A child thinks that a speed in km/h only holds when the trip lasts a full hour. For 90 km in 2 hours a child multiplies and says 180 km/h, where dividing gives 45 km/h.
