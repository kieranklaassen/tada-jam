## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 explains why 4985 is about 5000 and not about 500: "it has four digits, so it is thousands, and it is a little under 5 thousand." The child explains why a primary school cannot have 30,000 pupils: "a class has about 30 children and a school has about 10 classes, so about 300." The child explains why 0.098 is close to one tenth.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Getallen* (numbers), under *weten waarom* (understanding why). It stands in row A, *Notatie, taal en betekenis* (notation, language and meaning); 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The verb is reasoning out (*beredeneren*): the statement asks for the argument and not only for the answer. It does not say which kinds of number are meant and sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child judges the size of a number by its first digit and not by the number of digits, so 9800 is called greater than 12,000. A child does not know how many times greater a million is than a thousand. A child gives an order of magnitude with no reason.
