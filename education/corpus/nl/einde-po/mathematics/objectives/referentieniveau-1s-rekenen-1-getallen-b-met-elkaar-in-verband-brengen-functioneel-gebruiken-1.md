## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

translating a complex situation into a calculation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a situation with several pieces of information, picks out what is needed and writes the calculation down before working it out. For a school trip of 28 children at 7.50 euros each with a bus that costs 180 euros, the child writes 28 × 7.50 + 180 and finds 390 euros. The child says which number stands for what and leaves out information that is not needed.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading B (relating to one another). The statement says "complex" without saying what makes a situation complex: it names no kinds of numbers, no number of steps and no number range. The wording is about translating a situation into a calculation; carrying out the calculation is not named in it.

### Common mistakes

A child uses every number in the text, also the ones that are not needed. A child chooses the operation from a single word ("more" is taken to mean adding) and not from the situation. A child works out one step and gives that result as the answer.
