## Official wording

grafische representaties: diagrammen, grafieken en infographics.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 12 A e.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

graphical representations: charts (*diagrammen*), graphs (*grafieken*) and infographics.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads and draws a bar chart of favourite sports and a line graph of the temperature during one day. The child reads an infographic about household waste that combines pictures, numbers and short texts, and says which numbers belong to which picture.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 12 A, which says that the pupil interprets and represents data; 12 A sits under core goal 12. It explains the term "graphical representations" used in items 12 A c and 12 A d. In Dutch school use *diagram* is mostly the word for a bar chart, pie chart or pictogram, and *grafiek* mostly for a line graph; the law itself names no kinds within the three. Tables are in item 12 A a and are not in this list. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads a value between two points of a line graph where the data have nothing in between. In a pie chart a child reads a slice as a count and not as a share of the whole. In an infographic a child looks at the pictures and skips the numbers, or reads the size of a picture as the amount.
