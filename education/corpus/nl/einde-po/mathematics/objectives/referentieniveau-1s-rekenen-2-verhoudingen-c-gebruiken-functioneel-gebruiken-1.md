## Official wording

gebruik dat «geheel» 100% is

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 2 Verhoudingen, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Functioneel gebruiken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

use that "whole" is 100%

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads that 35% of the class comes by bicycle and works out that 65% does not, because the whole class is 100%. With a discount of 20% the child reasons that 80% of the price remains. When 40 euros is 80% of the old price, the child goes through 10% (5 euros) to 100% and finds 50 euros.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Verhoudingen (ratios and proportions), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The source sets "whole" in quotation marks. The Dutch *gebruik* can be read as a noun (the use of this fact) or as an instruction (use this fact); the content is the same in both readings. The statement does not say what is taken as the whole in a situation, and it names no percentages and no numbers. The meaning of percentages above 100 is a separate statement of this level (sub-heading C, *weten waarom*).

### Common mistakes

A child takes the wrong amount as 100%: after a discount of 20% the child adds 20% of the new price of 40 euros (8 euros) to go back and gets 48 euros, where the old price was 50. A child does not notice that the named parts of one whole do not add up to 100%, for example 35% and 55% with nothing else named. A child thinks 100% always means 100 things.
