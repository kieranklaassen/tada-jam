## Official wording

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising fraction notation also as ¾

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 sees ¾ printed in a recipe or on a package, with a small 3 and a small 4 on either side of a slanting stroke, and reads it at once as three quarters. The child matches it, without working anything out, to the same fraction written with a horizontal bar and to three of four equal parts of a strip.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading A (notation, language and meaning). The statement prints one form of fraction notation, ¾: a small numerator and a small denominator on either side of a slanting stroke. The word "also" (*ook*) shows that this form is in addition to another way of writing fractions, which the statement does not name. ¾ is an example of the form, not a limit on the fractions. The verb is recognising; writing this form is not mentioned. The statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads ¾ as two separate numbers, 3 and 4, or as a division that still has to be worked out, and does not see it as one number. A child reads a mixed number written this way, such as 2¾, as 23 over 4.
