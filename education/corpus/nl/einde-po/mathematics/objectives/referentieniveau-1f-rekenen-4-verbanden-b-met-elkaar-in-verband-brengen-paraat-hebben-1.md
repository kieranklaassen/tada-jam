## Official wording

eenvoudige tabel gebruiken om informatie uit een situatiebeschrijving te ordenen

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 4 Verbanden, niveau 1F, B Met elkaar in verband brengen / Paraat hebben / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using a simple table to order information from a description of a situation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a short text about how a class comes to school: 12 children by bike, 9 on foot and 5 by car. The child draws a table with one column for the way of travelling and one for the number of children, and fills in the three rows. The child sets up such a table without being told which rows and columns to use.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for arithmetic, domain *Verbanden* (relationships), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). It stands in part B, *Met elkaar in verband brengen* (relating things to each other). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names one form, a simple table, and one source, a description of a situation. It does not say how many rows or columns make a table simple, and it names no number range. It asks for ordering the information; calculating with information from tables and drawing conclusions is a separate statement in part C.

### Common mistakes

A child copies the sentences of the text into the table instead of splitting them into a category and a number. A child gives the columns no headings, so the numbers cannot be read back. A child leaves out a piece of information that stands in another place in the text.
