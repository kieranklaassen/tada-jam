## Official wording

relaties leggen tussen grootheden en eenheden, tussen grootheden onderling en tussen eenheden onderling.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel B, kerndoel 11 A e.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making connections between quantities (*grootheden*, things that can be measured) and units, between quantities themselves and between units themselves.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says that litres belong to volume and square metres to area (quantity and unit). The child says that speed links distance and time, and that a larger box does not have to be a heavier box (quantities with each other). The child changes 2.5 kilometres into 2500 metres and 90 minutes into one and a half hours, and fills a 1-litre jug from a cube of 10 by 10 by 10 centimetres (units with each other).

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 11 A, which says that the pupil measures, reasons and calculates with quantities and the units that go with them; 11 A sits under core goal 11. It names three kinds of connection. It does not say which units or which conversions are meant, and it names no prefixes such as kilo or milli. The quantities are listed in item 11 A a. This item states no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child changes units in the wrong direction: 2.5 kilometres becomes 0.0025 metres. A child uses the factor for lengths with areas or volumes: 1 square metre is said to be 100 square centimetres. A child changes time as if it went in tens: 1.5 hours becomes 1 hour 50 minutes.
