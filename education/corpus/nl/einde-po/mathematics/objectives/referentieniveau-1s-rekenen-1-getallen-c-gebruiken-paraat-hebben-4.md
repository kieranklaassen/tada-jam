## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 works out 4 + 6 × 5 as 34, not 50, and 20 − 12 ÷ 4 as 17, without having to look up a rule. In (4 + 6) × 5 the child works out the brackets first and gets 50.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain Getallen (numbers), under *paraat hebben* (knowing by heart, having ready). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It stands under sub-heading C (using). The statement is a fragment without a verb: read with its kind, it concerns having the order of operations ready. It does not state the rule, and it does not say which operations are meant or whether brackets and powers are included. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child works strictly from left to right and gives 4 + 6 × 5 = 50. A child thinks addition always goes before subtraction, or multiplication always before division, and gives 12 − 4 + 3 = 5 (it is 11) or 24 ÷ 4 × 2 = 3 (it is 12).
