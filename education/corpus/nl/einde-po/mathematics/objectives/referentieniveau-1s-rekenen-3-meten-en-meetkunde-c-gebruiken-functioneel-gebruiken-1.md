## Official wording

formules gebruiken bij berekenen van oppervlakte en inhoud van eenvoudige figuren

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 2, 3 Meten en Meetkunde, niveau 1S, C Gebruiken / Functioneel gebruiken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using formulas when calculating area (*oppervlakte*) and *inhoud* (volume or capacity) of simple figures

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 measures a rectangular table top of 120 cm by 80 cm and uses length × width to find 9600 cm². For a box of 5 dm by 3 dm by 2 dm the child uses length × width × height and finds 30 dm³. The child says which formula belongs to which figure before calculating.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1S for arithmetic, domain *Meten en Meetkunde* (measuring and geometry), under *functioneel gebruiken* (using in practical situations). It stands in part C, *Gebruiken* (using). 1S is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for using formulas, in practical situations, to calculate the area and the *inhoud* of simple figures. It names no formula and no figure, and it does not say which figures count as simple. *Inhoud* covers both volume and capacity. The statement sets no number range. Explaining the formulas is a separate statement in this part under *weten waarom*.

### Common mistakes

A child uses the calculation for the perimeter where the area is asked. A child multiplies lengths given in different units without converting first. A child writes an area in cm or a volume in cm².
