## Official wording

verwoorden van het verschil tussen een mening en een feit volgens de natuurwetenschappen;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting into words the difference between an opinion and a fact according to the natural sciences;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 sorts statements on cards: "a spider has eight legs" and "ice floats on water" go with the facts, "spiders are scary" goes with the opinions. The child says what the difference is: a fact in science can be checked by observing or measuring, and others who check it find the same; an opinion is what someone thinks or feels about something. For a doubtful card the child says how it could be checked.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 D, which says that the pupil explores the nature of the natural sciences and technology; 29 D sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item asks for putting the difference into words; it gives no definition of a fact or of an opinion. The words "according to the natural sciences" can be read with "a fact" or with the whole difference; the gloss keeps the Dutch order. The item does not say whether this is spoken or written.

### Common mistakes

A child treats as a fact whatever is said firmly, is printed, or is said by many people. A child calls a statement it agrees with a fact and one it disagrees with an opinion. A child calls a claim that can be checked and is false, such as "the earth is flat", an opinion.
