## Official wording

beschrijven van verschillende soorten energiebronnen en manieren van opslaan en transporteren van energie;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing different kinds of energy sources and ways of storing and transporting energy;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 names energy sources it knows from its surroundings: the sun on solar panels, the wind on a wind turbine, natural gas, petrol, and food for the body. The child says how energy is stored (in a battery, as fuel in a tank, as water held behind a dam, as hot water in an insulated tank) and how it is moved (electricity through cables, gas through pipes, fuel in a tanker). The child sorts cards under source, storage and transport.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 30 C, which says that the pupil shows insight into and experiments with light, sound, energy and forces; 30 C sits under core goal 30. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is describing. The item names no source, no way of storing and no way of transporting, and it does not say by what the kinds of source are told apart. It states no unit and no quantity. Converting energy from one form into another is not named in this item.

### Common mistakes

Many children think that electricity, or the wall socket, is an energy source; electricity is generated from a source such as wind, sunlight or gas, and carries the energy to the house. Many children think that energy is used up and gone; it changes into another form, in the end mostly heat. A child uses "energy" and "force" as the same word.
