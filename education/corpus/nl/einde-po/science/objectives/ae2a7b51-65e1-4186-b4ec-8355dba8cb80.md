## Official wording

De leerlingen leren hoe je weer en klimaat kunt beschrijven met behulp van temperatuur, neerslag en wind.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO, bijlage, kerndoel 43.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn how you can describe weather and climate with the help of temperature, precipitation and wind.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads an outdoor thermometer in the shade at the same time each day, measures the rain that fell into a straight-sided container, and notes from which direction the wind blows and how hard. The child keeps these readings in a table for some weeks and describes the weather of one day with them. For the climate the child compares the average temperature and precipitation per month of two places, taken over many years, and says how the two places differ.

### Limits

This 2006 goal is still in force. It is the fourth of seven goals under the heading *Natuur en techniek* (nature and *techniek*, a word read as technology or as engineering). The goal names three quantities to describe with: temperature, precipitation and wind. It names no unit, no instrument and no scale, and it states no numbers. It asks for describing; explaining how weather or climate comes about is not in this goal. It does not say what the difference between weather and climate is.

### Common mistakes

A child treats weather and climate as the same: one cold week is called the climate, while climate is the average weather of a place over many years. A child thinks that an east wind blows towards the east; a wind is named after the direction it comes from. A child reads a thermometer that hangs in the sun and takes the reading as the temperature of the air.
