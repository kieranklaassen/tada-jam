## Official wording

onderzoeken van vraagstukken over natuurwetenschappelijke verschijnselen, technische systemen, gezondheid, natuurbeheer, veiligheid, duurzaamheid en ruimtelijke inrichting;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating (*onderzoeken*, also read as examining) issues (*vraagstukken*, questions or problems to work on) about phenomena of the natural sciences, technical systems, health, nature management, safety, sustainability and spatial planning (*ruimtelijke inrichting*, how land and space are laid out);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 turns an issue into a question that can be investigated, such as how the school can throw away less paper. The child weighs the paper bin of the classroom on a scale each day for a week, writes the weights in a table and notes what kind of paper is in it. From the table the child says where most of the paper comes from and proposes one change. For an issue about safety the child counts the cars and bicycles that pass the school gate in ten minutes at the start of the school day.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 A, which says that the pupil explores the living environment and investigates issues from it; 29 A sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item lists seven fields that the issues are about; it sets no level of depth for any of them and does not say on how many a pupil works. It names no method; item 29 C b names steps of investigating and designing. Safety is one of the fields, and the item states no safety condition for the pupil's own work.

### Common mistakes

A child takes a whole field as its question ("sustainability") and does not narrow it to something that can be investigated. A child looks the answer up and copies it, without collecting observations. A child counts or measures once and treats the result as true for every day.
