## Official wording

onderbouwen van conclusies met gebruik van vaktaal.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

supporting conclusions with the use of subject language (*vaktaal*, the terms of the subject).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says or writes a conclusion and gives the evidence for it: "Sugar dissolves faster in warm water than in cold water: in each of our three trials the sugar in the warm water had dissolved first." The child uses the terms of the subject, such as "dissolve" where it would otherwise say "melt" or "disappear", and "evaporate" where it would say "dry up".

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 C, which says that the pupil uses scientific and technological ways of working in discovering and understanding the world; 29 C sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names no terms and gives no list of subject language. It does not say whether the conclusion is spoken or written. Item 29 C d is about interpreting measurements and observations.

### Common mistakes

A child states a conclusion without evidence ("we were right"). A child's conclusion goes further than the results: one test becomes "always". A child uses an everyday word that means something else in science, such as "melt" for dissolve.
