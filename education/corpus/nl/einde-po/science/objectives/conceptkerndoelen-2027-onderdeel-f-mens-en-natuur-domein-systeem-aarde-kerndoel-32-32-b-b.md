## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 covers a bowl of water with clear film and sets it in the sun: drops form on the underside of the film and fall back. The child draws the cycle with arrows and tells it in order: water evaporates from the sea, the land and plants; the water vapour cools as it rises and forms clouds of very small drops; rain or snow falls; and the water runs through the ground and through rivers back to the sea.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into the water cycle, weather and climate and explores these in the pupil's own living environment; 32 B sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item asks how the cycle is put together: its parts and how they connect. It names no part: the words evaporation, condensation and precipitation are not in it. It names no place and no quantity.

### Common mistakes

Many children think clouds are water vapour or smoke; water vapour cannot be seen, and a cloud is made of very small drops of water or ice crystals. A child thinks water evaporates only when it boils, or only from the sea. A child draws sea, cloud and rain and leaves out the water in the ground and the water that plants give off.
