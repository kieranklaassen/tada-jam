## Official wording

beschrijven van frequentie en duur van extreem weer in gebieden: orkanen, windhozen, langdurige droogte, hittegolf en zware neerslag;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 14.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing frequency and duration of extreme weather in regions: hurricanes, whirlwinds (*windhozen*, also translated as tornadoes), prolonged drought, heat wave and heavy precipitation;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 makes a table with a row for each kind of extreme weather and fills in, from an atlas or a book, in which regions it occurs, how often and for how long. The child marks on a world map the warm seas over which hurricanes form. The child says that a hurricane lasts for days, a whirlwind usually for minutes, and a drought for weeks or months.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into the water cycle, weather and climate and explores these in the pupil's own living environment; 32 B sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. What the pupil describes is frequency and duration; causes and effects of extreme weather are not named in this item. "In regions" (*in gebieden*) names no region. After the colon the item names five kinds of extreme weather and does not say whether the list is complete. The source prints "heat wave" (*hittegolf*) in the singular among plurals, and the gloss follows it. The item gives no number or unit for frequency or duration.

### Common mistakes

Many children mix up a hurricane and a tornado: a hurricane is hundreds of kilometres wide, forms over warm sea and lasts for days; a tornado is narrow, forms under a thundercloud and usually lasts minutes. A child takes one event that the child remembers as proof of how often it happens. A child mixes up how often an event occurs with how long it lasts or how strong it is.
