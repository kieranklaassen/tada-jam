## Official wording

benoemen van gevaren rond statische elektriciteit en elektrische stroom, geluid, onweer en zonlicht;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming dangers around static electricity and electric current, sound, thunderstorms (*onweer*, thunder and lightning) and sunlight;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says what can go wrong and why. Electric current from a wall socket can pass through the body and injure or kill, and wet hands and damaged cables make that more likely; a spark of static electricity can set fire to petrol vapour. Loud sound, such as music through earphones at high volume, can damage hearing permanently. During a thunderstorm lightning can strike a person in an open field or under a single tree, and a building or a closed car is a safer place; too much sunlight burns the skin, and looking into the sun damages the eyes.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 30 C, which says that the pupil shows insight into and experiments with light, sound, energy and forces; 30 C sits under core goal 30. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item asks for naming dangers around five things: static electricity, electric current, sound, thunderstorms and sunlight. It names no danger, and it states no safety rule, no protective measure and no number (no voltage, sound level or time in the sun). The verb of this item is naming; it asks for no experiment with these dangers.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks that a tree gives safe shelter in a thunderstorm. A child thinks that the sun can burn the skin only when it feels hot; the ultraviolet part of sunlight also burns on a cool or lightly clouded day. A child thinks that ears that ring after loud sound always recover fully, and does not know that damage to hearing builds up and is permanent.
