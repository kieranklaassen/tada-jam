## Official wording

verkennen op welke plek in een biotoop planten, dieren en schimmels zich bevinden;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 14.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring in which place in a biotope (*biotoop*, an area with its own living conditions) plants, animals and fungi are found;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a wood a child of 11 or 12 looks in the crowns of the trees, on the trunks, among the plants on the ground, under a log and in the layer of dead leaves, and notes what is found where: a bird's nest high in a tree, woodlice in the damp under the log, white threads of a fungus among the dead leaves, mushrooms on dead wood. The child draws the wood from the side and marks each find at its place.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 C, which says that the pupil explores different ecosystems and explains interactions within the system; 32 C sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is exploring (*verkennen*). The item names plants, animals and fungi; bacteria are not named in it. It says "a biotope" and names none. It asks where the organisms are found; how living and non-living parts are connected is in item 32 C d. The item names no quantity.

### Common mistakes

A child looks for animals only, and for large ones, and passes over plants, small animals and fungi. A child sees a fungus only where there is a mushroom, and does not know that most of the fungus is threads in the soil or the wood. A child thinks organisms are spread over a place by chance, and does not see that each is found where the damp, the light and the food suit it.
