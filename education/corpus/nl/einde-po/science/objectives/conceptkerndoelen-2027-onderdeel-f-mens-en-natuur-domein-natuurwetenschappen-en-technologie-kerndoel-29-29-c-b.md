## Official wording

toepassen van onderzoeks- en ontwerpstappen op een systematische en iteratieve wijze;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

applying research steps and design steps (*onderzoeks- en ontwerpstappen*; *onderzoek* is read as research or as investigation) in a systematic and iterative way (*iteratief*, in repeated rounds);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 investigates in a fixed order: a question, a prediction, a plan, carrying out and measuring, writing down the results, a conclusion. In a design task the child writes down what the product must be able to do, sketches ideas, chooses one, makes it and tests it. When the test shows a fault the child goes back a step, changes one thing and tests again: a flat paper bridge sags, so the child folds the paper into pleats and tests the new version.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 C, which says that the pupil uses scientific and technological ways of working in discovering and understanding the world; 29 C sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names no steps and no number of steps or rounds; the steps given above are a common school form and are not in the text. It states the manner: systematic and iterative. It names no subject matter and states no safety condition.

### Common mistakes

A child treats the steps as a list to go through once and does not go back after a failed test. A child skips the prediction or the requirements and starts doing. A child changes its prediction afterwards so that it matches the result.
