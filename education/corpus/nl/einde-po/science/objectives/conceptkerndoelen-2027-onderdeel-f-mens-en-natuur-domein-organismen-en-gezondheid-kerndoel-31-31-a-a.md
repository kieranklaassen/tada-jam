## Official wording

beschrijven van de levenscyclus van bacteriën, schimmels, planten en dieren;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing the life cycle of bacteria, fungi (*schimmels*, a word that also means moulds), plants and animals;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays picture cards of a frog in a circle: spawn, tadpole, young frog, adult frog, and spawn again. The child sows a bean and draws the seedling, the flowering plant and the pod with new seeds. The child keeps a slice of bread in a closed bag and sees mould grow from spores that are too small to see. For bacteria the child tells from a drawing that one bacterium grows and divides into two.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 31 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into organisms; 31 A sits under core goal 31. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names four groups: bacteria, fungi, plants and animals. It names no species and does not say how many life cycles a pupil describes; it says "the life cycle" in the singular for all four groups. It names no stage of a life cycle and no quantity. The word *mensen* (humans) is not printed in this item, and the wording does not say whether *dieren* (animals) leaves humans out; human reproduction and growing up are named in item 31 A c. Viruses are not named in this item.

### Common mistakes

A child draws a life as a line that ends with death and leaves out that the organism produces young, so the cycle does not close. A child takes the caterpillar and the butterfly, or the tadpole and the frog, for two different animals. Many children do not think of bacteria and moulds as living things that grow and multiply, and many think a mushroom is a plant that grows from a seed; a mushroom is part of a fungus and grows from spores.
