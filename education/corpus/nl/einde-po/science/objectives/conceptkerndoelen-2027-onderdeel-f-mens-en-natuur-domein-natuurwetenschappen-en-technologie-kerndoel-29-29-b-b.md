## Official wording

redeneren met structuur, vorm, eigenschap en functie;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning with structure, form, property and function;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 pulls a piece of corrugated cardboard apart, sees the wavy layer between two flat sheets, and says that this structure makes the cardboard stiff and light, which fits its use for boxes. The child leaves a metal spoon and a wooden spoon in a cup of warm tea and feels that the handle of the metal one becomes warm: metal passes heat on well and wood does so poorly. The child uses this to say why a spoon for stirring in a pan is often made of wood.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 B, which says that the pupil uses scientific and technological ways of thinking in discovering and explaining the world; 29 B sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names four terms to reason with and no subject matter; it does not say whether living things, substances or made objects are meant.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up a property of the material with a property of the object: "steel sinks, so a steel ship cannot float", while the hollow shape makes the ship as a whole, with the air inside it, lighter than the same volume of water. A child explains a form in a living thing by what the animal or plant wants ("the giraffe grew a long neck to reach the leaves"). A child names what a thing is for and does not link it to its structure or its material.
