## Official wording

beschrijven van de kenmerken en spreiding van klimaten op aarde;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 14.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing the characteristics and distribution of climates on earth;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 colours the climate zones on a world map and sees that they lie roughly in bands from the equator to the poles. The child reads two climate graphs, each with the temperature and the precipitation for every month, one for the Netherlands and one for a place near the equator. The child describes each climate: winters that are not very cold, summers that are not very hot and rain in every season in the first, and warm all year in the second.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into the water cycle, weather and climate and explores these in the pupil's own living environment; 32 B sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names two things to describe: the characteristics of climates and their distribution on earth. It names no climate, no number of climates and no system for classifying them, and no quantity or unit. Weather is in item 32 B c and climate change in item 32 B e.

### Common mistakes

Many children think it is hot near the equator because that part of the earth is closer to the sun; the cause is that sunlight strikes the ground more steeply there, so the same light falls on a smaller area. A child gives one climate to a whole continent, for example "Africa is hot and dry". A child thinks every desert is hot and sandy; a desert is a dry area, and a desert can be cold.
