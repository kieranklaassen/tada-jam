## Official wording

De leerling gebruikt natuurwetenschappelijke en technologische denkwijzen bij het ontdekken en verklaren van de wereld.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil uses scientific (*natuurwetenschappelijke*, of the natural sciences) and technological ways of thinking in discovering and explaining the world.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 sees that the puddles on the playground are gone sooner on a sunny, windy day than on a cloudy, still day. The child names the pattern, gives a cause (water evaporates faster when it is warm and when the air moves) and draws the puddle with arrows for the water that goes into the air. With a spanner or the beak of a bird the child says how the shape fits what it is used for.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 29, which is about exploring the world from a scientific and technological perspective. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. Its items 29 B a to 29 B e name five kinds of reasoning: with patterns and cause and effect; with structure, form, property and function; with scale, ratios and quantities; with systems and models; and with cycles. The sentence names no subject matter. Goal sentence 29 C is about ways of working.

### Common mistakes

A child explains by a purpose or a wish and not by a cause: "the puddle wants to go away". A child treats two things that happen together as cause and effect without checking.
