## Official wording

ordenen van stoffen gebaseerd op verschillen in eigenschappen;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

ordering (*ordenen*, read as putting in order or as sorting into groups) substances (*stoffen*; *stof* is read as substance or as material) based on differences in properties;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 gets a tray with samples and sorts them into groups by one property: solid or liquid at room temperature, dissolves in water or does not, attracted by a magnet or not. The child puts water, cooking oil and syrup in a row from the one that flows fastest to the one that flows slowest, by letting a spoonful of each run down a tilted plate. The child says which property it used each time.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 30 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into and experiments with substances and the changes they can undergo; 30 B sits under core goal 30. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names no property, no substance and no group. *Ordenen* covers putting things in a sequence and sorting them into groups; the item does not choose. Items 30 B a and 30 B b are about naming properties. The item states no safety condition.

### Common mistakes

A child sorts by a property of the object, such as size or shape, and not of the substance. A child changes the property it sorts by halfway through. A child puts all white powders or all clear liquids together as the same substance.
