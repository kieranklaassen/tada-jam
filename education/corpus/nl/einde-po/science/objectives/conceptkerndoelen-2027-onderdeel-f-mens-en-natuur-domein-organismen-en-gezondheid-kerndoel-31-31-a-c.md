## Official wording

beschrijven van verschillen en overeenkomsten tussen lichamen; verloop van de menselijke voortplanting; de menstruatiecyclus en de lichamelijke en mentale veranderingen bij de mens: bij het opgroeien, in de puberteit en de invloed van hormonen hierop;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing differences and similarities between bodies; the course of human reproduction; the menstrual cycle and the physical and mental changes in humans: while growing up, in puberty and the influence of hormones on these;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says in what ways bodies are alike and in what ways they differ. The child tells in order how human reproduction proceeds, says what the menstrual cycle is, and names changes in the body and in the mind that come with growing up and with puberty. The child says that hormones have an influence on these changes.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 31 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into organisms; 31 A sits under core goal 31. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item prints three parts separated by semicolons, all under "describing": differences and similarities between bodies; the course of human reproduction; and the menstrual cycle together with the physical and mental changes in humans. The text names no parts, acts or situations beyond those it prints and sets no level of detail. The first part says only "bodies" and does not say whose bodies or which differences are meant. After the colon the item names growing up, puberty, and the influence of hormones; the third is not a period of life like the first two, and the source prints the list that way. The item names no age and no quantity.

### Common mistakes

Many children think the changes of puberty start at the same age for everyone; the age differs from child to child. A child describes the changes in the body and leaves out the mental changes, which the item also names. A child names the differences between bodies and leaves out the similarities.
