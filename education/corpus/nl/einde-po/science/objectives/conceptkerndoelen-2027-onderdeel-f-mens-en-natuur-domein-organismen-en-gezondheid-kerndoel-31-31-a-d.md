## Official wording

beschrijven van de verzorging van planten en dieren;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing the care of plants and animals;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks after a plant and tells what the care consists of: feeling whether the soil is dry before watering, putting the pot where there is light, and repotting when the roots fill the pot. For an animal such as a rabbit the child tells what it needs each day: food that suits the animal, fresh water, a clean and roomy place, and rest. The child compares two plants or two animals and says where the care differs.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 31 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into organisms; 31 A sits under core goal 31. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item asks for describing the care; it does not say that the pupil carries the care out. It names no plant and no animal, and does not say whether pets, farm animals or wild animals are meant. It names no quantity, such as how much water or food or how often.

### Common mistakes

Many children think more water is always better for a plant; roots that stand in water for a long time rot. A child gives every plant or every animal the same care, where a cactus needs far less water than a fern and a rabbit needs other food than a cat. A child describes care as feeding only and leaves out cleaning, space, light and rest.
