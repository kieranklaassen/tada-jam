## Official wording

verklaren hoe de beweging en positie van hemellichamen leiden tot dag-nacht, seizoenen, jaren en getijden;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

explaining how the movement and position of celestial bodies lead to day and night, seasons, years and tides;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a darkened room a child of 11 or 12 holds a globe on its tilted axis beside a lamp and turns it once to show a day and a night. The child carries the globe once around the lamp, with the axis pointing the same way all the time, to show a year, and shows that the northern half is tilted towards the lamp at one point of the round and away from it half a round later. The child reads a tide table for a place on the Dutch coast, finds about two high waters in a day, and says that the pull of the moon on the sea water is the main cause.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into and explores changes to the earth's surface through natural phenomena and human action; 32 A sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item is about celestial bodies and not about the earth's surface; the draft prints it under 32 A. It names four results: day and night, seasons, years and tides. It does not name the celestial bodies: the words sun, earth and moon are not in it. The phases of the moon and eclipses are not among the four results. The item names no quantity: no length of a day or a year and no angle.

### Common mistakes

Many children think it is summer when the earth is closer to the sun; the seasons come from the tilt of the earth's axis, and the earth is closest to the sun in early January. A child explains day and night by the sun moving around the earth, or by the sun going behind clouds or behind the moon; the cause is that the earth turns on its axis. A child takes tides for waves made by the wind.
