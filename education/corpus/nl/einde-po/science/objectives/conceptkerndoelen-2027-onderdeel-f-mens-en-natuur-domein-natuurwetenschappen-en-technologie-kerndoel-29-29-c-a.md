## Official wording

oriënteren op verschillende manieren om tot oplossingen en antwoorden te komen;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting an orientation on different ways to arrive at solutions and answers;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 gets a set of questions on cards and says for each which way of working fits. Which birds visit the playground calls for observing and counting over some weeks; which kitchen paper takes up the most water calls for a fair test; how deep the North Sea is calls for a source or an expert; a stand that keeps a book open calls for designing, making and testing. The child gives a reason for each choice.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 C, which says that the pupil uses scientific and technological ways of working in discovering and understanding the world; 29 C sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is getting an orientation on (*oriënteren op*); the item sets no level of mastery. It names no ways of working. Item 29 C b names steps of investigating and designing.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks that an experiment is the only way in science, or uses the same way for every question. A child goes straight to looking the answer up where its own observation would answer the question. A child does not see the difference between a question that asks how something is and a problem that asks for something to be made.
