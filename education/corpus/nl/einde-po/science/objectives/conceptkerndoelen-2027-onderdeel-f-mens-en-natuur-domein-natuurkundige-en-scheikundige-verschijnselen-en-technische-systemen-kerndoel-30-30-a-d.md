## Official wording

experimenteren met overbrengingsprincipes van beweging en energie, verbindingsprincipes, constructies en profielen;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experimenting with principles of transmission (*overbrengingsprincipes*, ways of passing on) of movement and energy, principles of joining (*verbindingsprincipes*), constructions and profiles (*profielen*, the cross-section shapes of beams and strips);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 builds two gear wheels of different size with a construction set and sees that the small wheel turns faster than the large one and in the opposite direction. The child joins cardboard strips with glue, with a split pin and with a slot, and feels which joint can turn and which is fixed. The child makes a square frame of four strips and four pins, pushes it out of shape, and adds a diagonal strip that stops this. The child lays a flat strip of paper across a gap, then folds the same strip into a U shape, and compares how much each carries.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 30 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into and experiments with objects and technical systems from the living environment; 30 A sits under core goal 30. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names four subjects to experiment with. It names no particular mechanism, no particular kind of joint, no particular construction and no particular profile shape; gear wheels, a split pin, a square frame and a folded strip are the pack's examples. It names no materials or tools and states no safety condition.

### Common mistakes

A child expects two gear wheels that mesh to turn in the same direction. A child thinks that a large gear wheel driven by a small one turns faster; it turns more slowly. A child thinks that more material always makes a construction stronger, and does not expect that a folded strip carries more than a flat strip of the same paper.
