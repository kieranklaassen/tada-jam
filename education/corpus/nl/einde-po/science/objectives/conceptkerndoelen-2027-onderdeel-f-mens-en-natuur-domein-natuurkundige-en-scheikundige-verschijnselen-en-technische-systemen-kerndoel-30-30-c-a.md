## Official wording

begrip tonen van kenmerken van licht en geluid: bron, richting, voortplanting en sterkte;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

showing understanding of characteristics of light and sound: source, direction, propagation (*voortplanting*, how it travels) and strength;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 sorts things into those that give light themselves (the sun, a lamp, a flame) and those that only reflect it (the moon, a mirror, a white wall). The child lines up three cards with a hole each and sees the light of a torch pass only when the holes are in one straight line. The child feels a plucked elastic band vibrate, hears a tap on the table more clearly with an ear on the table top, and says that sound needs something to travel through. The child says that both light and sound get weaker farther from the source.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 30 C, which says that the pupil shows insight into and experiments with light, sound, energy and forces; 30 C sits under core goal 30. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. After the colon the item names four characteristics for light and sound together: source, direction, propagation and strength. Colour and pitch are not named. It states no unit, no instrument and no speed. The verb of this item is showing understanding; experimenting is named in goal sentence 30 C.

### Common mistakes

A child counts the moon or a mirror as a source of light. Many children think that seeing goes from the eye to the object. A child mixes up loud and high, or thinks that sound travels through air only and not through water, wood or metal.
