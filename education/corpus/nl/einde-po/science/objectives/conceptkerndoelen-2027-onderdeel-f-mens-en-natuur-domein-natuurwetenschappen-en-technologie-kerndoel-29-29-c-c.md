## Official wording

gebruiken van instrumenten, gereedschappen en materialen op doelmatige, veilige en duurzame wijze;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using instruments, tools and materials in an efficient (*doelmatig*, fitting the purpose), safe and sustainable (*duurzaam*, a word that also means durable) way;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 chooses the instrument that fits: a ruler for the length of a leaf and a tape measure for the length of the classroom. The child reads a thermometer at eye level and puts a measuring jug on a flat table before reading it. When cutting cardboard the child keeps the holding hand out of the path of the scissors, takes no more tape and paper than the work needs, and puts usable leftovers back.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 C, which says that the pupil uses scientific and technological ways of working in discovering and understanding the world; 29 C sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names no instruments, tools or materials. It says that the use is safe, and it sets no safety rule: no protective equipment, no supervision and no list of permitted tools are stated. *Duurzaam* can be read as sustainable (sparing with materials and the environment) and as durable (so that things last); the item does not choose.

### Common mistakes

A child reads a scale from an angle, or measures from the end of the ruler and not from the zero mark. A child picks an instrument that is too coarse for the job, such as a tape measure in whole centimetres for a seed. A child takes much more material than is needed and throws usable leftovers away.
