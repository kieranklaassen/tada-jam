## Official wording

redeneren met kringlopen.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning with cycles (*kringlopen*, courses that return to where they began).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 follows water around: it evaporates from the sea and from puddles, the water vapour rises and cools, it condenses into the droplets of a cloud, it falls as rain, and it flows through the ground and the rivers back to the sea. The child draws this as a closed loop with arrows and names what happens at each arrow. With fallen leaves the child reasons in the same way: small animals, fungi and bacteria break the leaves down, the nutrients go into the soil, and the tree takes them up again through its roots.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 B, which says that the pupil uses scientific and technological ways of thinking in discovering and explaining the world; 29 B sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names no cycle. *Kringloop* is used in Dutch for cycles in nature, such as that of water, and for the reuse of materials and goods; the item does not say which is meant.

### Common mistakes

Many children think that water that evaporates is gone. Many children think that a cloud is water vapour or smoke; a cloud is made of very small drops of liquid water or of ice crystals, and water vapour cannot be seen. A child draws a line with a beginning and an end and not a closed loop.
