## Official wording

interpreteren van kwalitatieve en kwantitatieve metingen en waarnemingen;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

interpreting qualitative and quantitative measurements and observations;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has a table with the temperature of the water in two cups, one of them wrapped in a woollen cloth, read every five minutes. The child draws a line graph, says which cup cooled faster and by how many degrees (quantitative), and adds what was noticed without numbers, such as which cup still felt warm to the hand at the end (qualitative). The child says what the results mean together and marks a reading that does not fit the others.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 C, which says that the pupil uses scientific and technological ways of working in discovering and understanding the world; 29 C sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names two kinds, qualitative (in words) and quantitative (in numbers). The Dutch can be read with both kinds belonging to the measurements and the observations, or to the measurements only; the gloss keeps the Dutch order. It states no unit, no instrument, no kind of table or graph and no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the numbers out and does not say what they mean. A child leaves out a reading that does not fit what it expected, or builds a conclusion on one reading. A child mixes an observation with an explanation that is wrong: "the wool gives off heat", while the wool only slows down the loss of heat from the water.
