## Official wording

De leerling toont inzicht in en experimenteert met licht, geluid, energie en krachten.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil shows insight into and experiments with light, sound, energy and forces (*krachten*; *kracht* is read as force or as strength or power).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 makes a shadow with a torch and a hand and changes its size by moving the hand. The child plucks an elastic band stretched over an open box and hears a higher note when the band is pulled tighter and a louder one when it is plucked harder. The child lets a toy car roll down a ramp from a higher and from a lower starting point and compares how far it rolls. The child feels two magnets pull towards each other and push each other away.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 30, which is about insight into, and experimenting with, natural phenomena and technical systems. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. Its items 30 C a to 30 C e name characteristics of light and sound, kinds of energy source and ways of storing and transporting energy, forces and change in movement, dangers around electricity, sound, thunderstorms and sunlight, and the force of magnetism, air and liquid. The sentence does not name electricity or magnetism; they come in through items 30 C d and 30 C e. It names no unit, no instrument and no number, and it states no safety condition for the experimenting. The lane's frame notes that this sentence is worded differently in the curriculum institute's open data; the record follows the draft decree.

### Common mistakes

Many children think that we see because something goes from the eye to the object; light goes from a source to the object and from there into the eye. Many children think that a moving thing stops by itself when the push is used up; it stops because friction slows it down. A child uses "energy" and "force" as the same word.
