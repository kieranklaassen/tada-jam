## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 measures the shadow of a stick in the playground every hour on a sunny day and writes the lengths in a table. The child sees the pattern: the shadow is shortest around the middle of the day and longer in the morning and in the late afternoon. The child gives the cause (the sun stands highest in the sky around the middle of the day, and a higher sun gives a shorter shadow) and predicts the lengths for the next sunny day.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 B, which says that the pupil uses scientific and technological ways of thinking in discovering and explaining the world; 29 B sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names no subject matter, no kind of pattern and no number of observations.

### Common mistakes

A child treats two things that happen together as cause and effect: more ice creams are sold on days when more people get sunburnt, so the one is said to cause the other, while both come from sunny weather. A child sees a pattern after two or three observations and does not check further. A child names one cause where several things changed at once.
