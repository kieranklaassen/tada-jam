## Official wording

De leerling toont inzicht in en experimenteert met stoffen en veranderingen die stoffen kunnen ondergaan.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil shows insight into and experiments with substances (*stoffen*; *stof* is read as substance or as material) and changes that substances can undergo.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 stirs salt, sugar and sand into three glasses of water and sees which dissolve. The child lets an ice cube melt and puts the water back in the freezer: the same substance returns as ice. The child adds vinegar to baking soda, sees bubbles of gas form, and says that a substance has formed that was not there before.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 30, which is about insight into, and experimenting with, natural phenomena and technical systems. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. Its items 30 B a to 30 B e name observable properties of a substance at a particular temperature, common substances with their distinguishing properties and dangers, ordering substances by their properties, physical changes, and chemical changes that can be observed through a change in properties. The sentence names no substance and no change. Particles such as atoms and molecules are not mentioned in the sentence or in its items. It states no safety condition; item 30 B b is about naming dangers of substances.

### Common mistakes

Many children think that a substance that dissolves has disappeared; the salt is still in the water and comes back when the water evaporates. A child says that sugar "melts" in tea, and so mixes up melting and dissolving. Many children do not count air and other gases as substances and think that they weigh nothing.
