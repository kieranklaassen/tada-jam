## Official wording

De leerlingen leren oplossingen voor technische problemen te ontwerpen, deze uit te voeren en te evalueren.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO, bijlage, kerndoel 45.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to design solutions for technical problems, to carry these out and to evaluate them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 gets a problem, such as a bridge of paper and tape that must carry a toy car across the gap between two tables. The child writes down what the bridge must be able to do, sketches two ideas and chooses one. The child builds it, tests it with the car, notes where it sags, changes the design and tests again. At the end the child says whether the problem is solved and what could still be better.

### Limits

This 2006 goal is still in force. It is the sixth of seven goals under the heading *Natuur en techniek* (nature and *techniek*, a word read as technology or as engineering). The goal names three steps: designing a solution, carrying it out and evaluating it. It names no particular technical problem, no materials and no tools, and it states no safety condition. It does not say how often the steps are repeated, or whether the pupil works alone or with others.

### Common mistakes

A child starts building at once, without a sketch and without saying what the solution must be able to do. A child judges the result by how it looks and does not test it against the problem. A child sees a failed test as the end of the work and not as information for the next version.
