## Official wording

verkennen waarom wetenschappers en technologen niet alles weten.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring why scientists and technologists do not know everything.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 collects questions to which science has no answer yet, such as whether there is life on another planet. The child measures the same thing five times, gets slightly different results, and says that no measurement is exact. The child hears how a new instrument, such as the telescope or the microscope, showed things that nobody could see before, and that knowledge changed after that.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 D, which says that the pupil explores the nature of the natural sciences and technology; 29 D sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is exploring (*verkennen*). The item gives no reasons itself and names no examples.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks that scientists know everything, or that what is in a book is final. A child concludes the opposite: because scientists do not know everything, any idea is as good as another. A child sees a changed scientific idea as proof that scientists were lying, and not as the result of new observations.
