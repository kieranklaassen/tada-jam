## Official wording

analyseren van het weer aan de hand van temperatuur, luchtdruk, bewolking, neerslag, luchtvochtigheid en wind;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 14.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

analysing the weather by means of temperature, air pressure, cloud cover, precipitation, humidity and wind;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a thermometer, a barometer, a rain gauge and a hygrometer each day, looks at how much of the sky is covered by cloud, and notes the direction and strength of the wind. After two weeks the child lays the days side by side in a table and looks for what goes together, for example that days on which the air pressure fell were often followed by cloud and rain.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into the water cycle, weather and climate and explores these in the pupil's own living environment; 32 B sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is analysing (*analyseren*). The item names six elements: temperature, air pressure, cloud cover, precipitation, humidity and wind. It names no unit and no instrument, and it does not say whether the pupil measures or works with given data. It names no place and no period of time; goal sentence 32 B adds exploring in the pupil's own living environment.

### Common mistakes

A child reads a wind direction as where the wind blows to; a west wind comes from the west. A child takes high humidity to mean that it rains; humidity is about the water vapour in the air, which cannot be seen. A child lists the six readings one by one and does not connect them.
