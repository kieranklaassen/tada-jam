## Official wording

beschrijven van kenmerken van landschappen in Nederland, Europa en de wereld: reliëf, vulkanen, rivieren, kusten en vegetatie;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing features of landscapes in the Netherlands, Europe and the world: relief, volcanoes, rivers, coasts and vegetation;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With an atlas a child of 11 or 12 follows the Rhine from the Alps to the North Sea and describes the land along it: high mountains at the start, low flat land at the end. The child describes the Dutch coast with its dunes, points to Etna on Sicily as a volcano, and compares the vegetation in photos of a tropical rainforest, a desert and the tundra.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into and explores changes to the earth's surface through natural phenomena and human action; 32 A sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names three scales: the Netherlands, Europe and the world. After the colon it names five features: relief, volcanoes, rivers, coasts and vegetation; it does not say whether the list is complete. It names no landscape, country, river or volcano, and no number or quantity. The verb is describing; how landscapes came into being is in the items 32 A d and 32 A e.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the green on a relief map as forest or grass; on such a map green stands for low land. A child says that the whole of the Netherlands lies below sea level; only part of it does. A child thinks every mountain is a volcano.
