## Official wording

verwoorden wat het vraagstuk en mogelijke oplossingen met je doen;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting into words what the issue (*vraagstuk*, a question or problem to work on) and possible solutions do to you;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who has worked on an issue, such as hedgehogs that are run over on a road near the school, says or writes what this does to them: "I find it sad", "it makes me angry that cars drive so fast there". For a possible solution the child does the same: "a tunnel under the road reassures me", "a lower speed limit makes my ride to football longer, and I do not mind". The child gives a reason with each response.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 A, which says that the pupil explores the living environment and investigates issues from it; 29 A sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item asks the pupil to put a personal response into words; it does not ask for a correct answer or for a choice between solutions. "Met je doen" (do to you) can cover feelings and practical consequences for the pupil; the item does not say which. It names no form, spoken or written.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often give a verdict on the solution ("that is a good idea") and leave out what it does to them. Many children repeat what a classmate or an adult said, or use only words such as "fun" and "boring".
