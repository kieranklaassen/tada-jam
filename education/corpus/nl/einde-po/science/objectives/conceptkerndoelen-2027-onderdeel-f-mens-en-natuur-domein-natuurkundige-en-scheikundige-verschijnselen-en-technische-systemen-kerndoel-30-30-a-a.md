## Official wording

communicatie-, meet-, regel-, productie- en transportsystemen;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

communication, measuring, control (*regel-*, regulating), production and transport systems;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks for an example of each kind in its own surroundings: a telephone (communication), a kitchen scale or a thermometer (measuring), a thermostat that switches the heating on when the room is colder than the set temperature (control), a bakery where dough goes in and bread comes out (production), and a bus line or a conveyor belt (transport). The child sorts picture cards under the five names. For one example the child says what goes in, what happens and what comes out.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 30 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into and experiments with objects and technical systems from the living environment; 30 A sits under core goal 30. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item has no verb of its own: it lists five kinds of the "technical systems" that goal sentence 30 A and the sentence of core goal 30 name. It gives no example of any kind and does not say that the five are the only kinds.

### Common mistakes

A child puts each device under one kind only, while many belong to more than one: a thermostat measures and controls. A child mixes up measuring and controlling: a thermometer only shows the temperature, and a thermostat also acts on it. A child thinks that only electric or digital things are systems.
