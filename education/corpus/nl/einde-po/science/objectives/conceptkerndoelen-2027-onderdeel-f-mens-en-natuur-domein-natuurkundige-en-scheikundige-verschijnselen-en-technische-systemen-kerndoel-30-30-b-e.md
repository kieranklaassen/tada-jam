## Official wording

experimenteren met scheikundige veranderingen van stoffen, waarneembaar door een verandering in stofeigenschappen.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experimenting with chemical (*scheikundige*, of chemistry) changes of substances (*stoffen*; *stof* is read as substance or as material), observable through a change in the properties of the substance (*stofeigenschappen*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 adds vinegar to baking soda in a cup and sees it fizz: a gas forms that was not there before. The child leaves one wad of steel wool wet and one dry for some days and compares them: the brown, crumbly rust on the wet one is a different substance from the grey steel. The child names which properties changed, such as the colour or the forming of a gas, and says that a new substance has formed.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 30 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into and experiments with substances and the changes they can undergo; 30 B sits under core goal 30. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item ties a chemical change to what can be observed: a change in the properties of the substance. It names no particular chemical change and no particular substance, and it names no particles or formulas; the fizzing of vinegar with baking soda and the rusting of steel wool are the pack's examples. It states no safety condition, also not for fire or heating. Physical changes are in item 30 B d.

### Common mistakes

A child calls every visible change a chemical one, such as ice that melts or sugar that dissolves. Many children think that a substance that burns disappears; new substances form, most of them gases that go into the air. A child sees rust as dirt on the iron and not as a new substance that forms from iron with oxygen and water.
