## Official wording

De leerlingen leren bij producten uit hun eigen omgeving relaties te leggen tussen de werking, de vorm en het materiaalgebruik.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO, bijlage, kerndoel 44.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn, for products from their own surroundings, to make connections between how they work (*werking*), their form and the use of materials.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes a wooden clothes peg apart and lays out the two halves and the steel spring. The child says that the spring presses the two ends together, that the long halves work as levers when they are squeezed, and that steel is used for the spring because it springs back after bending. With a cooking pan the child says why the base is metal and the handle is plastic: metal passes heat on well and plastic does so poorly.

### Limits

This 2006 goal is still in force. It is the fifth of seven goals under the heading *Natuur en techniek* (nature and *techniek*, a word read as technology or as engineering). The products come from the pupils' own surroundings; the goal names none. It names three things to connect: how a product works, its form and the materials used. Designing and making are in goal 45 of this lane.

### Common mistakes

A child says what a product is for and does not link this to its shape or its material. A child gives the same reason for every material ("because it is strong") and does not name the property that matters, such as bending without breaking, being waterproof, or passing heat on well or poorly. A child reads form as decoration only: colour and looks.
