## Official wording

verklaren van de ontstaanswijze van berg-, kust- en rivierlandschappen door natuurlijke processen en menselijk handelen.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

explaining how mountain, coastal and river landscapes came into being through natural processes and human action.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a tray of sand a child of 11 or 12 lets a thin stream of water run and sees it wear away the outside of a bend, lay sand down on the inside, and build a small delta where it runs into still water. The child then builds a dike of clay along the stream and tells what changes. With a photo of a Dutch river the child points to what the river made and to what people made, such as the dikes.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into and explores changes to the earth's surface through natural phenomena and human action; 32 A sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names three kinds of landscape: mountain, coastal and river landscapes. It names natural processes and human action together as the causes, and it names no single process (the words erosion and sedimentation are not in it) and no single human action. It names no place and no quantity. Processes in the earth are in item 32 A d. It is the last item of 32 A.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks a landscape was made at one moment and has not changed since. A child takes a landscape shaped by people, such as a polder or a straightened river with dikes, for a natural one. A child thinks water only takes material away, and leaves out that rivers and the sea also lay sand and clay down and so build land.
