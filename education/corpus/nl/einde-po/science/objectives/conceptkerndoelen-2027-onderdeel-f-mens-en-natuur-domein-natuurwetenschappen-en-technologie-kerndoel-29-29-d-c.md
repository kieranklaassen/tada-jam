## Official wording

afwegen of alles mag wat kan: maakbaarheid en ethische grenzen;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

weighing whether everything that can be done may be done: makeability (*maakbaarheid*, the idea that things can be made or changed at will) and ethical limits;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes a case, such as a drone with a camera that can film every garden in the street, or dogs bred with so flat a nose that they breathe with difficulty. The child lists what the technology makes possible, who gains from it and who or what is harmed. The child says where it would set the limit and why, and listens to classmates who set it elsewhere.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 D, which says that the pupil explores the nature of the natural sciences and technology; 29 D sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names no cases and no technologies, and it sets no level of detail. It asks for weighing; it gives no answer to the question and asks for no particular conclusion. After the colon it names two terms, makeability and ethical limits, without explaining them.

### Common mistakes

A child answers with yes or no and gives no reasons. A child mixes up "can it be done" with "may it be done", and treats what is possible, or what the law allows, as right. A child judges a technology as wholly good or wholly bad and does not look at separate uses.
