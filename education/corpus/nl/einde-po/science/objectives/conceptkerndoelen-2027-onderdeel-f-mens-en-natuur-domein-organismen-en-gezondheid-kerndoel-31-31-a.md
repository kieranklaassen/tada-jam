## Official wording

De leerling toont inzicht in organismen.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil shows insight into organisms.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 sorts cards of living things into bacteria, fungi, plants and animals, and puts humans with the animals. The child tells how a bean plant or a frog begins, grows and produces young, and points to a part of a plant or of the body and says what it does.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 31, which is about organisms and their health. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. Its items 31 A a to 31 A e name the life cycle of bacteria, fungi, plants and animals; the structure, form and function of plants and humans; bodies, human reproduction and the changes of growing up and puberty; the care of plants and animals; and roles of organisms in food production. The sentence itself names no organism and no quantity. Lifestyle, health and illness are the subject of goal sentence 31 B, which the pack records under practical life and feelings.

### Common mistakes

Many children do not count humans among the animals, and many do not count plants as living because plants do not move from their place. Many children take fungi for plants; a fungus does not make its own food with light, as a green plant does. Many children think bacteria are only things that make people ill.
