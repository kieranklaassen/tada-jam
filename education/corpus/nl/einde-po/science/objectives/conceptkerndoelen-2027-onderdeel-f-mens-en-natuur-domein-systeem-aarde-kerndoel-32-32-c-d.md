## Official wording

beredeneren hoe biotische en abiotische onderdelen van een ecosysteem met elkaar samenhangen;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 14.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning out how biotic and abiotic parts of an ecosystem are connected with one another;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 compares a shaded and a sunny spot of the same wood or field: the child measures the light, the temperature and the damp of the soil, and counts the kinds of plants. The child reasons in both directions: under the trees little light reaches the ground, so fewer plants grow there, and the trees themselves make the shade and keep the soil damp. The child sorts cards into living parts (plants, animals, fungi, bacteria) and non-living parts (light, water, air, temperature, kind of soil) and lays a string between two cards for each connection the child can explain.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 C, which says that the pupil explores different ecosystems and explains interactions within the system; 32 C sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is reasoning out (*beredeneren*). The item uses the terms biotic (the living parts) and abiotic (the non-living parts) and names no part of either kind. It says "an ecosystem" and names none. The item names no quantity.

### Common mistakes

A child names the living and the non-living parts and does not say how one acts on another. A child reasons in one direction only, from the non-living to the living (light makes plants grow), and leaves out that living things change the non-living parts, as trees make shade and earthworms loosen the soil. A child swaps the two terms.
