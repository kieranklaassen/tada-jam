## Official wording

oriënteren op beroepsbeelden en rolmodellen vanuit eigen interesses, ambities en capaciteiten.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting an orientation on images of occupations (*beroepsbeelden*, pictures of what a job involves) and role models, from one's own interests, ambitions and capacities.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 asks someone who works in science or technology, such as a fitter of solar panels, a laboratory worker or a forester, what a working day looks like and what they like about it. The child lists what it likes doing, what it would like to become able to do and what it is good at, and compares the list with what it heard. The child says which parts of the work would fit and which would not.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 A, which says that the pupil explores the living environment and investigates issues from it; 29 A sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is getting an orientation on (*oriënteren op*); the item does not ask for a choice of occupation. It names no occupation and no field of work of its own; it sits under a core goal about science and technology. The starting point is the pupil's own interests, ambitions and capacities.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often know only the occupations they have seen, and leave out the many jobs in science and technology that they have never met. Many children think that such work is for one kind of person, for example for boys or for the cleverest children. A child treats what it likes and what it is good at as the same thing.
