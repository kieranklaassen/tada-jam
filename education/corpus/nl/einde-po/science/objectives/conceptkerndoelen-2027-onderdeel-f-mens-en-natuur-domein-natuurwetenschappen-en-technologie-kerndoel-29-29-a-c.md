## Official wording

onderscheiden van verschillende perspectieven op en mogelijke oplossingen van een vraagstuk;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing different perspectives on, and possible solutions of, an issue (*vraagstuk*, a question or problem to work on);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes an issue, such as a plan to replace a hedge at school with bicycle racks, and lists who and what is affected: the children who cycle, the neighbours, the caretaker, the birds that nest in the hedge. The child writes down what matters to each. The child lays two or three possible solutions next to each other and says for each one whom it helps and whom it does not.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 29 A, which says that the pupil explores the living environment and investigates issues from it; 29 A sits under core goal 29. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item asks for distinguishing; choosing or defending one solution is not asked. It does not say how many perspectives or solutions, and it names no issue.

### Common mistakes

A child sees only its own view and calls the other views wrong. A child expects exactly one right solution. A child mixes up a perspective (what matters to someone) with a solution (what could be done).
