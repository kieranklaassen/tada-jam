## Official wording

verbanden leggen tussen klimaatverandering en natuurlijke processen en menselijk handelen.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 14.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making connections between climate change and natural processes and human action.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 draws a diagram with climate change in the middle and lines to it from two sides. On one side the child sets natural processes, such as a large volcanic eruption that cools the earth for a year or more, and the ice ages of the past. On the other side the child sets human action, such as burning coal, oil and gas, which adds carbon dioxide to the air and warms the earth. The child says in a sentence what each line means.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into the water cycle, weather and climate and explores these in the pupil's own living environment; 32 B sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is making connections (*verbanden leggen*); the item does not say in which direction: causes of climate change, its effects, or both. It names no single process, gas, human action or consequence, and it gives no weight to natural processes against human action. It names no quantity. It is the last item of 32 B.

### Common mistakes

Many children think the hole in the ozone layer causes the warming of the earth; these are two different matters. A child takes one cold winter as proof that the climate is not warming, which mixes up weather and climate. Many children think the greenhouse effect is made only by people; there is a natural greenhouse effect, without which the earth would be much colder, and human action makes the effect stronger.
