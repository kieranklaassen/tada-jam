## Official wording

beschrijven hoe de mens afhankelijk is van ecosystemen en wat de invloed van de mens is op de biodiversiteit.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 14.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing how humans depend on ecosystems and what the influence of humans on biodiversity is.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lists what people get from ecosystems, with an example of each: fish from the sea, wood from a forest, the pollination of fruit trees by insects. The child describes with examples how people change the number of species in a place: a meadow that is mown many times a year has few kinds of flowers, and a verge sown with wild flowers is visited by more kinds of insects.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 C, which says that the pupil explores different ecosystems and explains interactions within the system; 32 C sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item has two parts: how humans depend on ecosystems, and the influence of humans on biodiversity. It says "the influence" and does not say whether the influence is harmful or helpful. It names no ecosystem, no species and no example, and it does not say what biodiversity covers. It names no quantity. It is the last item of 32 C.

### Common mistakes

Many children see people as standing outside nature: food comes from the shop and water from the tap. A child takes biodiversity to mean the number of animals, where it means the variety of living things: how many different species there are, of plants, fungi and small animals as well as large ones. A child describes the influence of people only as harm to well-known large animals, and leaves out insects and plants and the ways in which people help species.
