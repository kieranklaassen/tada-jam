## Official wording

verkennen hoe een voorwerp werkt, geconstrueerd is en welke materialen zijn gebruikt;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring how an object works, how it is constructed and which materials have been used;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes a bicycle pump or a stapler apart as far as it comes apart without tools and lays the parts out in order. The child draws how the parts are joined (screwed, clicked, glued or riveted) and names the materials: a steel spring, a plastic handle, a rubber ring. The child moves the parts slowly to see how the object works: in the pump the piston pushes the air out through the opening when the handle is pressed in.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 30 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into and experiments with objects and technical systems from the living environment; 30 A sits under core goal 30. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names three things to explore about one object: how it works, how it is constructed and which materials were used. It names no object. Taking an object apart is not named, and the item states no safety condition.

### Common mistakes

A child looks at the outside only and does not look at how the parts are joined. A child calls everything "metal" or "plastic" and does not tell materials apart, or names a part where a material is asked. A child guesses how the object works from its looks and does not try it.
