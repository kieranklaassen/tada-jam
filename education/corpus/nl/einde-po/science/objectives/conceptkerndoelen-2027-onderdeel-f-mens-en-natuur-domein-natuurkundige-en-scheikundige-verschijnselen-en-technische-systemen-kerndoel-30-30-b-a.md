## Official wording

benoemen van waarneembare eigenschappen van een stof bij een bepaalde temperatuur;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming observable properties of a substance (*stof*, read as substance or as material) at a particular temperature;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at and feels substances at room temperature and says what it observes: water is a colourless liquid, cooking oil is a yellow liquid that flows more slowly than water, an iron nail is solid, grey and hard, candle wax is solid and soft enough to scratch with a fingernail. The child compares butter straight from the refrigerator with butter that has stood in the room: hard at the one temperature, soft at the other. The child says the temperature with the property: "at room temperature", "from the freezer".

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 30 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into and experiments with substances and the changes they can undergo; 30 B sits under core goal 30. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item ties the properties to "a particular temperature"; it names no temperature, no unit and no thermometer. It names no property and no substance. The properties are observable ones; the item does not say with which senses or instruments. It states no safety condition.

### Common mistakes

A child names a property of the object and not of the substance: large, round, heavy. A child treats the state as fixed ("water is a liquid", "iron is solid") and leaves out the temperature. A child gives a use where a property is asked ("it is for drinking").
