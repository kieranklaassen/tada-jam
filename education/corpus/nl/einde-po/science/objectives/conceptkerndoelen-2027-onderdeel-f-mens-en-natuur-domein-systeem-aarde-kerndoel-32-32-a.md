## Official wording

De leerling toont inzicht in en verkent veranderingen aan het aardoppervlak door natuurverschijnselen en menselijk handelen.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil shows insight into and explores changes to the earth's surface through natural phenomena and human action.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 pours water over a heap of sand in a tray and sees the water cut a channel and lay the sand down further on. The child compares an old and a new map of the Netherlands and finds land that was water on the old map, such as the polders of Flevoland. The child says which changes people made and which the sea, the rivers and the wind made.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 32, which is about the system earth. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. Its items 32 A a to 32 A e name how planets in our solar system meet conditions for life; how day and night, seasons, years and tides come about; features of landscapes; processes inside the earth; and how mountain, coastal and river landscapes came into being. The first two items are about planets and other celestial bodies and not about the earth's surface; the draft prints them under this sentence. The sentence names both natural phenomena and human action, and names no landscape, place or quantity.

### Common mistakes

Many children think the landscape has always looked as it does now. A child counts only fast events such as an eruption or a flood as change and leaves out slow change, such as a river that cuts its valley deeper over a very long time. A child takes land made by people, such as a polder, for natural land.
