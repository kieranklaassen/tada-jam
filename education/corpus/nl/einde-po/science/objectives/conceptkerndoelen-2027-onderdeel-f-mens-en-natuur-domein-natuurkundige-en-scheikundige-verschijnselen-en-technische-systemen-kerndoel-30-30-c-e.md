## Official wording

experimenteren met de kracht van magnetisme, lucht en vloeistof.

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experimenting with the force (*kracht*, read as force or as strength or power) of magnetism, air and liquid.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 brings two magnets together and feels that two like poles push each other away and two unlike poles pull towards each other, and tests whether a magnet still attracts a paper clip through a sheet of cardboard. The child joins two plastic syringes without needles with a tube, first filled with air and then with water, pushes one plunger in and compares how the other moves: air can be pressed together and water hardly at all. The child pushes an empty closed bottle under water and feels the water push it up.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 30 C, which says that the pupil shows insight into and experiments with light, sound, energy and forces; 30 C sits under core goal 30. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The item names three things: magnetism, air and liquid. *Kracht* can be read as the force that these exert and as their strength or power; the item does not choose. It names no liquid, no instrument, no unit and no number, and it states no safety condition.

### Common mistakes

Many children think that a magnet attracts every metal; it attracts iron and steel and not aluminium or copper. Many children think that a larger magnet is always the stronger one. Many children think that air is nothing: that it weighs nothing and cannot push.
