## Official wording

De leerlingen leren onderzoek doen aan materialen en natuurkundige verschijnselen, zoals licht, geluid, electriciteit, kracht, magnetisme en temperatuur.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO, bijlage, kerndoel 42.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to do research on (*onderzoek doen aan*, read as investigating or as examining) materials and physical phenomena (*natuurkundige verschijnselen*, phenomena that physics studies), such as light, sound, electricity, force (*kracht*, a word that also means strength or power), magnetism and temperature.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 asks a question that can be tested, such as which things in a pencil case a magnet attracts, and writes down a prediction. The child tests each thing and records the result: the steel paper clip is attracted; the wooden pencil, the plastic ruler and a piece of aluminium foil are not. With a torch and a toy figure the child moves the figure nearer to the torch and sees its shadow on the wall grow. The child says what was found and whether the prediction was right.

### Limits

This 2006 goal is still in force. It is the third of seven goals under the heading *Natuur en techniek* (nature and *techniek*, a word read as technology or as engineering). The word "zoals" (such as) introduces six examples of physical phenomena; the list is not closed. Materials are named without examples. The goal names no steps of an investigation, no instrument, no unit and no number, and it states no safety condition, although electricity and temperature are among its examples. The source prints *electriciteit* with a c; the current spelling is *elektriciteit*.

### Common mistakes

A child changes more than one thing at a time and then cannot say which change caused the result. A child tries something once and treats the result as certain. Many children think that a magnet attracts every metal; it attracts iron and steel, and not aluminium or copper.
