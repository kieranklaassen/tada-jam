## Official wording

De leerlingen leren dat de positie van de aarde ten opzichte van de zon leidt tot natuurverschijnselen zoals seizoenen en dag-/nachtritme.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO, bijlage, kerndoel 46.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn that the position of the earth relative to the sun leads to natural phenomena such as seasons and the rhythm of day and night.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 holds a globe next to a lamp in a darkened room and turns the globe about its axis: the half that faces the lamp has day and the other half has night, and one full turn is one day. The child then carries the globe in a circle around the lamp while the tilted axis keeps pointing to the same wall. At one place in the circle the northern half leans towards the lamp and gets light for more hours of each turn (summer there); half a circle later it leans away from the lamp (winter there).

### Limits

This 2006 goal is still in force. It is the seventh and last of seven goals under the heading *Natuur en techniek* (nature and *techniek*, a word read as technology or as engineering). The word "zoals" (such as) introduces two examples, the seasons and the rhythm of day and night; the list is not closed. The goal speaks of "the position of the earth relative to the sun" and does not name the turning of the earth about its axis, its path around the sun or the tilt of its axis. It states no numbers (no hours, days or angle) and does not mention the moon.

### Common mistakes

Many children think that it is summer when the earth is closer to the sun; the seasons come from the tilt of the earth's axis, and the earth is closest to the sun in early January. A child thinks that the sun moves around the earth each day, or that night comes when the sun goes behind clouds or behind the moon. A child thinks that it is the same season everywhere on earth; when it is summer in the northern half it is winter in the southern half.
