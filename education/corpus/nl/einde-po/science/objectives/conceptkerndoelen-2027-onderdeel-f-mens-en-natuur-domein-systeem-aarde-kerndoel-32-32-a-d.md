## Official wording

beredeneren hoe processen in de aarde leiden tot verschuivende continenten, gebergtevorming en vulkanisme;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning out how processes in the earth lead to shifting continents, mountain building and volcanism;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 cuts the continents out of a world map and fits the east coast of South America against the west coast of Africa. The child pushes two flat slabs of clay against each other and sees folds rise, as mountains rise where two plates of the earth's outer layer press together. On a world map the child marks volcanoes, sees that most lie in lines along the edges of the plates, and reasons from the heat inside the earth to the slow movement of the plates.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 32 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into and explores changes to the earth's surface through natural phenomena and human action; 32 A sits under core goal 32. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is reasoning out (*beredeneren*). The item names three results: shifting continents, mountain building and volcanism. It leaves the processes unnamed: the words plate and plate tectonics are not in it, and no layer of the earth is named. Earthquakes are not among the three results. The item names no quantity: no speed, depth or span of time.

### Common mistakes

Many children think the continents float on water or on a sea of liquid rock; the layer under the plates is almost entirely solid rock that moves very slowly. A child thinks the continents moved long ago and now lie still; they still move a few centimetres a year. A child thinks volcanoes stand at random places or only in hot countries.
