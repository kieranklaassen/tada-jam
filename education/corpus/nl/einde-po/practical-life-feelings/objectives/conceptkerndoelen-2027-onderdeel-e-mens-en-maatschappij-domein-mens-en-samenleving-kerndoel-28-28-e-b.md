## Official wording

kiezen van veilige routes en vermijden van gevaarlijke situaties;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

choosing safe routes and avoiding dangerous situations;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 compares two routes to a place he or she goes to, on a map or on the street, and picks one by what is on it: a cycle path, a crossing with lights, a busy junction, a place where lorries turn. The child says which places on the route he or she stays away from and why.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 28 E, which says that the pupil takes part in traffic in a safe and responsible way; 28 E sits under core goal 28. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. Two things are named: choosing safe routes, and avoiding dangerous situations. The item does not say what makes a route safe or a situation dangerous, and does not say in which role (on foot, by bicycle, as a passenger). It names no route, destination or situation. It states no safety condition such as adult supervision, and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

A child takes the shortest route to be the best one. A child takes a route he or she knows well to be a safe one. A child does not recognise a situation as dangerous when nothing has gone wrong there before.
