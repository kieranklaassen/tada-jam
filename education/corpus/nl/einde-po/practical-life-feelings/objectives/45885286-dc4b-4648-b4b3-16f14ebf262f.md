## Official wording

De leerlingen leren zich te gedragen vanuit respect voor algemeen aanvaarde waarden en normen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO, bijlage, kerndoel 37.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to behave out of respect for generally accepted values and norms (*waarden en normen*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school keeps to rules of behaviour that hold in a group, such as waiting for a turn, telling the truth and leaving another person's things alone, and says which value a rule serves, such as honesty or respect for others.

### Limits

This 2006 goal is still in force. It is one numbered sentence of the decree, set for the end of primary school, under the heading *Mens en samenleving* (people and society). The verb is learning to behave, and the ground named is respect for generally accepted values and norms. In Dutch usage *waarden* are what people find important and *normen* are the rules of behaviour that follow from them. The goal does not say which values and norms are generally accepted or who decides that. It names no situations or acts and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out is the value that a rule serves: they keep a rule when an adult is watching and do not keep it when no one is. Many also take the habits of their own home for what is generally accepted.
