## Official wording

bespreekbaar maken van wensen, grenzen, mogelijkheden en beperkingen;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 10.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making wishes, boundaries, *mogelijkheden* (possibilities or abilities) and *beperkingen* (limitations or impairments) open to discussion;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says what he or she would like and where his or her boundary is ("I do not want that"), and hears the same from a classmate without making fun of it. The child says what he or she can do and what he or she finds hard or cannot do.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 28 D, which says that the school contributes to the social and emotional development of pupils and promotes respectful interaction with others; 28 D sits under core goal 28. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. Like 28 D, it says what the school does and is not a requirement on the pupil. The verb is making open to discussion (*bespreekbaar maken*): the school makes it possible to talk about these things; the item does not say that a pupil must tell about himself or herself. Four things are named: wishes, boundaries, *mogelijkheden* and *beperkingen*. *Mogelijkheden* can mean possibilities and can mean abilities; *beperkingen* can mean limitations and can mean impairments. The item does not say whose these are or in what field, names no situations and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

This item is about what the school does, so it has no typical error of a child. Children most often state a boundary softly or with a laugh and then take it that the other person has understood. Many also hide what they cannot do and do not ask for help.
