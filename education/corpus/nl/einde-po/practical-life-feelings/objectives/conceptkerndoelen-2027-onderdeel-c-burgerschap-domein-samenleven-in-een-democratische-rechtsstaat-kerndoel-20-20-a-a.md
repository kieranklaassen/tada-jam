## Official wording

beschrijven van de betekenis van basiswaarden van de democratische rechtsstaat: vrijheid, gelijkwaardigheid en solidariteit;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing the meaning of basic values of the democratic state under the rule of law (*democratische rechtsstaat*): freedom, equal worth (*gelijkwaardigheid*) and solidarity;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says in his or her own words what freedom, equal worth and solidarity mean, and gives a case of each: being allowed to say what one thinks, a rule that holds for every pupil alike, classmates who help one who cannot join in.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 20 A, which says that the pupil reasons about the importance of basic values of the democratic *rechtsstaat*; 20 A sits under core goal 20. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is describing. After the colon the item names three values: freedom, *gelijkwaardigheid* and solidarity; it does not say whether there are other basic values. *Gelijkwaardigheid* means that people are of equal worth; it is a different word from *gelijkheid* (equality, being the same). The item gives no definition of any of the three, names no situations and sets no level of detail. Items 20 A b and 20 A e use the term basic values without naming them.

### Common mistakes

A child takes freedom to mean doing whatever one wants, without the limit set by the freedom of others. A child takes equal worth to mean that everyone is the same or must get exactly the same. A child knows solidarity only as helping friends.
