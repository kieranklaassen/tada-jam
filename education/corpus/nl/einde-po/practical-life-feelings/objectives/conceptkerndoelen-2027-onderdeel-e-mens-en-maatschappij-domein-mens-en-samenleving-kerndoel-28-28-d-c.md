## Official wording

stimuleren van veilige sociale omgang, weerbaarheid, samenwerking en conflicthantering;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 10.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

stimulating safe social interaction (*omgang*), *weerbaarheid* (being able to stand up for oneself, or resilience), cooperation and the handling of conflicts;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says "no, I do not want that" when pressed and keeps to it, shares out the tasks in a shared piece of work and does his or her part, and in a quarrel says what he or she wants, hears the other, and looks for a way that both accept. The child deals with others in a way that leaves no one afraid or left out.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 28 D, which says that the school contributes to the social and emotional development of pupils and promotes respectful interaction with others; 28 D sits under core goal 28. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. Like 28 D, it says what the school does and is not a requirement on the pupil. The verb is stimulating. Four things are named: safe social interaction, *weerbaarheid*, cooperation and the handling of conflicts. The Dutch *weerbaarheid* can mean being able to stand up for oneself and can mean resilience; the item does not choose. The item does not say what makes social interaction safe and states no safety condition. It names no situations, methods or ways of handling a conflict, and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

This item is about what the school does, so it has no typical error of a child. Children most often take standing up for oneself to mean being hard or loud. In a conflict many look for who started it and leave out what each side wants.
