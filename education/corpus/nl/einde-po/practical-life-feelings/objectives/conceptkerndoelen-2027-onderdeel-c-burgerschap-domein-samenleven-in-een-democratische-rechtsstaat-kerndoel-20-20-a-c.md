## Official wording

redeneren over hoe vrijheid, gelijkwaardigheid en solidariteit met elkaar op gespannen voet kunnen staan;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about how freedom, equal worth (*gelijkwaardigheid*) and solidarity can be in tension with one another;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes a case in which two values ask different things, for example saying freely what one thinks about someone and treating that person as an equal, and says what each value asks. The child gives reasons for a way of weighing them, and hears a classmate who weighs them otherwise.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 20 A, which says that the pupil reasons about the importance of basic values of the democratic *rechtsstaat*; 20 A sits under core goal 20. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is reasoning. The three values are the ones named in item 20 A a. The Dutch uses a fixed expression (*op gespannen voet staan*) for being in tension or at odds. The item says that the values can be in tension; it names no case, does not say which value weighs more, and gives no outcome that the pupil must reach. It names no situations and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

A child takes it that one value always wins and does not see that both hold in the case. A child chooses a side at once and gives no reasons. A child sees the tension only between persons ("he wants this, she wants that") and not between the values.
