## Official wording

beschrijven van aspecten van diversiteit: godsdienst, levensovertuiging, politieke gezindheid, afkomst, geslacht, handicap en seksuele gerichtheid;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing aspects of diversity: religion, belief (*levensovertuiging*, a religious or non-religious view of life), political conviction, origin, *geslacht* (sex or gender), disability and sexual orientation;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 names the seven aspects and says in his or her own words what each is about, for example that people hold different religions or views of life, come from different places, or have or do not have a disability. The child describes each as a way in which people in one society differ.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 20 B, which says that the pupil explores how he or she can deal with diversity in society; 20 B sits under core goal 20. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is describing. After the colon the item lists seven aspects: religion, *levensovertuiging*, political conviction, origin, *geslacht*, disability and sexual orientation. The Dutch *geslacht* can mean sex and can mean gender; the item does not choose. *Levensovertuiging* is a view of life, religious or not; *afkomst* (origin) is not narrowed to a country, a family or a culture. The item does not say whether the list is complete. It names no groups, examples or situations and sets no level of detail. Item 20 B b uses the term aspects of diversity without listing them.

### Common mistakes

A child describes an aspect by a stereotype about a group ("people of that religion always ..."). A child mixes up aspects, for example origin and religion. A child counts as diversity only what can be seen, and leaves out belief, conviction and orientation.
