## Official wording

De leerlingen leren hoofdzaken over geestelijke stromingen die in de Nederlandse multiculturele samenleving een belangrijke rol spelen, en ze leren respectvol om te gaan met seksualiteit en met diversiteit binnen de samenleving, waaronder seksuele diversiteit.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO, bijlage, kerndoel 38.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn main points about spiritual and philosophical movements (*geestelijke stromingen*, religions and other views of life) that play an important part in the Dutch multicultural society, and they learn to deal respectfully with sexuality and with diversity within society, including sexual diversity.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school names main points of several religions and views of life found in the Netherlands, such as a holy book, a feast day or a house of prayer, and says to which movement each belongs. The child speaks about sexuality and about differences between people, among them differences in sexual orientation, in words that do not mock or insult.

### Limits

This 2006 goal is still in force. It is one numbered sentence of the decree, set for the end of primary school, under the heading *Mens en samenleving* (people and society). The sentence has two parts: learning main points about *geestelijke stromingen*, and learning to deal respectfully with sexuality and with diversity within society. The first part is limited to main points and to movements that play an important part in the Dutch multicultural society; the goal names none of them and does not say how many. In the second part the word "including" (*waaronder*) marks sexual diversity as one kind of diversity among others, which are not named. The goal does not say what makes dealing with these respectful. It names no situations, acts or groups and sets no level of detail. The frame of this lane notes that the open data of the curriculum institute adds a clause to this goal that the law does not have; this record follows the law.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up main points of different religions, for example the holy books or the feast days. A child describes a movement by a stereotype of its followers. In the second part pupils most often leave out that respect also holds when no one of the group concerned is present: they use a word for a sexual orientation as a term of abuse.
