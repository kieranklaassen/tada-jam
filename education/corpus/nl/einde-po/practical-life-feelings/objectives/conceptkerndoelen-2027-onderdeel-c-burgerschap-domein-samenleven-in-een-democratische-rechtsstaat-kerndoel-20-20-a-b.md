## Official wording

beschrijven hoe de Grondwet, kinder- en mensenrechten basiswaarden van de democratische rechtsstaat mogelijk maken;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing how the *Grondwet* (the Dutch Constitution), children's rights and human rights make basic values of the democratic state under the rule of law (*democratische rechtsstaat*) possible;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes a right from the Constitution, from children's rights or from human rights, for example the ban on discrimination or the right to give one's opinion, and says which basic value it makes possible and how. The child says what a person could not do or count on if the right were not laid down.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 20 A, which says that the pupil reasons about the importance of basic values of the democratic *rechtsstaat*; 20 A sits under core goal 20. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is describing. Three sources of rights are named: the *Grondwet*, which is the written constitution of the Netherlands, children's rights and human rights. The item names no article, treaty or right and does not say how many the pupil knows. It names no basic value; item 20 A a names three. It names no situations and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

A child takes a right to be a wish ("I have a right to a telephone") and not something laid down that binds the government and others. A child lists rights and does not say what they make possible. Many children think that rights hold for adults only.
