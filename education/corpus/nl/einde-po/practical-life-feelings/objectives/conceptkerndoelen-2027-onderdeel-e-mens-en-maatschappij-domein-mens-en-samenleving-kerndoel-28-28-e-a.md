## Official wording

benoemen en toepassen van de verkeersregels die gelden voor voetgangers, fietsers en passagiers;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 10.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming and applying the traffic rules that hold for pedestrians, cyclists and passengers;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says what a traffic sign, a road marking or a priority rule means for someone on foot or on a bicycle, and does what it says on the street: stops at a red light, gives way at a give-way marking, signals a turn with the arm. As a passenger in a car the child wears the seat belt.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 28 E, which says that the pupil takes part in traffic in a safe and responsible way; 28 E sits under core goal 28. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. Two verbs: naming and applying. Three roles are named: pedestrians, cyclists and passengers; the item does not say of which vehicles the pupil is a passenger. It names no rule, sign or situation and does not say how many rules. It states no safety condition such as adult supervision, and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

A child knows a rule for a test and does not apply it on the street. A child mixes up who goes first at a junction of equal roads. A child takes having priority to mean being safe, and does not check whether the other road user gives way.
