## Official wording

verklaren hoe ziektes ontstaan en hoe ze voorkómen en behandeld kunnen worden;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 13.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

explaining how illnesses arise and how they can be prevented and treated;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes an illness he or she knows, for example flu or tooth decay, and explains in steps how it arises, what can be done so that it does not arise, such as washing hands, a vaccination or brushing teeth, and how it is treated.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 31 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into lifestyle, health and illness and explores choices in these; 31 B sits under core goal 31. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is explaining (*verklaren*), which asks for causes and not only for a description. Three things are explained: how illnesses arise, how they can be prevented, and how they can be treated. The source prints *voorkómen* with an accent, which marks the reading "prevent" (without the accent the word can also mean "occur"). The item names no illness, cause, means of prevention or treatment, does not say how many illnesses, and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

A child takes every illness to be catching. A child thinks that an antibiotic works against every illness, also one caused by a virus. A child mixes up preventing and treating: a vaccination is taken to be a medicine for someone who is already ill.
