## Official wording

verkennen hoe samenwerking invloed heeft op welzijn en welvaart;

Source: Ministerie van Onderwijs, Cultuur en Wetenschap (via internetconsultatie.nl), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen overige leergebieden (consultation draft), page 10.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring how cooperation influences well-being (*welzijn*) and prosperity (*welvaart*);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at something people make or do together, for example a market, a shared garden or a product with parts from several makers, and says what each contributes and what all gain that none would have alone. The child says what this does for how people fare (well-being) and for what they have to live on (prosperity).

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 28 A, which says that the pupil explores economic choices of people and possible consequences; 28 A sits under core goal 28. This is a draft goal, not yet in force. The verb is exploring. One cause is named, cooperation, and two things it influences: *welzijn* (well-being, how people fare) and *welvaart* (prosperity, what people have to live on). The item does not say who cooperates (persons, companies or countries) and does not say that the influence is favourable. It names no examples or situations and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

A child treats well-being and prosperity as the same thing: more money is taken to mean a better life. A child sees cooperation only as helping a friend, and not as people each doing a part of a larger piece of work.
