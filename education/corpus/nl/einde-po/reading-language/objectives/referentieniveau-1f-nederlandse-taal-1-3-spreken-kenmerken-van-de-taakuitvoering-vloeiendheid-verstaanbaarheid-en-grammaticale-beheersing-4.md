## Official wording

Pauzes, valse starts en herformuleringen komen af en toe voor.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing / 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Pauses, false starts and reformulations occur now and then.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 tells the class about a visit to a museum. The pupil begins a sentence, stops, and begins it again in another way: "We went to the, eh, we first had to wait for the guide." Now and then the pupil is silent for a moment to think of what comes next, and then goes on. The class can follow the account.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), *Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing* (fluency, intelligibility and command of grammar). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance). It is the last statement of its cell, which opens with the cross-reference *Zie Gesprekken +* (see Conversations, plus), another record of this lane, so it comes in addition to what holds for conversations. Its position in the cell is 4: the decree prints the plus sign as a paragraph of its own in position 2, which the pack joins to the cross-reference and does not hold as a record. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement describes the pupil's speech at this level; it is not something the pupil sets out to do. Pauses, false starts and reformulations occur, and they occur "now and then" (*af en toe*). The wording leaves open whether "now and then" is meant as an allowance (they may occur) or as a bound (not more often than that). It gives no number.

### Common mistakes

A pupil fills each pause with "eh" or *en toen* (and then), which is harder to listen to than a silence. A pupil who makes a false start goes back to the beginning of the whole account instead of the beginning of the sentence. A pupil who cannot go on stops altogether and waits for help.
