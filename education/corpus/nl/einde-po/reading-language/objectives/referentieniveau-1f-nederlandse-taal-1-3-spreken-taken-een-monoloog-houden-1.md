## Official wording

Kan alledaagse aspecten beschrijven, zoals mensen, plaatsen en zaken.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 1F, Taken / Een monoloog houden / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can describe everyday aspects, such as people, places and *zaken* (things or matters).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 describes a grandfather to a classmate: tall, a grey beard, always a cap, and a shed full of clocks. The pupil describes the way from school to the swimming pool, and what the new bicycle looks like. The listener can imagine the person, the place or the thing.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), task *Een monoloog houden* (giving a monologue). It stands under *Taken* (tasks). The task has one other statement at 1F, on giving a report and on a short, prepared story or presentation. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is about describing, and what is described is everyday (*alledaagse*). People, places and *zaken* come after "such as": they are examples of the level, not limits. *Zaken* can mean things (objects) or matters (affairs); the statement does not choose. The source says "everyday aspects" (*alledaagse aspecten*) without saying aspects of what; it is glossed as it stands. The statement sets no length and no number of details.

### Common mistakes

A pupil names one or two features and stops, so the listener cannot imagine what is described. A pupil gives details in no order, and alternates between what a person looks like and what the person does. A pupil gives an opinion ("He is really nice") where a description was asked for.
