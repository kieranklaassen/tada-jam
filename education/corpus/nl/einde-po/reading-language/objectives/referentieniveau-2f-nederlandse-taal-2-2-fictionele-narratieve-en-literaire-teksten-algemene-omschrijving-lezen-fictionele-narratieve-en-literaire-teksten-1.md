## Official wording

Kan eenvoudige adolescentenliteratuur herkennend lezen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 2F, Algemene omschrijving Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can read simple literature for adolescents with recognition (*herkennend lezen*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who reads at this level reads a simple novel written for young teenagers, about a main character at school, at home and among friends. While reading, the child recognises situations and feelings from the child's own life, such as a quarrel with a friend or a first day at a new school, and says so: "I know that feeling."

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading fictional, narrative and literary texts (*Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*), row *Algemene omschrijving* (general description). It is the one general sentence of the section; the rows on text characteristics and characteristics of task performance are separate records of this lane. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. Two words bound the statement: the literature is "simple", and it is literature for adolescents (*adolescentenliteratuur*); the wording speaks of adolescents and names neither primary school nor an age. The statement does not define *herkennend lezen*: it can be read as reading in which the reader recognises situations, events and feelings from their own experience, or more generally as reading in which the reader recognises what the text presents; the gloss keeps the Dutch term. The statement names no titles, authors, genres or length.

### Common mistakes

A child judges a book only by whether its world is like the child's own and stops reading a story set in another time or place. A child follows the events but does not connect a character's feelings with anything the child knows. A child picks a book far above this level because of its cover or topic and stops after a few pages.
