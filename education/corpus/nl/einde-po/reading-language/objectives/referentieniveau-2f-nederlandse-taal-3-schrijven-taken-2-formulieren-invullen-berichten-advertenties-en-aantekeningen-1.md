## Official wording

Kan notities, berichten en instructies schrijven waarin eenvoudige informatie van onmiddellijke relevantie voor vrienden, docenten en anderen wordt overgebracht. Kan een advertentie opstellen om bijvoorbeeld spullen te verkopen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 3 Schrijven, niveau 2F, Taken / 2. Formulieren invullen, berichten, advertenties en aantekeningen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can write notes, messages and instructions in which simple information of immediate relevance to friends, teachers and others is conveyed. Can draw up an advertisement, for example to sell things.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who writes at this level leaves a note for a friend that says where and when they meet, sends a teacher a message that the child will be absent on Thursday, and writes down for someone else the steps for feeding a pet. The child writes a small advertisement to sell a bicycle: what it is, its condition, the price and how to reach the seller.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, writing (*Schrijven*), row *2. Formulieren invullen, berichten, advertenties en aantekeningen* (filling in forms, messages, advertisements and notes). The row stands under *Taken* (tasks); this is the first of the two paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The information is "simple" and "of immediate relevance" to the reader. The readers named are friends, teachers (*docenten*) and others, so the reader is not limited. Selling things is given as an example of what an advertisement is for. The row's name mentions filling in forms; this statement does not. It names no length.

### Common mistakes

A child leaves out the one fact the reader needs, such as the time, the place or who wrote the note. A child writes instructions in an order that cannot be carried out, or skips a step the child finds obvious. A child writes an advertisement that praises the thing but gives no price or way to reply.
