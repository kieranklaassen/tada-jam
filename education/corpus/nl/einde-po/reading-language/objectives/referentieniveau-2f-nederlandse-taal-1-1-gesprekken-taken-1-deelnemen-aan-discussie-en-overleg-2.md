## Official wording

Kan tijdens een discussie of overleg (op beleefde wijze) een probleem verhelderen, een overtuiging of mening, instemming of afkeuring uitdrukken en commentaar geven op de visie van anderen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 2F, Taken / 1. Deelnemen aan discussie en overleg / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can, during a discussion or *overleg* (a talk to arrange or decide something together), (in a polite way) clarify a problem, express a conviction or opinion, agreement or disapproval, and comment on the view of others.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: in the pupils' council a pupil of about 12 says: "The problem is not that the bicycle shed is too small, but that bicycles are put in crosswise." The pupil says "I think we need lines on the ground", tells one member "I agree with you about the lines", tells another "I do not think a fine is a good idea", and adds "Your plan would work, but only if someone checks it." The pupil says all of this calmly and insults nobody.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), row *Deelnemen aan discussie en overleg* (taking part in discussion and in *overleg*). It stands under *Taken* (tasks), as task 1, and is the second of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names three things done during a discussion or *overleg*: clarifying a problem; expressing a conviction or opinion, agreement or disapproval; and commenting on the view of others. *Op beleefde wijze* (in a polite way) stands in brackets in the source, before the first of the three; the statement does not say whether it applies to all three, and the gloss keeps the brackets where the source has them. The statement names no subjects, no number of participants and no kind of meeting; the pupils' council is the pack's example.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out is the comment on another person's view: they state their own opinion and do not react to what was said before. Many also express disapproval without a reason, or forget to be polite when they disagree.
