## Official wording

De structuur is helder.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 2F, Teksten / Tekstkenmerken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The structure is clear.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who reads at this level reads a story in which it is clear who tells it, where a chapter or scene begins and ends, and in what order things happen. When the story moves to another time or place, the text shows it, for example with a new chapter or a line such as "The next morning", so the child can say at any point where and when the story is.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading fictional, narrative and literary texts (*Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*), row *Tekstkenmerken* (text characteristics). The row stands under *Teksten* (texts); this is the first of the three paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement describes the texts read at this level, not what the pupil does. It is one short sentence and does not say what makes a structure clear; it names no number of storylines, no narrator, no length and no titles.

### Common mistakes

This statement describes texts, so it is not a skill with a typical error. The statement does not say what makes a structure clear, so a designer who chooses a story for this level has to judge that on the story itself; the pack's reading is a story that shows who tells it and marks each move to another time or place. What pupils most often leave unused are those marks, such as a chapter title or a white line that shows that a new part begins.
