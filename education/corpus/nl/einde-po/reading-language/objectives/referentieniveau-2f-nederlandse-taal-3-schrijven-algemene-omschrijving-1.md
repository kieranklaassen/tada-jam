## Official wording

Kan samenhangende teksten schrijven met een eenvoudige, lineaire opbouw, over uiteenlopende vertrouwde onderwerpen uit de (beroeps)opleiding en van maatschappelijke aard.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 3 Schrijven, niveau 2F, Algemene omschrijving / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can write coherent texts with a simple, linear structure, about diverse familiar topics from (vocational) education (*(beroeps)opleiding*) and of a societal nature.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who writes at this level writes a text of several paragraphs in which each part follows from the one before: first this, then that, and an ending. The topics are ones the child knows, from school subjects, such as a report on a class visit to a water treatment plant, and from society, such as a piece on litter in the street.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, writing (*Schrijven*), row *Algemene omschrijving* (general description). It is the one general sentence of the section; the rows on tasks and characteristics of task performance are separate records of this lane. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The structure is "simple" and "linear": one part after another. The topics are "diverse" but "familiar", and they come from two fields: (vocational) education and society. The wording speaks of *(beroeps)opleiding*, (vocational) education or training, and does not name primary school; reading the first field as the pupil's own schooling is the pack's reading. The statement names no length, no number of paragraphs and no kinds of text; the task rows name kinds of text.

### Common mistakes

A child writes sentences that each stand alone, without words that connect them. A child leaves the planned order partway and returns to an earlier point, so the text is no longer linear. A child writes for a reader who already knows the topic and leaves out what a new reader needs.
