## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The statement is a cross-reference and names no action of its own. As an illustration of what it refers to, a pupil of about 12 gives a talk about a visit to a museum and has the words for what was seen there. Not knowing the word for a display case, the pupil says "the glass box the old coins lie in" and continues.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), row *Woordgebruik en woordenschat* (word use and vocabulary). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the only statement in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The whole statement is two words, "Zie Gesprekken", which refer the reader to section 1.1 *Gesprekken* (conversations). It is read here as referring to the row with the same heading under *Gesprekken* in the same level column, which has one statement, another record of this lane; the source does not say this in words. Nothing follows the cross-reference in this cell. The statement itself names no words, no number of words and no subjects, and the source prints no full stop after it.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error: the statement is a cross-reference. The typical errors belong to the statement it refers to: a pupil who lacks a word stops or leaves the point out and does not describe what is meant, or uses a vague word such as "thing" so often that the listeners cannot tell what is meant.
