## Official wording

De leerling verkent de betrouwbaarheid van verschillende bronnen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 C.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil explores the reliability of different sources.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has three sources on one question, for example a page of an encyclopedia, a video by an unknown maker and a leaflet from a shop. The child looks at who made each one, what kind of text it is and when it appeared, notices what looks odd, and says which source the child would trust most and why.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 2, which is about understanding texts. Its items 2 C a to 2 C c name getting an orientation on features of provided sources (maker, kind of text and date of publication), naming elements of content and form that are misleading or raise questions, and judging sources by comparison on the basis of their reliability. The verb of the sentence is "explores" (*verkent*); the sentence does not ask for a final verdict on whether a source is true. It does not say what counts as a source or how many sources there are. The sentence sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child trusts a source because it looks neat, has many pictures or was found first. A child treats reliability as a matter of yes or no and does not compare one source with another. A child believes the source that agrees with what the child already thought.
