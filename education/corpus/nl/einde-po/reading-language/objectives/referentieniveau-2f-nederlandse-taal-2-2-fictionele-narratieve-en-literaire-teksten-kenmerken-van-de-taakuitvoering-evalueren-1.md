## Official wording

Evalueert de tekst ook met realistische argumenten en kan persoonlijke reacties toelichten met voorbeelden uit de tekst. Kan met medeleerlingen leeservaringen uitwisselen en kan de interesse in bepaalde genres of onderwerpen motiveren.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Evalueren / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Evaluates the text also with realistic arguments (*realistische argumenten*) and can explain personal reactions with examples from the text. Can exchange reading experiences with fellow pupils and can give reasons for the interest in certain genres or topics.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says what the child thinks of a book and gives a reason that is about how much the book is like real life: "A real twelve-year-old would not talk like that" or "The quarrel went the way quarrels go at our school". The child says "I found the ending sad" and reads out the passage that caused it. In a talk about books with other pupils the child tells what reading the book was like, listens to what the others found, and says why the child likes to read, for example, detective stories or stories about animals.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading fictional, narrative and literary texts (*Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*), row *Evalueren* (evaluating). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance). 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The word *ook* ("also") says that realistic arguments are used besides other arguments, which this statement does not name. *Realistische argumenten* can be read as arguments about how realistic the text is, or as arguments that are themselves realistic; the gloss does not choose. Personal reactions are explained "with examples from the text". Reading experiences are exchanged with fellow pupils (*medeleerlingen*), and the interest for which reasons are given is in "certain genres or topics". The statement names no titles and does not say whether the evaluation is spoken or written.

### Common mistakes

A child says only "good" or "boring" and gives no reason. A child gives a reason that is not tied to the text, so that no passage can be pointed to. A child retells the book where an opinion about it is asked.
