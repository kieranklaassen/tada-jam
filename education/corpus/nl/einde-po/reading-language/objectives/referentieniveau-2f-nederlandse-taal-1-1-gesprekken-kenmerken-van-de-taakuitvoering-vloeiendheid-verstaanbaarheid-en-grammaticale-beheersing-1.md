## Official wording

De uitspraak is duidelijk verstaanbaar, ondanks een eventueel accent, af en toe een verkeerd uitgesproken woord en/of haperingen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pronunciation is clearly intelligible, despite a possible accent, an occasional mispronounced word and/or falterings (*haperingen*, short stops in the flow of speech).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 explains to a new classmate how the lunch break works. The pupil speaks with the accent of the region or of another home language, pronounces one long word wrongly and stops for a moment now and then. The classmate understands every sentence and does not need to ask for a repeat.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), row *Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing* (fluency, intelligibility and command of grammar). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the first of three statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement sets one quality, pronunciation that is clearly intelligible, and names three things that may be present with it: a possible accent, a mispronounced word, and falterings. *Af en toe* (now and then) qualifies the mispronounced word; the statement gives no number for it and no measure for the falterings. It does not say which accent and does not ask for speech without an accent. Under *Spreken* the row with the same heading refers to *Gesprekken* with the words "Zie Gesprekken", another record of this lane.

### Common mistakes

A pupil speaks so fast or so softly that the listener has to ask again. A pupil does not pronounce the ends of words, or says the last words of a sentence too softly to hear. A pupil guesses where the stress falls in a long word that he or she knows only from reading.
