## Official wording

Kan situaties en verwikkelingen in de tekst beschrijven.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can describe situations and complications (*verwikkelingen*, turns in the plot) in the text.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who has read a chapter tells where the characters are, who is with whom and what the problem is at that moment. The child tells what then goes wrong or changes, for example that a letter reaches the wrong person and two friends quarrel because of it, and how one event leads to the next.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading fictional, narrative and literary texts (*Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*), row *Begrijpen* (understanding). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance); this is the third of the five paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement asks for describing: saying what the situations and the complications in the text are. It does not ask why the writer built the story that way. It does not say whether the description is spoken or written, and it names no length of text and no titles.

### Common mistakes

A child lists events as "and then, and then" without saying how one causes the next. A child leaves out the starting situation, so a listener cannot follow why an event is a problem. A child mixes up who did what when a story has several characters or two storylines.
