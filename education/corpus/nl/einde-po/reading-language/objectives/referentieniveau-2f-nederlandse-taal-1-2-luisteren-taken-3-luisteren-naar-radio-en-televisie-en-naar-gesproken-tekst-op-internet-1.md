## Official wording

Kan de hoofdpunten begrijpen van (nieuws)berichten, documentaires, reclameboodschappen en discussieprogramma’s over vertrouwde onderwerpen. Kan films en televisieseries geschikt voor de leeftijd volgen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 2F, Taken / 3. Luisteren naar radio en televisie en naar gesproken tekst op internet / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can understand the main points of (news) reports, documentaries, advertising messages and discussion programmes about familiar subjects. Can follow films and television series suitable for the age.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 watches the children's news and says afterwards what the three main items were. The pupil watches a documentary about wolves returning to the country and a studio discussion among children about telephones in school, and names the main points of each. At home the pupil follows a film made for children of this age from beginning to end, and knows what happened in last week's episode of a series.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), row *Luisteren naar radio en televisie en naar gesproken tekst op internet* (listening to radio and television and to spoken text on the internet). It stands under *Taken* (tasks), as task 3, and is the only statement in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The record holds two sentences. The first asks for understanding the main points of four kinds of text: reports (with "news" in brackets), documentaries, advertising messages and discussion programmes. "About familiar subjects" follows the list; the statement does not say whether it applies to all four or to the last only, and the gloss leaves it as open. The second sentence asks that the pupil can follow films and television series that are suitable for the age; it does not say which age or who decides what is suitable. The statement names no titles and sets no length.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out is the main point of a text that is not a story: of a news report or a documentary they remember the pictures and one striking detail. With an advertising message many leave out who is speaking and treat it as information.
