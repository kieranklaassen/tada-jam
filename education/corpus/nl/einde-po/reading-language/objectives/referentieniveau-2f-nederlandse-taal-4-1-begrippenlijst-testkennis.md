## Official wording

Aanduidingen voor tekstsoorten en genres (ook: aanduidingen voor gespreksvormen), hoofdgedachte (van tekst), tekstthema.
Metatalige vormen: Woorden, zinnen en tekstfragmenten die informatie geven over de rest van de tekst (zoals signaalwoorden, prospectieve en retrospectieve tekstelementen in inleiding, samenvattende zin aan slot).

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.1 Begrippenlijst, niveau 2F, Testkennis.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Designations for kinds of text and genres (also: designations for forms of conversation), main idea (of a text), text theme.
Metalinguistic forms: Words, sentences and text fragments that give information about the rest of the text (such as signal words, prospective and retrospective text elements in the introduction, summarising sentence at the end).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 at this level names what kind of text lies on the table, for example a report, an argument (*betoog*), a recipe or a fairy tale, and what kind of conversation was held, for example an interview or a discussion. The child says what the main idea of a text is and what its theme is. In the text the child marks the words and sentences that tell the reader about the text itself: a signal word such as *daarom* (therefore), a sentence in the introduction that says what will follow, and a last sentence that sums up.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, terms and language care, list of terms (*Begrippenlijst*), row *Testkennis*. It is the cell of this row in the column for level 2F of the decree's table of terms (Table 1); the table prints no sign, and the column gives the level. The decree prints the row label as *Testkennis*; *Tekstkennis* (knowledge of texts) is plainly meant. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The decree introduces the list as terms for talking about language: the point is that teachers (and pupils) can name certain phenomena in language situations with a rich context. The cell holds two paragraphs, each on its own line in the record. The first lists terms: designations for kinds of text (*tekstsoorten*) and genres, with designations for forms of conversation (*gespreksvormen*) added after "also", the main idea (*hoofdgedachte*) of a text, and the text theme (*tekstthema*). The second explains *metatalige vormen* (metalinguistic forms) as words, sentences and text fragments that give information about the rest of the text; what follows *zoals* ("such as") are examples: signal words (*signaalwoorden*), prospective (looking ahead) and retrospective (looking back) text elements, and a summarising sentence. The wording leaves open whether both the prospective and the retrospective elements are "in the introduction". The cell does not list the designations for kinds of text, genres or forms of conversation. The pack's examples, all correct Dutch: signal words, *omdat* (because), *maar* (but), *daarom* (therefore), *ten eerste* (first), *kortom* (in short); a prospective sentence, *In deze tekst lees je hoe een vulkaan ontstaat.* (In this text you read how a volcano comes into being.); a summarising sentence, *Kortom: zonder bijen groeit er minder fruit.* (In short: without bees, less fruit grows.).

### Common mistakes

A child gives one word, the topic, where the main idea is asked, which takes a sentence. A child reads signal words without using them, for example does not expect a contrast after *maar* (but). A child skips the sentence in the introduction that announces what follows and the summarising sentence at the end, and so misses the plan of the text.
