## Official wording

schrijven op letter-, schrift- en tekstniveau met een leesbaar handschrift en typschrift.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 3 C e.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing at the level of the letter, the script (*schrift*) and the text with legible handwriting and typed script (*typschrift*, text typed on a keyboard).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 makes notes by hand that the child can still read a week later: clear letters, words that stand apart, and lines and headings that show the order. The child types a summary on a keyboard with spaces, capitals and paragraphs in place.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 3 C, which says that the pupil writes in order to build knowledge or reach understanding; 3 C sits under core goal 3. It has the same wording as the writing part of item 3 A d, without the speaking. The law does not explain the three levels (letter, script, text). *Schrift* can be read as the running script (how letters are joined into words and lines) or as writing in general; the pack does not choose. The item states no speed of writing or typing, no kind of script (joined or unjoined) and no typing method, and does not say for whom the writing has to be legible.

### Common mistakes

A child writes notes so fast that the child cannot read them later. A child writes neatly and so slowly that part of the content is missed. A child types without capitals, punctuation or paragraphs.
