## Official wording

Redelijk accuraat gebruik van eenvoudige zinsconstructies.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Reasonably accurate use of simple sentence constructions.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 tells a friend about a birthday party in short main clauses and in sentences joined with *en* (and), *maar* (but), *want* (because) and *toen* (then). Most of these sentences are built correctly. Now and then a verb form or the word order is wrong, and the friend still understands.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), *Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing* (fluency, intelligibility and command of grammar). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance). The row has one other statement at 1F, on pronunciation. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. This is a fragment without a verb; it names a quality of the pupil's speech in conversations. Two wordings set the level: the use is "reasonably" (*redelijk*) accurate, so errors occur, and the constructions are simple (*eenvoudige*). The statement does not say which constructions count as simple, and it names no number of errors. The statement *Zie Gesprekken* in the row of the same name under *Spreken* at 1F, another record of this lane, refers to this row.

### Common mistakes

A pupil gets the word order wrong in a subordinate clause, where Dutch puts the verb at the end, for example after *omdat* (because). A pupil uses a regular past form for an irregular verb, such as *loopte* for *liep* (walked), or the wrong article, *de* or *het*. A pupil joins a long account with *en toen* (and then) and no longer finishes the sentence that was begun.
