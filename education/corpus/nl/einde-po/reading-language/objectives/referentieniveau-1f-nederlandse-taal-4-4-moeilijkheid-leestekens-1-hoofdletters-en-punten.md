## Official wording

1. Hoofdletters en punten

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.4 Moeilijkheid, niveau 1F, Leestekens / 1. Hoofdletters en punten.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

1. Capital letters and full stops

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 starts every sentence it writes with a capital letter and ends it with a full stop. The pack's example: *We gingen naar het strand. Het water was koud.* (We went to the beach. The water was cold.), two sentences, each with its capital letter and its full stop. Reading its text over, the child finds a place where two sentences run on and puts in the full stop and the capital letter.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Leestekens* (punctuation marks), item 1, of the table of difficulty. It is one row of the decree's table 2, in the section *Moeilijkheid* (difficulty), which sets levels for spelling, punctuation and the grammatical terms for verb spelling. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The table marks this row with one plus sign (+), in the 1F column. The heading of the table gives the measure of mastery (*beheersing*): 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of a correct answer. The heading does not itself name the plus sign; that the plus sign stands for this mastery at the level of its column is the pack's reading of the heading. The record holds the name of the item only: capital letters and full stops. The list in section 4.3.2 of the decree, which is in no record, words the same item as capital letters and full stops for marking sentences (*bij zinsmarkering*). Capital letters for proper names and in direct speech are another row of the table, and that row carries no plus sign in the 1F column.

### Common mistakes

A child writes a row of sentences joined by *en* (and) or by commas and puts one full stop at the end. A child puts the full stop and goes on with a small letter. A child puts a full stop before a subordinate clause and writes *Ik bleef thuis. Omdat ik ziek was.*; correct is one sentence, *Ik bleef thuis omdat ik ziek was.* (I stayed at home because I was ill.)
