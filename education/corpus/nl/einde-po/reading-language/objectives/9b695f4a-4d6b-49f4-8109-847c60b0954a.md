## Official wording

De leerling begrijpt teksten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil understands texts.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school reads an article in a children's newspaper, listens to a story that is read aloud or watches a short news film, and afterwards says in a few sentences what it was about. The child points to the place in a text that answers a question, and says what the maker wants the reader to think or do. With two texts on the same subject the child says where they agree and where they differ.

### Limits

This is the sentence of core goal 2 itself. Three goal sentences say what it covers, each with its own items: 2 A (showing understanding of non-fiction and literary texts), 2 B (evaluating and reflecting on those texts) and 2 C (exploring the reliability of sources). The sentence does not say what counts as a text; item 2 A a names listening and watching as well as reading, so a text here can also be heard or watched. The goal sets no level: it names no text length, no difficulty of text and no reading speed. The reference levels 1F and 2F, which are other records of this lane (sections *Luisteren*, listening, *Zakelijke teksten*, non-fiction texts, and *Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*, fictional, narrative and literary texts), are worded in more detail.

### Common mistakes

A child reads every word correctly and cannot say what the text as a whole says. A child gives one striking detail as the main point. A child answers a question from what the child already knew about the subject and not from what the text says.
