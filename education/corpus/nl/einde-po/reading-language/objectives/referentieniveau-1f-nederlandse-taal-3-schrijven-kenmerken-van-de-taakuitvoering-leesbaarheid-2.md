## Official wording

Voorziet een brief op de gebruikelijke plaats van datering, adressering, aanhef en ondertekening.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 3 Schrijven, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Leesbaarheid / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Provides a letter with date, address, salutation and signature in the usual place.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 writes a letter and puts the place and the date at the top, the name and address of the person it is for above the text, the salutation on its own line before the first sentence, and its own name under the closing.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Schrijven* (writing), *Leesbaarheid* (readability). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the second of three statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names four parts of a letter: date (*datering*), address (*adressering*), salutation (*aanhef*) and signature (*ondertekening*). "The usual place" (*de gebruikelijke plaats*) is not spelled out. The statement does not say whether the letter is written by hand or typed. The wording of the salutation and the closing is a statement under *Afstemming op publiek*.

### Common mistakes

A child leaves out the date. A child writes its own name at the top and does not sign at the bottom. A child writes the salutation on the same line as the first sentence.
