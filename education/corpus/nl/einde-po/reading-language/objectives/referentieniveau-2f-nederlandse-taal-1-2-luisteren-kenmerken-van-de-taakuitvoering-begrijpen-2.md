## Official wording

Kan wanneer nodig de betekenis van onbekende woorden afleiden uit de vorm, woordsoort, samenstelling of context. Kan beeldspraak herkennen. Kan een relatie leggen tussen tekst en beeld.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can, when necessary, derive the meaning of unknown words from the form, the word class, the composition (*samenstelling*: how a compound word is built) or the context. Can recognise figurative language (*beeldspraak*). Can make a connection between text and image.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 watches a school television item about dikes and hears the unknown word *dijkbewaking*. The pupil reasons from its two parts (*dijk*, dike, and *bewaking*, guarding) and from the pictures of people walking along a dike that it means keeping watch over the dike. When the presenter says that the river "swallowed" the fields, the pupil recognises that this is not meant literally. The pupil connects the map on the screen with what is being said about which villages were flooded.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), row *Begrijpen* (understanding). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the second of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The record holds three sentences and three abilities. The first, deriving the meaning of unknown words, has the condition *wanneer nodig* (when necessary) and names four sources: the form of the word, its word class, its *samenstelling* and the context. *Samenstelling* is the grammar term for a compound word and also the general word for how something is put together. The second asks that the pupil recognise figurative language; it does not ask for naming or explaining kinds of it. The third asks for a connection between text and image; "text" here is spoken text, and the statement does not say what kind of image. The statement names no words, no figures of speech and no kinds of text.

### Common mistakes

A pupil takes a figure of speech literally. A pupil guesses an unknown word from one part that looks familiar, does not check the guess against the context, and gets it wrong. A pupil watches the pictures and stops following the spoken text, or does the reverse, and does not connect the two.
