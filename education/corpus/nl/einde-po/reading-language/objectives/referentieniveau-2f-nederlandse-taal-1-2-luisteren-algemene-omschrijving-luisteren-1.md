## Official wording

Kan luisteren naar teksten over alledaagse onderwerpen, onderwerpen die aansluiten bij de leefwereld van de leerling of die verder van de leerling af staan.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 2F, Algemene omschrijving Luisteren / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can listen to texts about everyday subjects, subjects that connect with the pupil's world of daily life (*leefwereld*) or that stand further away from the pupil.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 listens to a classmate's account of a weekend (an everyday subject), to a talk by a visiting youth football trainer (a subject close to the pupil's own world), and to a radio item for children about how a parliament votes on a law (a subject further away). Each time the pupil listens to the end and can say afterwards what it was about.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), row *Algemene omschrijving* (general description). It is the whole general description of the skill at this level; the tasks, the features of the texts and the features of task performance are other records. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. "Texts" here are spoken texts: what the pupil hears. The statement names three kinds of subject: everyday subjects, subjects that connect with the pupil's world of daily life, and subjects that are further from the pupil. It lists no subjects and names no kinds of text. It says nothing about length or structure; those stand in the rows *Lengte* and *Opbouw*, other records of this lane. It does not say what the pupil does with what is heard.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out with a subject far from their own world is checking their understanding: they hear unfamiliar words, stop following, and do not ask or reason out what was meant. With an everyday subject pupils often attend to what they already know and miss what is new.
