## Official wording

inzetten en uitbreiden van woordenschat, kennis over taal en kennis van de wereld;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 A b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting to use and extending vocabulary, knowledge about language and knowledge of the world;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 finds an unknown word in a text and works out what it means from the sentence around it, from a part of the word that the child knows, or by looking it up. (The pack's example: in the Dutch word *onbewoonbaar*, uninhabitable, a child finds *bewonen*, to live in a place, between *on-*, not, and *-baar*, able to be.) The child uses what the child already knows about a subject, such as the weather or the Middle Ages, to follow a text about it. After reading, the child uses a new word from the text when talking or writing about the subject.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 2 A, which says that the pupil shows understanding of non-fiction and literary texts; 2 A sits under core goal 2. It names three kinds of knowledge (vocabulary, knowledge about language and knowledge of the world) and two things done with them: putting them to use and extending them. The item gives no word list and no number of words, and does not say which knowledge about language or about the world is meant. Knowledge about the form of texts is a separate item, 2 A c.

### Common mistakes

A child reads on after an unknown word and does not notice that the sentence is no longer understood. A child takes the everyday meaning of a word that has another meaning in the text (the pack's example: the Dutch word *bank* is a bench, a sofa or a bank). A child learns the meaning of a word for one text and does not recognise the word in the next text.
