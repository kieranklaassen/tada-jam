## Official wording

Onderwerp, lijdend voorwerp, hoofdzin, bijzin, gezegde, persoonsvorm.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.1 Begrippenlijst, niveau 1F, Grammaticale kennis.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Subject (*onderwerp*), direct object (*lijdend voorwerp*), main clause (*hoofdzin*), subordinate clause (*bijzin*), *gezegde* (the predicate of a sentence, or a saying), finite verb (*persoonsvorm*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes a sentence apart and names the parts. In the pack's example, *De hond eet het brood* (The dog eats the bread), the child finds the finite verb *eet* by putting the sentence in the past tense (*De hond at het brood*) and seeing which word changes, asks "who eats?" to find the subject *de hond*, and asks "what does the dog eat?" to find the direct object *het brood*; the predicate is *eet*. In *Ik blijf thuis omdat ik ziek ben* (I am staying at home because I am ill) the child names *Ik blijf thuis* as the main clause and *omdat ik ziek ben* as the subordinate clause.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Grammaticale kennis* (grammatical knowledge) of the list of terms (*Begrippenlijst*). It is the 1F cell of that row in the decree's table 1, which gives the terms about language by level. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The cell lists six terms and nothing more: no definitions and no mark of how well a pupil must know them. The introduction to the list in the decree, which is in no record, says that a limited number of terms is needed for talking about language and that most of them have been dealt with by the end of primary school. *Gezegde* has two readings in Dutch: the predicate of a sentence, and a saying. In this row on grammar the predicate fits; the same word stands in the row *Stilistiek en semantiek*, next to the word for a proverb, where a saying fits. *Persoonsvorm* is the Dutch school term for the finite verb, the verb form that changes with the person and number of the subject and with the tense.

### Common mistakes

A child takes the first word group of the sentence for the subject, so in *Morgen komt oma* (Grandma is coming tomorrow) it names *morgen*; the subject is *oma*. A child takes the past participle for the finite verb, so in *Ik heb gefietst* (I have cycled) it names *gefietst*; the finite verb is *heb*. A child calls every word group after the verb a direct object, also a place such as *naar school* (to school).
