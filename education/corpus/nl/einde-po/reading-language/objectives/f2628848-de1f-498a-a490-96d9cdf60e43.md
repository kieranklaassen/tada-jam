## Official wording

De leerling toont inzicht in regels en procedures voor spelling, formulering en interpunctie.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 6 B.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil shows insight into rules and procedures for spelling, phrasing (*formulering*) and punctuation.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 writes *paard* (horse), makes the word longer to *paarden* (horses), hears the *d*, and so explains the *d* at the end of *paard* (the pack's example of a spelling procedure). The child puts a capital letter at the start and a full stop at the end of each sentence of a text, and rewrites a sentence that is too long as two sentences.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 6, which is about insight into language as a system. Its items 6 B a to 6 B e name correct phrasing at word, sentence and text level, connecting form and meaning to find a spelling, spelling awareness and spelling conscience, reflecting on choices in words and sentences, and the use of aids and sources. The sentence names three areas: spelling, phrasing and punctuation. Neither the sentence nor its items list a spelling category, a rule, a punctuation mark or a word, and the law sets no level: it does not say which words or which verb forms a pupil spells correctly. The reference levels 1F and 2F for Dutch, which are other records of this lane (section 4, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging*, list of terms and language care), are worded in more detail.

### Common mistakes

A child knows a rule and does not apply it while writing a text, because the attention goes to the content. A child applies a rule where it does not hold: writes *Wordt jij ook ziek?* with a *t*, where *Word jij ook ziek?* (are you getting ill too?) is correct (the pack's example). A child puts a full stop or a comma where they would pause for breath, not where a sentence or a part of it ends.
