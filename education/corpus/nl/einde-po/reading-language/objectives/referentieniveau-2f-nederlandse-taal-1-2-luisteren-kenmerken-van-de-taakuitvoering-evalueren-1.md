## Official wording

Kan een oordeel over de waarde van een tekst(deel) of televisie- of radioprogramma (of fragment ervan) verwoorden voor zichzelf en kan dit oordeel toelichten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Evalueren / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can put a judgement about the value of a text (or part of a text) or of a television or radio programme (or a fragment of it) into words for oneself (*voor zichzelf*) and can explain this judgement.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 has watched an episode of the children's news. The pupil says: "The item about plastic in the sea was worth watching, because it showed what happens to the animals and I did not know that. The item about the football transfer told me nothing new." Asked why, the pupil gives the reasons again in other words.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), row *Evalueren* (evaluating). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the only statement in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names two abilities: putting into words a judgement about the value of what was heard or watched, and explaining that judgement. *Voor zichzelf* can be read in two ways: the pupil puts the judgement into words for himself or herself, so that it need not be presented to others, or the judgement is about the value the text has for the pupil. The gloss keeps the Dutch. The things judged are a text or part of one, and a television or radio programme or a fragment of one. The statement names no criteria for value and no kinds of programme, and sets no length.

### Common mistakes

A pupil says "it was nice" or "it was boring" and can give no reason. A pupil judges the subject and not the text or programme: a programme about a favourite animal is called good whatever its quality. A pupil repeats the judgement of a friend or of the group as his or her own.
