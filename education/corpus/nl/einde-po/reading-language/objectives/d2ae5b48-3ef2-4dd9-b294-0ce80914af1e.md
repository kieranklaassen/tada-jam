## Official wording

De leerling toont inzicht in taal als systeem.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 6.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil shows insight into language as a system.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school takes a word apart and says what each part does, moves the words of a sentence around and says what changes in the meaning, and explains a spelling from the way the word is built. For *hij wordt* (he becomes) the child points to the stem *word* and the added *t* (the pack's example).

### Limits

This is the sentence of core goal 6 itself. Two goal sentences say what it covers, each with its own items: 6 A (the relation between the form and the meaning of language) and 6 B (rules and procedures for spelling, phrasing and punctuation). The sentence of the goal itself names no spelling category, no grammatical term, no punctuation mark and no list of words. Some of its items print terms about language, for example stress and intonation (6 A a) and word order and sentence parts (6 A c); none of them names a single sentence part (subject, verb, object), a word class, a spelling category or a punctuation mark, and none gives a list of words. The law sets no level here. Core goal 6 is one of two goals of the domain language (*taal*), with core goal 7. The reference levels 1F and 2F for Dutch, which are other records of this lane (section 4, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging*, list of terms and language care), are worded in more detail.

### Common mistakes

A child learns a spelling rule as a line to recite and does not connect it with how the word is built or what it means. Many children can do a grammar exercise and do not use the same knowledge when they write a text of their own.
