## Official wording

Standpunt, argument, feit, mening, tekstsoort en gespreksvormen, paragraaf.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.1 Begrippenlijst, niveau 1F, Testkennis.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Point of view (*standpunt*), argument (*argument*), fact (*feit*), opinion (*mening*), text type and forms of conversation (*tekstsoort en gespreksvormen*), section (*paragraaf*, a numbered part of a text).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads the pack's example *Honden zijn de leukste huisdieren, want ze zijn trouw* (Dogs are the nicest pets, for they are loyal) and names *Honden zijn de leukste huisdieren* as the point of view and *ze zijn trouw* as the argument for it. The child says that *Een hond heeft vier poten* (A dog has four legs) is a fact, which can be checked, and that *Honden zijn leuk* (Dogs are nice) is an opinion. The child names what kind of text it has in front of it, such as a letter or a news report, and what kind of conversation it is having, such as an interview or a discussion.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row printed as *Testkennis* of the list of terms (*Begrippenlijst*). It is the 1F cell of that row in the decree's table 1, which gives the terms about language by level. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The decree prints the row label as *Testkennis*; *Tekstkennis* (knowledge of texts) is plainly meant. The cell lists six items and nothing more: no definitions and no mark of how well a pupil must know them. The introduction to the list in the decree, which is in no record, says that a limited number of terms is needed for talking about language and that most of them have been dealt with by the end of primary school. *Tekstsoort en gespreksvormen* is printed as one item, the first word in the singular and the second in the plural, and the cell lists no text types and no forms of conversation. *Paragraaf* is a numbered section of a text, larger than a paragraph; the Dutch for a paragraph is *alinea*, a term in the row *Opmaak*.

### Common mistakes

A child takes an opinion that is stated firmly for a fact. A child mixes up the point of view with the argument and gives the reason when asked what the writer thinks. A child uses *paragraaf* for a paragraph, which is *alinea* in Dutch.
