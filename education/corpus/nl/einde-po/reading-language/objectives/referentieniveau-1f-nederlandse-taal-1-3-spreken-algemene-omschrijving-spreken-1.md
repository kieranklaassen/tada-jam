## Official wording

Kan in eenvoudige bewoordingen een beschrijving geven, informatie geven, verslag uitbrengen, uitleg en instructie geven in alledaagse situaties in en buiten school.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 1F, Algemene omschrijving Spreken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can, in simple wording, give a description, give information, give a report, give explanation and *instructie* (instructions for a task, or teaching) in everyday situations in and outside school.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 describes to the caretaker what the lost coat looks like, tells the class what time the bus leaves for the match, and reports to the group what happened during the visit to the farm. The pupil explains to a younger child how the pencil sharpener on the wall works and tells the child what to do, step by step. Each time the pupil speaks alone for a short while, in plain words.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), *Algemene omschrijving* (general description). It is the whole general description of this skill at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names what a pupil does when speaking: giving a description, giving information, giving a report, and giving explanation and *instructie*. *Instructie* can mean instructions for carrying something out or teaching how something is done; the statement does not choose. Two wordings set the level: the wording is simple (*eenvoudige bewoordingen*) and the situations are everyday (*alledaagse*), in and outside school. The statement names no topics, no audience and no speaking time. Arguing a case is not among the things it names. The task statements under *Een monoloog houden* (giving a monologue) at 1F, other records of this lane, say more about what is told.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out when they speak alone is what the listener does not yet know: who, where and when. They begin with the event itself, as if the listener had been there. In an explanation they most often leave out a step.
