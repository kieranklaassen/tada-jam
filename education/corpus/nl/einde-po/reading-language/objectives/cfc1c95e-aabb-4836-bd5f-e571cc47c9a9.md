## Official wording

beschrijven van verschillende hoofd- en bijfiguren, helpers en tegenspelers en hun karaktereigenschappen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 9 A b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing different main and minor characters, helpers and opponents (*tegenspelers*, characters who work against the main character) and their character traits;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 makes a list or a drawing of the characters of a book: who the story is about, who appears beside them, who helps the main character and who works against. For each the child writes a few traits, such as brave, jealous or stubborn, and points to something the character does or says that shows the trait.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 9 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into narrative texts; 9 A sits under core goal 9. The item names four kinds of character (main characters, minor characters, helpers and opponents) and asks for their character traits. It does not say how many characters or traits. Change in a character during the story is not mentioned. The item names no length or level of story.

### Common mistakes

A pupil describes how a character looks (tall, red hair) where a character trait is asked. A pupil sorts characters into good and bad only and gives every opponent the trait "mean". A pupil names a trait and cannot point to an action or a sentence that shows it.
