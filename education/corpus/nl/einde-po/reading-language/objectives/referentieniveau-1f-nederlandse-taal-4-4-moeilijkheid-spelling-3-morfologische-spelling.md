## Official wording

3. Morfologische spelling
Hieronder vallen de schrijfwijze van achtervoegsels, de meervoudsvorming, de verkleinwoordsvorming, regel van gelijkvormigheid bij assimilatie (zakdoek/zaddoek), vorming bijvoeglijk naamwoord

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.4 Moeilijkheid, niveau 1F, Spelling / 3. Morfologische spelling.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

3. Morphological spelling
This includes the spelling of suffixes, the forming of plurals, the forming of diminutives, the rule of uniformity under assimilation (*zakdoek*/*zaddoek*), the forming of the adjective

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 spells a word by looking at the parts it is built from. The pack's examples: the suffixes in *vrolijk* (cheerful) and *waarheid* (truth) are written *-lijk* and *-heid*, which the sound alone does not settle; the plurals *boeken* (books), *katten* (cats) and *tafels* (tables); the diminutives *huisje* (little house), *boompje* (little tree) and *balletje* (little ball); and the adjective with *-e* in *een mooie dag* (a fine day) and *de grote hond* (the big dog). The child writes *zakdoek* (handkerchief) with a *k* because the word is built from *zak* (pocket) and *doek* (cloth), also where the *k* is not heard.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Spelling*, class 3, of the table of difficulty. It is one row of the decree's table 2, in the section *Moeilijkheid* (difficulty), which sets levels for spelling, punctuation and the grammatical terms for verb spelling. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The table marks this row with one plus sign (+), in the 1F column. The heading of the table gives the measure of mastery (*beheersing*): 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of a correct answer. The heading does not itself name the plus sign; that the plus sign stands for this mastery at the level of its column is the pack's reading of the heading. The record holds the heading of the row and its explanation, which the decree prints as two paragraphs in one cell, each on its own line; neither ends with a full stop. The source leaves out the article before "rule" and before "forming" of the adjective; the gloss reads them as plainly meant. The explanation names five things: the spelling of suffixes, the forming of plurals, the forming of diminutives, the rule of uniformity under assimilation, and the forming of the adjective. In the brackets, *zakdoek* is the correct spelling and *zaddoek* is the spelling by sound, which is wrong. The explanation gives no list of suffixes and no word list. The decree's running list of spelling rules in section 4.3.1, which carries no level and is in no record, names two suffixes, *-heid* and *-lijk*; the pack's words *waarheid* and *vrolijk* are built with them. Under this row the table prints six rows of difficult cases (*Moeilijke gevallen*), other records of this lane; none of them carries a plus sign in the 1F column.

### Common mistakes

A child writes a suffix by its sound, such as *vrolek* for *vrolijk*. A child writes *zaddoek* or *zagdoek* for *zakdoek*. A child forgets the spelling change when *-e* or *-en* is added and writes *groote* for *grote* or *katen* for *katten*.
