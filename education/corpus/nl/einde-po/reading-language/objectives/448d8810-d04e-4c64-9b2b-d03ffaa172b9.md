## Official wording

verbinden van vorm en betekenis om de correcte spelling te achterhalen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 6 B b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

connecting form and meaning in order to find out the correct spelling;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who has to write *leiden* or *lijden*, two words that sound the same, asks what is meant: *leiden* is to lead and *lijden* is to suffer, and the child picks the spelling by the meaning (the pack's example). For *hij vindt* (he finds) the child asks who does it and when: *hij* (he), the third person, now, so the stem *vind* plus *t*. For *hartelijk* (warm-hearted) the child thinks of *hart* (heart) and writes a *t*, not the *d* of *hard* (hard).

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 6 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into rules and procedures for spelling, phrasing and punctuation; 6 B sits under core goal 6. The item names a way of working (connecting form and meaning) and its purpose (finding out the correct spelling). It names no particular spelling category and gives no list of words; verb spelling, words that sound alike and word families are the pack's examples. The item sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child spells by sound alone and writes *hij vind*, because the *t* after the *d* is not heard; correct is *hij vindt* (the pack's example). A child mixes up two words that sound the same and differ in meaning, such as *leiden* and *lijden*. A child does not ask which form is meant and writes *het is gebeurt* where *het is gebeurd* (it has happened) is correct.
