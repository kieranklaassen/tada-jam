## Official wording

benoemen van verschillen en overeenkomsten in feiten, meningen en perspectieven;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 B b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming differences and similarities in facts, opinions and perspectives;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays two texts about the same event side by side, for example a news report and a reader's letter, and marks in one colour what both state as a fact and in another colour what only one of them says. The child says which sentences are opinions and whose they are, and how the two writers' views of the event differ.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 2 B, which says that the pupil evaluates and reflects on non-fiction and literary texts; 2 B sits under core goal 2. Three things are compared: facts, opinions and perspectives. The item does not say where the differences and similarities are looked for; the neighbouring items 2 B a and 2 B c say "within and between texts", and this item has no such words. It does not ask the pupil to decide which fact is correct. The item sets no level, no number of texts and no kinds of text.

### Common mistakes

A child takes an opinion that is stated firmly for a fact ("everyone knows that ..."). A child calls a statement an opinion because the child disagrees with it. A child lists what each text says and does not say where they differ or agree.
