## Official wording

Kan de ontwikkeling van de hoofdpersoon beschrijven. Kan de geschiedenis chronologisch navertellen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 5.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can describe the development of the main character. Can retell the *geschiedenis* (history; the story's course of events) chronologically.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who has finished a book says how the main character was at the beginning, how at the end, and what happened in between that caused the change, for example a shy character who dares to speak up after defending a friend. The child retells the events in the order in which they happened, and puts a memory or a flashback that the book tells later back in its place in time.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading fictional, narrative and literary texts (*Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*), row *Begrijpen* (understanding). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance); this is the fifth of the five paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The paragraph holds two sentences: one about the development of the main character (one character, not all), and one about retelling in chronological order. *Geschiedenis* means "history" and also "story" or "the course of events of a story"; the gloss keeps the Dutch word and gives both, and the statement stands in a row on reading fiction. Chronological order is the order in which the events happened, which can differ from the order in which a text tells them. The statement does not say whether the retelling is spoken or written, and it names no titles and no length.

### Common mistakes

A child retells the events in the order of the book and does not move a flashback to its place in time. A child describes the main character at one moment and does not say what changed. A child gives the change but not what caused it.
