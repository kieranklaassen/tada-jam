## Official wording

Kan systematisch informatie zoeken (op bijvoorbeeld het internet of de schoolbibliotheek) bijvoorbeeld op basis van trefwoorden.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Opzoeken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can search for information systematically (on, for example, the internet or the school library), for example on the basis of keywords.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who has a question, such as why hedgehogs sleep in winter, first writes down keywords, types them into a search engine or a library catalogue, and looks at the titles and first lines of the results before opening one. If the results do not fit, the child changes a keyword. In a book the child goes to the table of contents or the index, finds the page and reads the part that answers the question.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading factual texts (*Lezen zakelijke teksten*), row *Opzoeken* (looking up). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance). 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The internet and the school library are given as examples of places to search, and keywords as an example of a basis for searching; both follow the word "for example", so neither is a limit. The statement does not say what "systematically" consists of, names no search tool, and does not mention judging whether what is found is reliable.

### Common mistakes

A child types a whole question or one very broad word and takes the first result. A child reads a whole page from top to bottom to find one fact, without using headings, the index or a search function. A child copies a sentence that contains the keyword without checking that it answers the question.
