## Official wording

Kan jeugdliteratuur belevend lezen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 1F, Algemene omschrijving Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can read youth literature (*jeugdliteratuur*, books written for children and young people) in an experiencing way (*belevend*, experiencing the story while reading).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a chapter of an adventure story written for children and afterwards tells what it saw in its mind and which moment it found most exciting. While reading, the child laughs at a funny line, reads faster when the main character is in danger, and wants to know how the story goes on.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten* (reading fictional, narrative and literary texts), *Algemene omschrijving* (general description); the row label in full is *Algemene omschrijving Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*. It is the whole general description of this skill at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names one kind of text, youth literature, and one way of reading, *belevend*: reading in which the reader experiences what happens in the story. It names no titles, no authors, no genres and no length. The other statements of this skill at 1F say what understanding, interpreting and evaluating involve, and the statement under *Tekstkenmerken* describes the texts.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the words and forms no picture of what happens, so it cannot say what it saw or felt. A child picks a book that is too hard, needs all its attention for the words, and does not become involved in the story. A child reads only for what happens next and skips the passages that tell how a character feels.
