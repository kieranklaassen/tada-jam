## Official wording

De leerling voert gesprekken.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil holds conversations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school has a conversation with a classmate about how to divide the tasks of a shared project, asks a neighbour questions in an interview, and joins a group conversation about a book. The child listens, reacts to what the other person said, and says a point again in other words when the other person does not follow.

### Limits

This is the sentence of core goal 4 itself. Two goal sentences say what it covers, each with its own items: 4 A (conversations adjusted to purpose, partner or partners and context) and 4 B (conversations to arrive at knowledge building, understanding or an approach). The sentence names no kind of conversation, no subject, no number of partners and no length, and it does not say whether a conversation is face to face, by telephone or on a screen. The law sets no level for conversations here. Core goal 4 is one of four goals of the domain communication (*communicatie*), with core goals 2, 3 and 5. The reference levels 1F and 2F for Dutch, which are other records of this lane (section *Gesprekken*, conversations), are worded in more detail.

### Common mistakes

A child waits for a turn and does not take in what the other person says, so the reply does not connect with it. A child speaks to an adult they do not know in the same way as to a friend. Many children treat a conversation as something to win and keep their first opinion whatever is said.
