## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 explains what words and fixed phrases mean and names what kind they are. In the pack's examples the child says that *blij* and *vrolijk* (glad, cheerful) are synonyms; that *de kat uit de boom kijken* means, taken literally, watching the cat come out of the tree and, taken figuratively, waiting to see what happens before doing anything, and that it is an expression; that *Wie het laatst lacht, lacht het best* (Who laughs last, laughs best) is a proverb; and that *computer* is a loanword from English. The child says which language is its mother tongue and whether it speaks a dialect or another language at home.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Stilistiek en semantiek* (stylistics and semantics) of the list of terms (*Begrippenlijst*). It is the 1F cell of that row in the decree's table 1, which gives the terms about language by level. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The cell lists seventeen terms and nothing more: no definitions and no mark of how well a pupil must know them. The introduction to the list in the decree, which is in no record, says that a limited number of terms is needed for talking about language and that most of them have been dealt with by the end of primary school. *Gezegde* has two readings in Dutch: a saying, and the predicate of a sentence. Next to *spreekwoord* (proverb) in this row a saying fits; the same word stands in the row *Grammaticale kennis*, where the predicate fits. Because the cell gives no definitions, it does not say how an expression (*uitdrukking*), a proverb (*spreekwoord*) and a saying (*gezegde*) differ. *Formeel en informeel taalgebruik* is printed as one item.

### Common mistakes

A child takes a figurative expression literally. A child calls every fixed phrase a proverb; in common school usage a proverb is a whole fixed sentence that states a general lesson, and an expression is a fixed phrase that is fitted into a sentence. A child thinks that two synonyms can replace each other in every sentence.
