## Official wording

stimuleren van betekenisvolle activiteiten waarin school- en vaktaal en vakspecifieke taalvaardigheden verworven kunnen worden;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 1 B a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

stimulating meaningful activities in which school language and subject language (*school- en vaktaal*: the language of lessons and the terms of subjects) and subject-specific language skills can be acquired;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At a school that does this, a child of 11 or 12 carries out an experiment, a task with a map or a calculation with others and needs the words of the subject to do it: predict, measure, scale, source. The child hears the terms, uses them while working, and tells or writes what was done in the way the subject asks, for example a report with a question, a method and a result.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 1 B, which says that the school stimulates the pupil's language development in all learning areas; 1 B sits under core goal 1. Like 1 B, it says what the school does and is not a requirement on the pupil. The item says that the language "can be acquired" in the activities: it describes an opportunity and sets nothing a pupil must have acquired. Two things are named: school language and subject language, and subject-specific language skills. Neither is explained, and the item gives no word list and names no subjects. Item 3 C d, which is addressed to the pupil, also names school language and subject language. The item sets no level.

### Common mistakes

This item is about what the school does, so it has no typical error of a child. In such activities children most often do the task and describe it in everyday words, without the terms of the subject, or use a term they heard without knowing what it means.
