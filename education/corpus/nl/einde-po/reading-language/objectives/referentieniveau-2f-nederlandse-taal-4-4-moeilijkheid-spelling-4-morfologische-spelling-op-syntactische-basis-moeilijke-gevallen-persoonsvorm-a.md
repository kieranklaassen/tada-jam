## Official wording

a) homofone gevallen: tegenwoordige tijd stam op -d enkelvoud (hij wordt/word)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.4 Moeilijkheid, niveau 2F, Spelling / 4. Morfologische spelling op syntactische basis / Moeilijke gevallen / Persoonsvorm / a).

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

a) homophone cases: present tense, stem ending in -d, singular (*hij wordt/word*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 at this level writes the present tense of a verb whose stem ends in *d*, such as *worden*, *vinden* or *houden* (to become, to find, to hold), and adds the *t* that cannot be heard where the subject asks for it: *ik word* but *hij wordt*, *ik vind* but *zij vindt*. The child checks by putting in its place a verb in which the *t* can be heard: *ik loop*, *hij loopt* (I walk, he walks), so *ik word*, *hij wordt*.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, terms and language care, table of difficulty (*Moeilijkheid*), row a) under *Persoonsvorm* (finite verb) of the difficult cases (*Moeilijke gevallen*) under *Spelling*, 4. *Morfologische spelling op syntactische basis* (morphological spelling on a syntactic basis, where the spelling depends on the word's role in the sentence). In this table (Table 2 of the decree) the row has a plus sign in the column for level 2F. The table's caption describes its levels as mastery: 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of getting it right. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The row bounds the case in four ways: homophone forms (forms that sound the same and are written differently), the present tense, a stem ending in -d, and the singular. The brackets print after *hij* the two written forms that sound the same, *wordt* and *word*; with *hij* only *wordt* is correct, and *word* is the form that goes with *ik*. The row does not mention the order of subject and verb; the pack's examples keep the subject before the verb. The decree's running list of spelling rules in section 4.3.1, which carries no level and is not a record of the pack, prints *ik word* (I become), *jij wordt* (you become) and *hij wordt* (he becomes) for this case, each beside the form with the subject after the verb. The pack's examples, all correct Dutch: *ik vind* (I find), *zij vindt* (she finds), *hij houdt* (he holds), *de juf antwoordt* (the teacher answers).

### Common mistakes

A child writes what is heard and leaves off the *t* in the third person (the wrong *hij word* and *zij vind*). A child who has learned the rule writes *dt* everywhere, also in the first person (the wrong *ik wordt*). A child carries the *dt* over to the past tense and writes the wrong *hij werdt* where *hij werd* (he became) is right.
