## Official wording

De leerlingen verwerven een adequate woordenschat en strategieën voor het begrijpen van voor hen onbekende woorden. Onder «woordenschat» vallen ook begrippen die het leerlingen mogelijk maken over taal te denken en te spreken.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 12.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils acquire an adequate vocabulary (*adequate*, sufficient and fitting for its purpose) and strategies for understanding words unknown to them. "Vocabulary" also includes concepts that make it possible for pupils to think and speak about language.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 meets an unknown word in a text and acts on it: reads the sentences around it, looks for a known part inside the word (a prefix such as *on-*, an ending such as *-loos*, two words joined into one), looks at a picture, asks someone, or uses a dictionary. The child uses newly learned words when speaking and writing. The child also uses words about language, such as sentence, verb, synonym or rhyme, when talking about a text.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is goal 12 of the core goals for Dutch, the last of three under the heading *Taalbeschouwing, waaronder strategieën* (reflection on language, including strategies), goals 10 to 12. The first sentence names two things the pupils acquire: an adequate vocabulary, and strategies for understanding words unknown to them. The second sentence counts under vocabulary the concepts with which pupils think and speak about language. The goal gives no number of words and no word list, names no strategy and no concept, and does not say for what the vocabulary is adequate. It sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A pupil reads past an unknown word and does not notice that the sentence is no longer understood. A pupil guesses a meaning from one known part of the word and does not check the guess against the sentence. A pupil knows a word in one meaning only and is confused when it is used in another meaning or figuratively.
