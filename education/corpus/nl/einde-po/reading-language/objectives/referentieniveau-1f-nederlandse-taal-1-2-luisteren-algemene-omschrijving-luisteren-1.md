## Official wording

Kan luisteren naar eenvoudige teksten over alledaagse, concrete onderwerpen of over onderwerpen die aansluiten bij de leefwereld van de leerling.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 1F, Algemene omschrijving Luisteren / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can listen to simple texts about everyday, concrete topics or about topics that connect with the pupil's world of experience (*leefwereld*, the world the pupil lives in).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 listens to the teacher telling how the school vegetable garden will be laid out, to a classmate's talk about keeping rabbits, and to a short item in a children's news programme about a new skate park. Afterwards the pupil can say what each was about.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), *Algemene omschrijving* (general description). It is the whole general description of this skill at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The skill is listening, so "texts" (*teksten*) here are spoken texts. The statement bounds them in two ways: the texts are simple (*eenvoudige*), and the topics are everyday and concrete, or connect with the pupil's own world. It names no topics and no kinds of text. The length and build of the texts are worded in the statements under *Tekstkenmerken* (text features) of *Luisteren* at 1F, and what the pupil does with a text in the statements under tasks and under features of task performance, other records of this lane.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out when listening is checking their own understanding: they do not notice that they no longer follow, or they notice it and do not ask. They also tend to keep single details that interested them and to leave out what the text was mainly about.
