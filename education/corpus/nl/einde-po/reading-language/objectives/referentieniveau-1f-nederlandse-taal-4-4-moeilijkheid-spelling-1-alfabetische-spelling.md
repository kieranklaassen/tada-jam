1. Alphabetic spelling

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 writes a word as it sounds, with one letter or fixed letter pair for each sound, without having to think about it. The pack's examples: *pen* (pen), *boom* (tree), *stoel* (chair) and *muis* (mouse), in which every sound has its usual letter or letter pair (*oo*, *oe*, *ui*) and no rule changes the spelling.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Spelling*, class 1, of the table of difficulty. It is one row of the decree's table 2, in the section *Moeilijkheid* (difficulty), which sets levels for spelling, punctuation and the grammatical terms for verb spelling. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The table marks this row with one plus sign (+), in the 1F column. The heading of the table gives the measure of mastery (*beheersing*): 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of a correct answer. The heading does not itself name the plus sign; that the plus sign stands for this mastery at the level of its column is the pack's reading of the heading. The record holds the name of the class only. The running text of section 4.4 of the decree, which is in no record, describes the class: the spelling follows standard Dutch pronunciation, the same sound has the same letter, the basis is the link between sound and letter, also for pairs such as *oe* and *ui*, and the alternation of *v* and *f* and of *z* and *s* may be counted with it. That text says that this class is mastered by the end of *groep* 3 (about age 7).

### Common mistakes

A child leaves out a sound in a group of consonants and writes *kaf* for *kalf* (calf). A child swaps two letters and writes *weps* for *wesp* (wasp). A child writes a word as it is said in its own region and not as in standard pronunciation.
