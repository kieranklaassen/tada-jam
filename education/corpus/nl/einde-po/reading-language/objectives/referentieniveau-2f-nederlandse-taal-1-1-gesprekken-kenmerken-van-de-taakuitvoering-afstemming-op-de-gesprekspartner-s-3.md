## Official wording

Maakt de juiste keuze voor het register en het al dan niet hanteren van taalvariatie (dialect, jongerentaal).

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Afstemming op de gesprekspartner(s) / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Makes the right choice of register and of whether or not to use language variation (dialect, youth language).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 who speaks dialect at home uses it with a grandparent on the telephone and speaks standard Dutch when asking a question at the library desk. With friends the pupil uses the words young people use among themselves, and leaves them out in a conversation with the head teacher about a plan of the class.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), row *Afstemming op de gesprekspartner(s)* (attuning to the conversation partner or partners). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the third of three statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It is a fragment without a subject; the pupil is meant. The statement asks for the right choice on two points: the register (the kind of language that fits a situation) and whether or not to use language variation. The brackets name dialect and youth language (*jongerentaal*). The statement does not say what makes a choice right, names no situations, and does not ask that the pupil speak a dialect or avoid one. The same sentence opens a statement under *Spreken*, row *Afstemming op publiek*, another record of this lane.

### Common mistakes

A pupil uses youth language with an adult who does not know the words and is not understood. A pupil uses a formal register with friends, or an informal one in a formal talk, and does not notice the effect. A pupil thinks dialect is always wrong and avoids it where it fits, for example with family.
