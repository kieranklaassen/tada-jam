## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

b) present tense of (sound-constant or weak) verbs (*klankvaste of zwakke werkwoorden*), singular

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 at this level writes the singular present tense of a weak verb by finding the stem and adding *t* where the subject asks for it: *ik speel*, *jij speelt*, *hij speelt* (I play, you play, he plays). The child writes the stem with *f* or *s* where the infinitive has *v* or *z* (*leven*: *ik leef*, *zij leeft*; *reizen*: *ik reis*, *hij reist*) and adds no second *t* when the stem already ends in *t* (*hij zet*, *zij wacht*).

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, terms and language care, table of difficulty (*Moeilijkheid*), row b) under *Persoonsvorm* (finite verb) of the difficult cases (*Moeilijke gevallen*) under *Spelling*, 4. *Morfologische spelling op syntactische basis* (morphological spelling on a syntactic basis, where the spelling depends on the word's role in the sentence). In this table (Table 2 of the decree) the row has a plus sign in the column for level 2F. The table's caption describes its levels as mastery: 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of getting it right. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The row bounds the case in three ways: the present tense, the singular, and verbs that are *klankvast* or *zwak*. *Klankvast* (sound-constant: the vowel of the stem stays the same in the past tense) and *zwak* (weak) read as two names for one kind of verb, though the word "or" leaves open whether two kinds are meant. The row gives no example and no rule in words. The source joins "present tense" and the verbs without a linking word; the present tense of these verbs is plainly meant. The pack's examples, all correct Dutch: *ik speel*, *hij speelt* (I play, he plays); *ik leef*, *zij leeft* (I live, she lives); *ik reis*, *hij reist* (I travel, he travels); *ik zet*, *hij zet* (I put, he puts); *ik wacht*, *zij wacht* (I wait, she waits).

### Common mistakes

A child leaves off the *t* after *jij*, *hij* or *zij* where it is hard to hear (the wrong *hij fiets* and *zij lach*, where *hij fietst* and *zij lacht* are right). A child writes *d* for the final *t* under the influence of the past participle (the wrong *hij speeld*, compare *gespeeld*, played, where *hij speelt* is right). A child keeps the *v* or *z* of the infinitive in the stem (the wrong *hij leevt* and *hij reizt*, where *hij leeft* and *hij reist* are right).
