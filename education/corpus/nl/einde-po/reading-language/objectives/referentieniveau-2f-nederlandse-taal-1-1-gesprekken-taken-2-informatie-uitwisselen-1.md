## Official wording

Kan informatie vragen en geven aan instanties binnen en buiten school.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 2F, Taken / 2. Informatie uitwisselen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can ask for information from, and give information to, bodies (*instanties*, offices or organisations) inside and outside school.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 goes to the school office to ask when the forms for the sports day have to be handed in, and gives the secretary his or her name and class. The pupil telephones the public library to ask whether the class may visit and how many pupils may come at one time, and answers the librarian's questions about the date and the size of the group.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), row *Informatie uitwisselen* (exchanging information). It stands under *Taken* (tasks), as task 2, and is the first of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names two directions, asking for information and giving it, and one kind of partner: *instanties* inside and outside school. *Instantie* is a general word for an office, organisation or authority that people turn to; the statement names none, and the school office and the library are the pack's examples. The Dutch joins both verbs to the *instanties* with one word (*aan*), which the gloss renders as "from" and "to". The statement does not say whether the contact is face to face or by telephone, and it names no subjects.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out is the information the other side needs: they ask their question and do not say who they are, for whom they ask or what it is for. Many also end the contact without checking that they have understood the answer.
