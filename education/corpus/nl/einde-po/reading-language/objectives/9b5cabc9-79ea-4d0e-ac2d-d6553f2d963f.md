## Official wording

verkennen hoe woordvolgorde en zinsdelen de betekenis van een zin bepalen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 6 A c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring how word order and sentence parts (*zinsdelen*, the groups of words a sentence is built from) determine the meaning of a sentence;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays word cards in a row and swaps two: *De hond bijt de man* (the dog bites the man) becomes *De man bijt de hond* (the man bites the dog), and the child says who does what now (the pack's example). The child moves the verb to the front, *Bijt de hond de man?*, and hears a question. The child cuts a sentence into its parts and moves a whole part, such as *in de tuin* (in the garden), to another place.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 6 A, which says that the pupil considers the relation between the form and the meaning of language; 6 A sits under core goal 6. The item names two things that determine the meaning of a sentence: word order and sentence parts. It names no sentence part (no subject, verb or object) and no word class. The verb is exploring; the item does not mention parsing, the naming of every part of a sentence. It sets no level and no sentence length.

### Common mistakes

A child takes the first noun of a sentence for the one who acts, so in *De man wordt door de hond gebeten* (the man is bitten by the dog) the man is taken to bite (the pack's example). A child moves single words where a whole part has to move, and gets a sentence that is not Dutch. A child sees no difference in meaning between two word orders that stress different things.
