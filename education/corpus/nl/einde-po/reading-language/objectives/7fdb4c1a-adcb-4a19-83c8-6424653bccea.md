## Official wording

benoemen van inhoudelijke relaties binnen en tussen verschillende teksten;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 B a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming relations in content within and between different texts;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a text about bees and says that the second paragraph gives the cause of what the fourth paragraph describes. Then the child reads a second text, about fruit growing, and says how the two connect: the first says that there are fewer bees, the second says what that means for fruit growers. The child points to the sentences that the link is based on.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 2 B, which says that the pupil evaluates and reflects on non-fiction and literary texts; 2 B sits under core goal 2. It names relations in two places: inside one text and between different texts. The item gives no example of a relation in content; the examples above are the pack's. It does not say how many texts are compared or how long they are. The verb is naming. The item sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child says that two texts are "about the same thing" and cannot say what connects them. A child connects two sentences because they stand next to each other and not because of what they say. A child treats each paragraph as separate and does not see that a later one explains an earlier one.
