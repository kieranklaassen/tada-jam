## Official wording

Kan een duidelijk verhaal houden met een samenhangende opsomming van punten en kan daarbij het belangrijkste punt duidelijk maken.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Samenhang / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can give a clear *verhaal* (a story, or an account or talk) with a coherent enumeration of points and can in doing so make the most important point clear.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 tells the class why the school should have a vegetable garden. The pupil gives the points one after another, each connected with the one before (it costs little, so every class can take part, and what grows can be eaten at school), and says before and after the list: "The most important thing is that we learn where food comes from." The class can say afterwards which point was the most important.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), row *Samenhang* (coherence). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the first of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. *Verhaal* has two readings in Dutch: a story, and an account or talk (*een verhaal houden* is to give a talk or to tell one's account); the gloss keeps the Dutch. The statement asks for three things: the *verhaal* is clear, it has a coherent enumeration of points, and the pupil makes the most important point clear. It names no number of points and no subject, sets no speaking time, and does not say by what means the most important point is made clear; the repeated sentence in the illustration is the pack's example.

### Common mistakes

A pupil gives the points in the order he or she thinks of them, joined by "and then", and the listener cannot tell how they belong together. A pupil treats every point as equally important, and the listener cannot tell which one matters most. A pupil names the most important point once, in the middle, and does not return to it.
