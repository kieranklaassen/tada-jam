## Official wording

benoemen van kenmerkend taalgebruik in literaire genres: figuurlijk taalgebruik, rijm en ritme;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 9 B b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming characteristic language use in literary genres: figurative language, rhyme and rhythm;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a poem aloud and claps or taps the beat. The child underlines the words that rhyme and says where in the lines they stand. The child finds a comparison or a phrase that does not mean what the words say, such as "the wind whispered", and says what is meant.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 9 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into genre features of literature; 9 B sits under core goal 9. After the colon the item names three: figurative language, rhyme and rhythm; it does not say whether the list is complete. It asks for naming. It names no kinds of figurative language (such as comparison or personification) and no kinds of rhyme, and it does not say in which genres.

### Common mistakes

A pupil takes figurative language literally ("the wind cannot whisper"). A pupil counts as rhyme only the last words of two lines that follow each other, and misses rhyme that skips a line. A pupil mixes up rhythm with the speed of reading.
