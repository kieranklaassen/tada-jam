## Official wording

De teksten hebben een heldere structuur. Verbanden in de tekst worden duidelijk aangegeven. De teksten hebben overwegend een lage informatiedichtheid en zijn niet te lang.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 2F, Teksten / Tekstkenmerken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The texts have a clear structure. Connections in the text are clearly indicated. The texts have predominantly a low information density and are not too long.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who reads at this level reads a text with a title, headings and paragraphs that each treat one point. Words such as *omdat*, *daarom* and *maar* (because, therefore, but) show how the sentences are connected, so the child does not have to work out the connection alone. Most paragraphs do not bring many new facts at once, and they explain the facts they bring.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading factual texts (*Lezen zakelijke teksten*), row *Tekstkenmerken* (text characteristics). The row stands under *Teksten* (texts). 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement describes the texts read at this level, not what the pupil does. It gives four characteristics: a clear structure, connections that are clearly indicated, an information density that is "predominantly" low (so not in every passage), and a length that is "not too long". It gives no number of words or pages, no measure of information density, and no titles.

### Common mistakes

This statement describes texts, so it is not a skill with a typical error. A designer who chooses a text for this level looks at the text itself: whether its structure is easy to see, whether the connections are marked, for example with a linking word between a cause and its effect, and whether passages with many new facts in few sentences are the exception. What pupils most often leave unused in such a text are the signs it gives: they skip the headings and read over the linking words.
