## Official wording

Kan schematische informatie lezen en relaties met de tekst expliciteren.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Opzoeken / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can read schematic information and make relations with the text explicit.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a small table of rainfall per month that stands beside a text about the weather. The child reads along the row and down the column to find the wettest month, then points to the sentence in the text that is about the same thing and says how the two fit: "the text says autumn is the wettest season, and the table shows the highest number in October."

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Zakelijke teksten* (reading factual texts), *Opzoeken* (looking up). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the second of two statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement has two parts: reading schematic information, and saying in words how it relates to the text. It does not name kinds of schematic information such as a table, a diagram or a graph, and it sets no bound on how complex the scheme is; the statement under *Lezen van informatieve teksten* speaks of simple schematic overviews. It names no number range.

### Common mistakes

A child looks at the scheme and the text as two separate things and does not notice that they are about the same information. A child reads a number from a table without reading the heading of its row or column, so it does not know what the number stands for. A child follows the wrong row or column to the crossing point.
