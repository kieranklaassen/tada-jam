## Official wording

schrijven op letter-, schrift- en tekstniveau met een leesbaar handschrift en typschrift, en verstaanbaar spreken;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 3 A d.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing at the level of the letter, the script (*schrift*) and the text with legible handwriting and typed script (*typschrift*, text typed on a keyboard), and speaking intelligibly;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 writes a card by hand so that someone else can read it without asking: the letters are formed clearly, the words stand apart, the lines are straight and the page has a margin and paragraphs. The child types the same text on a keyboard with spaces, capitals and new lines in the right places. When speaking to a group, the child speaks loudly and clearly enough to be understood at the back of the room.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 3 A, which says that the pupil speaks and writes in a way adjusted to purpose, audience and context; 3 A sits under core goal 3. It has three parts: writing at three levels (letter, script, text), legibility of both handwriting and typed script, and intelligible speaking. The law does not explain the three levels. *Schrift* can be read as the running script (how letters are joined into words and lines) or as writing in general; the pack does not choose. The item states no speed of writing or typing, no kind of script (joined or unjoined) and no typing method. Item 3 C e repeats the writing part without the speaking.

### Common mistakes

A child writes legibly when copying and illegibly when writing a text of the child's own. A child forms the letters clearly and leaves no space between words, or lets the lines slope upward. When speaking to a group, a child speaks too fast or too softly, or drops the ends of words.
