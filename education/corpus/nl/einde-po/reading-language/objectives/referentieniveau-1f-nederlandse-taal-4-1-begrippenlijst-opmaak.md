## Official wording

Bladzijde, woord, zin, hoofdletter, uitspraak, titel, hoofdstuk, regel, lettertype, alinea, kopje.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.1 Begrippenlijst, niveau 1F, Opmaak.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Page (*bladzijde*), word (*woord*), sentence (*zin*), capital letter (*hoofdletter*), *uitspraak* (pronunciation, or a statement someone makes), title (*titel*), chapter (*hoofdstuk*), *regel* (a line of text, or a rule), typeface (*lettertype*), paragraph (*alinea*), heading (*kopje*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 opens a book and points out what each term names: the title on the cover, a chapter, a heading above a paragraph, the paragraph, one line in it, a sentence, a word, and the capital letter at the start of the sentence. The child notices that the heading is printed in another typeface than the text, and says on which page it found something.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Opmaak* (layout) of the list of terms (*Begrippenlijst*). It is the 1F cell of that row in the decree's table 1, which gives the terms about language by level. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The cell lists eleven terms and nothing more: no definitions and no mark of how well a pupil must know them. The introduction to the list in the decree, which is in no record, says that a limited number of terms is needed for talking about language and that most of them have been dealt with by the end of primary school. Two of the terms have two readings in Dutch, and the cell does not say which is meant: *uitspraak* is pronunciation or a statement that someone makes, and *regel* is a line of text or a rule. Among terms for layout a line of text fits *regel*; neither reading of *uitspraak* is a term for layout, and the source prints it in this row. *Alinea* is the Dutch for a paragraph; the Dutch word *paragraaf*, a numbered section of a text, is a term in the row printed as *Testkennis*.

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up *alinea* (paragraph), *regel* (line) and *zin* (sentence), for example counts lines when asked for sentences. A child calls a heading inside the text a title. A child calls every large or bold letter a capital letter.
