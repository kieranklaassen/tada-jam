## Official wording

2. Vraagtekens, uitroeptekens en aanhalingstekens

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.4 Moeilijkheid, niveau 1F, Leestekens / 2. Vraagtekens, uitroeptekens en aanhalingstekens.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

2. Question marks, exclamation marks and quotation marks

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 ends a question with a question mark and a call or a strong feeling with an exclamation mark, and puts quotation marks around the words that someone says. The pack's examples: *Waar is mijn jas?* (Where is my coat?); *Wat een mooie tekening!* (What a fine drawing!); and *'Ik kom eraan!' riep hij.* ('I am coming!' he called.), with one quotation mark before the spoken words and one after them.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Leestekens* (punctuation marks), item 2, of the table of difficulty. It is one row of the decree's table 2, in the section *Moeilijkheid* (difficulty), which sets levels for spelling, punctuation and the grammatical terms for verb spelling. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The table marks this row with one plus sign (+), in the 1F column. The heading of the table gives the measure of mastery (*beheersing*): 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of a correct answer. The heading does not itself name the plus sign; that the plus sign stands for this mastery at the level of its column is the pack's reading of the heading. The record holds the name of the item only: three marks, with no rules for their use. The comma and the colon, which often stand next to quotation marks in direct speech, and the capital letter in direct speech are in other rows of the table, and those rows carry no plus sign in the 1F column.

### Common mistakes

A child ends a question with a full stop. A child puts a question mark after an indirect question and writes *Hij vroeg of ik meeging?*; correct is *Hij vroeg of ik meeging.* (He asked whether I was coming along.) A child opens quotation marks and does not close them, or puts *zei hij* (he said) inside them as well.
