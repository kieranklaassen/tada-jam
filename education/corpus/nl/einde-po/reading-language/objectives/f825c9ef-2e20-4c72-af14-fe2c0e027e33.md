## Official wording

De leerling verkent taalvariatie en taalverandering in het Nederlandse taalgebied.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 7 B.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil explores language variation and language change in the Dutch language area (*het Nederlandse taalgebied*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 listens to recordings of people from different regions who say the same sentence, and marks what differs. The child asks a grandparent for words that were common when the grandparent was young, and collects words that came into Dutch not long ago, such as *appen* (to send a message with a chat app; the pack's example).

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 7, which is about exploring the use of language. Its items 7 B a to 7 B e name varieties of Dutch, the contexts in which languages and varieties are used, similarities and differences between Dutch and other languages and varieties, beliefs about languages and varieties, and changes in language use. The sentence names two subjects, variation and change, and one area: the Dutch language area. It does not say which countries or regions that area covers. The verb is exploring: the sentence does not ask the pupil to speak or understand a variety, and the law sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks a regional variety is Dutch spoken badly. A child thinks language used to be fixed and changes only now, or that new words make the language worse. A child takes the way people around them speak for the Dutch that everyone speaks.
