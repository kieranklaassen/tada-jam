## Official wording

Kan gesprekken voeren om informatie en meningen uit te wisselen, uitleg of instructie te geven en te volgen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Afstemming op doel / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can hold conversations in order to exchange information and opinions, to give and to follow explanation or *instructie* (instructions for a task, or teaching).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 tells a classmate what time the swimming lesson starts and asks which bus goes there, and the two say what they think of the new playground rules. The pupil explains to a younger child how a board game is played, step by step. Later the pupil follows a classmate's explanation of how to fold a paper box and does each step.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), *Afstemming op doel* (fit to the purpose). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance). The row has one other statement at 1F, on recognising conversation situations and using fitting routines. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names what conversations at this level are for: exchanging information, exchanging opinions, and giving and following explanation or *instructie*. *Instructie* can mean instructions for carrying something out or teaching how something is done; the statement does not choose. The source joins the purposes with a comma where "and" would be expected; the gloss follows the source. The statement names no topics and no partners, and it names no other purposes.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. In an explanation pupils most often leave out a step or the order of the steps, because they assume that the listener knows what they know. In an exchange of opinions they most often leave out the question back: they give their own view and do not ask for the other person's.
