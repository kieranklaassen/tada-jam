## Official wording

onderscheiden van poëzie-, proza- en dramateksten op basis van eenvoudige genrekenmerken;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 9 B a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing poetry, prose and drama texts on the basis of simple genre features;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 gets three sheets without titles and says which is a poem, which is part of a story and which is a scene of a play. The child gives the features: lines that stop before the edge of the page, and stanzas; sentences that run on in paragraphs; the name of a speaker before each turn, and notes on what the players do.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 9 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into genre features of literature; 9 B sits under core goal 9. The item names three kinds of text: poetry, prose and drama. The genre features are simple ones (*eenvoudige*), and the item does not list them. It asks for telling these three apart; telling apart kinds of prose, such as a fairy tale and a detective story, is not mentioned in it. It names no titles and no level of text.

### Common mistakes

A pupil decides by rhyme alone: a rhyming story is called a poem, and a poem without rhyme is called a story. A pupil takes a story with much dialogue for a play. A pupil decides by the subject and not by the form of the text.
