## Official wording

Kan de gesprekspartner(s) redelijk volgen tenzij ze voor onverwachte wendingen in het gesprek zorgen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Afstemming op de gesprekspartner(s) / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can follow the conversation partner(s) reasonably well unless they cause unexpected turns in the conversation.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 plans a class party with two classmates and follows who brings what, as long as the talk stays on the party. When one of them suddenly starts about a football match of the evening before, the pupil no longer knows what the talk is about and asks, "Wait, what are we talking about now?" After such a turn the pupil needs a moment to follow again.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), *Afstemming op de gesprekspartner(s)* (fit to the conversation partner or partners). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance). The row has one other statement at 1F, on supporting words with non-verbal behaviour. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. Two wordings set the level: the pupil follows the partner or partners "reasonably well" (*redelijk*), not fully, and the statement makes an exception for unexpected turns in the conversation. It does not say what counts as an unexpected turn, and it does not say what the pupil does when one occurs. The bracketed plural shows that there may be one partner or more; no number is named.

### Common mistakes

A pupil keeps answering the earlier subject after the partner has moved on to another one. A pupil who no longer follows nods and says nothing instead of asking what was meant. In a group a pupil follows the last speaker only and misses what an earlier speaker said.
