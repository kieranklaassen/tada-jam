## Official wording

c) verleden tijd (klankvaste of zwakke) werkwoorden met stam op -d of -t

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.4 Moeilijkheid, niveau 2F, Spelling / 4. Morfologische spelling op syntactische basis / Moeilijke gevallen / Persoonsvorm / c).

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

c) past tense of (sound-constant or weak) verbs (*klankvaste of zwakke werkwoorden*) with a stem ending in -d or -t

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 at this level writes the past tense of a weak verb whose stem ends in *d* or *t* by adding *-de* or *-te* to the whole stem, so the letter is written twice although only one is heard: *antwoorden*, stem *antwoord*, *hij antwoordde* (he answered); *wachten*, stem *wacht*, *zij wachtte* (she waited). For the plural the child adds *-den* or *-ten*: *wij antwoordden* (we answered), *zij wachtten* (they waited).

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, terms and language care, table of difficulty (*Moeilijkheid*), row c) under *Persoonsvorm* (finite verb) of the difficult cases (*Moeilijke gevallen*) under *Spelling*, 4. *Morfologische spelling op syntactische basis* (morphological spelling on a syntactic basis, where the spelling depends on the word's role in the sentence). In this table (Table 2 of the decree) the row has a plus sign in the column for level 2F. The table's caption describes its levels as mastery: 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of getting it right. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The row bounds the case in three ways: the past tense, verbs that are *klankvast* or *zwak*, and a stem ending in -d or -t. *Klankvast* (sound-constant: the vowel of the stem stays the same in the past tense) and *zwak* (weak) read as two names for one kind of verb, though the word "or" leaves open whether two kinds are meant. The row gives no example and no rule in words, and it does not mention singular or plural. The source joins "past tense" and the verbs without a linking word; the past tense of these verbs is plainly meant. The decree prints *antwoordde* for this case in its running list of spelling rules in section 4.3.1, which carries no level and is not a record of the pack, and *antwoordde(n)* in the row *4. Morfologische spelling op syntactische basis* of this table, another record of this lane, which has its plus sign in the 1F column. The pack's examples, all correct Dutch: *zij wachtte* (she waited), *zij wachtten* (they waited), *hij praatte* (he talked), *het vliegtuig landde* (the plane landed), *zij redde de kat* (she rescued the cat); and, with the decree's verb, *hij antwoordde* (he answered) and *wij antwoordden* (we answered).

### Common mistakes

A child writes one *d* or *t* because only one is heard (the wrong *hij antwoorde* and *zij wachte*). A child writes the past plural like the present plural (*zij wachten* where the past *zij wachtten* is meant), because both sound the same. A child doubles the letter in a verb whose stem does not end in *d* or *t* (the wrong *hij fietstte* where *hij fietste*, he cycled, is right).
