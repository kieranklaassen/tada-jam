## Official wording

inzetten en uitbreiden van school- en vaktaal;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 3 C d.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting to use and extending school language and subject language (*school- en vaktaal*: the language of lessons and the terms of subjects);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who writes about an experiment uses the words of the subject and of school work: evaporate, compare, cause, result. (The pack's example in Dutch: the child writes *het water verdampt*, the water evaporates, where the child would say *het water gaat weg*, the water goes away, to a friend.) The child keeps a list of new terms, each with its meaning in the child's own words, and uses them the next time.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 3 C, which says that the pupil writes in order to build knowledge or reach understanding; 3 C sits under core goal 3. It names school language and subject language as one pair, and two things done with them: putting them to use and extending them. It gives no word list and no number of words, and names no subjects. The item sits under a goal sentence about writing. Item 1 B a, which is addressed to the school, also names school language and subject language. The item sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child uses an everyday word where the subject has a precise term. A child uses a subject term without knowing what it means. A child knows a word from everyday use and does not see that it means something else in a subject (the pack's example: the Dutch word *tafel* is a table to sit at and also a multiplication table).
