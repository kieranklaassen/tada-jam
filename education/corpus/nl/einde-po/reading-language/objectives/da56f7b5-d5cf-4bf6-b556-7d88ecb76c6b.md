## Official wording

inzicht tonen in de opbouw van basale woorden om de betekenis af te leiden;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 6 A b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

showing insight into the structure of basic words (*basale woorden*) in order to derive the meaning;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 meets *onbreekbaar* (unbreakable) in a text, covers *on-* and *-baar* with two fingers and finds *breek* (break). The child says: "*-baar* means it can be done, *on-* means not, so it cannot be broken" (the pack's example). The child splits *fietsenmaker* into *fietsen* (bicycles) and *maker* (maker) and says it is someone who repairs bicycles.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 6 A, which says that the pupil considers the relation between the form and the meaning of language; 6 A sits under core goal 6. The item states the purpose: deriving the meaning. It speaks of basic words (*basale woorden*) and does not say which words count as basic. It gives no list of words, prefixes or suffixes, and it does not name compound words. The verb is showing insight, where the neighbouring items 6 A a, 6 A c and 6 A d say exploring. The item sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child finds a known word inside a longer word where it is not a building block: *kast* (cupboard) in *kasteel* (castle) (the pack's example). A child takes the first part of a compound word as its main meaning, so a *tafelpoot* (table leg) is taken for a kind of table (the pack's example). A child skips a long unknown word without trying to take it apart.
