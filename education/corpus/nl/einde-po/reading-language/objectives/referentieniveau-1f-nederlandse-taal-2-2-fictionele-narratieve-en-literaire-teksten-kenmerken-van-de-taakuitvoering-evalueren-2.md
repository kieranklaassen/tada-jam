## Official wording

Kan met medeleerlingen leeservaringen uitwisselen. Kan interesse in bepaalde fictievormen aangeven.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Evalueren / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can exchange reading experiences with fellow pupils. Can indicate an interest in certain forms of fiction.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 tells another pupil what a book it read was about and what it thought of it, listens to what the other pupil read, and asks a question about it. The child says which kinds of fiction it likes to read, for example stories about animals, comic strips or frightening stories.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten* (reading fictional, narrative and literary texts), *Evalueren* (evaluating). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the second of two statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The first sentence names the partners: fellow pupils (*medeleerlingen*). The second speaks of "certain forms of fiction" (*bepaalde fictievormen*) and does not name them. The statement does not say whether the exchange is spoken or written, and it names no number of books and no titles.

### Common mistakes

A child tells the whole story and says nothing about its own experience of reading it. A child tells about its own book and does not react to what the other pupil tells. A child says it likes "everything" or "nothing" and cannot name a kind of story.
