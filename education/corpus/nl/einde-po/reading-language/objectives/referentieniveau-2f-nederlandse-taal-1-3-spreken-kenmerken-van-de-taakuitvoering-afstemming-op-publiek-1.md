## Official wording

Kan het verschil tussen formele en informele situaties hanteren.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Afstemming op publiek / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can handle (*hanteren*, to use or deal with) the difference between formal and informal situations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 tells friends at the lunch table about a new game in quick, loose sentences with the group's own words for things. Presenting the same game to the class and the teacher, the pupil greets the audience, speaks in full sentences and leaves out the playground words. At the leaving ceremony of the school the pupil thanks the teachers in the name of the class and addresses the parents with *u* (the polite Dutch form of "you").

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), row *Afstemming op publiek* (attuning to the audience). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the first of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names the difference between formal and informal situations and asks that the pupil can handle it. It names no situations of either kind and no forms of address or words that belong to them; the polite form *u* in the illustration is the pack's example. A statement with the same wording stands under *Gesprekken*, row *Afstemming op de gesprekspartner(s)*, another record of this lane; here the row heading names an audience.

### Common mistakes

A pupil gives a talk to an audience in the same way as a chat with friends, with jokes that only the friends understand. A pupil reads a formal text aloud word for word and does not look at or speak to the listeners. A pupil starts in a formal tone and changes to informal words when a question comes.
