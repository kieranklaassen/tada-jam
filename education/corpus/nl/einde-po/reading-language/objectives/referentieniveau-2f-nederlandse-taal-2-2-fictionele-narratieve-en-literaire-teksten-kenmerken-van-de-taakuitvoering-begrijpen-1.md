## Official wording

Herkent het genre.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Recognises the genre.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads the first pages of a book and says what kind of story it is, for example a detective story, a fantasy story, a historical story or a story about daily life, and names what shows it: a crime that must be solved, magic, or a setting long ago. The child sorts a pile of books and poems by kind and says of a poem that it is a poem because of its lines and verses.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading fictional, narrative and literary texts (*Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*), row *Begrijpen* (understanding). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance); this is the first of the five paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The verb is recognising: the statement does not ask the pupil to define a genre or to write in one. It names no genres and no number of genres. It stands in the section on fictional, narrative and literary texts, so the genres are those of such texts. It names no titles.

### Common mistakes

A child names the topic ("it is about horses") where the genre is asked. A child decides by one outward feature, for example calls every story with an animal in it a fairy tale. A child does not see that a story can show features of two genres, such as a detective story set in the past.
