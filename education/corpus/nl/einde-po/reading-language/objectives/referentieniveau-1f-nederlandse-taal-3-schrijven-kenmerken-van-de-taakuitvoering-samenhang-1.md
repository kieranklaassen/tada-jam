## Official wording

De informatie is zodanig geordend, dat de lezer de gedachtegang gemakkelijk kan volgen en het schrijfdoel bereikt wordt. De meest bekende voegwoorden (en, maar, want, omdat) zijn correct gebruikt, met andere voegwoorden komen nog fouten voor.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 3 Schrijven, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Samenhang / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The information is ordered in such a way that the reader can easily follow the line of thought and the purpose of the writing is achieved. The best-known conjunctions (*en*, *maar*, *want*, *omdat*: and, but, for, because) are used correctly; with other conjunctions errors still occur.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 writes how to feed its rabbit and puts the steps in the order in which they are done. The child joins sentences with the four conjunctions and gets the word order right. The pack's examples: *Ik ging naar huis, want het regende* (I went home, for it was raining), where the verb keeps its normal place after *want*; and *Ik bleef thuis omdat ik ziek was* (I stayed at home because I was ill), where the verb goes to the end after *omdat*.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Schrijven* (writing), *Samenhang* (coherence). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the first of two statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement describes the written text. The order of the information has two tests: the reader can easily follow the line of thought, and the purpose of the writing is achieved. Four conjunctions are named as used correctly: *en*, *maar*, *want* and *omdat*. With other conjunctions the statement says that errors still occur, so correct use of those is not part of this statement. It names no length and no kind of text.

### Common mistakes

A child uses the word order of a main clause after *omdat* and writes *omdat ik was ziek*; correct is *omdat ik ziek was*. A child joins every sentence to the next with *en toen* ("and then"). A child writes the facts in the order in which it thought of them, so the reader has to put them in order.
