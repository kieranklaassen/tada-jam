## Official wording

verwoorden, onderbouwen en ordenen van gedachten, verworven inzichten en kennis in een tekst of schema;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 3 C c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting into words, substantiating and ordering thoughts, acquired insights and knowledge in a text or diagram (*schema*, also a chart or outline);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has done an experiment with plants in the light and in the dark, and writes what the child found, what the child concludes and what the conclusion is based on ("the plant in the cupboard turned yellow, so ..."). The child orders the points in a table with two columns, in a diagram with arrows, or in a text with a paragraph for each point.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 3 C, which says that the pupil writes in order to build knowledge or reach understanding; 3 C sits under core goal 3. It names three actions (putting into words, substantiating and ordering), three things they are done to (thoughts, acquired insights and knowledge) and two forms (a text or a diagram). The Dutch *schema* covers a diagram, a chart and an outline. The item does not say what counts as substantiating: it names no rule for reasons, evidence or sources. It names no subjects and sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child states a conclusion and gives no reason for it. A child gives a reason that only repeats the claim in other words. A child writes the points down in the order in which the child thought of them, so a reader cannot see what belongs together.
