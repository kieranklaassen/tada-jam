## Official wording

Kan in grote lijnen redenen en verklaringen geven voor eigen meningen, plannen en handelingen en kan een kort verhaal vertellen. Kan informatie verzamelen om over een onderwerp uit eigen interessegebied een voorbereide presentatie te geven. Kan vragen beantwoorden naar aanleiding van deze presentatie.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 2F, Taken / Een monoloog houden / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can give, in broad outline, reasons and explanations for one's own opinions, plans and actions and can tell a short *verhaal* (a story, or an account). Can collect information in order to give a prepared presentation about a subject from one's own field of interest. Can answer questions arising from this presentation.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 tells the class why he or she wants to join the scouts and why he or she stopped judo, giving the main reasons and not every detail, and tells briefly what happened at the first scout meeting. For a presentation about sharks the pupil looks things up in two library books and on a website, prepares the presentation and gives it to the class. When a classmate asks "Do sharks sleep?", the pupil answers from what he or she found.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), row *Een monoloog houden* (holding a monologue). It stands under *Taken* (tasks) and is the only statement in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The record holds three sentences. The first asks for reasons and explanations for the pupil's own opinions, plans and actions, with the qualifier *in grote lijnen* (in broad outline), and for telling a *verhaal* that is short; *verhaal* can be a story or an account, and the gloss keeps the Dutch. The second asks for collecting information for a presentation that is prepared and whose subject comes from the pupil's own field of interest. The third asks for answering questions that arise from that presentation. The statement sets no speaking time and no number of sources, names no aids such as slides, and does not say who the audience is or who asks the questions.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out are the reasons: they state an opinion or a plan and do not say why. In a presentation many read the collected information aloud as they found it, and then cannot answer a question that their text does not answer.
