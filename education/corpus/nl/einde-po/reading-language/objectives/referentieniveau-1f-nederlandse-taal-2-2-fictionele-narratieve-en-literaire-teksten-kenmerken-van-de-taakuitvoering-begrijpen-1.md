## Official wording

Herkent basale structuurelementen, zoals wisselingen van tijd en plaats, rijm en versvorm. Kan meeleven met een personage en uitleggen hoe een personage zich voelt. Kan gedichten en verhaalfragmenten parafraseren of samenvatten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Recognises basic structural elements, such as changes of time and place, rhyme and verse form. Can feel along with a character and explain how a character feels. Can paraphrase or summarise poems and story fragments.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a story fragment and says where it jumps: "here it is the next morning" or "now they are at the grandmother's house". In a poem the child points to the words that rhyme at the ends of the lines and says that each stanza has four lines. The child explains that a girl in the story is frightened because she hears footsteps and nobody answers, and then retells the fragment in a few sentences of its own.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten* (reading fictional, narrative and literary texts), *Begrijpen* (understanding). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement has three sentences. In the first, the structural elements are basic (*basale*), and after "such as" (*zoals*) come the examples: changes of time and place, rhyme and verse form; they are examples of the level, not a limit. The second asks two things about a character: feeling along with it, and explaining how it feels. The third names poems and story fragments, not whole books, and joins paraphrasing and summarising with "or". The statement names no titles and no length.

### Common mistakes

A child misses a jump back in time and reads the events as if they followed one another. A child names the feeling it would have itself instead of the feeling the text shows for the character. A child's summary retells the fragment sentence by sentence and is as long as the fragment.
