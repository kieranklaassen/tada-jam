## Official wording

Aarzelingen en fouten in zinsbouw zijn eigen aan gesproken taal en komen dus voor, maar worden zonodig hersteld.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Hesitations and errors in sentence construction are characteristic of spoken language and therefore occur, but are repaired where necessary.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 says in a group talk: "Then we went, eh, then the bus did not come, so we ... I mean, so we had to walk." The pupil hears that the sentence went wrong and starts it again, because the others would not have understood it otherwise. A small error that confuses nobody is left as it is.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), row *Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing* (fluency, intelligibility and command of grammar). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the third of three statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement says two things: hesitations and errors in sentence construction belong to spoken language and so occur, and they are repaired where necessary. The source prints *zonodig* as one word (present-day spelling: *zo nodig*). The statement does not say who repairs them; the speaker is plainly meant. It does not say when a repair is necessary, and it does not ask that every error be repaired or that speech be free of hesitations.

### Common mistakes

A pupil leaves a sentence that went wrong unrepaired, and the listener no longer knows what was meant. A pupil repairs every small error and restarts so often that the account is hard to follow. A pupil fills every hesitation with the same filler word.
