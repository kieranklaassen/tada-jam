## Official wording

verkennen hoe letterklanken, klemtoon, intonatie en ritme samenhangen met de betekenis van taal;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 6 A a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring how letter sounds (*letterklanken*, the sounds that letters stand for), stress, intonation and rhythm are connected with the meaning of language;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says *voorkomen* twice, once with the stress on *voor* (to occur) and once with the stress on *ko* (to prevent), and hears that the stress changes the meaning (the pack's example). The child changes one sound and gets another word: *man* (man) becomes *maan* (moon). The child says one sentence as a statement, as a question and as an order, and claps the rhythm of a line of verse.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 6 A, which says that the pupil considers the relation between the form and the meaning of language; 6 A sits under core goal 6. The item names four features of sound: letter sounds, stress, intonation and rhythm. The verb is exploring; the item asks for no rule and no term. It gives no example and no list of words, and it sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child reads aloud in one tone and does not hear that a question and a statement sound different. A child puts the stress on the wrong part of a word they know only from reading. A child does not hear the difference between a short and a long vowel sound, as in *man* and *maan*, and takes one word for the other (the pack's example).
