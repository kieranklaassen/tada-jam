## Official wording

De leerlingen leren informatie en meningen te ordenen bij het lezen van school- en studieteksten en andere instructieve teksten, bij systematisch geordende bronnen, waaronder digitale.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 6.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to order information and opinions (*ordenen*, to arrange, to put in order) when reading school and study texts and other instructive texts, with systematically ordered sources, including digital ones.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a chapter of a school book about the water cycle and makes a diagram or a list: the main points, the details under each, and apart from these what the writer thinks. With a dictionary, an encyclopedia or a website with a menu, the child uses the alphabet, the table of contents or the headings to find an entry and puts what is found under the right heading of the list.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is goal 6 of the core goals for Dutch, under the heading *Schriftelijk taalonderwijs* (written language education), goals 4 to 9. The goal says what is ordered (information and opinions) and where: when reading school and study texts and other instructive texts, and with systematically ordered sources, including digital ones. The source joins the second phrase to the first with a comma and no "and"; it is glossed as printed and read here as a second setting. The goal gives no examples of systematically ordered sources, does not say how the ordering is done or shown, and sets no level. Ordering when writing is goal 8.

### Common mistakes

A pupil copies sentences from the text in the order in which they stand and calls that a summary. A pupil does not tell a main point from a detail, or a fact from the writer's opinion. In an alphabetical source a pupil looks only at the first letter of a word.
