## Official wording

inzetten van hulpmiddelen en bronnen om regels en procedures correct toe te passen en teksten te redigeren.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 6 B e.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting aids and sources to use in order to apply rules and procedures correctly and to edit texts.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks up a word in a dictionary or a word list, and follows the steps on a card for verb spelling. The child uses a spelling checker on a typed text and decides for each marked word whether the marking is right. Then the child corrects the text.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 6 B, which says that the pupil shows insight into rules and procedures for spelling, phrasing and punctuation; 6 B sits under core goal 6. The item names two purposes: applying rules and procedures correctly, and editing texts. It names no aid and no source: no dictionary, rule card or spelling checker is named, and digital aids are neither named nor excluded. It does not say whose texts are edited. The item sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child accepts everything a spelling checker suggests and trusts what it does not mark: a checker that looks at single words does not mark *hij word*, because *word* exists as a form, and correct is *hij wordt* (he becomes) (the pack's example). A child cannot find a word in a dictionary, because they look under the wrong first letters or under a conjugated form. A child uses no aid at all, because looking something up takes time.
