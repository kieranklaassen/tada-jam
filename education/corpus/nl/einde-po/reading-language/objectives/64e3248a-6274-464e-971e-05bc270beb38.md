## Official wording

experimenteren met klanken, woorden, zinnen, literaire genres, taalregels, taalconventies en visuele vormen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 3 B c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experimenting with sounds, words, sentences, literary genres, language rules, language conventions and visual forms;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 makes a tongue twister or a row of words that all begin with the same sound, joins two words into a new one, writes a sentence without verbs, turns a news report into a poem, breaks a spelling rule on purpose for an effect, and writes a poem in the shape of its subject. (The pack's example of play with sounds in Dutch: the tongue twister *De kat krabt de krullen van de trap*, the cat scratches the curls off the stairs.)

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 3 B, which says that the pupil uses language in a creative way; 3 B sits under core goal 3. It names seven things to experiment with: sounds, words, sentences, literary genres, language rules, language conventions and visual forms. None is specified: no genre, rule or convention is named, and "visual forms" is not explained. The verb is experimenting; the item sets no standard for the result and no level.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often keep to a form they know, such as a rhyming poem of four lines, and do not try another. They experiment with words and rhyme and leave out the sentences, the rules and the visual form.
