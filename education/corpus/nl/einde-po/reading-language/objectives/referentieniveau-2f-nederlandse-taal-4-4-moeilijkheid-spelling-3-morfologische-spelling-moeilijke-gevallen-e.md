## Official wording

e) meervouds -n bij zelfstandig gebruikte verwijzing (allen versus alle)

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.4 Moeilijkheid, niveau 2F, Spelling / 3. Morfologische spelling / Moeilijke gevallen / e).

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

e) plural -n in a reference word used independently (*allen* versus *alle*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 at this level writes *alle* when a noun follows (*alle kinderen*, all children) and *allen* when the word stands alone and means persons (*Allen waren op tijd.*, All were on time.). The child does the same with *sommige* and *sommigen* (some), *beide* and *beiden* (both), *vele* and *velen* (many).

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, terms and language care, table of difficulty (*Moeilijkheid*), row e) of the difficult cases (*Moeilijke gevallen*) under *Spelling*, 3. *Morfologische spelling* (morphological spelling, the spelling of word forms such as plurals and diminutives). In this table (Table 2 of the decree) the row has a plus sign in the column for level 2F. The table's caption describes its levels as mastery: 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of getting it right. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The row names the plural -n in a reference word that is used independently (*zelfstandig gebruikt*, standing alone without a noun) and sets *allen* against *alle* as its one pair. It states no rule in words, does not say which other words are meant, and does not mention persons. The pack's examples, all correct Dutch: *Alle kinderen waren op tijd.* (All children were on time.) and *Allen waren op tijd.* (All were on time.); *Sommige kinderen bleven thuis.* (Some children stayed at home.) and *Sommigen bleven thuis.* (Some stayed at home.). In standard Dutch the form with -n is used when the word stands alone and refers to persons; for things the form has no -n, as in *Ik las drie boeken; alle waren spannend.* (I read three books; all were exciting.). That rule is not in the row's wording. The decree's running list of spelling rules in section 4.3.1, which carries no level and is not a record of the pack, does speak of persons: its item is the plural -n in reference words to persons and not persons, used independently and as adjectives, with the words *alle*, *vele*, *weinige* and *allen*, *weinigen*, *velen*. It does not spell the rule out either.

### Common mistakes

A child never writes the -n, because *alle* and *allen* sound the same in everyday speech. A child adds the -n before a noun (the wrong *allen kinderen*). A child adds the -n when the word refers to things, as in the wrong *Ik las drie boeken; allen waren spannend.*
