## Official wording

De teksten kunnen redelijk informatiedicht zijn.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 2F, Tekstkenmerken / Opbouw / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The texts can be reasonably dense in information (*informatiedicht*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 listens to a short news item that gives, within a minute, who was involved, where and when it happened, two figures and a cause. Little is repeated. The pupil follows the item and can say afterwards what happened and why.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), row *Opbouw* (structure). It stands under *Tekstkenmerken* (features of the texts) and is the second of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement describes the texts a pupil at this level listens to, not something the pupil does. It has two qualifiers that are the level: the texts *can* be dense in information, so not every text is, and the density is *redelijk* (reasonable, fair). *Informatiedicht* means that much information comes in few words, with little repetition. The statement gives no measure of density and no number of facts for a text, and names no kinds of text.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error: the statement describes the texts. What pupils most often miss in a dense spoken text are the points that come straight after one they stopped to think about. Many also keep the figures and names and forget how they relate, for example which number belonged to which year.
