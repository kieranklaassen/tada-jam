## Official wording

De teksten zijn eenvoudig van structuur; de informatie is herkenbaar geordend. De teksten hebben een lage informatiedichtheid; belangrijke informatie is gemarkeerd of wordt herhaald. Er wordt niet te veel (nieuwe) informatie gelijktijdig geïntroduceerd. De teksten bestaan voornamelijk uit frequent gebruikte (of voor de leerlingen alledaagse) woorden.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 1F, Teksten / Tekstkenmerken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The texts are simple in structure; the information is ordered in a recognisable way. The texts have a low information density; important information is marked or is repeated. Not too much (new) information is introduced at the same time. The texts consist mainly of frequently used words (or words that are everyday for the pupils).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a text that has a title, short paragraphs that each deal with one point, a few key words in bold, and a last paragraph that says the main point again. The child uses the title and the bold words to find its place, and knows nearly every word it meets.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Zakelijke teksten* (reading factual texts), *Tekstkenmerken* (features of the texts). It stands under *Teksten* (texts). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement describes the texts a pupil at this level can read, not something the pupil does. It gives four features: a simple structure with information ordered in a recognisable way; a low information density, with important information marked or repeated; not too much (new) information at the same time; and mainly frequently used or everyday words. None of these is given as a number: the statement names no text length, no sentence length and no word list, and "mainly" (*voornamelijk*) and "not too much" (*niet te veel*) are not made more exact. It names no titles.

### Common mistakes

This statement describes texts, so it is not a skill with a typical error. With a text that goes beyond this description, a child most often can no longer follow it when one paragraph brings in several new facts at once, or stops at a word that is not an everyday one. A child also passes over the marking of important information, such as a bold word or a heading, instead of using it.
