## Official wording

verkennen van het eigen talige repertoire in relatie tot hoe je wilt overkomen en tot welke groepen je wilt behoren: talen en taalvariëteiten, gebaren, lichaamstaal;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 7 A a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring one's own language repertoire (*talige repertoire*, all the language means a person has) in relation to how you want to come across and to which groups you want to belong: languages and language varieties, *gebaren* (gestures, or signs), body language;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 makes a chart of their own language use: which languages and varieties they use, with whom and where, and which gestures and ways of standing or moving go with them. The child tries how a greeting changes: a handshake and *goedemorgen* (good morning) for the head teacher, a raised hand and a short word for a friend (the pack's example). The child says what each greeting shows about how they want to be seen and with whom they belong.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 7 A, which says that the pupil explores how you express identity with language; 7 A sits under core goal 7. After the colon the item names what the repertoire holds: languages and language varieties, *gebaren*, and body language. The Dutch *gebaren* means gestures and also the signs of a sign language; the item does not say which, and the gloss keeps both. The item names two things the repertoire is explored in relation to: how you want to come across, and which groups you want to belong to. It names no language, variety or group, and it does not say how many languages a pupil has. The verb is exploring, and the item sets no level.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Pupils most often leave out gestures and body language and think of words only. Pupils who use one language at home and at school often say there is nothing to explore, and leave out the varieties within that one language.
