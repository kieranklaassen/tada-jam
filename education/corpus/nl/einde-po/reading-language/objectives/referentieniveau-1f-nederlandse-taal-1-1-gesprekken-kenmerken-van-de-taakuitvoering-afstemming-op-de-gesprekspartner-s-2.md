## Official wording

Kan woorden ondersteunen met non-verbaal gedrag.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Afstemming op de gesprekspartner(s) / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can support words with non-verbal behaviour.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 explains to a new classmate where the gym is, and points down the corridor and to the left while saying it. The pupil shows with two hands how big the box for the craft lesson has to be, nods while agreeing, and looks at the person being spoken to.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), *Afstemming op de gesprekspartner(s)* (fit to the conversation partner or partners). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance). The row has one other statement at 1F, on following the conversation partner or partners. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names non-verbal behaviour in general and gives no examples of it; pointing, nodding and looking at the listener are the pack's examples. It says that the behaviour supports the words; it does not speak of behaviour in place of words. It sets no degree or amount.

### Common mistakes

A pupil looks at the floor or at the table while speaking, so the listener gets no support from face or eyes. A pupil points and says "there" or "that one" without the words that say what is meant, so the gesture replaces the words instead of supporting them. A pupil's face or tone does not fit the words, for example smiling out of nervousness while telling something sad.
