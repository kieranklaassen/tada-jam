## Official wording

Beschikt over voldoende woorden om zich te kunnen uiten. Het kan soms nodig zijn een omschrijving te geven van een onbekend woord.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Woordgebruik en woordenschat / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Has enough words available to be able to express oneself. It may sometimes be necessary to give a description of an unknown word.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 tells a classmate about a visit to the orthodontist and has the words for what happened and how it felt. The pupil does not know the word for a brace and says "that metal wire on your teeth that pulls them straight". The classmate understands, and the conversation continues.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), row *Woordgebruik en woordenschat* (word use and vocabulary). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the only statement in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The first sentence is a fragment without a subject; the pupil is meant. The statement sets one measure, enough words to express oneself, and allows one aid: a description in place of a word that is not known. *Soms* (sometimes) is part of the level: the description is the exception, not the rule. The statement does not say to whom the word is unknown. It gives no number of words, no word list and no subjects. Under *Spreken* the row with the same heading refers to *Gesprekken* with the words "Zie Gesprekken", another record of this lane.

### Common mistakes

A pupil who lacks a word stops speaking or says "never mind" and does not describe what is meant. A pupil uses a vague word such as "thing" or "do" so often that the listener cannot tell what is meant. A pupil uses a word that sounds like the right one and means something else.
