## Official wording

Kan in gesprekken binnen en buiten school informatie geven en vragen en kan kritisch luisteren naar deze informatie.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 1F, Taken / 2. Informatie uitwisselen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can give and ask for information in conversations inside and outside school and can listen critically to this information.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: at a friend's house a pupil of about 12 asks the friend's mother what time the birthday party on Saturday ends and what to bring, and tells her who will come to collect the pupil. At school the pupil tells a classmate who was ill what the homework is. When a classmate says that the swimming pool is closed all week, the pupil asks, "How do you know that?"

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), task 2, *Informatie uitwisselen* (exchanging information). It stands under *Taken* (tasks). The task has one other statement at 1F, on judging information and giving a response. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names giving information, asking for it and listening critically to it, in conversations inside and outside school. Beyond "inside and outside school" it names no particular places, and it names no partners or topics; it does not say what listening critically consists of. It sets no amount or difficulty of the information; the general description of *Gesprekken* at 1F, another record of this lane, speaks of simple conversations about familiar topics.

### Common mistakes

A pupil gives information that is incomplete for the listener, for example a time without the day or a place without the address, because the pupil knows the rest. A pupil asks a question that is too wide ("How does it work here?"), and the answer does not hold what the pupil wanted to know. A pupil accepts what is said without asking how the speaker knows it.
