## Official wording

De leerlingen leren informatie te verwerven uit gesproken taal. Ze leren tevens die informatie, mondeling of schriftelijk, gestructureerd weer te geven.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to obtain information from spoken language. They also learn to present that information, orally or in writing, in a structured way.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child near the end of primary school listens to an explanation, a talk by another pupil or a news item for children, and notes the main points while listening or straight after. The child then tells it to someone who was not there, or writes a short account, in an order a listener or reader can follow: first what it was about, then the main points, then a detail or an example.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is goal 1 of the core goals for Dutch, the first of three under the heading *Mondeling taalonderwijs* (oral language education), goals 1 to 3. The goal has two sentences: obtaining information from spoken language, and presenting that information in a structured way, orally or in writing. It does not say what kind of spoken language is meant, how long it is or on what subjects, and it sets no level. The reference levels 1F and 2F, which are other records of this lane, are worded in more detail.

### Common mistakes

A pupil retells loose details in the order heard and leaves out the main point. A pupil tries to write down everything and so misses what is said next. A pupil mixes what was said with the pupil's own opinion or with what the pupil already knew.
