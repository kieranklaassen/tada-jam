## Official wording

Langere teksten; luisterduur tot ongeveer 20 minuten komt voor. Meer kan mits er enige mate van interactie mogelijk is (zoals vragen stellen).

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 2F, Tekstkenmerken / Lengte / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Longer texts; a listening time of up to about 20 minutes occurs. More is possible provided that some degree of interaction is possible (such as asking questions).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 listens to a guest speaker who talks for about 20 minutes about work on a farm, and follows the talk to the end. In a lesson that lasts longer, the pupil can go on listening because there are moments to ask a question, for example "What did you mean by crop rotation?"

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), row *Lengte* (length). It stands under *Tekstkenmerken* (features of the texts) and is the only statement in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement describes the texts a pupil at this level listens to, not something the pupil does. It gives one figure: a listening time of up to about 20 minutes "occurs" (*komt voor*). The figure is approximate and is not a minimum: the statement says that such a listening time occurs, not that every text is that long. Longer listening is possible on one condition, that some degree of interaction is possible. Asking questions comes after *zoals* (such as): it is an example of interaction, not the only form. The statement does not say what the texts are longer than. Its opening is a fragment without a verb. It gives no word count.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error: the statement describes the texts. What pupils most often miss in a long spoken text is the middle part: they stop attending after some minutes and attend again near the end. Where questions are allowed, many pupils do not ask any and go on listening without understanding.
