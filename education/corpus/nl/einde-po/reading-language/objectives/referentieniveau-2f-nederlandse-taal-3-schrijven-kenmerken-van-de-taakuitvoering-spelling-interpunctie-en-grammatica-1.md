## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who writes at this level applies the spelling and punctuation rules while writing a text and checks them when rereading it: the endings of verbs, capital letters, and the marks at the end of sentences. What the child is expected to master is not stated here but in the level description this statement points to.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, writing (*Schrijven*), row *Spelling, interpunctie en grammatica* (spelling, punctuation and grammar). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance); this is the first of the two paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement is a cross-reference and sets no requirement of its own: it points to the level description for language care (*Taalverzorging*) in section 4 of the same annex. In this lane the records of that section at level 2F are those under *4.1 Begrippenlijst* (list of terms) and *4.4 Moeilijkheid* (difficulty).

### Common mistakes

This statement names no skill of its own, so it has no typical error. What children most often leave out when writing a text is applying a spelling rule that they can apply in an exercise, for example the verb ending in *hij wordt* (he becomes), because their attention is on the content.
