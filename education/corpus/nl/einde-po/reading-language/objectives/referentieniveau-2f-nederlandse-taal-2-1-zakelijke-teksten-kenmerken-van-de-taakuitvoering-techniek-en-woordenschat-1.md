## Official wording

Op dit niveau is de woordenschat geen onderscheidend kenmerk van leerlingen meer. De woordenschat van de leerling is voldoende, om teksten te lezen en wanneer nodig kan de betekenis van onbekende woorden uit de vorm, de samenstelling of de context afgeleid worden.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Techniek en woordenschat / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

At this level, vocabulary is no longer a distinguishing characteristic of pupils. The pupil's vocabulary is sufficient to read texts, and when necessary the meaning of unknown words can be derived from the form, the *samenstelling* (composition of the word; compound) or the context.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who reads at this level meets the word *onbewoonbaar* in a newspaper article and works it out from its parts: *on-* (not), *bewonen* (to live in) and *-baar* (able to be), so a house that cannot be lived in. The child splits a compound such as *regenwoud* into *regen* and *woud* (rain, forest), or reads on to the next sentence, where the text explains the word, and then continues reading.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading factual texts (*Lezen zakelijke teksten*), row *Techniek en woordenschat* (technique and vocabulary). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance). 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The first sentence is a remark about the level, not a task: it says that vocabulary no longer distinguishes pupils at this level. The second names three sources for the meaning of an unknown word: its form, its *samenstelling* and the context. *Samenstelling* can be read as how the word is put together or as the compound word (a word made of two words); the gloss keeps both readings. Deriving a meaning is asked "when necessary". The statement sets no number of words and no word list, and it says nothing about reading speed or reading aloud, although the row's name includes technique.

### Common mistakes

A child skips an unknown word and reads on, also when the sentence cannot be understood without it. A child takes a word with two meanings in the wrong one, for example reads *voorkomen* as "to occur" where "to prevent" is meant. A child settles for a rough meaning from the context ("something bad") where the text needs the exact one.
