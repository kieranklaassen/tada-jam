## Official wording

De teksten hebben een heldere structuur. Verbanden en denkstappen worden duidelijk aangegeven.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 2F, Tekstkenmerken / Opbouw / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The texts have a clear structure. Connections and steps in the reasoning (*denkstappen*) are clearly indicated.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 listens to an explanation in which the speaker says at the start what the three parts are, says "that was the first cause, now the second", and says "so that is why" before the conclusion. The pupil uses these spoken signs to know which part the speaker has reached and how one point follows from another.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), row *Opbouw* (structure). It stands under *Tekstkenmerken* (features of the texts) and is the first of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement describes the texts a pupil at this level listens to, not something the pupil does. It names two features: the texts have a clear structure, and connections and steps in the reasoning are clearly indicated. It says nothing about texts in which connections are left unstated. It does not say by what means the connections are indicated; the announcements and linking words in the illustration are the pack's examples. It names no kinds of text.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error: the statement describes the texts. What pupils most often leave unused are the signs of structure: they hear "first", "on the other hand" and "so" and do not use them to order what they hear. They then remember separate facts and not how the points follow from one another.
