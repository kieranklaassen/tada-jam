## Official wording

Fouten met verwijswoorden komen voor. Samenhang in de tekst en binnen samengestelde zinnen is niet altijd duidelijk.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 3 Schrijven, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Samenhang / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Errors with reference words (*verwijswoorden*, words that point back, such as *hij*, *die*, *dit*) occur. Coherence in the text and within compound sentences is not always clear.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 writes *Tom en Ali speelden buiten. Hij viel.* (Tom and Ali were playing outside. He fell.), and a reader cannot tell who fell. Reading the text over, the child finds some of these places and puts the name in place of the word. In a long sentence with two or three parts the child sometimes leaves out the word that says how the parts belong together.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Schrijven* (writing), *Samenhang* (coherence). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the second of two statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement describes what writing at this level still shows: errors with reference words occur, and the coherence is not always clear, in the text as a whole and within compound sentences (*samengestelde zinnen*, sentences made of more than one clause). It states no aim; it says what is found at this level. It does not say how many errors or which reference words.

### Common mistakes

A child writes *hij* or *zij* where two people have been named, so the reader does not know who is meant. A child writes *die* after a *het*-word, as in *het meisje die*; correct is *het meisje dat* (the girl who). A child starts a text with *hij* (he) or *daar* (there) before saying who or where that is.
