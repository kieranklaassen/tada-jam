## Official wording

verkennen van creatief taalgebruik van anderen in literaire en zakelijke teksten;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 3 B a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring the creative use of language by others in literary texts and non-fiction texts (*zakelijke teksten*);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a poem, a picture book, an advertisement and a newspaper headline and marks what the maker did with language: a rhyme, a word with two meanings, a comparison, a made-up word, a sentence that is left unfinished. The child says what effect it has and collects examples the child likes. (The pack's example: a Dutch hairdresser's sign that reads *Haargenau*, a word that means exactly and that begins with *haar*, hair.)

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 3 B, which says that the pupil uses language in a creative way; 3 B sits under core goal 3. The verb is exploring. Two groups of text are named, literary and non-fiction, so the creative use of language is not looked for in literature only. "Others" is not explained: the item does not say whose use of language is meant. The item gives no example of creative use of language; the examples above are the pack's. It sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child looks for creative language only in poems and stories and overlooks it in an advertisement or a headline. A child takes a comparison or a play on words literally. A child notices that something is funny or beautiful and cannot say what the maker did with the language.
