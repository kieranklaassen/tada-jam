## Official wording

Kan hoofdzaken uit de tekst halen. Kan via selectie belangrijke informatie uit de tekst halen en kan de manier van luisteren daar op afstemmen (bijvoorbeeld globaal, precies, selectief/gericht).

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can get the main matters (*hoofdzaken*) out of the text. Can get important information out of the text by selection and can adjust the way of listening to that (for example global, precise, selective/targeted).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: at a classmate's talk about volcanoes a pupil of about 12 wants to know roughly what it is about and listens globally. When the teacher reads out the rules of a new game, the pupil listens precisely, because every rule counts. When the teacher reads out the programme of the sports day, the pupil listens only for the time and the field of their own group and writes those two things down.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), *Begrijpen* (understanding). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance). It is the only statement in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The cell holds two sentences. The first asks for the main matters of the text. The second asks for selecting the important information and for fitting the way of listening to it. The brackets give three ways of listening after "for example": global, precise, and selective or targeted. They are examples of the level, not a closed list. The statement does not say who decides what is important, and it names no kinds of text. The source prints *daar op* as two words.

### Common mistakes

A pupil listens to everything in the same way and tries to remember every detail, also when only one piece of information is needed. A pupil takes the most striking or the funniest detail for the main matter. A pupil stops listening once the needed piece of information has been heard, and misses a correction that comes after it.
