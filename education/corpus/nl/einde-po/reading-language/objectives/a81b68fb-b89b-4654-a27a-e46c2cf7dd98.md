## Official wording

De leerlingen leren informatie en meningen te vergelijken en te beoordelen in verschillende teksten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 7.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to compare and to judge information and opinions in different texts (*verschillende*, "several" or "of different kinds").

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays two texts about one subject side by side, for example a leaflet from a zoo and a newspaper piece about keeping animals in zoos. The child marks what both say, what only one says and where they disagree, and says which sentences give facts and which give the writer's opinion. The child says which text is more to be trusted for a given question, and why.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is goal 7 of the core goals for Dutch, under the heading *Schriftelijk taalonderwijs* (written language education), goals 4 to 9. The goal names two actions (comparing and judging) and two things they are done to (information and opinions). The Dutch *verschillende teksten* can mean several texts or texts of different kinds, and the goal does not say whether the comparing is between texts or within one text. It does not say by what the information is judged, names no kinds of text or source, and sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A pupil believes the text read last, or the one that looks most official. A pupil reads each text on its own and does not notice that two texts contradict each other. A pupil takes a writer's opinion for a fact when it is stated firmly.
