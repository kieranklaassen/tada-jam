## Official wording

Maakt de juiste keuze voor het register en het al dan niet hanteren van taalvariatie (dialect, jongerentaal). Kan de luisteraar(s) boeien door middel van concrete voorbeelden en ervaringen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Afstemming op publiek / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Makes the right choice of register and of whether or not to use language variation (dialect, youth language). Can hold the interest of the listener(s) by means of concrete examples and experiences.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 gives a talk to the class about moving house. The pupil speaks standard Dutch in a tone that fits a talk, and uses one dialect word from the old village on purpose and explains it. To hold the interest of the class, the pupil tells what the first night in the empty room was like and gives an example of how far the new school is: "forty minutes by bicycle, also when it rains".

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), row *Afstemming op publiek* (attuning to the audience). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the second of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The record holds two sentences. The first is a fragment without a subject; the pupil is meant. It asks for the right choice on two points: the register (the kind of language that fits a situation) and whether or not to use language variation. The brackets name dialect and youth language (*jongerentaal*). The first sentence does not say what makes a choice right. The second sentence names one aim, holding the interest of the listener or listeners (*boeien*, to captivate), and one means: concrete examples and experiences. It does not say whose experiences and names no other means. The first sentence also stands as a statement of its own under *Gesprekken*, row *Afstemming op de gesprekspartner(s)*, another record of this lane.

### Common mistakes

A pupil speaks only in general statements ("moving is hard") and gives no example or experience, and the listeners stop attending. A pupil gives so many anecdotes that the listeners no longer know what the talk is about. A pupil uses words of his or her own group that part of the audience does not know.
