## Official wording

Kan relaties leggen tussen de tekst en de werkelijkheid. Kan spannende, humoristische of dramatische passages in de tekst aanwijzen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Interpreteren / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can make connections between the text and reality. Can point out exciting, humorous or dramatic passages in the text.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says of a story about a boy who moves to another town that this happens in real life too, and that the talking cat in the same story does not. The child turns back through the pages, finds the passage it found most exciting or funniest, and reads it out or lays a finger on where it begins and ends.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten* (reading fictional, narrative and literary texts), *Interpreteren* (interpreting). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the first of two statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement does not say what "reality" (*de werkelijkheid*) covers: the pupil's own experience or the world in general. It names three kinds of passage, exciting, humorous or dramatic, joined by "or". Pointing out is what is asked; the statement does not ask the pupil to explain why a passage is exciting or funny. It names no titles.

### Common mistakes

A child takes an invented story for a report of things that happened, or says that nothing in a story could be real. A child names a whole chapter where a passage is asked for. A child remembers that something was funny and cannot find the passage again.
