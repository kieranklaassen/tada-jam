## Official wording

benoemen van overeenkomsten en verschillen tussen het Nederlands en andere talen en taalvariëteiten op klank-, woord- en zinsniveau;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 7 B c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming similarities and differences between Dutch and other languages and language varieties at sound, word and sentence level;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 lays the Dutch *water* next to the English "water" and the German "Wasser" and says what is alike and what differs in sound and in letters (the pack's example). The child puts a Dutch sentence next to the same sentence in a language or variety that a classmate knows, and points at the place of the verb in each. The child says that Dutch has two words for "the", as in *de hond* (the dog) and *het huis* (the house), where English has one.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 7 B, which says that the pupil explores language variation and language change in the Dutch language area; 7 B sits under core goal 7. The item names three levels at which the comparison is made: sound, word and sentence. Dutch is one side of the comparison; the other side is "other languages and language varieties", and none is named. The item does not say which languages, how many, or whether they are languages the pupil knows. The verb is naming: the item does not ask the pupil to speak or learn another language. It sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child translates word for word and expects the same word order in the other language. A child takes two words that look alike for words that mean the same. A child names differences only, or compares single words only and nothing at the level of sounds or sentences.
