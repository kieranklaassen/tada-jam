## Official wording

Articulatie, klemtoon, intonatie, spreekpauze.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.1 Begrippenlijst, niveau 1F, Klanken.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Articulation (*articulatie*), stress (*klemtoon*), intonation (*intonatie*), speaking pause (*spreekpauze*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says a word in syllables and marks which one is stressed: in the pack's example *banaan* (banana) the stress is on the second syllable, *ba-NAAN*. The child hears that *voorkomen* means "to occur" with the stress on the first syllable and "to prevent" with the stress on the second. The child says *Je komt morgen* (You are coming tomorrow) once as a statement, with the voice going down, and once as a question, with the voice going up, and names the difference as intonation. Reading aloud, the child pauses briefly at a comma and longer at a full stop.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Klanken* (sounds) of the list of terms (*Begrippenlijst*). It is the 1F cell of that row in the decree's table 1, which gives the terms about language by level. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The cell lists four terms and nothing more: no definitions and no mark of how well a pupil must know them. The introduction to the list in the decree, which is in no record, says that a limited number of terms is needed for talking about language and that most of them have been dealt with by the end of primary school. The cell names terms for talking about speech; it sets no standard for how a pupil speaks.

### Common mistakes

A child takes stress to mean saying the whole word louder. A child mixes up stress (*klemtoon*), which falls on a syllable, with intonation (*intonatie*), which runs over the whole sentence. A child reading aloud pauses at the end of each line instead of at the punctuation marks.
