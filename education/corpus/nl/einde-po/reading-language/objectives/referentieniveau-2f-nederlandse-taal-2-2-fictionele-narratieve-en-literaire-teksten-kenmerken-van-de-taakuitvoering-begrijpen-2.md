## Official wording

Herkent letterlijk en figuurlijk taalgebruik.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Recognises literal and figurative use of language.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads in a story that a character "has a heart of stone" and says that this is not about a real stone: the character feels no pity. In a poem the child marks the lines that mean exactly what they say and the lines that say one thing and mean another, and says in plain words what a figurative line means.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading fictional, narrative and literary texts (*Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*), row *Begrijpen* (understanding). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance); this is the second of the five paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The verb is recognising: the statement does not ask the pupil to name a figure of speech or to use one. It names no kinds of figurative language and gives no examples. It stands in the section on fictional, narrative and literary texts, and it names no titles.

### Common mistakes

A child takes a figurative line literally and pictures something the writer did not mean. A child calls every unusual or difficult sentence figurative. A child sees that a line is figurative but explains it with another figure of speech instead of plain words.
