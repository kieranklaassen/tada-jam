## Official wording

De leerlingen leren informatie te achterhalen in informatieve en instructieve teksten, waaronder schema’s, tabellen en digitale bronnen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to find information in informative and instructive texts, including diagrams (*schema's*, also charts, outlines or schedules), tables and digital sources.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has a question, such as when the last bus leaves or how long a seed takes to sprout, and looks for the answer in a timetable, a table in an information book, the instructions on a seed packet or a web page. The child uses headings, the row and column of a table, an index or a search field, and reads the part that holds the answer.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is goal 4 of the core goals for Dutch, the first of six under the heading *Schriftelijk taalonderwijs* (written language education), goals 4 to 9. The goal names two kinds of text: informative and instructive. "Including" (*waaronder*) introduces three that are counted among them: *schema's*, tables and digital sources; nothing says the three are the whole list. The goal does not say how long or how hard the texts are, and it sets no level. Comparing and judging information is goal 7. The reference levels 1F and 2F, which are other records of this lane, are worded in more detail.

### Common mistakes

A pupil reads a text from the first line to the last to find one fact, and does not use headings or an index. In a table a pupil reads the right row and the wrong column. On a web page a pupil takes the first sentence that holds a word from the question as the answer.
