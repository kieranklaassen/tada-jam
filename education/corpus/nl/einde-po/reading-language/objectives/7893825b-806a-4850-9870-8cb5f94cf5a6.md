## Official wording

De leerlingen krijgen plezier in het lezen en schrijven van voor hen bestemde verhalen, gedichten en informatieve teksten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 9.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils come to take pleasure in reading and writing stories, poems and informative texts intended for them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 picks a novel for children, a book of poems for children or an information book about a favourite subject and reads on without being asked. The child writes a story or a poem and wants to read it aloud to someone. The child tells someone about a book the child liked.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is goal 9 of the core goals for Dutch, the last of six under the heading *Schriftelijk taalonderwijs* (written language education), goals 4 to 9. The goal is about pleasure, in reading and in writing, and names three kinds of text: stories, poems and informative texts, "intended for them", that is, for the pupils. As printed, "intended for them" also goes with the texts the pupils write; the source does not explain this. The goal names no titles, no writers, no level and no number of books or texts, and it does not say how the pleasure is to be seen.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out is one of the two activities or one of the kinds of text: a pupil who enjoys reading stories may not enjoy writing, and many pupils do not think of poems or information books as something to read for pleasure. Pleasure often lessens when a pupil is given texts that are too hard, or reads only to answer questions.
