## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Verb (*werkwoord*), tense of the verb (*tijd van het werkwoord*), number (*getal*), person (*persoon*), finite verb (*persoonsvorm*), past participle (*voltooid deelwoord*), stem (*stam*), whole verb (*hele werkwoord*, the infinitive), subject (*onderwerp*), weak/strong verbs (*zwakke/sterke werkwoorden*), verbal predicate (*werkwoordelijk gezegde*), ‘*kofschip*’ (a memory word for the past-tense ending)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 uses these terms to work out how a verb form is spelled. In the pack's example, *Gisteren fietste ik naar school* (Yesterday I cycled to school), the child names *fietste* as the finite verb, *ik* as the subject, first person singular, the past as the tense, *fietsen* as the whole verb and *fiets* as the stem. The stem ends in *s*, one of the consonants of *'t kofschip* (t, k, f, s, ch, p), so the past tense takes *-te*, *fietste*, and the past participle ends in *-t*, *gefietst*. For *spelen* (to play) the stem is *speel*; *l* is not in *'t kofschip*, so the forms are *speelde* and *gespeeld*.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Grammaticale begrippen voor werkwoordsspelling* (grammatical terms for verb spelling) of the table of difficulty. It is one row of the decree's table 2, in the section *Moeilijkheid* (difficulty), which sets levels for spelling, punctuation and the grammatical terms for verb spelling. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The table marks this row with one plus sign (+), in the 1F column. The heading of the table gives the measure of mastery (*beheersing*): 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of a correct answer. The heading does not itself name the plus sign; that the plus sign stands for this mastery at the level of its column is the pack's reading of the heading. The row lists twelve items without definitions, and the source prints no full stop at the end. The last item, *kofschip*, is printed between two opening quotation marks and is not explained. It is the memory word *'t kofschip*: when the stem of a weak verb ends in one of its consonants (t, k, f, s, ch, p), the past tense takes *-te(n)* and the past participle *-t*, and otherwise *-de(n)* and *-d*. The row names terms; the spelling of the verb forms is the row *4. Morfologische spelling op syntactische basis* of the same table.

### Common mistakes

A child applies *'t kofschip* to the last letter of the *ik*-form instead of the stem: for *verhuizen* (to move house) the stem is *verhuiz*, and *z* is not in *'t kofschip*, so the past tense is *verhuisde*, not *verhuiste*. A child takes the past participle for the finite verb. A child cannot find the finite verb when the subject comes after it, as in *Morgen fietst hij naar school* (Tomorrow he cycles to school).
