## Official wording

De uitspraak is duidelijk genoeg om de spreker te kunnen volgen, ondanks een eventueel accent, verkeerde intonatie, onduidelijke articulatie en/of haperingen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pronunciation is clear enough for the speaker to be followed, despite a possible accent, wrong intonation, unclear articulation and/or hesitations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 tells two classmates what happened on the school trip. The pupil speaks with the accent of the region or of another home language, sometimes stresses a word wrongly, pronounces the end of a sentence unclearly and stops halfway to start again. The classmates can still follow what is told.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), *Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing* (fluency, intelligibility and command of grammar). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance). The row has one other statement at 1F, on simple sentence constructions. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The measure is whether the speaker can be followed, not whether the pronunciation is free of faults. The statement itself names four things that may be present at this level: a possible accent, wrong intonation, unclear articulation and hesitations, joined by "and/or". It names no standard pronunciation and no speaking rate. The statement *Zie Gesprekken* in the row of the same name under *Spreken* at 1F, another record of this lane, refers to this row.

### Common mistakes

A pupil speaks too softly or too fast when nervous, so the words are not heard apart and the listener cannot follow. A pupil does not pronounce the ends of words or sentences. A pupil turns away from the listener while speaking.
