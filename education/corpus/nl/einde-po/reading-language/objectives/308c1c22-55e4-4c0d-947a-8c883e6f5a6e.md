## Official wording

De leerling evalueert en reflecteert op zakelijke en literaire teksten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 B.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil evaluates and reflects on non-fiction texts (*zakelijke teksten*) and literary texts.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has read two texts about keeping rabbits and says which one helps more with choosing a hutch, and why. After a book the child says what it meant to the child, whether the ending fitted the story, and what the child would ask the writer. For each judgement the child gives a reason and points to a place in the text.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 2, which is about understanding texts. Its items 2 B a to 2 B e name naming relations in content within and between texts, naming differences and similarities in facts, opinions and perspectives, naming contradictory and corresponding content, evaluating the usefulness of texts, and reflecting on their value, content and form. The Dutch gives the two verbs one shared object and prints only the preposition of the second verb (*evalueert en reflecteert op*); the gloss follows the source. The sentence names the same two groups of text as 2 A. It sets no level: no text length, no number of texts and no criteria for evaluating. The reliability of sources is in goal sentence 2 C.

### Common mistakes

A child gives a judgement without a reason ("I liked it", "it was boring"). A child judges a text by whether the child likes the subject and not by what the text does. A child retells the text where the child's view of it is asked.
