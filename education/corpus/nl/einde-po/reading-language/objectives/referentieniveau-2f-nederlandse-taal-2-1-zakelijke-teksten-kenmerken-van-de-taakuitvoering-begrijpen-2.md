## Official wording

Herkent beeldspraak (letterlijk en figuurlijk taalgebruik).

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Recognises imagery (*beeldspraak*, figurative expressions) (literal and figurative use of language).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads in a news article that a football club "is drowning in debt" and says that nobody is in water: the writer means that the club owes far too much money. The child marks in a text which expressions are meant literally and which are not, and says in plain words what a figurative one means.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading factual texts (*Lezen zakelijke teksten*), row *Begrijpen* (understanding). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance); this is the second of the two paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The verb is recognising: the statement does not ask the pupil to explain how an image works or to use imagery. The brackets name literal and figurative use of language; the statement names no kinds of imagery (such as comparison or metaphor) and gives no examples. It stands in the section on factual texts, so it concerns imagery met in such texts.

### Common mistakes

A child takes a figurative expression literally and builds a wrong picture of what happened. A child sees that an expression cannot be literal but cannot say what it does mean. A child who does not know a fixed expression reads it word by word.
