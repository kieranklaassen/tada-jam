## Official wording

Geeft spreekdoel duidelijk vorm (instruerend, informatief, onderhoudend enz.) zodat het voor de luisteraar herkenbaar is.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Afstemming op doel / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Gives clear shape to speaking purpose (*instruerend* (giving directions, or teaching), informative, entertaining etc.) so that it is recognisable for the listener.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 who shows the class how to fold a paper boat says "I am going to show you how to make this, step by step", and gives the steps in order as things to do. Telling a funny story about a holiday, the same pupil starts with "You will not believe what happened" and keeps the surprise for the end. Both times the class knows from the first sentences what kind of talk it is.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), row *Afstemming op doel* (attuning to the purpose). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the only statement in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It is a fragment without a subject, and the source prints no article before *spreekdoel* (speaking purpose); the pupil and the pupil's own purpose are meant. The statement asks that the speaking purpose is given a clear shape, with one test: the listener can recognise it. The brackets name three purposes, *instruerend*, informative and entertaining, and end with "enz." (etc.), so the list is open; the statement names no others. *Instruerend* has two readings: telling someone how to do something, and teaching. The statement does not say by what means the purpose is given shape; the opening sentences in the illustration are the pack's examples.

### Common mistakes

A pupil mixes purposes without choosing: a talk meant to inform becomes a series of jokes, and the listeners do not know what they are meant to learn. A pupil who gives instructions tells them as a story ("and then I did this"), so the listener cannot tell what to do. A pupil begins without an introduction and never says or shows what the talk is for.
