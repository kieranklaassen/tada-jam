## Official wording

Kan met behulp van standaardformuleringen eenvoudige zakelijke brieven produceren en schriftelijke verzoeken opstellen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 3 Schrijven, niveau 2F, Taken / 1. Correspondentie / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can, with the help of standard phrasings, produce simple formal letters (*zakelijke brieven*) and draw up written requests.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who writes at this level writes a short letter to an organisation, for example to ask a museum whether the class may visit, and uses fixed phrases: *Geachte heer of mevrouw* (Dear Sir or Madam) at the top, a sentence that says why the child writes, the question itself, and *Met vriendelijke groet* (With kind regards) above the name. The letter states who writes, what is asked and how the reader can answer.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, writing (*Schrijven*), row *1. Correspondentie* (correspondence). The row stands under *Taken* (tasks); this is the second of the two paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The letters are "simple" and are produced "with the help of standard phrasings" (*standaardformuleringen*). The wording leaves open whether the standard phrasings also apply to the written requests; the gloss follows the Dutch order. *Zakelijk* means business-like or formal. The statement gives no list of standard phrasings, names no reader and no length.

### Common mistakes

A child writes to an organisation in the tone of a message to a friend. A child does not state the request itself clearly, so the reader does not know what to do. A child leaves out what the reader needs in order to answer, such as a name, a date or an address.
