## Official wording

De teksten hebben een eenvoudige structuur. De informatie is herkenbaar geordend, met een duidelijk gebruik van verwijs-, verbindings- en signaalwoorden.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 1F, Tekstkenmerken / Opbouw / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The texts have a simple structure. The information is ordered in a recognisable way, with a clear use of referring words, linking words and signal words (*verwijs-, verbindings- en signaalwoorden*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 listens to an explanation that begins with "I will tell you three things about bees" and goes on with "first", "then" and "finally". The speaker says "the queen" and afterwards "she", and it is clear who "she" is. The pupil uses these words to hear where one part ends and the next begins, and counts along on three fingers.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), *Opbouw* (structure, how a text is built). It stands under *Tekstkenmerken* (text features), which describe the texts a pupil listens to at this level, not what the pupil does. The row holds three statements at 1F; the other two are about information density and about how much information is introduced at the same time. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The cell holds two sentences: the structure is simple, and the information is ordered recognisably, with a clear use of three kinds of words. Referring words point back to something said before (such as "she" or "that"), linking words join sentences (such as "because" or "but"), and signal words announce a step or a relation (such as "first" or "for example"); these explanations and examples are the pack's, and the statement gives none. It does not say which structures count as simple.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error; it describes the text, not the pupil. What pupils most often leave out is using the words the statement names: they hear "because" or "but" and do not notice the relation it signals. They also link a referring word such as "he" or "that" to the wrong person or thing when two have just been named.
