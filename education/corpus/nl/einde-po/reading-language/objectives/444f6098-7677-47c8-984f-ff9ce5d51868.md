## Official wording

ruimte bieden aan verschillende talen en taalvariëteiten van leerlingen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 1 A e.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

giving room to pupils' different languages and language varieties (*taalvariëteiten*, forms of a language, such as a dialect).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At a school that does this, a child of 11 or 12 who speaks another language or a dialect at home uses it at school too: the child explains a word to a classmate in the language they share, compares how something is said at home and in the Dutch of school, or brings a book or a song in the home language. Children hear that classmates speak in different ways and ask about it.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 1 A, which says that the school provides a rich language and reading environment; 1 A sits under core goal 1. Like 1 A, it says what the school does and is not a requirement on the pupil. The verb is giving room. The item speaks of the languages and language varieties "of pupils", those the pupils themselves have. It names no language and no variety (the example of a dialect in the gloss is the pack's), and does not say how much room, when or for what. It does not ask that a pupil learns another language. The item sets no level.

### Common mistakes

This item is about what the school does, so it has no typical error of a child. Children most often keep a home language or a dialect out of school because they think it does not belong there, or treat a way of speaking that differs from their own as wrong.
