## Official wording

Kan eenvoudige instructieve teksten lezen, zoals (eenvoudige) routebeschrijvingen en aanwijzingen bij opdrachten (uit de methode).

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 1F, Taken / 2. Lezen van instructies / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can read simple instructive texts, such as (simple) route descriptions and directions with assignments (from the *methode*, the textbook series a school uses).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a route description and follows it on a map with a finger, one step at a time: out of the school gate, left, second street on the right. The child reads the instruction above an exercise in a workbook, "underline the verbs and then write down the stem of each", and does both things in that order.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Zakelijke teksten* (reading factual texts), *Lezen van instructies* (reading instructions). It is task 2 under *Taken* (tasks). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The texts are simple (*eenvoudige*). After "such as" (*zoals*) the statement gives two examples: route descriptions, marked "(simple)", and directions that go with assignments, marked "(from the *methode*)". They are examples of the level, not a limit. The statement names no number of steps and no length.

### Common mistakes

A child starts doing after reading the first step and does not read the rest. A child skips a step or does the steps in another order. A child does only the first of two things that one instruction asks for.
