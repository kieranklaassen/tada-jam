## Official wording

Kan informatie opzoeken in duidelijk geordende naslagwerken, zoals woordenboeken, telefoongids e.d.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Opzoeken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can look up information in clearly ordered reference works, such as dictionaries, telephone directory and the like.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks up a word in a dictionary: it opens the book near the right letter, uses the two words printed at the top of the page to see whether its word falls between them, follows the alphabet to the second and third letter, and reads the meaning. The child finds a name in an alphabetical list of names by going to the first letter of the surname and then reading down.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Zakelijke teksten* (reading factual texts), *Opzoeken* (looking up). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the first of two statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The reference works are clearly ordered (*duidelijk geordende*). After "such as" (*zoals*) the statement gives two examples, dictionaries and the telephone directory, and "and the like" (*e.d.*) leaves the list open; they are examples of the level, not a limit. The statement does not say whether a reference work is on paper or digital, and it names no way of ordering; both examples are ordered by the alphabet.

### Common mistakes

A child orders by the first letter only and then reads every word under that letter. A child looks for the form it met in the text (*liep*, *huizen*) instead of the form that is the entry (*lopen*, *huis*). A child takes the first meaning listed although another meaning fits the sentence it was reading.
