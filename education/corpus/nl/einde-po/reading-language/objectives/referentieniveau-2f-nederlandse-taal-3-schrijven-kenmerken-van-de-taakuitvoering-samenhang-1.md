## Official wording

Gebruikt veelvoorkomende verbindingswoorden (als, hoewel) correct. De tekst bevat een volgorde; inleiding, kern en slot.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 3 Schrijven, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Samenhang / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Uses frequently occurring connecting words (*als*, *hoewel*: if or when, although) correctly. The text contains an order; introduction, body and conclusion.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who writes at this level joins two thoughts with the right connecting word, as in *Als het regent, blijven we binnen.* (If it rains, we stay inside.) and *Hoewel het regende, gingen we naar buiten.* (Although it rained, we went outside.). The child's text begins with an introduction that says what it is about, goes on with a body, and closes with a conclusion.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, writing (*Schrijven*), row *Samenhang* (coherence). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance); this is the first of the three paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The connecting words are "frequently occurring" ones, and the brackets name two: *als* (if, when, as) and *hoewel* (although). The brackets do not say whether these two are the whole set or examples. The text has an order of three parts: introduction, body and conclusion. The source prints a semicolon before the three parts where a colon is plainly meant. The statement names no length. The two Dutch sentences under "In a child's hands" are the pack's examples, both correct Dutch.

### Common mistakes

A child uses a connecting word with the wrong meaning, for example *omdat* (because) where *hoewel* (although) is meant. A child puts the verb in the wrong place after the connecting word, as in the wrong *omdat hij is ziek* where *omdat hij ziek is* (because he is ill) is right. A child writes a body without an introduction or stops without a conclusion.
