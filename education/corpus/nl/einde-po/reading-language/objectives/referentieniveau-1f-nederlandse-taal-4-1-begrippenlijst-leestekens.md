## Official wording

Dubbele punt, punt, komma, puntkomma, uitroepteken, vraagteken, aanhalingsteken.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.1 Begrippenlijst, niveau 1F, Leestekens.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Colon (*dubbele punt*), full stop (*punt*), comma (*komma*), semicolon (*puntkomma*), exclamation mark (*uitroepteken*), question mark (*vraagteken*), quotation mark (*aanhalingsteken*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a sentence and names each mark in it. In the pack's example, *Oma vroeg: 'Wil je thee, koffie of melk?'* (Grandma asked: 'Do you want tea, coffee or milk?'), the child names the colon after *vroeg*, the quotation marks around what Grandma says, the comma after *thee* and the question mark at the end. In *Het regende; we bleven binnen.* (It was raining; we stayed in.) the child names the semicolon and the full stop, and in *Kijk uit!* (Watch out!) the exclamation mark.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Leestekens* (punctuation marks) of the list of terms (*Begrippenlijst*). It is the 1F cell of that row in the decree's table 1, which gives the terms about language by level. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The cell lists the names of seven marks and nothing more: no definitions, no rules for their use and no mark of how well a pupil must know them. The introduction to the list in the decree, which is in no record, says that a limited number of terms is needed for talking about language and that most of them have been dealt with by the end of primary school. Rules for capital letters, full stops, question marks, exclamation marks and quotation marks are rows of the decree's table of difficulty, other records of this lane (under *4.4 Moeilijkheid*, *Leestekens*).

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up the names *punt*, *dubbele punt* and *puntkomma*. A child calls quotation marks "high commas" and does not know their name. A child takes the apostrophe in a word such as *zo'n* (such a) for a quotation mark.
