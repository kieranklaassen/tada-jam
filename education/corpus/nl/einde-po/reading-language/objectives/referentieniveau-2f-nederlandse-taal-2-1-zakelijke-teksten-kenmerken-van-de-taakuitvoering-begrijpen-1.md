## Official wording

Kan de hoofdgedachte van de tekst weergeven en maakt onderscheid tussen hoofd- en bijzaken. Legt relaties tussen tekstdelen (inleiding, kern, slot) en teksten. Ordent informatie (bijvoorbeeld op basis van signaalwoorden) voor een beter begrip.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can give the main idea of the text and distinguishes between main and side issues. Makes connections between text parts (introduction, body, conclusion) and texts. Orders information (for example on the basis of signal words) for a better understanding.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads an informative text and says what the writer mainly wants to tell. The child underlines the sentences that carry the main points and leaves the examples and details unmarked. The child points to where the introduction ends and the body begins, says what the conclusion takes up again from the introduction, and uses words such as *ten eerste*, *daarna* and *daardoor* (first, after that, as a result) to put the steps of an explanation in order.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading factual texts (*Lezen zakelijke teksten*), row *Begrijpen* (understanding). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance); this is the first of the two paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The brackets name three text parts: introduction, body and conclusion. Signal words (*signaalwoorden*, words that mark a connection, such as "because" or "but") are given as an example of a basis for ordering information, not as the only one. The second sentence can be read as connections between the parts of one text and between different texts; the wording does not say how many texts. The statement does not say whether the main idea is given orally or in writing, and it names no length of text and no title.

### Common mistakes

A child gives the topic in one word ("bees") where the main idea is asked (what the text says about bees). A child takes the most striking detail or the first sentence for the main idea. A child reads over signal words, for example takes what follows *maar* (but) as an addition where it is a contrast.
