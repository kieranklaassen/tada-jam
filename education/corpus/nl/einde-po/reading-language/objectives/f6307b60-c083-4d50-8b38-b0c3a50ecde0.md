## Official wording

benoemen van verteltechnieken die een schrijver gebruikt om spanning op te bouwen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 9 A c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming narrative techniques that a writer uses to build up suspense (*spanning*, also "tension");

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads an exciting chapter and marks the places where the suspense rises. The child says what the writer does there: stops the chapter just before the answer, tells the reader something the main character does not know, makes the sentences short, or leaves the character little time. The child says it in everyday words or with a term learned for it.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 9 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into narrative texts; 9 A sits under core goal 9. The item asks for naming and lists no techniques. It is about techniques for building up suspense; techniques with another purpose are not mentioned. It does not say which terms the pupil uses, and it names no length or level of story.

### Common mistakes

A pupil names the exciting event ("the bear came") and not what the writer did to make it exciting. A pupil thinks suspense comes only from what happens, and does not see that the order and the pace of the telling add to it. A pupil uses one learned term, such as cliffhanger, for every exciting passage.
