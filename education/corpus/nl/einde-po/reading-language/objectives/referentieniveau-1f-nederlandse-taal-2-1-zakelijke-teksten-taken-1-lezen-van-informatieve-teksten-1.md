## Official wording

Kan eenvoudige informatieve teksten lezen, zoals zaakvakteksten, naslagwerken, (eenvoudige) internetteksten, eenvoudige schematische overzichten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 1F, Taken / 1. Lezen van informatieve teksten / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can read simple informative texts, such as subject-lesson texts (*zaakvakteksten*, texts for geography, history and nature lessons), reference works, (simple) internet texts, simple schematic overviews.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a page of a schoolbook about how a river is formed and tells the steps in order. The child reads the entry about an animal in an encyclopedia for children, a simple web page with the visiting rules of a museum, and a simple overview such as a timeline with five dates.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Zakelijke teksten* (reading factual texts), *Lezen van informatieve teksten* (reading informative texts). It is task 1 under *Taken* (tasks). 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The texts are simple (*eenvoudige*). After "such as" (*zoals*) the statement gives four examples: subject-lesson texts, reference works, internet texts and schematic overviews. The word "simple" is printed again with the last of these, and in brackets with the internet texts. They are examples of the level, not a limit. The statement names no length, no titles and no subjects.

### Common mistakes

A child reads only the running text and passes over headings, captions and tables that hold part of the information. A child remembers one striking detail and cannot say what the text as a whole is about. On a web page a child reads the first lines only and takes them for the whole text.
