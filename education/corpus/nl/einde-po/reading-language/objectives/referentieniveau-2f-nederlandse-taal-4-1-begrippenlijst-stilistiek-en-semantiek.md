## Official wording

Homoniem, homofoon, vakjargon, stilistische adequaatheid (publiekgericht), presentatiekenmerken (van mondelinge en schriftelijke tekst).

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.1 Begrippenlijst, niveau 2F, Stilistiek en semantiek.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Homonym, homophone, technical jargon (*vakjargon*, the special words of a trade or subject), stylistic adequacy (directed at the audience), presentation characteristics (of oral and written text).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 at this level says that *bank* is a homonym, because one word names a seat and a place for money, and that *hart* (heart) and *hard* (hard) are homophones, because they sound the same and are written differently. The child notices that a text about football uses words such as *buitenspel* (offside) and calls that *vakjargon*. When looking at a text or a talk, the child says whether its style fits the audience and names features of how it is presented, such as headings and pictures in a written text or a clear voice in a talk.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, terms and language care, list of terms (*Begrippenlijst*), row *Stilistiek en semantiek* (stylistics and semantics). It is the cell of this row in the column for level 2F of the decree's table of terms (Table 1); the table prints no sign, and the column gives the level. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The decree introduces the list as terms for talking about language: the point is that teachers (and pupils) can name certain phenomena in language situations with a rich context. The cell names five terms: *homoniem* (homonym), *homofoon* (homophone), *vakjargon* (technical jargon), *stilistische adequaatheid* (stylistic adequacy), to which the brackets add "directed at the audience" (*publiekgericht*), and *presentatiekenmerken* (presentation characteristics), which the brackets tie to both oral and written text. It gives no definitions and no examples. The pack's explanations: a homonym is one word form with different meanings, and homophones are words that sound the same. The pack's examples, all correct Dutch: a homonym, *bank* (a seat; a bank for money); homophones, *hart* (heart) and *hard* (hard), *lijden* (to suffer) and *leiden* (to lead); jargon, *buitenspel* (offside) in football.

### Common mistakes

A child uses the two terms as one and cannot say that *hart* and *hard* are alike in sound only, while the two meanings of *bank* are alike in sound and in spelling. A child takes a word with a figurative meaning for a homonym. A child thinks that jargon is always difficult or wrong language, and does not see that it fits an audience that knows the subject.
