## Official wording

De leerling toont begrip van zakelijke en literaire teksten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 A.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil shows understanding of non-fiction texts (*zakelijke teksten*) and literary texts.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads an information text about volcanoes and a chapter of a children's novel. After the first, the child explains how an eruption happens; after the second, the child says why the main character ran away and how the story could go on. The child shows the understanding by retelling, by answering a question, by making a drawing with labels or by carrying out a written instruction.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 2, which is about understanding texts. Its items 2 A a to 2 A f name attentive listening, watching and reading, the knowledge that is put to use and extended (vocabulary, knowledge about language, knowledge of the world and knowledge about the form of texts), describing perspectives, purposes, audience and context, giving the main points and the main idea in one's own words, and applying approaches and strategies. The sentence names two groups of text, non-fiction and literary. The verb is "shows": the understanding has to be visible, and the sentence does not say in what way. It sets no level: no text length, no difficulty of text and no list of kinds of text.

### Common mistakes

A child understands each sentence and does not connect them, so a cause and its result, or a contrast marked by "but", is missed. In a story a child reports what happens and leaves out why a character does it. In a non-fiction text a child skips the headings, pictures and captions and misses information that is given only there.
