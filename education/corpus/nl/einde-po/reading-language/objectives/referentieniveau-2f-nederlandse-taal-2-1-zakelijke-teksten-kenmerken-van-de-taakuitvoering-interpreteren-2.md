## Official wording

Kan de bedoeling van de schrijver verwoorden.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Interpreteren / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can put the writer's intention into words.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a leaflet about cycling with the lights on and says in own words what the writer wants: readers must switch their lights on, so the text is written to persuade. For a text about how a lock in a canal works, the child says that the writer wants to explain. The child points to parts of the text that show this, such as a call to do something or a row of steps.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading factual texts (*Lezen zakelijke teksten*), row *Interpreteren* (interpreting). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance); this is the second of the two paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement is about the writer's intention and asks the pupil to put it into words; it does not say whether orally or in writing. It gives no list of intentions (such as informing, persuading, instructing or entertaining) and does not ask for a judgement of the intention. It names no kind of text, no length and no title.

### Common mistakes

A child names the topic of the text where the intention is asked. A child takes every text as "giving information" and misses that an advertisement or an opinion piece wants the reader to do or think something. A child gives the child's own opinion of the topic as the writer's intention.
