## Official wording

Kan verslag uitbrengen van gebeurtenissen, activiteiten en persoonlijke ervaringen. Kan een kort, voorbereid verhaal of presentatie houden en daarbij op eenvoudige vragen reageren.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 1F, Taken / Een monoloog houden / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can give a report of events, activities and personal experiences. Can give a short, prepared *verhaal* (a story, or an account or talk) or presentation and respond to simple questions while doing so.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: on Monday morning a pupil of about 12 tells the class what happened at the football tournament at the weekend: where it was, which matches were played and how the last one ended. Later in the year the pupil gives a short talk (*spreekbeurt*) about the fire brigade that was prepared at home. A classmate asks how heavy a fire hose is, and the pupil answers.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), task *Een monoloog houden* (giving a monologue). It stands under *Taken* (tasks). The task has one other statement at 1F, on describing everyday aspects. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement holds two sentences. The first names three things reported on: events, activities and personal experiences. The second names a *verhaal* or a presentation that is short (*kort*) and prepared (*voorbereid*), and questions that are simple (*eenvoudige*). *Verhaal* has two readings in Dutch: a story, and an account or talk (*een verhaal houden* is to give a talk or to tell one's account); the gloss keeps the Dutch. In the Dutch the two adjectives have the form that fits *verhaal*; the wording leaves open whether they also apply to the presentation, and the gloss keeps this open. "Short" is given no number of minutes. The statement does not say who asks the questions, and it names no topics.

### Common mistakes

A pupil reports an event as a row of sentences with "and then". A pupil memorises the prepared text or reads it out, and cannot go on after an interruption. A pupil answers a question that was not asked, or says "I do not know" to a question whose answer was in the talk.
