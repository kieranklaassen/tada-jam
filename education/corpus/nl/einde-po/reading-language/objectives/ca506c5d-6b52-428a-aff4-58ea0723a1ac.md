## Official wording

beschrijven van perspectieven, communicatieve doelen, publiek, context;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 A d.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing perspectives, communicative purposes, audience, context;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a leaflet, a letter or a story and says from whose point of view it is told, what the maker wants to achieve with it, for whom it is made and in what situation it appeared. With two reports of the same football match the child says which one was written by a supporter of the losing team and how that can be seen in the words.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 2 A, which says that the pupil shows understanding of non-fiction and literary texts; 2 A sits under core goal 2. The item lists four things the pupil describes: perspectives, communicative purposes, audience and context. The item gives no example of a perspective, a purpose, an audience or a context; the examples above are the pack's. It does not say whose perspectives are meant: the maker's, or those of a person or character in the text. The verb is describing; evaluating is in goal sentence 2 B. The item sets no level and names no kinds of text.

### Common mistakes

Asked for the purpose of a text, a child names its subject ("it is about dogs") where the purpose is to get the reader to adopt a dog. A child assumes that every text is made for children, and does not notice a text aimed at parents or at experts. A child takes the point of view of a character or of the writer for a fact.
