## Official wording

De leerlingen leren informatie te beoordelen in discussies en in een gesprek dat informatief of opiniërend van karakter is en leren met argumenten te reageren.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to judge information in discussions and in a conversation that is informative or opinion-giving in character (*opiniërend*, in which opinions are given or formed), and learn to respond with arguments.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes part in a group discussion about whether the lunch break should be shorter. The child listens to what another speaker says, asks "how do you know that?", and says which part is a fact and which an opinion. The child answers with a reason: "I do not agree, because the youngest children need longer to eat."

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is goal 3 of the core goals for Dutch, the last of three under the heading *Mondeling taalonderwijs* (oral language education), goals 1 to 3. The goal joins two things: judging information, and responding with arguments. It says where: in discussions, and in a conversation that is informative or *opiniërend*. It does not say by what the information is judged, on what subjects, or with how many speakers, and it sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A pupil accepts a statement because a friend or a confident speaker says it. A pupil gives an opinion as if it were an argument ("because I think so"), or repeats the opinion. A pupil responds to the person and not to what was said.
