## Official wording

beschrijven welk probleem of welke wens van een hoofdpersoon de motor van het verhaal is en hoe het verhaal zich ontwikkelt;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 9 A a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing which problem or which wish of a main character is the engine of the story (*motor*, what makes it move forward) and how the story develops;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says in one sentence what starts a story: "she wants to find her father", "he has broken something and does not dare to say so". The child then tells what the main character tries, what goes wrong, and how it is resolved or not. The child lays cards with the main events in order and marks the card where the problem or wish first shows.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 9 A, which says that the pupil shows insight into narrative texts; 9 A sits under core goal 9. The item has two parts: the problem or wish of a main character that drives the story, and the way the story develops. It says "a main character" and does not say that a story has only one. It names no terms for the parts of a story and no length or level of story.

### Common mistakes

A pupil names the most exciting event as the problem of the story. A pupil retells the events and does not link them to what the main character wants. A pupil describes the beginning and the end and leaves out how the one leads to the other.
