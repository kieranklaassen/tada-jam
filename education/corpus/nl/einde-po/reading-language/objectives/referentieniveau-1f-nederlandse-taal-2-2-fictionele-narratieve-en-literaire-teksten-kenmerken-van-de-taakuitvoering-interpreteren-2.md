## Official wording

Herkent verschillende emoties in de tekst, zoals verdriet, boosheid en blijdschap.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Interpreteren / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Recognises different emotions in the text, such as sadness, anger and joy.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads "He slammed the door and said nothing at dinner" and says the boy is angry. The child underlines the words in a fragment that show that a character is sad, and says where in the story the mood changes to joy.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten* (reading fictional, narrative and literary texts), *Interpreteren* (interpreting). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the second of two statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. After "such as" (*zoals*) the statement gives three examples: sadness, anger and joy; they are examples of the level, not a limit. It says "in the text" and does not say whose emotions these are, or whether the text names them or only shows them. It names no titles.

### Common mistakes

A child recognises an emotion only when the text names it with the word itself. A child gives one emotion to the whole story and misses that it changes. A child reads tears as sadness where the text shows joy or anger.
