## Official wording

Varieert het woordgebruik, fouten met idiomatische uitdrukkingen komen nog voor.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 3 Schrijven, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Woordgebruik en woordenschat / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Varies the word use, errors with idiomatic expressions still occur.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who writes at this level does not repeat *zei* (said) in every line of a story but also writes *riep*, *fluisterde* or *antwoordde* (called, whispered, answered), and replaces a repeated noun with another word for the same thing. When the child uses a fixed expression, it is sometimes wrong, with a wrong word in it or two expressions mixed.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, writing (*Schrijven*), row *Woordgebruik en woordenschat* (word use and vocabulary). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance). 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement has two parts: the pupil varies the word use, and errors with idiomatic expressions "still occur" (*komen nog voor*), which describes what may still go wrong at this level. It gives no size of vocabulary, no word list and no number of errors.

### Common mistakes

A child changes a word in a fixed expression, for example writes the wrong *iets in de knie hebben* where the expression is *iets onder de knie hebben* (to have mastered something). A child varies by choosing a word that does not mean the same, for example *schreeuwde* (shouted) for a character who speaks softly. A child uses a word from everyday speech in a formal text.
