## Official wording

evalueren van bruikbaarheid van teksten;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 B d.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

evaluating the usefulness of texts;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has a question, for example what a hedgehog eats in winter, and three texts: a story with a hedgehog in it, a page from an animal encyclopedia and an advertisement for garden products. The child looks through them and says which one answers the question, which one answers it partly and which one does not, each with a reason.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 2 B, which says that the pupil evaluates and reflects on non-fiction and literary texts; 2 B sits under core goal 2. It names one thing to evaluate: usefulness. It does not say useful for what or for whom; the item names no purpose and no question. Reliability is a separate matter, in goal sentence 2 C. The item names no criteria and no number of texts, and sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child calls a text useful because it is about the subject, although it does not answer the question. A child chooses the text that is shortest, has the most pictures or was found first. A child confuses useful with true or with pleasant to read.
