## Official wording

De leerling beschouwt de relatie tussen vorm en betekenis van taal.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 6 A.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupil considers (*beschouwt*, looks at and thinks about) the relation between form and meaning of language.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says one sentence in two ways and hears the difference: *Jij komt morgen.* as a statement and *Jij komt morgen?* with a rising voice as a question (the pack's example; the words mean "you are coming tomorrow"). The child changes one sound in a word, one part of a word, or the order of the words in a sentence, and says what happens to the meaning.

### Limits

This is a goal sentence (*doelzin*, the sentence printed under a core goal with a capital letter) under core goal 6, which is about insight into language as a system. Its items 6 A a to 6 A e name letter sounds, stress, intonation and rhythm; the structure of basic words; word order and sentence parts; word use, stylistic devices and the structure of texts; and the use of linguistic *begrippen* (concepts, or terms) and strategies for considering language when thinking and talking about spelling and grammar. The sentence itself names no grammatical term; item 6 A e speaks of linguistic *begrippen* and lists none. The law sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child treats form only as right or wrong and does not ask what a form does to the meaning. A child thinks a word has one meaning, whatever sentence it stands in. A child does not notice that the same words in another order say something else.
