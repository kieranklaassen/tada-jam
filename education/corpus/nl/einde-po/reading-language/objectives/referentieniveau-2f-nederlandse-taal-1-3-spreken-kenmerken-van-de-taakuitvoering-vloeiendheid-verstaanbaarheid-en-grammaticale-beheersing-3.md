## Official wording

Is goed te volgen en kan zich gemakkelijk uitdrukken. Vloeiendheid kan minder zijn als er nagedacht moet worden over de grammaticale vorm, de te kiezen woorden en herstel van fouten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can be followed well and can express oneself easily. Fluency may be less when thought has to be given to the grammatical form, the words to be chosen and repair of errors.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 tells the class about a hobby. The class follows the account without effort, and the pupil finds words without searching. At a part that needs a difficult sentence, at a word that does not come at once, or when correcting an error, the pupil slows down and stops for a moment, and then continues.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), row *Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing* (fluency, intelligibility and command of grammar). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the second and last record of its cell, in position 3. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. It follows the cross-reference "Zie Gesprekken +", another record of this lane, so it is read as what this row adds for speaking to what the cross-reference refers to. The decree prints the plus sign as a paragraph of its own in position 2; the pack joins it to the cross-reference and does not hold it as a record. The first sentence is a fragment without a subject; the pupil is meant. It sets two qualities: the pupil can be followed well, and the pupil expresses himself or herself easily. The second sentence allows less fluency in three cases: when the speaker has to think about the grammatical form, about the words to choose, and about the repair of errors. It does not say how much less, and the statement gives no speaking rate, no measure of pauses and no speaking time.

### Common mistakes

A pupil stops in the middle of a sentence to search for a word and does not finish the sentence. A pupil keeps speaking fast and does not repair an error that confuses the listeners. A pupil learns a talk by heart, says it fast in one tone, and cannot continue after forgetting one word.
