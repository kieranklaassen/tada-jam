## Official wording

aanbieden van een veelzijdig en actueel aanbod van jeugdliteratuur binnen een vaste leesroutine;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 1 A b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

offering a varied and up-to-date range of literature for young readers (*jeugdliteratuur*) within a fixed reading routine;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At a school that does this, a child of 11 or 12 reads at a set time, for example every day after the break, in a book the child has chosen from a collection with novels, poems and comic books, older titles and ones that have just appeared. The child hears about new books and exchanges tips with classmates.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 1 A, which says that the school provides a rich language and reading environment; 1 A sits under core goal 1. Like 1 A, it says what the school does and is not a requirement on the pupil. The verb is offering. Three conditions are named: the range is varied, it is up to date (*actueel*), and it is offered within a fixed reading routine. The item names no titles, no genres and no number of books, sets no reading time per day or week, and does not say what the routine consists of. It names literature for young readers; other texts are not named in this item. It sets no reading level.

### Common mistakes

This item is about what the school does, so it has no typical error of a child. In a fixed reading time, children most often keep to one series or one writer, or choose a book that is much too hard and change it after a few days. Some look at the pages without reading.
