## Official wording

verkennen van verschillende taalvariëteiten van het Nederlands: school- en vaktaal, groeps- en streektalen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 7 B a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring different language varieties of Dutch: school language and subject language (*school- en vaktaal*: the language of lessons and the terms of subjects), group languages and regional languages (*groeps- en streektalen*);

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 sorts word cards: *oppervlakte* (area) and *verdampen* (to evaporate) go to subject language, *vergelijk* (compare) and *conclusie* (conclusion) to school language, a word that only their group of friends uses to group language, and a word from their region to regional language (the pack's examples). The child listens to a short recording in a regional variety and picks out words that differ from the Dutch of the schoolbooks.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 7 B, which says that the pupil explores language variation and language change in the Dutch language area; 7 B sits under core goal 7. After the colon the item names four kinds of variety in two pairs: school language and subject language, group languages and regional languages. A group language (*groepstaal*) is the way of speaking of a particular group, and a regional language (*streektaal*) that of a region; these two explanations are the pack's. The item lists regional languages under the varieties of Dutch, and the gloss follows it. It names no dialect, regional language or group by name, and it does not say how many varieties are explored. The verb is exploring: the item does not ask the pupil to speak a variety, and it sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks a regional variety or a group's language is wrong Dutch and not a variety with rules of its own. A child does not see school language as a variety at all, because its words seem ordinary, and so does not notice which of them are hard. A child takes a subject term in its everyday meaning: *tafel* as a piece of furniture where the multiplication table is meant (the pack's example).
