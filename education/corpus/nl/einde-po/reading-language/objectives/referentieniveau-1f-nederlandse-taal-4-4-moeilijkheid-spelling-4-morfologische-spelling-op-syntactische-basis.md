## Official wording

4. Morfologische spelling op syntactische basis
Werkwoordspelling waarvan een deel zuiver morfologisch is, zoals tegenwoordige tijd meervoud, verleden tijd van werkwoorden met stam op -d (antwoordde(n)), hele werkwoord

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.4 Moeilijkheid, niveau 1F, Spelling / 4. Morfologische spelling op syntactische basis.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

4. Morphological spelling on a syntactic basis
Verb spelling of which a part is purely morphological, such as present tense plural, past tense of verbs with a stem in -d (*antwoordde(n)*), whole verb (*hele werkwoord*, the infinitive)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 spells a verb form by working out which form it is and building it from the stem. The pack's examples: the present tense plural is the same as the whole verb, as in *wij spelen* (we play) and *zij vinden* (they find); the past tense of a weak verb with a stem in *-d* is the stem plus *-de* or *-den*, so *landen* (to land), stem *land*, gives *het vliegtuig landde* (the plane landed) and *de vliegtuigen landden* (the planes landed), with two *d*'s although one is heard. The statement's own example, *antwoordde(n)*, is the past tense of *antwoorden* (to answer): *ik antwoordde*, *wij antwoordden*.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Spelling*, class 4, of the table of difficulty. It is one row of the decree's table 2, in the section *Moeilijkheid* (difficulty), which sets levels for spelling, punctuation and the grammatical terms for verb spelling. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The table marks this row with one plus sign (+), in the 1F column. The heading of the table gives the measure of mastery (*beheersing*): 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of a correct answer. The heading does not itself name the plus sign; that the plus sign stands for this mastery at the level of its column is the pack's reading of the heading. The record holds the heading of the row and its explanation, which the decree prints as two paragraphs in one cell, each on its own line; neither ends with a full stop. The explanation is about verb spelling "of which a part is purely morphological" and gives three examples after "such as" (*zoals*): the present tense plural, the past tense of verbs with a stem in *-d*, and the whole verb. The past-tense example names stems in *-d* only. Under this row the table prints rows of difficult cases (*Moeilijke gevallen*) for the finite verb (*Persoonsvorm*) and for the past participle (*Voltooid deelwoord*), among them the row *a) homofone gevallen: tegenwoordige tijd stam op -d enkelvoud (hij wordt/word)*; none of these rows carries a plus sign in the 1F column. The grammatical terms that go with this row are the row *Grammaticale begrippen voor werkwoordsspelling* of the same table.

### Common mistakes

A child writes the past tense as it sounds, with one *d*: *de vliegtuigen landen* where the past tense *landden* is meant, or *antwoorde* for *antwoordde*. A child reads *landden* and *landen* as the same word and does not see that the first is past tense and the second present tense. A child does not first decide which form it needs (present plural, past tense or whole verb) and writes the form that looks most familiar.
