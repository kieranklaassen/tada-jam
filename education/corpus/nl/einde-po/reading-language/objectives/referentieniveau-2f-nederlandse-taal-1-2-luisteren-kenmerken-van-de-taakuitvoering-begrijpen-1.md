## Official wording

Kan de hoofdgedachte van de tekst weergeven en kan onderscheid tussen hoofd- en bijzaken maken. Kan relaties tussen tekstdelen leggen. Kan informatie ordenen (bijvoorbeeld op basis van signaalwoorden) voor een beter begrip.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can render the main idea of the text and can distinguish between main and side issues. Can make connections between parts of the text. Can order information (for example on the basis of signal words) for a better understanding.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 listens to a talk about why hedgehogs are becoming rarer. Afterwards the pupil says what the speaker wanted to make clear, says which points were central (fewer hedges, more traffic) and which was a side issue (the speaker's story about one hedgehog in a garden), and connects the part about gardens with the later part about what people can do. While listening, the pupil notes the words "first", "because" and "as a result" and uses them to put the causes and effects in order.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), row *Begrijpen* (understanding). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the first of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The record holds three sentences and four abilities: rendering the main idea of the text; telling main issues from side issues; connecting parts of the text with each other; and ordering information for a better understanding. "Text" here is spoken text. Signal words (*signaalwoorden*: words that show how parts of a text relate, such as words for cause, order or contrast) come after *bijvoorbeeld* (for example): they are an example of a basis for ordering, not the only one. The statement names no kind of text and no length, and does not say whether the pupil renders the main idea aloud or in writing.

### Common mistakes

A pupil gives the subject ("it was about hedgehogs") where the main idea is asked. A pupil retells a striking detail or an example as if it were the main point. A pupil hears "but" or "because" and does not use it, and remembers a cause and its effect as two separate facts.
