## Official wording

Beschikt over voldoende woorden om te praten over vertrouwde situaties en onderwerpen, maar zoekt nog regelmatig naar woorden en varieert niet veel in woordgebruik.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Woordgebruik en woordenschat / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Has enough words to talk about familiar situations and topics, but still regularly searches for words and does not vary much in word use.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 tells a classmate how the hamster's cage is cleaned, and has the words for the cage, the food and the steps. Halfway the pupil stops to find a word, says "that thing you put the water in" and goes on. The pupil uses the same few words for "nice" and "good" all the way through.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), *Woordgebruik en woordenschat* (word use and vocabulary). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance). It is the only statement in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement sets a minimum and names two things that still belong to this level: enough words for familiar situations and topics, with regular searching for words and little variation in word use. It gives no number of words and no word list, and it names no topics. It says nothing about words for unfamiliar topics. The statement *Zie Gesprekken* in the row of the same name under *Spreken* at 1F, another record of this lane, refers to this row.

### Common mistakes

A pupil who cannot find a word stops talking instead of describing the thing in other words. A pupil uses general words such as *ding* (thing) and *doen* (do) where a precise word is needed, and the listener cannot tell what is meant. A pupil uses a word that sounds like the intended one but means something else.
