## Official wording

Kan bepalen in welke mate de personages en gebeurtenissen herkenbaar en realistisch zijn. Kan personages typeren, zowel innerlijk als uiterlijk. Kan het onderwerp van de tekst benoemen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Interpreteren / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can determine to what degree the characters and events are recognisable and realistic. Can characterise characters, both inwardly and outwardly. Can name the subject of the text.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says of a story which events could happen in real life and which could not, and whether the characters behave like people the child knows: "the school is like a real school, but nobody would keep such a secret for a year". The child describes a character from outside (tall, a red coat, always running) and from inside (stubborn, loyal, afraid of being laughed at), with a place in the text for each. The child says in a few words what the text is about, for example "friendship after a move".

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading fictional, narrative and literary texts (*Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*), row *Interpreteren* (interpreting). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance). 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The first sentence asks "to what degree", so a judgement of more or less, not of yes or no, about two qualities: recognisable and realistic. The second asks for characterising "both inwardly and outwardly". The third asks for naming the subject (*onderwerp*) of the text; it does not use the word theme and does not ask for the writer's message. The statement names no kind of story, no titles and no length.

### Common mistakes

A child treats "realistic" as "really happened" and calls every invented story unrealistic. A child describes how a character looks and leaves out what the character is like. A child retells the plot where the subject is asked.
