## Official wording

Kan hoofdpunten van korte en duidelijke berichten op radio en televisie en via internet begrijpen als onderwerp en context bekend zijn. Kan selectief luisteren om de benodigde informatie zoals openingstijden, adres, telefoonnummer te halen uit korte berichten op bijvoorbeeld een telefoonbeantwoorder.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 1F, Taken / 3. Luisteren naar radio en televisie en naar gesproken tekst op internet / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can understand main points of short and clear messages on radio and television and via the internet if topic and context are known. Can listen selectively in order to get the information needed, such as opening hours, address, telephone number, from short messages on, for example, a telephone answering machine.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 watches a short item in a children's news programme about a storm that is coming, a subject the class talked about that morning, and can say afterwards when the storm comes and what people are advised to do. The pupil rings the swimming pool, hears a recorded message, and listens for one thing only: until what time the pool is open on Saturday. The pupil writes the time down.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), task 3, *Luisteren naar radio en televisie en naar gesproken tekst op internet* (listening to radio and television and to spoken text on the internet). It stands under *Taken* (tasks). It is the only statement for this task at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The cell holds two sentences. The first asks for main points of messages that are short and clear, and only if topic and context are known. The second asks for listening selectively to short messages in order to get the information needed. Opening hours, address and telephone number come after "such as", and the telephone answering machine after "for example": they are examples of the level, not limits. The statement gives "short" no number and names no programmes or topics.

### Common mistakes

A pupil tries to follow every word of a recorded message and misses the one piece of information that was needed. A pupil writes down a number or a time wrongly because it is said once and fast, and does not listen again. A pupil no longer follows a broadcast item when the topic is unfamiliar, because a broadcast cannot be asked to repeat or explain.
