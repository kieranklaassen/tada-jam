## Official wording

aandachtig luisteren, kijken of aandachtig vloeiend lezen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 A a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

attentive listening, watching or attentive fluent reading;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 listens to a story or an explanation to the end and can say afterwards what was said at the start as well as at the end. The child watches a short film about a subject and notices what the images show as well as what the voice says. The child reads a page of a book at an even pace, without stopping at each word, and keeps the attention on what the text says.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 2 A, which says that the pupil shows understanding of non-fiction and literary texts; 2 A sits under core goal 2. It names three ways of taking in a text, joined by "or": listening, watching and reading. The source prints "attentive" (*aandachtig*) before listening and again before reading; it is not clear whether the first one also covers watching, and the gloss leaves that as open as the Dutch. "Fluent" is said of reading only. The item states no reading speed, no number of words per minute, no length of text and no length of time for paying attention.

### Common mistakes

A child reads aloud fast and without errors, takes this for understanding, and cannot say what was read. A child reads word by word, so the meaning of a sentence is lost before its end. While listening or watching, a child follows the pictures or the speaker's voice and misses the content.
