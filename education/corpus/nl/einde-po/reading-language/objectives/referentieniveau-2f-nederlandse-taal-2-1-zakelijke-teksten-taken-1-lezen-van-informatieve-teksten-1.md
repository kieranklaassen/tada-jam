## Official wording

Kan informatieve teksten lezen, waaronder schoolboek en studieteksten (voor taal- en zaakvakken), standaardformulieren, populaire tijdschriften, teksten van internet, notities en schematische informatie (waarin verschillende dimensies gecombineerd worden) en het alledaagse nieuws in de krant.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 2F, Taken / 1. Lezen van informatieve teksten / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can read informative texts, including school-book and study texts (for language subjects and *zaakvakken*, subjects such as geography, history and science), standard forms, popular magazines, texts from the internet, notes and schematic information (in which different dimensions are combined) and the everyday news in the newspaper.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who reads at this level reads a chapter of a geography book and answers questions on it, reads a standard form, such as one for joining a library, and says what each box asks for, and reads a short news report in a newspaper. The child reads a timetable or a table with rows and columns and finds the one cell where the right row and the right column meet, for example the bus that leaves from a given stop after a given time.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading factual texts (*Lezen zakelijke teksten*), row *1. Lezen van informatieve teksten* (reading informative texts). The row stands under *Taken* (tasks). 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The list follows the word *waaronder* ("including"), so it names kinds of informative text that fall under the statement and is not closed. Schematic information is bounded by its brackets: information in which different dimensions are combined (the pack's example: a table read by row and by column). The newspaper news is "everyday" news. The statement names no titles, no length and no reading speed. The source prints *schoolboek en studieteksten* without a hyphen after *schoolboek*; school-book texts and study texts are plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

A child reads a table along one dimension only, for example finds the right row but takes the number from the wrong column. A child reads a study text once from beginning to end and cannot find where an answer stood, because headings and marked words were not used. A child skips the small print on a form, such as the note that says which boxes must be filled in.
