## Official wording

Kan (in het kader van het leesdoel) belangrijke informatie uit de tekst halen en kan zijn manier van lezen daar op afstemmen (bijvoorbeeld globaal, precies, selectief/gericht).

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can (in the light of the reading purpose) take important information from the text and can adjust their way of reading to it (for example global, precise, selective/targeted).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says before reading what it wants from the text: for a talk about volcanoes it needs to know how an eruption starts. The child looks over the headings to find the part about eruptions, reads that part slowly, and underlines the sentences that say how an eruption starts. To find one departure time on a leaflet the child moves a finger down the page until it reaches the right day and reads only that line.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Zakelijke teksten* (reading factual texts), *Begrijpen* (understanding). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the second of two statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. What counts as important is tied to the reading purpose (*leesdoel*); the statement does not say what the purpose is or who sets it. The brackets give three ways of reading after "for example" (*bijvoorbeeld*): global, precise, and selective or targeted. They are examples of the level, not a complete list. The statement names no kind of text and no length.

### Common mistakes

A child reads every text from the first word to the last in the same way, whatever it is reading for. A child underlines nearly every sentence, so the important information can no longer be told from the rest. A child who is looking for one fact stops at an interesting detail that has nothing to do with the purpose and forgets what it was looking for.
