## Official wording

Houding, intonatie en mimiek ondersteunen het gesprokene.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.3 Spreken, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing / 3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Posture (*houding*, which can also mean attitude), intonation and *mimiek* (facial expression, or gestures and facial expression) support what is spoken.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 gives a talk about a favourite sport. The pupil stands upright and turned towards the class, lets the voice rise at a question and stresses the important word in a sentence. The pupil's face shows pleasure when telling about a match that was won.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Spreken* (speaking), *Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing* (fluency, intelligibility and command of grammar). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance). In this cell it follows the cross-reference *Zie Gesprekken +* (see Conversations, plus), another record of this lane, so it comes in addition to what holds for conversations. Its position in the cell is 3: the decree prints the plus sign as a paragraph of its own in position 2, which the pack joins to the cross-reference and does not hold as a record. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names three things that support what is spoken: *houding*, intonation and *mimiek*. *Houding* can mean the posture of the body or the attitude a speaker shows, and *mimiek* can mean facial expression alone or gestures and facial expression together; the statement does not choose. It does not name eye contact, and it sets no degree.

### Common mistakes

A pupil speaks in one tone throughout, so the listener cannot hear what is important or where a sentence ends. A pupil stands turned away from the audience or looks at the floor. A pupil reads out the whole text, and voice and face follow the reading instead of the meaning.
