## Official wording

Lijdende en bedrijvende vorm, vragende vorm.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.1 Begrippenlijst, niveau 2F, Grammaticale kennis.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Passive and active form (*lijdende en bedrijvende vorm*), interrogative form (*vragende vorm*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 at this level looks at two sentences about the same event and says which is active and which is passive: in the active one the subject does the action, in the passive one the subject undergoes it. The child says of a question such as *Leest het meisje het boek?* that it is in the *vragende vorm* (the form of a question). The child uses the words *bedrijvende vorm*, *lijdende vorm* and *vragende vorm* when talking about a text, for example to say that a news report has many passive sentences.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, terms and language care, list of terms (*Begrippenlijst*), row *Grammaticale kennis* (grammatical knowledge). It is the cell of this row in the column for level 2F of the decree's table of terms (Table 1); the table prints no sign, and the column gives the level. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The decree introduces the list as terms for talking about language: the point is that teachers (and pupils) can name certain phenomena in language situations with a rich context. The cell names three terms: *lijdende vorm* (passive form), *bedrijvende vorm* (active form) and *vragende vorm* (interrogative form, the form of a question). It gives no definitions and no examples, and it does not say that the pupil turns one form into another. The pack's examples, all correct Dutch: active, *Het meisje leest het boek.* (The girl reads the book.); passive, *Het boek wordt door het meisje gelezen.* (The book is read by the girl.); interrogative, *Leest het meisje het boek?* (Does the girl read the book?).

### Common mistakes

A child takes every sentence with *worden* for a passive, also *Hij wordt ziek.* (He becomes ill.), where *worden* is not the helping verb of the passive. A child thinks that a passive sentence is a sentence in the past tense. A child calls only sentences with a question word (*wie*, *wat*, *waar*: who, what, where) interrogative and misses the question that begins with the verb.
