## Official wording

De teksten hebben een lage informatiedichtheid doordat bijvoorbeeld informatie vaak herhaald wordt.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 1F, Tekstkenmerken / Opbouw / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The texts have a low information density because, for example, information is often repeated.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 listens to the teacher explaining the arrangements for the school trip. The teacher says the departure time at the start, says it again when talking about the bus, and says it once more at the end. A pupil who did not hear it the first time hears it the second time.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), *Opbouw* (structure, how a text is built). It stands under *Tekstkenmerken* (text features), which describe the texts a pupil listens to at this level, not what the pupil does. The row holds three statements at 1F; the other two are about a simple structure and about how much information is introduced at the same time. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement names one property, a low information density, and gives one cause after "for example": information is often repeated. Repetition is an example, so other causes of a low density are not excluded and not named. The statement gives no measure of density.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error; it describes the text, not the pupil. What pupils most often leave out is using the repetition: a pupil who has heard something once stops listening for it, and misses that it was said the second time with a change or an addition. Some pupils take a repeated point for a new one.
