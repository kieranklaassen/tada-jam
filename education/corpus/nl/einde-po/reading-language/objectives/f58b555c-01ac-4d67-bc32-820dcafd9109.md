## Official wording

luisteren naar, doorvragen op en ter discussie stellen van de ideeën en perspectieven van gesprekspartners;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 4 B c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

listening to, asking follow-up questions about (*doorvragen op*, asking further about what was said) and calling into question (*ter discussie stellen*) the ideas and perspectives of conversation partners;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 hears a classmate's plan to its end, asks "How would that work when it rains?", and then says "I doubt that part, because last time the paint did not dry." The child questions the idea, and says which part, without judging the person.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 4 B, which says that the pupil holds conversations to arrive at the building of knowledge, understanding or an approach; 4 B sits under core goal 4. The item names three actions on the ideas and perspectives of conversation partners: listening to them, asking follow-up questions about them, and calling them into question. The Dutch *ter discussie stellen* means opening something to discussion or doubt; it does not say that the idea is rejected. Accepting or rejecting is in item 4 B d. The item names no kind of question and sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child takes a critical question about their idea as an attack on themselves, or words a question to a partner as an attack. A child asks a follow-up question that changes the subject to their own idea. A child agrees with everything to avoid a quarrel and questions nothing.
