## Official wording

Kan een reactie uitstellen totdat hij de bijdrage van de ander geïnterpreteerd en beoordeeld heeft.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Beurten nemen en bijdragen aan samenhang / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can postpone a reaction until he has interpreted and judged the other's contribution.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: in a discussion about whether the class should give up the yearly trip to pay for new playground equipment, a pupil of about 12 hears a classmate argue for this. The pupil lets the classmate finish, considers what is meant and whether the reasons are good, and only then says: "You mean the equipment lasts longer than one day out. I agree with that, but we would miss the trip."

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), row *Beurten nemen en bijdragen aan samenhang* (taking turns and contributing to coherence). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the second of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names one action, postponing a reaction, and its end point: the pupil has interpreted the other's contribution and has judged it. The source writes *hij* (he) for any pupil. The statement does not say how long the pupil waits or how the interpreting and judging are shown; the restating of the classmate's point in the illustration is the pack's example. It names no kind of conversation.

### Common mistakes

A pupil reacts to the first words of a contribution and misses the point that comes after them. A pupil thinks about his or her own next sentence while the other speaks, and then answers something that was not said. A pupil waits until the other has finished but judges the speaker and not what was said.
