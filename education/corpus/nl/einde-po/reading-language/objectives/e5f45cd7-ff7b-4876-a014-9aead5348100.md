## Official wording

De leerlingen leren zich naar vorm en inhoud uit te drukken bij het geven en vragen van informatie, het uitbrengen van verslag, het geven van uitleg, het instrueren en bij het discussiëren.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to express themselves as to form and content (*naar vorm en inhoud*, both how and what is said) when giving and asking for information, giving a report, giving an explanation, instructing and when discussing.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 asks in a library where the books on volcanoes are, tells a group what happened on a school trip, explains to a younger child how a long division works, tells someone step by step how to fold a paper boat, and gives an opinion in a discussion. Each time the child chooses words and an order that fit the listener.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is goal 2 of the core goals for Dutch, under the heading *Mondeling taalonderwijs* (oral language education), with goals 1 and 3. The goal names five situations: giving and asking for information, giving a report, giving an explanation, instructing, and discussing. It asks for both form and content and does not say what good form is. The sentence itself does not say "orally"; the heading places it in oral language education. It names no listeners, no subjects and no length, and sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A pupil gives an instruction with the steps in the wrong order, or leaves out a step the listener needs. A pupil speaks as if the listener already knows what the pupil knows ("then you put that thing there"). A pupil speaks in the same way in every situation and to every listener.
