## Official wording

Herkent specifieke informatie, wanneer naar één expliciet genoemde informatie-eenheid gevraagd wordt (letterlijk begrip).

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 1F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Recognises specific information, when one explicitly stated unit of information is asked for (literal understanding).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reads a text about hedgehogs and is asked what hedgehogs eat. The child goes back into the text, puts a finger on the sentence that says it, and gives the answer in the words of the text. In a notice about a swimming pool the child finds the one line that gives the opening time on Saturday.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Zakelijke teksten* (reading factual texts), *Begrijpen* (understanding). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the first of two statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The statement is bounded in three ways: the question asks for one unit of information, that unit is stated explicitly in the text, and the understanding is literal. It asks for no inference and no combining of two places in the text. It names no kind of text, no length of text and no number of questions.

### Common mistakes

A child answers from what it already knows about the subject instead of from the text. A child picks a sentence that holds a word from the question but answers something else. A child copies a whole paragraph where one piece of information was asked for.
