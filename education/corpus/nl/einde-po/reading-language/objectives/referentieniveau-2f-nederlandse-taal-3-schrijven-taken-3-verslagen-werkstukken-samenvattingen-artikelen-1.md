## Official wording

Kan verslagen en werkstukken schrijven met behulp van een stramien en daarbij informatie uit verscheidene bronnen samenvoegen. Kan onderhoudende teksten schrijven en overtuigen met argumenten.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 3 Schrijven, niveau 2F, Taken / 3. Verslagen, werkstukken, samenvattingen, artikelen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can write reports and project papers (*werkstukken*) with the help of a set format (*stramien*) and combine information from several sources in them. Can write entertaining texts and persuade with arguments.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who writes at this level writes a project paper about, for example, volcanoes on a given outline: an introduction, a chapter for each sub-question, a conclusion and a list of sources. The child takes facts from two books and a website and puts them together in own sentences under the right sub-question. The child also writes a story or a funny piece to amuse a reader, and a piece that argues for something, such as a later bedtime, with reasons.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, writing (*Schrijven*), row *3. Verslagen, werkstukken, samenvattingen, artikelen* (reports, project papers, summaries, articles). The row stands under *Taken* (tasks); this is the first of the two paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. Reports and project papers are written "with the help of a *stramien*", a given format or outline; the statement does not say who gives it or what it holds. The information comes from "several" (*verscheidene*) sources; no number and no kind of source is named. The second sentence names two more things, without mention of a format: writing entertaining texts, and persuading with arguments. The statement names no length and no topics. The row's name mentions summaries and articles; this statement does not name them.

### Common mistakes

A child copies sentences from a source instead of putting the information in own words. A child places the information from each source one after the other, without combining what belongs together. A child gives an opinion without arguments, or repeats one argument in other words.
