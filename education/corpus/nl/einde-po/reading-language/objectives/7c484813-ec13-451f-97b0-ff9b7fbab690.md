## Official wording

De leerlingen leren informatie en meningen te ordenen bij het schrijven van een brief, een verslag, een formulier of een werkstuk. Zij besteden daarbij aandacht aan zinsbouw, correcte spelling, een leesbaar handschrift, bladspiegel, eventueel beeldende elementen en kleur.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 8.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn to order information and opinions (*ordenen*, to arrange, to put in order) when writing a letter, a report, a form or a project paper (*werkstuk*, a piece of school work on one subject). In doing so they pay attention to sentence construction, correct spelling, legible handwriting, page layout (*bladspiegel*, how the text is placed on the page), possibly visual elements and colour.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 collects notes for a project paper about a country, sorts them into piles by sub-topic, puts the piles in an order, and writes a paragraph with a heading for each. The child writes a letter with the date, a greeting, the request and a closing, each in its place. Before handing the work in, the child rereads the sentences and the spelling, writes so that it can be read, keeps margins, and adds a drawing or a map.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is goal 8 of the core goals for Dutch, under the heading *Schriftelijk taalonderwijs* (written language education), goals 4 to 9. The first sentence is about ordering information and opinions when writing one of four named things: a letter, a report, a form or a *werkstuk*. The source speaks of writing a form; nothing says whether filling one in or drawing one up is meant. The second sentence lists what the pupils pay attention to: sentence construction, correct spelling, legible handwriting, page layout and, "possibly" (*eventueel*), visual elements and colour; the wording leaves open whether "possibly" covers colour as well as visual elements. The goal names a legible handwriting as one point of attention and names no means of writing. It sets no level and no length; rules for spelling are named in goal 11.

### Common mistakes

A pupil writes the information down in the order in which it was found and makes no paragraphs. A pupil spends the time on the cover, the drawings and the colours and little on the order of the content. A pupil does not reread, so spelling errors the pupil could correct stay in the text.
