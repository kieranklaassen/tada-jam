## Official wording

Kan het denken, voelen en handelen van personages beschrijven.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.2 Fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Begrijpen / 4.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can describe the thinking, feeling and acting of characters.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 says about a character in a story what the character does, what the character thinks at that moment and how the character feels, and points to the sentences that show it. Where the text does not state a feeling, the child reads it from what the character says or does, for example that a character who slams a door and says nothing is angry.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading fictional, narrative and literary texts (*Lezen fictionele, narratieve en literaire teksten*), row *Begrijpen* (understanding). The row stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (characteristics of task performance); this is the fourth of the five paragraphs in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names three things to describe: what characters think, what they feel and what they do. It says "characters" in the plural and does not limit this to the main character. It asks for describing; judging how realistic the characters are and characterising them stand in the row *Interpreteren* (interpreting) of the same section. It names no titles and no length.

### Common mistakes

A child tells what a character does and leaves out what the character thinks or feels. A child gives the child's own feeling ("I would be scared") as the character's feeling. A child misses a feeling that the text shows only through behaviour and does not name.
