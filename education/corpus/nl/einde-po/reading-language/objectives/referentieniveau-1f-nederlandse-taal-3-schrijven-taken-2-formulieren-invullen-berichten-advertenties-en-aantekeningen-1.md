## Official wording

Kan een kort bericht, een boodschap met eenvoudige informatie schrijven.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 3 Schrijven, niveau 1F, Taken / 2. Formulieren invullen, berichten, advertenties en aantekeningen / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can write a short message (*bericht*), a note (*boodschap*, a message passed on) with simple information.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 answers the telephone at home and writes a note for a parent: who called, what it was about, and that football practice is cancelled tomorrow. The child writes a short message to say that it has lost a blue coat and where the coat can be handed in.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Schrijven* (writing), *Formulieren invullen, berichten, advertenties en aantekeningen* (filling in forms, messages, advertisements and notes). It is task 2 under *Taken* (tasks) and the first of three statements in this row at 1F. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The message is short (*kort*) and the information is simple (*eenvoudige*). The source sets *een kort bericht* and *een boodschap met eenvoudige informatie* side by side with a comma; the second reads as another name for the first. The row label names advertisements; none of the three 1F statements in this row does. The statement gives no length in words.

### Common mistakes

A child leaves out who the message is from or who it is for. A child writes down the whole conversation instead of the point of it. A child writes "tomorrow" or "later" without a day or a time, so a reader who finds the note afterwards cannot tell when that is.
