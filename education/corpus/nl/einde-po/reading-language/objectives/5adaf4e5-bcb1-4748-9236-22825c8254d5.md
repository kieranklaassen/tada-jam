## Official wording

benoemen van inhouds- en vormelementen die misleidend zijn of vragen oproepen;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 C b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming elements of content and form that are misleading or raise questions;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 looks at an advertisement, a headline or an online post and points to what does not seem right: a heading that says more than the text under it, a photo that does not belong to the event, a claim without a source, words such as "always" and "everyone", or a graph whose axis does not start at zero. The child says what is odd about it and what the child would want to know before believing it.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 2 C, which says that the pupil explores the reliability of different sources; 2 C sits under core goal 2. It names two kinds of element, of content and of form, and two grounds: the element is misleading, or it raises questions. The item gives no example of such an element; the examples above are the pack's. It does not ask the pupil to prove that something is false. The item sets no level.

### Common mistakes

A child looks only at the content and does not notice the form: capitals, colours, or a logo that imitates a known one. A child calls everything misleading that the child disagrees with. A child does not recognise an advertisement that is made to look like a news report.
