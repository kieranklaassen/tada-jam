2. Orthographic spelling

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 spells words in which a rule about letters and syllables decides the spelling, not the sound alone. The pack's examples: *boom* (tree) becomes *bomen* in the plural, with one *o*, because the long vowel now ends its syllable (*bo-men*); *bom* (bomb) becomes *bommen*, with a doubled *m* after the short vowel (*bom-men*). The child also writes fixed letter groups correctly, as in *school* (school), *bank* (bench), *nacht* (night) and *leeuw* (lion).

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Spelling*, class 2, of the table of difficulty. It is one row of the decree's table 2, in the section *Moeilijkheid* (difficulty), which sets levels for spelling, punctuation and the grammatical terms for verb spelling. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The table marks this row with one plus sign (+), in the 1F column. The heading of the table gives the measure of mastery (*beheersing*): 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of a correct answer. The heading does not itself name the plus sign; that the plus sign stands for this mastery at the level of its column is the pack's reading of the heading. The record holds the name of the class only. The running text of section 4.4 of the decree, which is in no record, describes the class as rules of their own that reach across the boundary of syllables, and counts in it, among other things, words with *sch*, *ng*, *nk*, *aai*, *ooi*, *oei*, *ch(t)*, *-eeuw*, *-ieuw*, *-uw* and *-ee*, the doubling of consonants, and open syllables.

### Common mistakes

A child keeps the double vowel of the singular and writes *boomen*; correct is *bomen*. A child writes a single consonant after a short vowel, so that *bommen* (bombs) becomes *bomen* (trees). A child writes *bangk* for *bank*.
