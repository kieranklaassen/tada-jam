## Official wording

Kan in gesprekken over alledaagse en niet alledaagse onderwerpen uit leefwereld en (beroeps)opleiding uiting geven aan persoonlijke meningen, kan informatie uitwisselen en gevoelens onder woorden brengen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 2F, Algemene omschrijving Gesprekken / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can, in conversations about everyday and non-everyday subjects from the world of daily life (*leefwereld*) and from (vocational) education (*(beroeps)opleiding*, schooling or training for an occupation), express personal opinions, can exchange information and put feelings into words.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 talks with two classmates and the teacher about the school camp (an everyday subject) and about a news item on a flood in another country (a subject that is not everyday). The pupil says what he or she thinks of the plan for the camp, asks what the others know about the flood and tells what he or she heard about it, and says that the pictures of it were frightening.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), row *Algemene omschrijving* (general description). It is the whole general description of the skill at this level; the tasks and the features of task performance are other records. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names three things done in conversation: expressing personal opinions, exchanging information and putting feelings into words. It names two kinds of subject, everyday and not everyday, and two areas they come from: *leefwereld* and *(beroeps)opleiding*. The brackets around *beroeps* (vocational) are in the source; the statement does not say what *opleiding* covers for a pupil in primary school. It lists no subjects, names no conversation partners and no number of them, and sets no length for a conversation.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out is the feeling: they give an opinion and facts and do not say how something affects them. On a subject that is not everyday, a pupil often gives an opinion and has no information to exchange with it.
