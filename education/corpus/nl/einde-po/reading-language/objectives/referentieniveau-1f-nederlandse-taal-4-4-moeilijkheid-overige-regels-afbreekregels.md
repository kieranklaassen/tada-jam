## Official wording

Afbreekregels

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.4 Moeilijkheid, niveau 1F, Overige regels / Afbreekregels.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Rules for dividing words at the end of a line (*afbreekregels*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 reaches the end of a line in the middle of a word, says the word in syllables, and breaks it between two syllables with a hyphen. The pack's examples: *lo-pen* (to walk), where one consonant between two vowels goes to the next line; *kat-ten* (cats), where the two consonants are divided; and *school-plein* (school yard), where a compound is divided between its two words.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Afbreekregels* under *Overige regels* (other rules) of the table of difficulty. It is one row of the decree's table 2, in the section *Moeilijkheid* (difficulty), which sets levels for spelling, punctuation and the grammatical terms for verb spelling. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The table marks this row with one plus sign (+), in the 1F column. The heading of the table gives the measure of mastery (*beheersing*): 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of a correct answer. The heading does not itself name the plus sign; that the plus sign stands for this mastery at the level of its column is the pack's reading of the heading. The record is one word: it names the rules and does not state them. The list of spelling rules in section 4.3.1 of the decree, which is in no record, calls these rules an interplay of morphological rules and spelling rules and prints four divisions of *getrokken* (pulled) without saying which are right: *ge-trokken*, *getrok-ken*, *get-rokken* and *getrokk-en*. The word is divided as *ge-trok-ken*, so the first two are right and the last two are wrong.

### Common mistakes

A child breaks a word after whatever letter the line ends on, as in *get-rokken* for *ge-trok-ken*. A child splits two letters that stand for one sound and writes *lac-hen*; correct is *la-chen* (to laugh). A child divides a compound by sound and not between its words, as in *schoolp-lein*; correct is *school-plein*.
