## Official wording

Kan een voorgelezen of verteld verhaal begrijpen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.2 Luisteren, niveau 2F, Taken / 2. Luisteren als lid van een live publiek / 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can understand a story that is read aloud or told.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: a pupil of about 12 listens while the teacher reads a chapter of a children's novel to the class, or while a storyteller in the library tells a folk tale without a book. Afterwards the pupil can say who the story was about, what happened and why the main character acted as he or she did.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Luisteren* (listening), row *Luisteren als lid van een live publiek* (listening as a member of a live audience). It stands under *Taken* (tasks), as task 2, and is the second of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names one kind of text, a story, and two ways in which it reaches the pupil: read aloud or told. It sets no qualifier on the story: no length, no kind of story and no level of difficulty. It does not say who reads or tells, and does not say how the pupil shows the understanding; the retelling in the illustration is the pack's example.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What pupils most often leave out is what the story does not say outright: they follow the events and miss why a character acts or what a character feels. Many also stop understanding when the story jumps in time or changes the person who tells it.
