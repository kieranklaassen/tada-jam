## Official wording

5. Logografisch: geen regels, maar kennis van vaststaande combinatie tussen klank en teken zoals /sj/ geschreven als -ch-

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.4 Moeilijkheid, niveau 1F, Spelling / 5. Logografisch.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

5. Logographic: no rules, but knowledge of a fixed combination between sound and sign, such as /sj/ written as -ch-

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 knows by heart how certain words are written, because no rule gives the spelling. The pack's examples of the statement's own case, the sound *sj* written with *ch*: *chef* (boss), *machine* (machine) and *douche* (shower). The child has seen and written these words often enough to write them at once, and does not try to build them from their sounds.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Spelling*, class 5, of the table of difficulty. It is one row of the decree's table 2, in the section *Moeilijkheid* (difficulty), which sets levels for spelling, punctuation and the grammatical terms for verb spelling. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The table marks this row with one plus sign (+), in the 1F column. The heading of the table gives the measure of mastery (*beheersing*): 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of a correct answer. The heading does not itself name the plus sign; that the plus sign stands for this mastery at the level of its column is the pack's reading of the heading. The record is the whole text of the row: the name of the class and a short description after a colon, without a closing full stop. The description says that there are no rules here, only knowledge of fixed combinations of a sound and a sign. It gives one example after "such as" (*zoals*): the sound /sj/ written as *ch*. That is an example of the level, not a limit; the row names no other combination and gives no word list. The running text of section 4.4 of the decree, which is in no record, lists more such combinations.

### Common mistakes

A child writes the word as it sounds: *sjef* for *chef* or *masjine* for *machine*. A child who has learned that *ch* can sound as *sj* uses it where *sj* is correct and writes *chaal* for *sjaal* (scarf). A child looks for a rule, finds none, and spells the same word differently each time.
