## Official wording

d) stoffelijke bijvoeglijke naamwoorden op -en

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.4 Moeilijkheid, niveau 2F, Spelling / 3. Morfologische spelling / Moeilijke gevallen / d).

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

d) material adjectives (*stoffelijke bijvoeglijke naamwoorden*, adjectives naming what something is made of) ending in -en

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 at this level writes an adjective that says what a thing is made of with *-en* at the end, although the *n* is often not heard in speech: *een houten tafel* (a wooden table), *de gouden ring* (the gold ring), *wollen sokken* (woollen socks). The child keeps the same form for one thing and for several: *een stenen muur*, *twee stenen muren* (a stone wall, two stone walls).

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, terms and language care, table of difficulty (*Moeilijkheid*), row d) of the difficult cases (*Moeilijke gevallen*) under *Spelling*, 3. *Morfologische spelling* (morphological spelling, the spelling of word forms such as plurals and diminutives). In this table (Table 2 of the decree) the row has a plus sign in the column for level 2F. The table's caption describes its levels as mastery: 75% of all pupils in the age group or level group have an 80% chance of getting it right. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The row names material adjectives ending in -en. It gives no examples and states no rule in words. The decree's running list of spelling rules in section 4.3.1, which carries no level and is not a record of the pack, gives two words for this case, *gouden* (gold) and *zilveren* (silver), and says that the form holds with a noun in the singular and in the plural. The pack's examples, all correct Dutch: *een houten tafel* (a wooden table), *een wollen trui* (a woollen jumper), *een ijzeren hek* (an iron fence), *een papieren zak* (a paper bag); and, with the decree's two words, *de gouden ring* (the gold ring) and *zilveren lepels* (silver spoons).

### Common mistakes

A child writes what is heard and leaves off the *n* (the wrong *een houte tafel*). A child takes the *-en* for a plural ending and drops it for one thing (the wrong *een goude ring*). A child writes the bare noun (the wrong *een hout tafel*).
