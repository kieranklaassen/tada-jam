## Official wording

Kan de juiste frase gebruiken om aan het woord te komen.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 1.1 Gesprekken, niveau 2F, Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering / Beurten nemen en bijdragen aan samenhang / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can use the right phrase to get a turn to speak.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The pack's illustration: in a class discussion about the rules for the football pitch, a pupil of about 12 waits for a pause and says "May I add something to that?" or "I would like to react to what Samira said." In a group of four the pupil says "Can I say something about this?" and then speaks.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, *Gesprekken* (conversations), row *Beurten nemen en bijdragen aan samenhang* (taking turns and contributing to coherence). It stands under *Kenmerken van de taakuitvoering* (features of task performance) and is the first of two statements in its cell. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names one means, a phrase, and one aim, getting a turn to speak. It gives no example phrases and does not say what makes a phrase right; the phrases in the illustration are the pack's examples. It names no kind of conversation and no number of participants, and it does not mention gestures such as raising a hand.

### Common mistakes

A pupil interrupts without any phrase and speaks while another pupil is still speaking. A pupil waits for a turn that nobody gives and says nothing in the whole discussion. A pupil uses a phrase that fits friends, such as "No, listen", in a formal discussion.
