## Official wording

De leerlingen leren een aantal taalkundige principes en regels. Zij kunnen in een zin het onderwerp, het werkwoordelijk gezegde en delen van dat gezegde onderscheiden.
De leerlingen kennen
– regels voor het spellen van werkwoorden;
– regels voor het spellen van andere woorden dan werkwoorden;
– regels voor het gebruik van leestekens.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit vernieuwde kerndoelen WPO (version in force until 31 July 2026), bijlage, kerndoel 11.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

The pupils learn a number of linguistic principles and rules. They can distinguish in a sentence the subject, the verbal predicate (*werkwoordelijk gezegde*, all the verbs of the clause together) and parts of that predicate.
The pupils know
– rules for spelling verbs;
– rules for spelling words other than verbs;
– rules for the use of punctuation marks.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes a Dutch sentence such as *De hond heeft de taart opgegeten* (the dog has eaten the cake), underlines who or what does it (*de hond*), circles the verbs that belong together (*heeft opgegeten*) and points to the two parts. The child writes a verb form and decides between -d, -t and -dt by a rule (*ik word*, *hij wordt*), and writes the plural of a noun by a rule. In a short dictation the child puts in the full stops, commas, question marks and quotation marks.

### Limits

This 2006 goal was replaced on 1 August 2026; schools may still use it until 1 August 2031. It is goal 11 of the core goals for Dutch, under the heading *Taalbeschouwing, waaronder strategieën* (reflection on language, including strategies), with goals 10 and 12. The goal has three sentences: learning a number of linguistic principles and rules; distinguishing in a sentence the subject, the verbal predicate and parts of that predicate; and knowing three groups of rules, printed as a list with dashes: for spelling verbs, for spelling other words, and for using punctuation marks. "A number of" is not filled in: the goal does not say which principles, which rules, which parts of the predicate or which punctuation marks. The verbs differ: the pupils "can" distinguish the sentence parts and "know" the rules. Other sentence parts and word classes are not mentioned. The goal sets no level. The reference levels 1F and 2F, which are other records of this lane, are worded in more detail.

### Common mistakes

A pupil takes the first noun or the first word of the sentence for the subject. A pupil spells a verb by its sound and writes *hij word* or *het gebeurd*, because -d, -t and -dt sound the same at the end of a Dutch word. A pupil applies a rule in an exercise and not in a text the pupil writes.
