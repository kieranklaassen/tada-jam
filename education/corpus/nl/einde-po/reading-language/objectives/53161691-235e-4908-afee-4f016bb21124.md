## Official wording

in eigen woorden weergeven van de hoofd- en bijzaken, de hoofdgedachte en betekenis van een tekst, passend bij het lees- of luisterdoel;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 A e.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

giving in one's own words the main points and minor points, the main idea and the meaning of a text, fitting the reading or listening purpose;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 has read an article about plastic in the sea and tells someone who has not read it what it says, in two or three sentences of the child's own. Asked for more, the child adds details and says which of them matter less. When the child read the text to find out one thing, for example how long plastic lasts in water, the child gives that and leaves the rest out.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 2 A, which says that the pupil shows understanding of non-fiction and literary texts; 2 A sits under core goal 2. It names what is given (the main points and minor points, the main idea and the meaning of a text), how (in the pupil's own words) and a condition (fitting the reading or listening purpose). A reading purpose and a listening purpose are named; a purpose for watching is not, although item 2 A a names watching. The item does not say whether the pupil gives this in speech or in writing, and it sets no length for the text or for what the pupil gives. Summarising content that has been read is a separate item, 3 C b.

### Common mistakes

A child copies sentences from the text where the child's own words are asked. A child gives the first sentence or the most exciting detail as the main idea. A child retells everything in the order of the text and does not separate main points from minor points.
