## Official wording

Kan teksten lezen over alledaagse onderwerpen, onderwerpen die aansluiten bij de leefwereld van de leerling en over onderwerpen die verder van de leerling af staan.

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 2.1 Zakelijke teksten, niveau 2F, Algemene omschrijving Lezen zakelijke teksten / 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Can read texts about everyday topics, topics that connect with the pupil's own world (*leefwereld*), and about topics that are further away from the pupil.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 who reads at this level reads a text about something from daily life, such as the rules of a swimming pool, and a text about something from the child's own world, such as a report on a sport the child plays. The child also reads a text about a topic the child has no experience of, such as how a town council decides on a new road, and says afterwards what it was about.

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 2F for Dutch, reading factual texts (*Lezen zakelijke teksten*), row *Algemene omschrijving* (general description). It is the one general sentence of the section; the rows on texts, tasks and characteristics of task performance are separate records of this lane. 2F is the higher level that many pupils reach at the end of primary school, above the basic level 1F. The statement names three ranges of topic: everyday topics, topics from the pupil's own world, and topics further away from the pupil. It does not say how far from the pupil a topic may be, and it names no kind of text, no length and no title.

### Common mistakes

A child reads the words of a text on an unfamiliar topic correctly but cannot say what the text is about, because the background knowledge is missing. A child fills a gap in understanding with what the child already believes about the topic instead of with what the text says. A child stops reading when the topic is far from the child's own interests.
