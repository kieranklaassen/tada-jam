## Official wording

Woordvorm, woorddeel, samengesteld, voorvoegsel, achtervoegsel, lettergreep. Getal (meervoud/enkelvoud), tijd (tegenwoordig, verleden, voltooid, onvoltooid). Verkleinwoord, verschijningsvormen werkwoord (stam, infinitief, bijvoeglijk naamwoord).

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit referentieniveaus Nederlandse taal en rekenen, bijlage 1, 4.1 Begrippenlijst, niveau 1F, Morfologie.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

Word form (*woordvorm*), word part (*woorddeel*), compound (*samengesteld*), prefix (*voorvoegsel*), suffix (*achtervoegsel*), syllable (*lettergreep*). Number (*getal*: plural/singular, *meervoud*/*enkelvoud*), tense (*tijd*: present, past, perfect, imperfect; *tegenwoordig*, *verleden*, *voltooid*, *onvoltooid*). Diminutive (*verkleinwoord*), forms in which the verb appears (*verschijningsvormen werkwoord*: stem, infinitive, adjective; *stam*, *infinitief*, *bijvoeglijk naamwoord*).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 takes words apart and names the parts. In the pack's examples the child splits *schooltas* (school bag) into the words *school* and *tas* and calls it a compound; names *on-* in *onveilig* (unsafe) as a prefix and *-lijk* in *gevaarlijk* (dangerous) as a suffix; says *wa-ter* (water) in two syllables; and gives *boeken* as the plural of *boek* (book) and *boekje* as its diminutive. For the verb *werken* (to work) the child names *werken* as the infinitive and *werk* as the stem, and says the tense of *ik werk* (present), *ik werkte* (past) and *ik heb gewerkt* (perfect).

### Limits

This is a statement of reference level 1F for Dutch, *Begrippenlijst en Taalverzorging* (list of terms and language care), the row *Morfologie* (morphology) of the list of terms (*Begrippenlijst*). It is the 1F cell of that row in the decree's table 1, which gives the terms about language by level. 1F is the level every pupil should reach at the end of primary school. The cell holds three groups of terms, printed as three sentences, and nothing more: no definitions and no mark of how well a pupil must know them. The introduction to the list in the decree, which is in no record, says that a limited number of terms is needed for talking about language and that most of them have been dealt with by the end of primary school. The first group has six terms. The second has number, with plural and singular in brackets, and tense, with four words in brackets: present, past, perfect (*voltooid*, completed) and imperfect (*onvoltooid*, not completed). The third has the diminutive and the forms in which a verb appears, with three in brackets: stem, infinitive and adjective. The brackets list the adjective (*bijvoeglijk naamwoord*) as a form of the verb; a verb form used as an adjective, as in *de gebakken vis* (the fried fish), is presumably meant, and the cell does not name the past participle. *Samengesteld* is printed as an adjective ("compound", said of a word).

### Common mistakes

A child mixes up a syllable (*lettergreep*), which is a unit of sound, with a word part (*woorddeel*), which carries meaning: *lopen* (to walk) has the syllables *lo-pen* and the parts *loop* and *-en*. A child takes the infinitive without *-en* as the stem and writes *lop*; the stem of *lopen* is *loop*. A child calls *ik heb gewerkt* simply past tense and does not see the difference between *verleden* (past) and *voltooid* (perfect).
