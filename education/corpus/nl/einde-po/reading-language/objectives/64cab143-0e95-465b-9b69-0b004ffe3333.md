## Official wording

inzetten en uitbreiden van kennis over de vorm van teksten: tekstsoorten, tekststructuren, literaire genres, verteltechnieken;

Source: Rijksoverheid (Basiswettenbestand, KOOP), Besluit kerndoelen primair en speciaal onderwijs 2026, bijlage 1, onderdeel A, kerndoel 2 A c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting to use and extending knowledge about the form of texts: kinds of text, text structures, literary genres, narrative techniques;

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of 11 or 12 picks up a text and says what kind it is, for example a recipe, a news report, a letter or a poem, and what a reader can expect from it. The child uses headings, paragraphs and signal words to follow how a text is built (the pack's examples of Dutch signal words: *eerst*, first, *daarna*, after that, *omdat*, because). In a story the child says who tells it and notices when the story goes back to an earlier time.

### Limits

This is an item under goal sentence 2 A, which says that the pupil shows understanding of non-fiction and literary texts; 2 A sits under core goal 2. The item lists four things that knowledge about the form of texts covers: kinds of text, text structures, literary genres and narrative techniques. It names no particular kind of text, structure, genre or technique, so this item requires none in particular. Item 3 A c, for producing texts, has the same list without literary genres. The item sets no level and gives no list of terms a pupil has to know.

### Common mistakes

A child knows the name of a kind of text and cannot say what it means for reading, for example that an advertisement is made to sell something. A child overlooks signal words, so a text that compares two things is read as a list of separate facts. In a story told by an "I", a child takes the narrator for the writer.
