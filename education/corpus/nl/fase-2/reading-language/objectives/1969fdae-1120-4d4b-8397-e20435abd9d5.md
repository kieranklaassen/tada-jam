## Official wording

herkennen van meningen die afwijken van de eigen mening

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/03/03; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 1969fdae-1120-4d4b-8397-e20435abd9d5.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising opinions that differ from one's own opinion

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a short text in which the writer finds, for example, that children should have no homework or that zoos should close, and says first what the writer thinks and then what they think themselves. The child says where the two differ. A child of 7 says "I do not think so" and points to the sentence; a child of 10 states the writer's opinion fairly in their own words before giving their own.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is recognising: the goal does not ask the child to change their opinion, to argue against the other one, or to weigh reasons. The opinions are ones that differ from the child's own; the wording does not say whose they are (the writer's, or those of people in the text). It names no kinds of text; its cluster is about reading non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Reading their own opinion into the text and not noticing that the writer says something else. Calling the other opinion wrong or untrue and not an opinion. Having formed no opinion of their own, so there is nothing to compare with.
