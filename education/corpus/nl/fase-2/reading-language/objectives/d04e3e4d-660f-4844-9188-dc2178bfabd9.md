## Official wording

(met hulp) reflecteren op eigen aandeel in en aanpak van gesprek

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/01/08; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau d04e3e4d-660f-4844-9188-dc2178bfabd9.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) reflecting on one's own part in and approach to a conversation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a group talk the child answers the adult's questions: "How often did you speak? Did you respond to someone? How did you go about it?". The child places a peg on a line between "I said little" and "I said much" and gives one example of something they said. With the group the child looks at the agreements made beforehand and says whether they kept to them. A child of 7 recalls something they said and whether they waited for their turn; a child of 10 says what their contribution did to the talk, what worked in their approach and what they would do differently.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints "met hulp" after the verb, without brackets. The statement does not say who helps or how. The reflection is on two things: the child's own part in the conversation, and the approach. The wording leaves open whether "own" also applies to the approach, that is, whether the approach is the child's or the group's. The statement names no points to reflect on and no form of reflection. NE/MT/01/08/03/fase2 is the goal about determining learning goals for a next conversation.

### Common mistakes

Answering "good" to every question, without an example. Telling again what the talk was about instead of saying something about their own part in it. Talking about what a classmate did wrong.
