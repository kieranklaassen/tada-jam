## Official wording

kennismaken met de opmaak van teksten (bijv. titels, kopjes, illustraties, datering, adressering, aanhef en ondertekening)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know the layout of texts (e.g. titles, headings, illustrations, dating, addressing, salutation and signature)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a real letter, a page of a magazine and a poster and points out their parts: the title, the headings, the picture, the date, the address, the greeting at the top and the name at the bottom. The child then gives their own text such parts: a title above a story, headings in a report, and a date, "Beste ..." (Dear ...) and their name in a letter. A child of 7 puts a title above a text and a greeting and a name in a card or letter; a child of 10 lays out a letter with date and address, and a report with headings and a picture.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is getting to know, not mastering. Everything in brackets follows "bijv." and is an example of layout: titles, headings, illustrations, dating, addressing, salutation and signature. The goal names no kinds of text; that the last four examples belong to letters and similar texts is the pack's observation. It does not say whether the texts are handwritten or typed, and it sets no rules for where each part goes.

### Common mistakes

Leaving out the date or the name of the writer in a letter. Writing a title that is a whole first sentence, or no title. Putting the greeting and the first sentence on one line, with no break between them.
