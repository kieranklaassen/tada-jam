recognising simple figurative language (*beeldspraak*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears "He is as strong as a bear" in a story and says that he is not a bear and that it means very strong. The child hears the Dutch expression *het regent pijpenstelen* (literally "it is raining pipe stems") and says that it means heavy rain. From sentences that are read aloud the child raises a hand at each one that is not meant literally. A child of 7 recognises a comparison with "as ... as"; a child of 10 also recognises a figure without "as" or "like" ("the classroom was an oven") and says what is compared with what.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "recognising": the statement does not ask the child to use figurative language. The figurative language is called simple; the statement does not define that, names no kinds (comparison, metaphor, saying) and gives no examples. The open data lists it under listening (NE/MT/02). The source prints the verb without *van* ("of") after it, unlike most neighbouring goals; the gloss reads it in the same form as theirs. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Taking the figure literally. Recognising that a sentence is not meant literally and giving it a wrong meaning from one of its words. Calling any unusual or difficult word figurative language.
