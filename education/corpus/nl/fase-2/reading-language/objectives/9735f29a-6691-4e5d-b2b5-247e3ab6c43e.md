## Official wording

(met hulp) reflecteren op de uitvoering van de luistertaak

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/02/09; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 9735f29a-6691-4e5d-b2b5-247e3ab6c43e.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) reflecting on the carrying out of the listening task

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After the listening task the adult asks "Did you find what you were listening for? What helped? What made it hard?", and the child answers with an example. The child compares their key words with a partner's and sees what they missed. The child names one thing to do at the next listening task, such as asking sooner or writing fewer words. A child of 7 says whether it went well and names something that helped; a child of 10 says which strategy they used, whether it fitted the purpose and what they would choose next time.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it at the end of the sentence. The statement does not say who helps or how. The reflection is on how the listening task was carried out, not on the content of the text; forming an opinion about the content is the subject of NE/MT/02/08/01/fase2. The statement names no points to reflect on and no form of reflection.

### Common mistakes

Saying whether the text was nice when asked how the listening went. Answering "good" every time, without an example. Naming only causes outside themselves ("it went too fast", "they were noisy") and nothing about their own listening.
