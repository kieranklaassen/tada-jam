## Official wording

schrijven langere zinnen, naast enkelvoudige ook samengestelde zinnen, grammaticaal complexer (spelfouten en grammaticale fouten kunnen worden maken

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing longer sentences, besides simple ones also composite sentences (*samengestelde zinnen*, sentences of more than one clause), grammatically more complex (spelling errors and grammatical errors may be made)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child joins two short sentences into one with a linking word: "Ik ging naar binnen. Het regende." becomes "Ik ging naar binnen, want het regende." The child reads the long sentence aloud to hear whether it sounds right and where it ends. A child of 7 joins two sentences with "en", "maar" or "want"; a child of 10 also writes sentences with a part that begins with "omdat", "toen" or "die", with the verb at the end of that part.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. "Langere" (longer) and "complexer" (more complex) are comparatives: the wording does not say than what, and it sets no sentence length and no number of clauses. Two kinds of sentence are named, simple and composite; the goal names no linking words and no kinds of clause, and the Dutch sentences and words above are the pack's examples. The brackets say that spelling errors and grammatical errors may be made, so the goal sets no level of correctness. The source prints the brackets as "(spelfouten en grammaticale fouten kunnen worden maken", with "maken" and without a closing bracket; "kunnen worden gemaakt)" (may be made) is plainly meant. The source words the goal as a clause without its subject ("schrijven langere zinnen"); writing longer sentences is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Joining clause after clause with "en toen" (and then), so that a sentence does not end. Keeping the word order of a main clause after "omdat": "omdat ik was ziek" for "omdat ik ziek was" (the pack's example). Writing a part that begins with "omdat" as a sentence of its own, with nothing it belongs to.
