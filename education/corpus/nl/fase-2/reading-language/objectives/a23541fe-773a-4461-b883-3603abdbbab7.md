summarising the text in one's own words

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After listening to a story, the child tells a classmate who was absent, in a few sentences, who it was about, what the problem was and how it ended. After a spoken informative text the child says in two or three sentences what it was about and what the main things said were. The child uses their own words and not sentences remembered from the text. A child of 7 retells the main events of a story in order; a child of 10 gives a summary of an informative text that leaves out details and examples.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The summary is in the child's own words. "The text" is a text that is listened to, since the open data lists the goal under listening (NE/MT/02). The statement itself sets no length of text or of summary. The content-line document prints text features for listening at fase 2 (kinds of text, a length for informative and for narrative texts, structure), which the lane's frame quotes; the two lengths are "informatieve teksten: 5 tot 10 minuten" (informative texts: 5 to 10 minutes) and "verhalende teksten: tot 15 minuten" (narrative texts: up to 15 minutes). Unlike NE/MT/02/07/01/fase2, the goal about selecting important information, this goal does not say "with help".

### Common mistakes

Retelling everything in order with every detail, so that the summary is nearly as long as the text. Repeating sentences from the text word for word. Giving one striking part and leaving out the ending or the main point.
