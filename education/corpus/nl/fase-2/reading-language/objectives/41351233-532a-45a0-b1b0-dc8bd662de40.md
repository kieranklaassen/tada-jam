## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child meets a long word such as "regenjas", "zakdoek" or "brandweerauto", draws a line between its parts, says what each part means and then what the whole means: "a coat for the rain". The child builds compound words from word cards and explains each one. A child of 7 splits "regenjas" into words they know and says what it means; a child of 10 splits a compound with a linking sound, such as "pannenkoek", and notices when the parts do not give the meaning.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two steps are named: recognising compound words, and deriving the meaning from this. "Hieruit" (from this) is not spelled out; the plain reading is from the parts of the compound. The goal names no words and sets no number of parts; the words above are the pack's examples. It does not mention words formed with prefixes or suffixes. Its cluster is about the approach to non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Taking the first part as the kind of thing: saying that a "bloempot" (flower pot) is a flower. Deriving a meaning from the parts where the whole means something else: "handschoen" (glove) explained as a shoe. Splitting at the wrong place: "kers-tomaat" (cherry tomato) read as "kerst-omaat".
