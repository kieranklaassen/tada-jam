## Official wording

oefenen met het gebruik van verwijswoorden en voegwoorden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

practising with the use of reference words (*verwijswoorden*, words that refer back) and conjunctions

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child replaces a repeated name with a word that refers back to it: "Sara heeft een hond. Sara laat de hond elke dag uit." becomes "Sara heeft een hond. Ze laat hem elke dag uit." The child joins two sentences with a conjunction such as "en", "maar", "want" or "omdat", and asks a reader whether it is clear who "ze" or "hij" is. A child of 7 uses "hij", "ze" and "het" for a person or thing just named and joins sentences with "en" and "maar"; a child of 10 also uses "die", "dat" and "deze", and conjunctions such as "omdat", "toen" and "terwijl".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. The verb is practising, not mastering. Two kinds of words are named: reference words and conjunctions. The goal lists no words of either kind; the Dutch sentences and words above are the pack's examples, and nothing in the wording says which words belong to this band. It sets no level of correctness, names no kind of text, and does not say that the child knows or uses the two terms.

### Common mistakes

Writing "hij" or "ze" after two people have been named, so that the reader cannot tell who is meant. Using "die" with a het-word: "het meisje die" for "het meisje dat" (the pack's example). Joining every sentence to the one before with "en toen" (and then).
