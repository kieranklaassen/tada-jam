## Official wording

(met hulp) reflecteren in tekstbesprekingen op het schrijfproces (oriënteren, formuleren en reviseren) en het schrijfproduct (structuur, samenhang, stijl, doel, publiek, vormaspecten)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) reflecting, in discussions of texts, on the writing process (orienting, formulating and revising) and the written product (structure, coherence, style, purpose, audience, aspects of form)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a small group the child reads their text aloud or lays it on the table, and the group talks about it: what was it for and for whom, is the order clear, do the sentences link up, and does it look neat. The child also tells how they went about it: what they did first, where they could not go on, and what they changed. A child of 7 says what was hard and hears from the group what is clear in the text and what is not; a child of 10 asks the group a question about their own text and names what they will do differently next time.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it after the verb. The brackets do not say from whom. The setting is discussions of texts (*tekstbesprekingen*); the wording does not say with whom or whose texts. Two things are reflected on. For the writing process the brackets name orienting, formulating and revising; for the written product they name structure, coherence, style, purpose, audience and aspects of form. Neither bracket carries "bijv.": the pack does not claim that the lists are complete or that they are only illustrations. None of the terms is explained, and the goal does not say that the child uses them.

### Common mistakes

Talking about what the text is about and not about how it is written. Defending the text ("but I meant ...") and not hearing what a reader did not understand. Commenting only on spelling and handwriting when discussing another's text.
