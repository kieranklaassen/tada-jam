## Official wording

kennismaken met complexere gespreksvaardigheden (bijv. doorvragen en het stellen van begripsvragen)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know more complex conversation skills (e.g. asking follow-up questions (*doorvragen*, asking further about an answer) and asking comprehension questions (*begripsvragen*))

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child interviews a classmate about a hobby; after the answer "I play hockey", the child asks "How often do you train?" instead of going on to the next question on the list. When a classmate explains the rules of a card game, the child asks "Do you mean that I have to put down two cards?". The child listens to the adult and a classmate who model a short talk, and names the follow-up questions they heard. A child of 7 asks a follow-up question after the adult's prompt "What more do you want to know about that?"; a child of 10 asks one without a prompt and checks their understanding by saying back what they heard.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "getting to know": the child meets these skills, and the statement does not ask for mastery. "More complex" is set against the elementary conversation skills of NE/MT/01/03/01/fase2. The two skills in brackets follow "e.g." and are examples; the statement gives no list. *Begripsvragen* are questions about understanding; the wording does not say whether the child checks their own understanding of what a partner said, or the partner's understanding. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out when they first try these skills is listening to the answer: they ask the next prepared question, or ask something that was just answered. Many do not notice that they have not understood something, and so do not ask.
