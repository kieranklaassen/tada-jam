## Official wording

(met hulp) duidelijk maken van spreekdoel en hieraan vasthouden tijdens het spreken

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/03/03; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 03344d96-e4e5-4fa3-b07e-d46dea85723b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) making the speaking purpose clear and keeping to it while speaking

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At the start of a talk the child says what it is for: "I am going to explain how you look after a guinea pig" or "I am going to tell you what we did on the school trip". During the talk the child continues to explain, or continues to tell what was done, and does not change to a long story about a holiday. A card with the purpose in a few words lies in front of the child, and the adult points to it when the child moves away from the purpose. A child of 7 says, in a sentence prepared with the adult, that the talk explains how to look after a guinea pig, and returns to explaining when the adult points to the card; a child of 10 says, in a sentence prepared with the adult, that the talk tells what happened on the school trip, and returns to that when the adult points to the card.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement begins with "with help" in brackets and does not say who helps or how. The statement has two parts: making the speaking purpose clear, and keeping to it while speaking. It does not say to whom the purpose is made clear, and names no speaking purposes. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/03/03, titled *Spreekdoelen* (speaking purposes).

### Common mistakes

Naming the topic instead of the purpose ("My talk is about guinea pigs"). Announcing an explanation and telling an anecdote instead. Stating the purpose and, after an interruption, not returning to it.
