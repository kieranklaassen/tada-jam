## English gloss

inferring meaning from *houding* (posture, or attitude), facial expression, gestures, use of voice and intonation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Watching a storyteller or a performance, the child says that the giant is angry before any word says so, from the raised shoulders, the frown and the low, loud voice. The child hears "That is a nice present" said in a flat voice with a sigh and says that the speaker does not mean it. The child hears from a rising voice that a sentence is a question, and from a whisper that something is secret. A child of 7 reads clear signs of joy, anger, fear and sadness; a child of 10 also notices when voice and body contradict the words, as in mockery or a polite refusal.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Five sources of meaning are listed, not as examples: *houding*, facial expression, gestures, use of voice and intonation. *Houding* can mean bodily posture or attitude, and the wording does not choose; NE/MT/01/07/01/fase2 and NE/MT/03/07/02/fase2 use the word *lichaamshouding* (body posture). The statement does not say what kind of meaning is inferred (a feeling, an intention, the kind of sentence). The open data lists it under listening (NE/MT/02). It does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Taking the words literally when the voice shows the opposite. Attending to one sign, such as a smile, and missing what the voice shows. Taking a loud voice for anger where it shows enthusiasm.
