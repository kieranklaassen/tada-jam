getting to know and using terms about language (*taalbegrippen*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While talking about a text or about their own writing, the child uses words such as "zin" (sentence), "hoofdletter" (capital letter), "lettergreep" (syllable), "werkwoord" (verb), "meervoud" (plural) and "alinea" (paragraph): "this sentence has no verb", "that word is plural". The child sorts word cards under such a term and says why a word belongs there. A child of 7 uses terms such as sentence, word, syllable and capital letter; a child of 10 also uses terms for kinds of words and for parts of a text.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal itself names no terms and sets no number; those above are the pack's examples. Two verbs are used: getting to know and using. The wording does not say for which part of language the terms are (words, sentences, texts, spelling or conversation). The cluster is named *Begrippenlijst* (list of terms): the content-line document prints lists of terms under the goal per pair of *groepen*, each term offered from the pair it stands under. The lists are not in the open data and are no part of this record; the lane's frame quotes the lists for *groep* 3-4 and *groep* 5-6, the pairs that hold the *groepen* of this fase.

### Common mistakes

Deciding the kind of word by meaning only: calling "wandeling" (a walk) a verb because walking is something one does, and not calling "is" a verb. Mixing up "letter" and "klank" (sound), or "woord" (word) and "lettergreep" (syllable). Saying a term without being able to point to an example of it.
