using memory aids (e.g. for a story/short talk/book talk)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a card with five key words or small drawings while giving a talk and looks at it between the parts. The child lays three objects on the table in the order of the story and picks up each in turn. For a book talk the child puts slips of paper in the book at the pages to show. A child of 7 uses pictures or objects as reminders of what comes next; a child of 10 uses a card with key words or a mind map, looks at it between the parts and speaks on in their own sentences.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names no kind of memory aid. What follows "e.g." are examples of speaking tasks for which an aid is used. The source prints them with hyphens and slashes, *verhaal-/praatje-/boekbespreking*, as if all three were compounds ending in *-bespreking*; the gloss reads them as a story, a short talk and a book talk (*boekbespreking*, presenting a book one has read), the three that NE/MT/03/09/02/fase2 prints without hyphens. NE/MT/03/08/02/fase2 names the mind map among simple means. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Writing the whole text on the card and reading it aloud. Making a card and not looking at it, and then leaving out a part. Writing key words so short that the child no longer knows what was meant by them.
