## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising different emotions in a story

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a passage and says how a character feels there and what in the text shows it: a word, something the character does, or something the character says. The child lays feeling cards (angry, afraid, proud, jealous) next to the parts of the story where they fit. A child of 7 recognises feelings the text names or shows plainly, such as crying or shouting; a child of 10 also recognises a feeling that is not named, and a character who is glad and afraid at once.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is recognising and the object is different emotions in a story; the goal names no emotions and sets no number. The feelings above are the pack's examples. The wording does not say whose emotions are meant: those of the characters, or those the story raises in the reader. It names no kind of story and does not say whether the story is read by the child or read aloud.

### Common mistakes

Saying how the child would feel and not how the character feels. Reducing every feeling to happy, angry or sad. Reading the feeling from the picture or from the event alone and missing what the words say, for example a character who smiles and is afraid.
