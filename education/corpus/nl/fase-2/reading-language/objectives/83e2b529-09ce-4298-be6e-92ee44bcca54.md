## English gloss

being aware of the function of visual support

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to a classmate's talk about bees, first without and then with the drawing of the hive, and says what the drawing added. Preparing a talk, the child chooses one photo out of five and says what the audience will understand better through it. Looking at a poster that is full of small text, the child says that nobody can read it and listen at the same time. A child of 7 says that a picture or an object shows what something looks like; a child of 10 says that visual support can make something clear, hold the attention or help the speaker remember the order, and that it can also distract.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "being aware"; putting simple means to use is the subject of NE/MT/03/08/02/fase2. The statement does not say what the function of visual support is and names no kinds of visual support. The open data lists it under speaking, in sub-cluster NE/MT/03/08, titled *Visuele ondersteuning* (visual support). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often choose a picture because it is pretty or funny, not for what it makes clear to the audience. Many treat the visual support as the talk itself and describe the picture, or think that more pictures are always better.
