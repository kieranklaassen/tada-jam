## Official wording

herkennen van woorden door gebruik te maken van de context van een woord

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising words by making use of the context of a word

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a sentence, stops at a word that did not come out right, reads the rest of the sentence and goes back to read the word again so that it fits. The child chooses between two ways of saying a word that is written the same, such as "voorkomen" (to occur, or to prevent), by what the sentence says. A child of 7 uses the sentence to check a word just sounded out; a child of 10 uses the sentences before and after it and corrects a misread word without help.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names one aid for recognising a word: the context of a word. Using the meaning of a word is a separate goal, NE/LE/09/06/fase2. The wording does not say what counts as the context (the sentence, the whole text or the pictures), and it names no words; the examples above are the pack's. It stands under technical reading and is about recognising the written word. What to do at a word whose meaning is unknown is in NE/LE/05/02/01/fase2.

### Common mistakes

Saying a word that fits the sentence but is not the word on the page: "huis" for "woning". Reading on after a word that does not fit and not going back. Using the context in place of the letters, so that guessing replaces decoding.
