## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using signal words (*signaalwoorden*, words that link sentences) and reference words (*verwijswoorden*, words that refer back, such as "he", "that")

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Telling about the school trip, the child says "first", "after that", "because", "but" and "so", so that the listener hears the order and the reasons. After naming "the zookeeper" once, the child says "he" for him and "there" for the zoo, and names the person again when a second person comes into the story. The child listens to a recording of their own story and says where a listener could not know who "she" was. A child of 7 tells about the trip in the order in which it happened and says who "he" is when a listener asks; a child of 10 links an event to its reason and names a person again when another person comes into the story.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two kinds of words are named: signal words and reference words. The statement names no words, no number of them and no level of difficulty. The open data lists it under speaking, in sub-cluster NE/MT/03/02, titled *Samenhang* (coherence). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Saying "he", "she" or "there" when the listener cannot know who or where is meant, most often with two persons in the story. Beginning nearly every sentence with "and then". Using "because" or "so" where there is no reason or result ("We went by bus, so it was fun").
