## Official wording

lezen van: - verhalende teksten (bijv. avonturenverhalen, verhalen over andere culturen, stripboeken of prentenboeken met een dubbele bodem); - diverse soorten poëzie (bijv. vormvastere en vormvrijere gedichten, lof-, feest- en klaagliederen, hekelende en satirische gedichten)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading: - narrative texts (e.g. adventure stories, stories about other cultures, comic books or picture books with a second layer of meaning (*dubbele bodem*, literally a double bottom)); - various kinds of poetry (e.g. poems with a more fixed form and poems with a freer form, songs of praise, festive songs and laments, sharply critical and satirical poems)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads an adventure story in parts over several days, reads a comic book alone, and reads a picture book twice: once for the story and once to find what the pictures or the words also say beneath it. The child reads poems aloud, one with rhyme and a fixed number of lines and one without, and says what differs. A child of 7 reads a story with pictures and a rhyming poem; a child of 10 reads an adventure book, and a poem in which the meaning is not stated directly.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is reading. Two groups are named: narrative texts and various kinds of poetry. Everything in brackets follows "bijv." and is an example; none is required. "Met een dubbele bodem" (with a second layer of meaning) may describe the picture books only or the comic books as well; the Dutch leaves it open. The goal itself sets no text length, no reading level, no reading speed and no number of texts, and it does not say whether the child reads silently or aloud. The content-line document prints, after the goals of this cluster, what the fiction of fase 2 is like (its subjects and its structure), which the lane's frame quotes; none of those features is a text length.

### Common mistakes

Reading a story with a second layer only for its events and not noticing the other meaning. Reading a poem as a story to be finished quickly, without attention to sound and line breaks. Taking a mocking or satirical poem literally.
