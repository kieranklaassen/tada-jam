## Official wording

deelnemen aan geplande en ongeplande gesprekken: - om informatie uit te wisselen (bijv. tijdens een samenwerkopdracht of een brainstorm); - voor discussie en overleg (bijv. over een groepsactiviteit, een mening of een gezamenlijke presentatie)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking part in planned and unplanned conversations: - to exchange information (e.g. during a collaborative assignment or a brainstorm); - for discussion and consultation (*overleg*, talking over what to do) (e.g. about a group activity, an opinion or a joint presentation)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group of four that makes a poster about birds, the child tells what they found in a book and listens to what the others found. In a brainstorm about a class party the child names ideas while a classmate writes them down. With two classmates the child talks over who presents which part of a joint presentation, and gives a reason when proposing something else. A child of 7 tells the group what they found and listens to what a classmate found; a child of 10 responds to a classmate's reason in a discussion and helps the group reach a decision.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two purposes are named: exchanging information, and discussion and *overleg*. What follows "e.g." in each pair of brackets is an example: a collaborative assignment and a brainstorm for the first purpose; a group activity, an opinion and a joint presentation for the second. The statement covers planned and unplanned conversations alike, and sets no group size, no length and no number of turns. It does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Saying an idea of one's own without responding to what was said just before. When talking over what to do, repeating one's own proposal instead of giving a reason or considering another proposal. Staying silent in a group and leaving the talk to one or two others.
