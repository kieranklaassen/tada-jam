## Official wording

afstemmen woordgebruik en stijlmiddelen op specifieke schrijfdoelen (plezier verschaffen, informeren, instrueren en overtuigen)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

matching word use and stylistic devices (*stijlmiddelen*) to specific writing purposes (giving pleasure, informing, instructing and persuading)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes about the same subject for two purposes and chooses other words each time: in a story that is meant to amuse, a comparison or an exaggeration; in the steps for a game, short commands with exact words, such as "Vouw het blad dubbel."; in a note that is meant to persuade, words that show what they want and why. The child reads both texts aloud and says which words fit which purpose. A child of 7 chooses funny or exciting words for a story and short commands for steps; a child of 10 also chooses between plain words to inform and strong words to persuade, and uses a comparison or a repetition on purpose.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. Two things are matched to the purpose: word use and stylistic devices. The brackets name four writing purposes: giving pleasure, informing, instructing and persuading, the same four as in NE/SCH/02/01/03/fase2; the brackets carry no "bijv.". The goal names no stylistic devices and gives no list of words; the devices and the Dutch sentence above are the pack's examples. It mentions no help, sets no text length and names no kind of text. Adapting word use to the audience is in NE/SCH/02/04/01/fase2.

### Common mistakes

Writing every text in the words they use when speaking, whatever the text is for. Writing the steps of an instruction as long sentences with details that do not belong to the step, so that a step is hard to find. In a text meant to persuade, making the wish stronger with capital letters and exclamation marks and not with chosen words and a reason.
