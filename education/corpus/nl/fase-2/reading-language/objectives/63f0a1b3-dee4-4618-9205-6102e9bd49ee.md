## Official wording

onderscheiden hoofdcomponenten van een tekst m.b.v. vragen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/02/05; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 63f0a1b3-dee4-4618-9205-6102e9bd49ee.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing the main components of a text by means of questions

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After listening to a story or a news item, the child answers the questions "Who? What? Where? When? Why?" (the pack's example of such questions) and so tells apart the persons, the event, the place, the time and the reason. The child lays cards with these question words on the table and puts a key word from the text next to each. After an informative text the child answers "What is it about?" and "What is said about it?". A child of 7 answers who, what and where when the questions are asked one by one; a child of 10 asks the questions themselves, also why and how, and tells what is central from what is secondary.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The distinguishing is done by means of questions (the source prints the abbreviation *m.b.v.*, for *met behulp van*, "with the help of"); the statement does not say which questions or who asks them, and it does not say what the main components of a text are. "Text" here is a text that is listened to, since the open data lists the goal under listening, in sub-cluster NE/MT/02/05, titled *Begrijpen en interpreteren* (understanding and interpreting). The source prints the verb without *van* ("of") after it, unlike most neighbouring goals; the gloss reads it in the same form as theirs.

### Common mistakes

Answering "who" with every person in the text, without telling the main person from the others. Giving the last or the most striking event as the answer to "what happened". Answering a "why" question with what happened next and not with the reason.
