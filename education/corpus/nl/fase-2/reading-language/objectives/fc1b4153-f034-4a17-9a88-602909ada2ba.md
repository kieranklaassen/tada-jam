## Official wording

doorlezen bij een onbekend woord in een tekst als dat niet belangrijk is voor het begrip, of onder begeleiding van de leraar terug of verder lezen in een tekst om te kijken of een onbekend woord omschreven wordt of dat er een bekend synoniem staat (als uitbreiding van (lees)woordenschatstrategieën)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading on at an unknown word in a text if it is not important for understanding, or, under the guidance of the teacher, reading back or further in a text to see whether an unknown word is described or whether a known synonym is there (as an extension of (reading) vocabulary strategies)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child meets a word they do not know, decides whether the sentence can be understood without it, and reads on if it can. If the word matters, the child and the teacher look at the sentences before and after it: is the word described there, or does a word the child knows stand there for the same thing? A child of 7 reads on at an unknown word in a sentence they understand without it; a child of 10, with the teacher, reads back in the text and finds a known synonym for a word that matters.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two ways of acting at an unknown word are named. The first is reading on, on the condition that the word is not important for understanding. The second is reading back or further to look for a description or a known synonym; "under the guidance of the teacher" stands after "of" (or), so it belongs to the second way. The closing brackets present both as an extension of vocabulary strategies, with "(lees)" (reading) in brackets of its own. The goal does not mention a dictionary or working out a word from its parts; compound words are in NE/LE/05/02/02/fase2. It names no kind of text; its cluster is about non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Stopping at every unknown word, so the child no longer follows the text. Reading on at every unknown word, also one that the text depends on. Taking a nearby word for a synonym when it means something else.
