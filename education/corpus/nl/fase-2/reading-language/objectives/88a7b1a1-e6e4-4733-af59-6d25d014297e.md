## Official wording

(met hulp) begrijpen van de bedoeling van de schrijver (onderscheiden van lezen om te weten, overtuigen of doen)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/03/02; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 88a7b1a1-e6e4-4733-af59-6d25d014297e.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) understanding the writer's intention (distinguishing between reading in order to know, to persuade or to do)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gets three short texts, for example a page about owls, an advertisement for a toy and a recipe, and says, with help, what each writer wants: that the reader knows something, is persuaded of something, or does something. The child points to what shows it: facts, words such as "the best", or numbered steps. A child of 7 tells an instruction from an information text; a child of 10 also sees that a text wants to persuade when it looks like information.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start. The content-line document prints other words: "begrijpen van de bedoeling van de schrijver - met hulp van de leraar" (understanding the writer's intention - with help from the teacher), with the help after the first clause and the teacher named as the helper. The second brackets name three purposes to tell apart: knowing, persuading and doing. The source prints them as kinds of reading ("lezen om te weten, overtuigen of doen"), although persuading is what a writer does; the plain sense is texts written to inform, to persuade or to instruct. The goal names no kinds of text; those above are the pack's examples. Giving pleasure is not among the three purposes; the cluster is about non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Naming the subject of the text when asked what the writer wants. Taking an advertisement as information because it holds facts. Deciding by the look of the text alone, so every text with numbers is called an instruction.
