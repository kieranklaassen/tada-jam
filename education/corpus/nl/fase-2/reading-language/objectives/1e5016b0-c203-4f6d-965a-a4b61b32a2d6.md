## Official wording

ervaren dat met taalgebruik bepaald effect/doel kan worden bereikt

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing that a certain effect/purpose can be achieved with the use of language

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child asks a classmate for the scissors once with "Give me that" and once with "May I borrow the scissors for a moment?", and notices which request is granted more readily. The child explains the rule of a game and sees that the others can now play it, or tells a joke and hears the group laugh. The child tries to persuade the class to choose a certain game for the gym lesson and finds out whether the reasons convinced them. A child of 7 notices an effect that follows at once, when the adult draws attention to it; a child of 10 chooses words beforehand to reach a chosen effect and says afterwards why it did or did not work.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "experiencing": the statement does not ask the child to name or plan the effect. The source prints "effect/purpose" (*effect/doel*) with a slash and does not say how the two differ. The statement names no effects and no kinds of language use, and gives no examples. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/01/02, titled *Gespreksdoelen* (conversation purposes).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is the link between the wording and the result: they explain a refusal by saying that the other person is unkind, not by how the request was worded. They think about what they say and not about what they want it to bring about.
