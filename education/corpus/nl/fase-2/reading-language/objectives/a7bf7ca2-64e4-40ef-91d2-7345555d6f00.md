## Official wording

herkennen van veel voorkomende onderwerpen (bijv. avonturen) en genres

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/07/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau a7bf7ca2-64e4-40ef-91d2-7345555d6f00.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising frequently occurring subjects (e.g. adventures) and genres

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a book from the shelf, looks at the cover, reads the first page, and says "this is an adventure book" or "this is a book of poems". The child sorts a pile of books by what they are about (animals, school, adventures) and by kind (comic, fairy tale, poem). A child of 7 recognises a fairy tale by its opening words or a comic by its pictures; a child of 10 recognises an adventure story from the first page of a book they have not seen before.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "Adventures" is the one subject named, as an example after "bijv."; no genre is named. "Veel voorkomende" (frequently occurring) is not defined further, and the Dutch allows it to describe the subjects only or the subjects and the genres. The verb is recognising: the goal does not say that the child names the features of a genre or uses the word "genre". The subjects and kinds above, other than adventures, are the pack's examples. Its cluster is about fiction.

### Common mistakes

Confusing what a book is about with what kind of book it is: "animals" given as a genre. Judging by the cover or the pictures alone, so every book with drawings is called a comic. Calling every story with something untrue in it a fairy tale.
