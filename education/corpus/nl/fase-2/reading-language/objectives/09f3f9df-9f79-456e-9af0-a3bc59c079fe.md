## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and understanding signal words that indicate a structure of contrast

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a text that sets two things against each other, for example life in a city and life in a village, and circles words such as "maar" (but), "toch" (yet), "echter" (however) and "daarentegen" (on the other hand). The child says what stands on each side of the word and how the two differ. A child of 7 understands "maar" within a sentence; a child of 10 follows a contrast between paragraphs that is marked by a word such as "echter".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. One function of signal words is named: indicating a structure of contrast. The goal names no words; the Dutch words above are the pack's examples. Signal words for other relations, such as cause, order in time or a list, are not named in this statement. Two verbs are used: recognising and understanding. Its cluster is about non-fiction texts. Among the features of the non-fiction texts of fase 2 that the content-line document prints, and the lane's frame quotes, is "signaalwoorden moeten worden afgeleid" (signal words must be inferred).

### Common mistakes

Reading past the signal word and remembering both statements as if they agreed. Finding the word and not being able to say which two things are set against each other. Taking "maar" as a contrast word where it means "only": "ik heb maar één pen" (I have only one pen).
