## English gloss

seeing (*inzien*, coming to understand) the importance of non-verbal communication

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child watches the adult give the same short talk twice, once with hands in the pockets and eyes on the floor and once standing upright and looking at the audience, and says which was easier to follow and why. The child says the first sentences of their own talk in these two ways and asks the listeners what they noticed. A child of 7 says that looking at the audience "is nicer for them"; a child of 10 explains that posture, eye contact and gestures hold the attention and can show what is important, and that they can also distract.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is *inzien* (seeing, coming to understand): the statement is about insight; putting body posture, eye contact and gestures to use is the subject of NE/MT/03/07/02/fase2. It does not say which forms of non-verbal communication are meant and gives no examples. NE/MT/01/07/02/fase2 is the matching goal for conversations, worded with "becoming aware". The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is that the audience sees them the whole time: they prepare the words and do not think about where they look or what their hands do. Some take non-verbal communication to mean acting things out, and overlook posture and looking at the audience.
