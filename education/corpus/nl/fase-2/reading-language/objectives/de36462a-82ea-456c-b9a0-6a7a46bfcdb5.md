## Official wording

gaande houden van een gesprek (bijv. door inhoudelijk op elkaar te reageren)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

keeping a conversation going (e.g. by responding to each other on the content)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a pair the child hears a classmate say that the book was sad and answers "Yes, when the dog ran away; did you think he would come back?". When the talk stops, the child asks a question or gives an example, so that the other has something to respond to. In a group of four the child turns to a classmate who has not spoken and asks what they think. A child of 7 answers a classmate with something about what the classmate said; a child of 10 asks a question when the talk stops, or sums up what has been said so far.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Responding to each other on the content follows "e.g." and is an example of how a conversation is kept going. The statement sets no length, no number of turns and no group size. It does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Answering with "yes", "no" or "me too" and stopping there. Responding with a story of one's own that is linked to the other's words by one word only. Asking a series of unconnected questions without responding to the answers.
