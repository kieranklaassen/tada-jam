## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making the elementary reading acts automatic (*elementaire leeshandelingen*, the basic steps of reading a word)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads rows of words and short texts many times, alone, aloud to another child or together with a group, until the words are read at once without sounding out. The child reads the same text again a day later and notices that it goes more easily. A child of 7 sounds out a word that is new to them and reads it at once when it comes back further on in the text; a child of 10 reads a row of words aloud to another child without sounding them out.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording does not list the elementary reading acts. In Dutch reading teaching the term usually means looking at the letters of a word in order, saying the sound of each, and blending the sounds into the word; that reading is the pack's, not the goal's. The verb is *automatiseren*: making automatic, so that it is done without conscious effort. The goal sets no reading speed, no number of words per minute, no word length and no text level. Kinds of words are named in NE/LE/09/02/fase2, NE/LE/09/03/fase2 and NE/LE/09/05/fase2.

### Common mistakes

Reading fast by guessing a word from its first letters, so "paard" becomes "paar". Going on sounding out every word, also short known ones. Reading fast and skipping or changing small words such as "de", "het" and "er".
