## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

speaking fluently and intelligibly for unfamiliar people

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells a visitor to the school, or a class of children they do not know, about a project, in sentences that follow one another, loudly enough for the listeners furthest away and at a pace they can follow. The child pronounces the ends of words and finishes sentences. When the child halts, they pause, look at their card and continue. A child of 7 tells a visitor to the school about their drawing and is understood; a child of 10 tells a class they do not know about a project and adapts their volume to the room.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The listeners are unfamiliar people; NE/MT/03/01/01/fase2 names a mostly familiar audience. The statement states no measure of fluency and does not say whether pauses, hesitations or new starts may occur. NE/MT/03/06/02/fase2 is the goal about becoming aware of fluency and intelligibility. The statement does not mention grammar, although the title of its sub-cluster in the open data, NE/MT/03/06, names grammatical control (*grammaticale beheersing*). It does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Speaking too softly or too fast in front of people the child does not know. Speaking toward the card, the floor or the board and not toward the listeners. Filling every pause with "uh", or beginning each sentence several times.
