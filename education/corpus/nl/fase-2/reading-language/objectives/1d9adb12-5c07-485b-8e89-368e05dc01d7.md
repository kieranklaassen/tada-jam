## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child meets words taken from other languages in a text, such as "computer", "cadeau", "garage" and "chocola", sees that the letters are not said in the usual Dutch way, and reads the word as it is said. The child collects such words on a list, sorted by the letters that are said differently, for example "eau" said as "oo". A child of 7 reads "computer" as a whole word they know; a child of 10 recognises a letter pattern such as "eau" in a loanword not read before and tries the pronunciation that goes with it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names no loanwords, no languages and no letter patterns; those above are the pack's examples. "Het unieke patroon" (the unique pattern) is singular and is not explained: the wording does not say whether it means the pattern of each loanword or of loanwords as a group. The verb is recognising and the goal stands under technical reading: it does not say that the child spells these words. It sets no number of words.

### Common mistakes

Reading the word with the usual Dutch letter sounds: "cadeau" with "e", "a" and "u" said one after the other, "garage" with a Dutch g twice. Not recognising a word the child knows well from speech because its written form looks unfamiliar.
