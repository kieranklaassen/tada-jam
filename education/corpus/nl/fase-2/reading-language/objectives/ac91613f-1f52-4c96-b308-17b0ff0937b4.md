## Official wording

schrijven: - verhalende teksten (bijv. verhaal, gedicht of brief); - informatieve teksten (bijv. verslag, observaties van bijvoorbeeld diergedrag en proefje); - instructieve teksten (bijv. speurtocht, routebeschrijving, gebruiks- of gedragsaanwijzingen); - betogende teksten (bijv. een boekverslag, een leus op een spandoek of een oproep)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing: - narrative texts (e.g. story, poem or letter); - informative texts (e.g. report, observations of for example animal behaviour and small experiment); - instructive texts (e.g. scavenger hunt, route description, instructions for use or for behaviour); - persuasive texts (e.g. a book report, a slogan on a banner or an appeal)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a story or a letter, a report of what the snails in the classroom did during a week, the route from school to the playground for someone who does not know it, and a slogan for a banner about litter. The child writes the text for a reader and gives it to that reader. A child of 7 writes a letter to someone they know and notes what they saw the snails do; a child of 10 writes a report with parts, and a book report that tries to persuade a reader to read the book.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is writing. Four groups of text are named: narrative, informative, instructive and persuasive (*betogende*) texts. Everything in brackets follows "bijv." and is an example; none is required. The source lists the book report among its examples of persuasive texts. In the informative item, "observaties van bijvoorbeeld diergedrag en proefje" does not make clear whether the small experiment (*proefje*) is something observed or a kind of text of its own. The goal sets no text length, no number of texts and no level of spelling or handwriting.

### Common mistakes

Writing every kind of text as a story in order of time ("and then ... and then"). In a route description or an instruction, leaving out a step that the writer finds obvious. In a book report, retelling the book and giving no opinion or reason.
