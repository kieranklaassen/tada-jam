## Official wording

(met hulp) herkennen van verschillende gesprekssoorten met bijbehorende doelen (bijv. uitwisseling over oplossing rekenprobleem of overleg over te organiseren tentoonstelling)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) recognising different kinds of conversation with the purposes that belong to them (e.g. an exchange about the solution to an arithmetic problem, or consultation (*overleg*, talking over what to do) about an exhibition that is to be organised)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After two children have compared how they solved a sum, the adult asks what kind of talk that was, and the child says that they told each other how they did it. Before the class plans an exhibition of clay work, the child says that this talk is for deciding together who does what. The child lays cards that each describe a talk (the pack's examples: a quarrel being settled, an interview with a visitor, a talk about a book) next to a card for what the talk is for. A child of 7 says, when the adult asks, that the talk about the book was for telling each other what they thought of it; a child of 10 says, when the adult asks, that the talk about the class party was for deciding together who does what.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it after the verb. The statement does not say who helps or how. The verb is "recognising": the statement does not ask the child to choose or lead a kind of conversation. The two kinds in brackets, each with its purpose, follow "e.g." and are examples. The statement gives no list of kinds of conversation and no number of kinds. NE/MT/01/08/01/fase2 is the goal about getting an orientation on the kind of conversation beforehand.

### Common mistakes

Naming the topic when asked what kind of talk it is ("a talk about sums"). Taking a difference of opinion in a discussion for a quarrel. Recognising the kind and not its purpose, so that a talk meant to reach a decision is treated as a round of telling.
