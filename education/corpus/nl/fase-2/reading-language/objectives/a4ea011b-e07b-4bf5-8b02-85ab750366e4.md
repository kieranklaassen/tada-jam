## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a class discussion about what to do with the money from a sale, the child first says in a few words what the previous speaker proposed and then gives a view of their own (the pack's example of a more complex rule). The child disagrees with "I see it differently, because ..." and not with "That is wrong". The adult gives turns, and the child speaks when given a turn. A child of 7 keeps such a rule while it lies on a card on the table and the adult points to it; a child of 10 keeps it after the adult has named it beforehand.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it after the verb. The statement does not say who helps or how. The rules are called "more complex"; the statement names none and gives no examples, so the rules in the note above are the pack's examples. Elementary conversation rules are named in NE/MT/01/08/01/fase2, with responding to what the other says as the example. The statement sets no group size and no kind of conversation.

### Common mistakes

Keeping the rule in the first minutes and no longer once the child disagrees strongly. Following the form of a rule without its content: saying "I agree with you, but" and then not responding to what was said. Correcting others loudly for breaking a rule and breaking it by doing so.
