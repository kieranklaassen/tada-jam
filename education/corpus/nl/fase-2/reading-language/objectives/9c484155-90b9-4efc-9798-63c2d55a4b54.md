## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child talks with others about words and ways of speaking: why "u" is said to one person and "jij" to another, where a word comes from, what a saying means, how something is said in the home language or dialect of someone in the group, and why a sentence in a text sounds strange. The child compares two ways of saying the same thing and says which fits a situation. A child of 7 notices and says that words sound alike or that someone says something differently; a child of 10 gives reasons, compares languages and talks about how language differs by person and place.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal itself names two subjects of conversation, language and language use, and nothing more exact: no terms, no aspects of language and no rules. The form is conversation; the goal itself does not mention written work and does not say with whom the conversations are held. Under the heading of this cluster the content-line document prints a sentence that the lane's frame quotes: "Onderstaande begrippen zijn van belang om te reflecteren op taal en taalgebruik in gesprekken met kinderen" (the terms below are of importance for reflecting on language and language use in conversations with children); it ties the document's list of terms to these conversations and speaks of conversations with children.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often talk about the thing a word names and not about the word itself: asked about the word "trein", they tell about trains. Many children believe there is only one right way to say something and call another dialect or language wrong.
