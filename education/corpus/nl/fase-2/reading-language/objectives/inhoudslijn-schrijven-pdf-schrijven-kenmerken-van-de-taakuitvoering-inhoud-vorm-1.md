## Official wording

schrijven langere teksten met enkele inhoudselementen en maken kennis met meerdere tekststructuren (bijv. opsomming, vraag-antwoord)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing longer texts with a few content elements and getting to know several text structures (e.g. enumeration, question-answer)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a text of several parts about one subject, for example about their rabbit: what it looks like, what it eats and what they do with it. The child tries out ways to build a text: points listed one after another, or a question with its answer under it. A child of 7 writes about what their rabbit looks like and what it eats, as a list of points; a child of 10 writes about their football club as questions with the answer under each.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. "Langere" (longer) is a comparative: the wording does not say longer than what, and it sets no text length. "Enkele" (a few) sets no number of content elements, and the wording does not explain "inhoudselement" (content element); the pack reads it as one part of what a text says, such as one point or one event. For text structures the verb is getting to know, not mastering; "meerdere" (several) sets no number, and enumeration and question-answer follow "bijv." and are examples. The goal names no kind of text; kinds of text are in NE/SCH/02/02/01/fase2. The source words both parts as clauses without their subject ("schrijven langere teksten", "maken kennis met"); writing such texts and getting to know such structures is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Making a text longer by adding sentences that begin with "en toen" (and then; the pack's example) and bring no new point. Putting sentences about the same point in different places in the text. Writing a question above a part and not answering it in that part.
