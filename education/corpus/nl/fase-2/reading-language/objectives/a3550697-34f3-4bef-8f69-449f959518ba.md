## Official wording

(met hulp) zich oriënteren voorafgaand aan het gesprek op soort gesprek en elementaire gespreksregels (bijv. reageren op dat wat de ander zegt)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) getting an orientation, before the conversation, on the kind of conversation and on elementary conversation rules (e.g. responding to what the other says)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before a group talk about the layout of the school garden, the adult asks what kind of talk this will be, and the child says that the group has to decide something together. The child reads the two rules for this talk from a card and says in their own words what "respond to what the other says" asks of them. The child thinks of one proposal to bring in. A child of 7, before a talk about a class outing, answers the adult's questions: it is a talk for deciding together, and the rule is to let each other finish; a child of 10, before a talk about a book, answers the adult's questions: it is a talk for telling each other what they thought, and the rule is to respond to what the other says.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints "met hulp" in the middle of the sentence, after "voorafgaand aan het gesprek" (before the conversation), without brackets. The statement does not say who helps or how. The orientation takes place before the conversation and covers two things: the kind of conversation and elementary conversation rules; responding to what the other says follows "e.g." and is an example of such a rule. NE/MT/01/02/01/fase2 is the goal about recognising kinds of conversation, and NE/MT/01/08/02/fase2 the goal about reflecting on one's own part in and approach to a conversation.

### Common mistakes

Beginning on the content at once when asked what kind of talk it will be. Naming the topic instead of the kind of talk. Saying the rule correctly beforehand and not applying it some minutes later.
