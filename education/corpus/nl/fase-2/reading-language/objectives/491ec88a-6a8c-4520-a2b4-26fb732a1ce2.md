## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

editing one's own text for spelling and punctuation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

When the text is written, the child reads it again slowly, word by word, with a pencil in hand, and corrects words that are spelled wrongly. The child reads it aloud to hear where a sentence ends and puts in missing full stops, capital letters and question marks. A child of 7 checks for a capital letter and a full stop at each sentence and corrects words from the spelling groups just learned; a child of 10 also checks commas, quotation marks and verb endings.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The text is the child's own; editing other people's texts is not named. Two things are edited: spelling and punctuation. The goal itself names no spelling categories and no punctuation marks, and sets no share of errors to be found. The content-line document prints more than the open data: it ends the goal with the cross-reference "(zie Taalbeschouwing: begrippenlijst en taalverzorging)" (see Taalbeschouwing: list of terms and language care). The lists of that line give punctuation marks and spelling categories per pair of *groepen*, and the lane's frame quotes the lists for *groep* 3-4 and *groep* 5-6, the pairs that hold the *groepen* of this fase. The marks and categories above are the pack's examples, taken from those lists: the capital letter at the start of a sentence, the full stop and the question mark stand under *groep* 3-4, and the comma, quotation marks and verb forms under *groep* 5-6. The goal does not mention help, or aids such as a dictionary or a spelling checker. Looking again at content and at the build-up of sentences is in NE/SCH/02/05/01/fase2.

### Common mistakes

Reading what they meant to write and not what stands on the page, so errors go unseen. Checking only the words the child was already unsure of. Putting a full stop at the end of each line and not at the end of each sentence.
