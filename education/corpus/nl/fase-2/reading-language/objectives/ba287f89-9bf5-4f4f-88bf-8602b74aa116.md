## Official wording

afstemmen op publiek door: - onderscheiden van formele en informele situaties en daarbij passend woordgebruik hanteren; - rekening houden met voorkennis (bijv. van medeleerlingen); - waarnemen van signalen (bijv. onbegrip)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adjusting to the audience by: - distinguishing formal and informal situations and using word choice that fits them; - taking prior knowledge into account (e.g. of fellow pupils); - perceiving signals (e.g. lack of understanding)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Giving a talk about a computer game, the child first says what kind of game it is, because some classmates do not know it. Presenting to parents at an open evening, the child says "Good evening, welcome" and leaves out words that they use only with friends. While speaking, the child looks at the listeners, sees puzzled faces and says a part again in other words. A child of 7 explains a term when a listener asks and greets a formal audience in the way that was practised; a child of 10 thinks beforehand about what the audience knows, chooses words to fit the situation and notices signals without a prompt.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three ways of adjusting are listed: distinguishing formal and informal situations and using fitting word choice; taking prior knowledge into account, with that of fellow pupils as the example after "e.g."; and perceiving signals, with lack of understanding as the example after "e.g.". The third is worded as perceiving; the statement does not say what the child then does. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/03/04, titled *Publiek* (audience). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Assuming that the audience knows the terms, people and places the child knows. Speaking to a formal audience as to friends, or using formal words copied from adults without knowing what they mean. Seeing a puzzled face and continuing as planned, or saying the same sentence again more loudly.
