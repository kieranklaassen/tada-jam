## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with texts as everyday means of communication and expression

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads the texts that they meet during a day and acts on them: the day plan on the board, a note from another child, the rules of a board game, a birthday card, the menu of the week. The child also reads texts in which someone expresses something, such as a poem written by another child or the words of a song. A child of 7 reads a note from another child and does what it asks; a child of 10 looks up in a timetable when the bus leaves.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names no kinds of text; those above are the pack's examples. Texts are named as means of two things: communication and expression. "Omgaan met" (dealing with) is a general verb: the wording does not say what the child does with the texts. The source prints "dagelijks" where the plural noun would take "dagelijkse"; "everyday" is plainly meant. The goal stands under reading pleasure and reading motivation (NE/LE/01).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often see reading as something done with books at school and do not count a note, a recipe or a sign as a text. A child may read such a text aloud correctly and not do what it asks.
