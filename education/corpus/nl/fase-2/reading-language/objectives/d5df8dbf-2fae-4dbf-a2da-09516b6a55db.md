## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

matching forms of address to (un)familiar conversation partners

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says *u* and *meneer* or *mevrouw* to a visitor they do not know, and *je* and the first name to a classmate. In a role play of a telephone call to a shop, the child begins with a greeting and their own name and uses *u*. Speaking to the teacher, the child uses the form of address that is usual at the school (*juf*, *meester* or the first name). A child of 7 uses *u* for an unfamiliar adult when reminded just before; a child of 10 chooses the form without a reminder and keeps to it through the talk.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about forms of address and names none; in Dutch the choice between *je* and *u* is the common case, and that is the pack's example. The partners are familiar and unfamiliar ones: the source prints *(on)bekende*, with the prefix in brackets. The statement does not say how the form differs for each, and names neither the age nor the role of a partner. It does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Saying *je* to every adult, or beginning with *u* and changing to *je* after one sentence. Mixing the two forms in one sentence ("Heeft u je jas?" for "Heeft u uw jas?"). Addressing an unfamiliar adult with "hey", or with no form of address.
