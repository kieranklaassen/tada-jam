## Official wording

schrijven ook over onderwerpen die minder concreet zijn en betrekking hebben op omgeving die minder nabij is (bijv. bij de zaakvakken). Ze hebben ook betrekking op verleden en toekomst

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing also about subjects that are less concrete and relate to surroundings that are less near (e.g. in the content subjects (*zaakvakken*; subjects such as geography and history)). They also relate to past and future

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes about how children lived in a castle long ago, about a country they have never been to, about what friendship is, or about what their town could look like in fifty years. The child uses what they read or heard about the subject, since they cannot write it from what they see around them. A child of 7 writes about a visit to a castle and adds something learned about how people lived there long ago; a child of 10 writes about a country they know from books, lessons or pictures.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "Ook" (also) says that these subjects come in addition to others; the wording does not name the others. Two features of the subjects are given: less concrete, and relating to surroundings that are less near; the content subjects are an example after "bijv.". The second sentence adds past and future; "Ze" (they) refers to the subjects. "Less" is not measured: the wording does not say how abstract or how far away. The goal sets no text length and names no kind of text.

### Common mistakes

Copying sentences from a source text and not writing in their own words. Writing about the past with things from the present, for example a knight who phones home. Leaving out explanations a reader needs, because the writer has just learned the subject and assumes the reader knows it.
