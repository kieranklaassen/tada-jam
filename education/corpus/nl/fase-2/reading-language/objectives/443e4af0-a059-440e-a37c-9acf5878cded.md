## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising facts and opinions in texts

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a short text about a zoo and marks in one colour the sentences that can be checked ("the zoo has two elephants") and in another colour the ones that say what someone thinks ("the monkeys are the funniest animals"). The child says which words show an opinion: "I think", "best", "ugly", "should". A child of 7 tells a plain fact from a plain opinion when each stands in a sentence of its own; a child of 10 finds an opinion inside an information text, and a sentence that mixes both.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is recognising and two things are named: facts and opinions, in texts. The goal names no kinds of text and no words that show an opinion; those above are the pack's examples. It does not say that the child checks whether a fact is true or judges whether an opinion is well supported. Its cluster is about non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Calling a statement a fact because the child agrees with it, and an opinion because the child does not. Calling a false statement an opinion, although a statement that can be checked and is wrong is not an opinion. Calling everything printed in a book or a newspaper a fact.
