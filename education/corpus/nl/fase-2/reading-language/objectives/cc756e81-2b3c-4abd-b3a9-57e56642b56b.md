## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

becoming aware of fluency and intelligibility (e.g. of falterings, slips of the tongue)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gives a short talk, listens with the adult to a recording of it and says where they halted, said a wrong word or mumbled. Practising the talk for a partner, the child hears from the partner which part was hard to understand. During the talk the child says a wrong word, notices it and corrects it. A child of 7 notices a slip when it is pointed out or played back; a child of 10 hears while speaking that they are going too fast or have halted, and knows that a pause or a new start helps the audience.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "becoming aware": speaking fluently and intelligibly is the subject of NE/MT/03/06/01/fase2. Falterings and slips of the tongue follow "e.g." and are examples of what the child becomes aware of. The statement names no measure of fluency or intelligibility and does not mention help. NE/MT/01/06/02/fase2 has the same wording for conversations; this goal is listed under speaking (NE/MT/03).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is what the listener hears: they know what they meant to say and so do not notice that a mumbled or hurried part could not be understood. Hearing a recording of their own voice, many attend to how strange it sounds and not to where the speech faltered.
