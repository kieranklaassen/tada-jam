## Official wording

kennismaken en ervaring opdoen met de schrijfdoelen: plezier verschaffen, informeren, instrueren en overtuigen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know and gaining experience with the writing purposes: giving pleasure, informing, instructing and persuading

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a funny story for younger children to enjoy, a short text that tells what a guinea pig needs, the steps for folding a paper boat, and a note that asks the head teacher for a longer break and gives a reason. Before writing, the child says what the text is for. A child of 7 writes a story to amuse and a list of steps for a game; a child of 10 writes a note that persuades and says how it differs from a text that informs.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verbs are getting to know and gaining experience, not mastering. Four writing purposes are named: giving pleasure, informing, instructing and persuading. The goal names no kinds of text for them (kinds of text are in NE/SCH/02/02/01/fase2) and sets no text length. It does not say that the child names the purposes.

### Common mistakes

Writing a story whatever the purpose: asked to explain how to do something, the child tells about the time they did it. In an instruction, leaving out a step or the things needed. In a text meant to persuade, stating the wish without a reason.
