## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

becoming aware of fluency and intelligibility (e.g. of falterings, slips of the tongue)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk the child stops halfway through a sentence, hears it and begins the sentence again. The child says "liberry", notices it and corrects it to "library". A classmate at the other side of the table says "I can't hear you", and the child realises that they were speaking toward the table. A child of 7 notices a slip or a halt when a partner reacts to it; a child of 10 hears it while speaking and knows that a pause or a new start helps the partner to follow.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "becoming aware": the statement does not ask the child to speak without faltering. Falterings and slips of the tongue follow "e.g." and are examples of what the child becomes aware of. The statement names no measure of fluency or intelligibility and does not mention help. NE/MT/03/06/02/fase2 has the same wording for speaking; this goal is listed under conversations (NE/MT/01).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is their own speech: they notice a classmate's slip at once, and often laugh at it, and do not hear their own. Some take every pause or new start for a fault and become afraid to speak.
