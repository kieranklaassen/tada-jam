## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

becoming aware of the interest and prior knowledge of conversation partners

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells a child from another class about a class project and notices that this partner does not know who "Juf Ans" is or what "the box" is; the child stops and explains. Before telling about a computer game the child asks "Do you know it?" and tells more or less after the answer. The child sees a partner look away during a long account of football results and asks what the partner would like to know. A child of 7 notices it when the partner says "What?" or the adult asks "Does she know that?"; a child of 10 thinks beforehand about what the partner knows and finds interesting.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "becoming aware": the statement does not ask the child to adapt what they say. Two things are named: the partners' interest and their prior knowledge. The statement gives no examples and does not mention help. NE/MT/03/04/01/fase2, a goal for speaking, names taking prior knowledge into account.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is what the partner knows and wants: they assume that the partner knows the people and places they mention and is as interested in the subject as they are. They notice a lack of interest later than a lack of understanding, because a bored partner often stays silent.
