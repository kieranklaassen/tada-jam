## Official wording

vormen van eigen mening over de inhoud van een tekst en die onderbouwen met argumenten

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/02/08; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 4e13a266-2a4d-4a67-8b1a-d8241d27c6cd.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

forming an opinion of one's own about the content of a text and supporting it with arguments

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story has been read aloud, the child says "I think the girl was right to keep the secret, because she had promised". After a classmate's plea for more school trips the child says whether they agree and gives a reason taken from what was said. The child hears a partner's different opinion and responds to the partner's reason. A child of 7 gives an opinion with a reason from their own experience; a child of 10 gives a reason taken from the text and keeps an opinion about the content apart from whether the listening was pleasant.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts: forming an opinion of one's own about the content of a text, and supporting that opinion with arguments. It sets no number of arguments and does not say where the arguments come from. The text is one that is listened to, since the open data lists the goal under listening, in sub-cluster NE/MT/02/08, titled *Evalueren* (evaluating). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Giving a reason that only repeats the opinion ("it is good because I like it"). Taking over the opinion of the adult or of the child who spoke first. Giving an opinion about the reader's voice or the pictures when the content is asked about.
