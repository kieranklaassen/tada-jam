## Official wording

kritisch kijken (met hulp) naar eigen en andermans teksten om te bepalen of schrijfdoelen zijn behaald

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/SCH/02/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 90f0fcc5-2535-47d9-b8e1-c280233176c8.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

looking critically (with help) at one's own and other people's texts to determine whether writing purposes have been achieved

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gives their written instruction for a card trick to another child, watches whether that child can do the trick from it, and marks where it went wrong. With help the child reads someone else's text and asks: was this meant to inform, and do I now know what I needed to know? A child of 7 says whether a text was clear or funny; a child of 10 names the purpose first and points to the places where the text does or does not reach it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets say "with help" and do not say from whom. The texts are the child's own and those of others; "andermans" (someone else's) does not say whose. The looking has one stated aim: to determine whether writing purposes have been achieved; purposes are named in NE/SCH/02/01/03/fase2. The goal does not say that the text is rewritten afterwards; revising is in NE/SCH/02/05/03/fase2.

### Common mistakes

Judging the text by its length, its handwriting or its spelling and not by its purpose. Finding their own text clear because they know what they meant. Saying only "good" about another's text, or only pointing out mistakes.
