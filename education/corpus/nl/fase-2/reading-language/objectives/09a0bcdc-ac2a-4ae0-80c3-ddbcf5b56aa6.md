## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting eye contact and body posture to use to support a contribution to the conversation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Giving a view in a group talk, the child turns toward the group, sits upright and looks at the classmates in turn, not only at the adult. Answering a classmate, the child looks at that classmate. When disagreeing, the child keeps facing the other and does not turn away. A child of 7 looks at the classmate they are answering; a child of 10 looks round the group while speaking and shows by posture that they want to say something or have finished.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two means are named: eye contact and body posture. Their role is to support a contribution to the conversation. Gestures are not named here; NE/MT/03/07/02/fase2, the goal for speaking, names body posture, eye contact and gestures, and says "with help", which this statement does not. The statement does not say how often or how long.

### Common mistakes

Looking at the table, only at the adult or only at one friend while speaking to the group. Staring fixedly at one partner after being told to make eye contact. Turning away or sitting bent over when unsure, so that the partners take the words as not meant for them.
