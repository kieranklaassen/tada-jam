## Official wording

bewust worden van eigen voorkeur voor onderwerpen, genres, schrijvers en taalgebruik

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/07/03; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 0e040b57-89e7-4580-b338-2fee28df93fa.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

becoming aware of one's own preference for subjects, genres, writers and language use

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child chooses a book in the library and says why this one: it is about football, it is a comic, it is by a writer whose other book they liked, or it has funny made-up words. The child keeps a list of books read and marks which ones they would read again. A child of 7 knows which subjects they like to read about; a child of 10 also names kinds of books and writers they prefer and says what kind of language they like, for example rhyme or jokes.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about becoming aware of a preference; it does not say what the preference should be. Four things are named that a preference can be for: subjects, genres, writers and language use. The goal names no books and sets no number of books read. Its cluster is about fiction.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often name one book or one series and do not say what it is about that book that they like. Many choose what a friend chooses and take that for their own preference.
