## Official wording

structureren van een tekst in samenspraak zodanig dat het verband tussen zinnen en tussen passages binnen de tekst duidelijk(er) wordt

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

structuring a text in consultation, in such a way that the connection between sentences and between passages within the text becomes clear(er)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a first version of a text together with someone else, and they talk about its order: which sentences belong together, which part comes first, where a new part begins. The child numbers the parts or cuts the text into strips, moves a sentence to the part it belongs to, and adds a word that shows how two sentences are linked. A child of 7 puts loose sentences about the same thing next to each other; a child of 10 changes the order of whole parts and says how each part follows from the one before.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. "In samenspraak" (in consultation, talking it over together) says that the structuring is not done alone; the wording does not say with whom. The aim is stated: the connection between sentences and between passages within the text becomes clear or clearer; the brackets in "duidelijk(er)" leave both open. "Passages" is not explained, and the goal does not use the word *alinea* (paragraph) and sets no number of parts. It names no means of showing the connection; practising with reference words and conjunctions is another goal of the row *inhoud/vorm* (record 4 of the row). The goal sets no text length and does not say whether the structuring happens before, during or after writing.

### Common mistakes

Writing things down in the order in which they think of them, so that sentences about one point stand in several places. Going to a new point without any sign for the reader, such as a new line or a linking word. Taking over what the other person proposes without reading whether the text has become clearer.
