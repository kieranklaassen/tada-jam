## Official wording

belangstelling hebben voor verhalende teksten (fictie, waaronder poëzie) en informatieve teksten (zakelijke teksten)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having an interest in narrative texts (fiction, including poetry) and informative texts (non-fiction texts, *zakelijke teksten*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child picks up a story book, a book of poems and a book about volcanoes from the reading table, leafs through them and starts to read one. The child asks what a text is about, asks for the next part of a story, and tells others a fact they read. A child of 7 chooses a book by its subject and its pictures; a child of 10 looks for another text on a subject or by a writer they already know.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about interest, in two groups of texts: narrative texts, which the brackets describe as fiction including poetry, and informative texts, which the brackets describe as *zakelijke teksten* (non-fiction texts). It names no texts and sets no reading level, no amount of reading and no text length. It does not say whether the child reads the texts or hears them.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often keep to one kind of text, for example only comics or only books about animals, and do not try the other group. A child who finds reading hard may show interest in a subject but not in reading about it.
