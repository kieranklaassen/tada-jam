## Official wording

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

asking questions about word meaning when that hinders listening/understanding

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

During an explanation about the Middle Ages the child hears "the knight's squire", does not know "squire" and asks what it means. Listening to a recorded text, the child writes down the unknown word and asks about it at the pause. The child does not ask about an unknown word that does not hinder understanding, such as the name of a rare plant in a list. A child of 7 asks "What is that?" about a word that stops them from following; a child of 10 judges whether the word is needed to follow the text and asks a more exact question ("Does 'scarce' mean that there is little of it?").

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The condition is part of the statement: the question is asked when not knowing what a word means hinders listening or understanding, so the child is not asked to ask about every unknown word. The wording's "that" points back loosely to the word meaning, and the source prints "listening/understanding" with a slash. NE/MT/02/06/02/fase2 is the goal about inferring the meaning of unknown words. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Not noticing that a word is unknown, and so not asking. Noticing it and staying silent, so as not to interrupt or not to be the only one who asks. Interrupting for every unknown word, also where the next sentence explains it.
