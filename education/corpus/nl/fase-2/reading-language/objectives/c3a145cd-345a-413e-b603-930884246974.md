## Official wording

beseffen dat verschillende taken een verschillende aanpak vergen (reciteren = oefenen, som uitleggen = nadenken over uitleg)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that different tasks require a different approach (reciting = practising, explaining a sum = thinking about the explanation)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In one week the child has two tasks: reciting a poem to the class and explaining to a classmate how they solved a sum. For the poem the child says it aloud many times, until it goes without the paper; for the sum the child thinks out which step to tell first and which example to use. Asked how they prepared, the child says that the two tasks needed different things. A child of 7 prepares each task in the way the adult indicates and notices afterwards that the ways differed; a child of 10 says beforehand what kind of task it is and chooses a preparation to fit.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "realising": the statement does not ask the child to choose the approach. The brackets name two pairs, printed with equals signs: reciting goes with practising, and explaining a sum goes with thinking about the explanation. The open data lists the goal under speaking, in sub-cluster NE/MT/03/09, titled *Aanpak* (approach). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is the difference: they prepare every speaking task in the same way, usually by writing out a text and learning it by heart. Many do not prepare an explanation at all, because they "already know the sum".
