## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Talking with a partner about two ways of solving a sum, the child says "compare", "difference" and "estimate". In a talk about a river after a geography lesson the child says "source", "mouth" and "dyke". The child hears the adult rephrase "it gets more" as "it increases" and says "increase" in the next sentence. A child of 7 uses the school words of daily tasks, such as "column", "underline" and "example"; a child of 10 also uses more abstract school words, such as "cause", "consequence" and "characteristic", and the terms of the subjects.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names two kinds of words in one compound and defines neither: *schooltaal* is commonly understood as the language of lessons and tasks across subjects, and *vaktaal* as the terms of one subject. It names no words, no subjects and no number of words. The source prints the verb without *van* ("of") after it, unlike most neighbouring goals; the gloss reads it in the same form as theirs. The open data lists the goal under conversations, in sub-cluster NE/MT/01/05, titled *Woordgebruik en woordenschat* (word use and vocabulary). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Understanding a school word in an instruction and still using an everyday phrase when speaking ("look what is the same and not the same" for "compare"). Using a subject term too widely, such as "area" for the outline of a shape as well as its surface. Saying a new term only directly after the adult has said it, and not later.
