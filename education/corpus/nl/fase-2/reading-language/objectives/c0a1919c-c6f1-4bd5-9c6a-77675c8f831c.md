recognising differences in conversation partners

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child talks about the school trip with a friend, with the teacher and with a grandparent who visits the class, and says afterwards what was different: the friend had been there and the grandparent had not. The child sorts cards with people (the pack's examples: a younger child, the head teacher, a new classmate, a shop assistant) by whether the child knows them and whether they are older or younger. A child of 7 recognises differences that can be seen, such as age and whether the person is known; a child of 10 also recognises differences in what a partner knows, wants and expects from the talk.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "recognising": the statement does not ask the child to speak differently to different partners. It does not say which differences are meant and gives no examples. NE/MT/01/04/03/fase2 is the goal about matching forms of address to partners, and NE/MT/01/04/04/fase2 the goal about becoming aware of partners' interest and prior knowledge. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Seeing only the difference between a child and an adult, and none between a familiar and an unfamiliar adult. Naming an outward difference (taller, older) where the difference that matters for the talk is what the partner knows. Assuming that every partner knows what the child knows, so that a visitor who was not present is not seen as different from a classmate who was.
