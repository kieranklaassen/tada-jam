## Official wording

onder aanmoediging van de leraar inzetten van schrijfstrategieën: - oriënteren met aandacht voor het doel en publiek; - plannen en maken van een opzet voor de tekst; - reflecteren en reviseren op inhoud (doel, publiek, opbouw van tekst) en vorm (opbouw van zinnen, spelling)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

with the teacher's encouragement, putting writing strategies to use: - orienting, with attention to the purpose and audience; - planning and making an outline for the text; - reflecting and revising on content (purpose, audience, build-up of text) and form (build-up of sentences, spelling)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before writing, the child says or notes who the text is for and what it is for, and writes a few key words in the order they will come: beginning, middle, end. After the first version, the child reads it again and changes things: a part that is missing for this reader, two sentences in the wrong order, a sentence that is too long, a word spelled wrongly. The teacher encourages each step. A child of 7 makes a plan of words or drawings and corrects a word afterwards; a child of 10 makes an outline with parts and changes content as well as sentences.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The strategies are put to use "onder aanmoediging van de leraar" (with the teacher's encouragement): the wording names the teacher and names encouragement. Three strategies are listed: orienting, with attention to purpose and audience; planning and making an outline; reflecting and revising. For the last, the brackets name what is looked at: for content the purpose, the audience and the build-up of the text, and for form the build-up of sentences and spelling. Punctuation is not among what the brackets name for form; it is in NE/SCH/02/06/02/fase2. The goal names no kind of text and no text length.

### Common mistakes

Starting to write at once and making the plan afterwards, or not at all. Taking revising to mean copying the text out neatly or correcting spelling only. Reading back what they meant and not what they wrote, so missing words go unnoticed.
