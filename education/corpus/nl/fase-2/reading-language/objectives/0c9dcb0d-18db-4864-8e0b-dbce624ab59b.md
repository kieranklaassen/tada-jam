## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making a distinction between a known audience from the direct, own surroundings and from less near surroundings, and adapting word use to that

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes about the same school trip twice: a note to a friend who was there, and a letter to someone further from their daily life, such as the guide at the museum. In the second text the child explains who people are and what things are called, and chooses a polite greeting and polite words. A child of 7 changes the greeting and adds a name or a place; a child of 10 also changes the words themselves, for example "u" for "jij", and leaves out nicknames and words only the class knows.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two audiences are told apart: a known audience from the child's direct, own surroundings, and an audience from less near surroundings; the Dutch leaves open whether "bekend" (known) also applies to the second. One thing is adapted: word use. The goal does not mention adapting content, length or layout, and it does not name an unknown or general audience. It names no kinds of text.

### Common mistakes

Writing to everyone as to a friend: "jij", nicknames and words only the class knows. Writing as if the reader was there: "he took it", without saying who or what. Thinking that adapting means only changing the greeting.
