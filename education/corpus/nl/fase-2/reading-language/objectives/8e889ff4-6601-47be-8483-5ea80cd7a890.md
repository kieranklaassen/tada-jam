## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming the message in a story

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story the child says in a sentence what the story wants to tell the reader: "you should not judge someone before you know them" or "working together goes better than working alone". The child points to the place in the story that shows it, for example the ending. A child of 7 says what the main character learned or what happened in the end; a child of 10 puts the message in a general sentence that is also true outside the story.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about one thing, the message in a story, and the verb is naming: saying what the message is. The wording says "de boodschap" (the message): it does not mention stories with more than one message or with none. It names no kind of story and does not say whether the story is read by the child or read aloud. It does not ask for the child's opinion of the message.

### Common mistakes

Retelling the story or its ending when asked for the message. Giving a rule that fits one detail and not the whole story ("you must not go into the forest"). Giving a message the child knows from other stories ("be kind") without checking it against this story.
