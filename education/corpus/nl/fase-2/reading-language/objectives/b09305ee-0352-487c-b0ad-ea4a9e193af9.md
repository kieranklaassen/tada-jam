## Official wording

(associatief) relaties leggen tussen een verhaal en de werkelijkheid

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/07/02; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau b09305ee-0352-487c-b0ad-ea4a9e193af9.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(associatively) drawing relations between a story and reality

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While reading or after it, the child says what the story brings to mind: "my grandfather also has a boat like that" or "something like that was in the news". The child puts a finger on a passage and tells where they have seen or heard something like it. A child of 7 tells an experience of their own that a detail of the story reminds them of; a child of 10 compares the story with something they know from other people, other places or other times.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The bracketed word "associatief" says how the relations are made: by association, that is, by what the story brings to the child's mind. The other side is "de werkelijkheid" (reality), without "own": the wording does not limit it to the child's own life. The goal names no kind of story and does not say whether the story is read by the child or read aloud. It does not ask for a judgement of whether the story is realistic.

### Common mistakes

Telling a long experience of their own that starts from one detail and has no further link with the story. Taking everything in a realistic story as having really happened. Not saying what the link is: "this reminds me of my dog", without saying what in the story is like the dog.
