## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing main and secondary characters and indicating the relations between them

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After reading a story, the child says who the story is mainly about and which characters appear only now and then. The child writes the names on cards, lays the main character in the middle and draws lines to the others with a word on each line: sister, friend, enemy, teacher. A child of 7 names the main character and says who is that character's friend or family; a child of 10 also sorts the other characters into main and secondary ones and says what a secondary character is to the main character.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has two parts: telling main characters from secondary characters, and indicating the relations between them. "Ertussen" (between them) does not say whether the relations are those between the main and the secondary characters or among all of them. The goal names no kind of story and no length, and it does not say whether the story is read by the child or read aloud; its cluster is about fiction. It does not say how the relations are indicated (in speech, in writing or in a drawing).

### Common mistakes

Calling the character who is named first, or the one the child likes most, the main character. Listing every character without saying which ones matter most to the story. Describing a relation by a single event ("he hit him") and not by what the two are to each other.
