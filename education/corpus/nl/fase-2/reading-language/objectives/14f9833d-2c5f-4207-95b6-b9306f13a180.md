## Official wording

samenvatten door het weglaten en verzamelen van informatie uit een tekst

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

summarising by leaving out and collecting information from a text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a text about one subject, strikes through the sentences that give examples or details, and marks or copies the few pieces of information that must stay. The child then tells or writes what is left in a few sentences. A child of 7 says who or what the text is about and what is said about it; a child of 10 crosses out details paragraph by paragraph and puts what remains together in a text of their own.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names the way of summarising: leaving out information and collecting information from a text. It does not explain the two further; the plain reading is leaving out what matters less and gathering what matters most. It sets no length for the text or for the summary and does not say whether the summary is spoken or written. Other ways of summarising, such as making a diagram, are not named. Its cluster is about study skills with non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Retelling everything in the same order, a little shorter. Keeping what is interesting or surprising and leaving out the main point. Copying whole sentences from the start of the text and stopping when the summary seems long enough.
