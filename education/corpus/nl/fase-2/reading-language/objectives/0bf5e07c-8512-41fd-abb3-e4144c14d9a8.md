## Official wording

gebruiken van specifiek aan het onderwerp gerelateerde inhouds- en functiewoorden

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using content words (*inhoudswoorden*, words that name things, actions, properties) and function words (*functiewoorden*, grammatical words such as conjunctions and prepositions) that are specifically related to the topic

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a talk about horse riding the child says "bridle", "girth" and "trot" and says in a few words what each means. Reporting on a test in a science lesson, the child says "we measured", "the result" and "as a result of that" (the last is the pack's example of a function word that belongs to a topic). Before the talk the child makes a list of the words the topic needs and afterwards checks with a partner which were used. A child of 7 uses the topic's own nouns in place of "thing" and "that"; a child of 10 uses the terms exactly, explains those the audience does not know and uses linking words that fit an explanation or a report.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two kinds of words are named, content words and function words, and both are to be specifically related to the topic. The statement does not say how a function word is related to a topic, and names no words, no topics and no number of words. The open data lists it under speaking (NE/MT/03); NE/MT/01/05/01/fase2 is the matching goal for conversations, with the two kinds in the other order. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Using "thing", "that" and "do" where the topic has a word of its own. Using a term the audience does not know without explaining it. Using a newly learned term wrongly because only part of its meaning is known.
