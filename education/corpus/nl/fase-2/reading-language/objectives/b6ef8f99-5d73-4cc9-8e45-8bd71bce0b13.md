## Official wording

herkennen van tussenkopjes en alinea's in een tekst

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising subheadings and paragraphs in a text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a page before reading it and points to the title, the subheadings and the blocks of text under them, and counts the paragraphs by the blank lines or indents. The child says from a subheading what the part under it will be about, and matches loose subheadings to the paragraphs they belong to. A child of 7 points to subheadings and sees where a new block starts; a child of 10 sees where a new paragraph begins under a subheading and goes to a part by its subheading.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two features of a text are named: subheadings and paragraphs. The verb is recognising: the goal does not say that the child writes them or states the main idea of a paragraph. Other features, such as captions, bold words or an introduction, are not named in this statement. Its cluster is about non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Taking every new line for a new paragraph. Taking a bold word or a caption for a subheading. Skipping the subheadings while reading, as if they were not part of the text.
