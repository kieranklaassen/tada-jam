## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising the listening purpose of a text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears the announcement or the first sentences of a spoken text and says what one listens to it for: to enjoy it, to learn something, to know what to do or to form an opinion (the pack's examples of purposes). The child hears three short fragments (the pack's examples: a route description, a joke, an account of how hedgehogs live) and lays a card with a purpose next to each. A child of 7 tells an instruction from a story and says "this is to know what to do"; a child of 10 also recognises that a text is meant to persuade, and that the same text can be listened to for different purposes.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "recognising"; matching the way of listening to the purpose is the subject of NE/MT/02/04/02/fase2. The wording speaks of "the listening purpose of a text" and names no purposes. "Text" here is a spoken text, since the open data lists the goal under listening (NE/MT/02). The source prints the verb without *van* ("of") after it, unlike most neighbouring goals; the gloss reads it in the same form as theirs. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Naming the topic when asked for the purpose ("it is about hedgehogs"). Taking every text that is read aloud at school as something to remember for questions afterwards. Not recognising that a text is meant to persuade, and taking it as plain information.
