(with help) putting listening strategies to use

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

During a spoken explanation of how a lock in a canal works, the child looks at the drawing on the board and links each step to it (the strategies in this note are the pack's examples). When the child can no longer follow, they raise a hand and say which part was unclear. Afterwards the child tells a partner the main points in a few sentences, to check whether it was understood. A child of 7 looks at the drawing on the board when the adult reminds them at that moment; a child of 10 agrees with the adult before the listening starts to raise a hand at an unclear part, and does so.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it at the end of the sentence. The statement does not say who helps or how. It names no listening strategies and no number of them. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/02/09, titled *Aanpak* (approach), between NE/MT/02/09/01/fase2 (orientation on the listening task) and NE/MT/02/09/03/fase2 (reflection on it); NE/MT/02/04/03/fase2, listed under *Luisterdoelen en -strategieën* (listening purposes and strategies), is identical in the open data and worded differently in the content-line document, which prints that goal as "inzetten van luisterstrategieën met hulp van de leraar" (with help from the teacher).

### Common mistakes

Sitting still and looking at the speaker without following, and not showing it. Asking for everything to be repeated instead of saying which part was unclear. Knowing the strategies by name and not using one when the listening becomes hard.
