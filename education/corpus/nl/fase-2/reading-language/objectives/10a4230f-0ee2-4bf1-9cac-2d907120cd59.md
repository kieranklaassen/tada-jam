## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using function words (*functiewoorden*, grammatical words such as conjunctions and prepositions) and content words (*inhoudswoorden*, words that name things, actions, properties) that are specifically related to the topic

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk about a test with magnets the child says "attract", "repel" and "pole", not "it sticks" and "it pushes away". The child links what happened with words that an explanation needs, such as "because of that" and "unless" (the pack's examples of function words that belong to a topic). Telling a classmate the way on a map, the child says "opposite", "along" and "past". A child of 7 uses the main nouns and verbs of the topic once they have been offered; a child of 10 also uses the linking words the topic needs and chooses the exact word without a prompt.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two kinds of words are named, function words and content words, and both are to be specifically related to the topic. The statement does not say how a function word is related to a topic, and names no words, no topics and no number of words. NE/MT/03/05/01/fase2 is the matching goal for speaking, with the two kinds in the other order. The source prints the verb without *van* ("of") after it (*gebruiken functie- en inhoudswoorden*), unlike most neighbouring goals; the gloss reads it in the same form as theirs. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Using general words ("thing", "do", "put") where the topic has an exact word. Using a topic word with only part of its meaning (calling every metal "magnetic"). Linking everything with "and then" where the topic asks for "because", "so" or "if".
