getting to know and using spelling categories and spelling rules

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child learns a group of words that share a spelling feature, sorts word cards into such groups, says the rule aloud and writes other words with it: "bomen" with one o and "bommen" with two m's, "hond" with a d because of "honden", words with "ei" or "ij", words that end in "-lijk" or "-ig". The child writes dictated words and sentences and says which category a hard word belongs to. A child of 7 writes words with fixed letter groups such as "sch", "ng" and "aai"; a child of 10 applies rules such as doubling a consonant or checking a longer form of the word.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal itself names no spelling categories and no rules, and sets no number; the categories and rules above are the pack's examples. The content-line document prints lists of spelling categories and rules under the goal per pair of *groepen*, each offered from the pair it stands under; the lists are not in the open data and are no part of this record, and the lane's frame quotes the lists for *groep* 3-4 and *groep* 5-6, the pairs that hold the *groepen* of this fase. A spelling category is a group of words that share a spelling feature; the wording uses the term without explaining it. Two verbs are used: getting to know and using. The goal sets no word length and no share of words spelled correctly, and it does not say whether the rules are used in dictated words or in the child's own texts.

### Common mistakes

Writing a word as it sounds where a rule applies: "hont" for "hond". Applying a rule where it does not belong: doubling a consonant after a long vowel, "boommen" for "bomen". Knowing the rule in a spelling exercise and not using it when writing a text of their own.
