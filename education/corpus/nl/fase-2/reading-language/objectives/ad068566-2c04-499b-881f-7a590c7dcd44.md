## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) applying elementary conversation skills

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk the child waits until the speaker has finished, looks at the speaker and then says something that fits what was said. The child answers a question that is put to them and stays with the topic (these are the pack's examples of elementary skills). When the adult gives an agreed sign, the child stops and lets a classmate who has not yet spoken have a turn. A child of 7 lets a classmate finish when the adult gives the agreed sign; a child of 10 stays with the topic of the talk after a reminder from the adult.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it after the verb. The statement does not say who helps or how. The skills are called elementary; the statement names none and gives no examples. NE/MT/01/03/02/fase2 is the goal about more complex conversation skills, with asking follow-up questions and comprehension questions as its examples. The statement sets no group size and no kind of conversation.

### Common mistakes

Waiting in silence for a turn without listening, and then saying something that was already said. Speaking only to the adult and not to the classmates in the group. Applying the skills while the adult sits at the table and no longer when the adult has left.
