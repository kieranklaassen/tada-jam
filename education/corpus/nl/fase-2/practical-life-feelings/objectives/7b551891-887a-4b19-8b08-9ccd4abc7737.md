## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that what you eat has an influence on your health

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child draws or lists what he or she eats and drinks in a day and sorts it into groups such as vegetables and fruit, bread and grains, dairy, and sweets and snacks. The child reads on the package how much sugar is in a drink and lays out that many sugar cubes. The child notices and tells how he or she feels in the morning with and without breakfast. A child of 7 says which foods help to grow and to have energy and which are for now and then; a child of 10 links kinds of food to what they do in the body, such as energy, growth and strong teeth, and reads labels to compare products.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising. The statement names no foods, food groups, nutrients or amounts, and does not say what the influence is.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out time: they expect an effect at once, and since one sweet does no harm today they conclude that food does nothing. They also sort food into "good" and "bad" by whether they like it, and they think health is only about not being ill.
