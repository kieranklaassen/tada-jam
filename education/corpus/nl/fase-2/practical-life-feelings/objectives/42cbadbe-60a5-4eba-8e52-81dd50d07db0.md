## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

expressing and discussing an opinion of one's own and realising that not everyone has the same opinion (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk about a question from daily life, such as whether homework is a good idea or what to do at the class party, a child says what he or she thinks and gives a reason. The child stands on a line between "agree" and "disagree", hears reasons from children at other spots and may move. A child of 7 says what he or she thinks and why, and hears that a friend thinks otherwise; a child of 10 asks about another child's reason, answers it, and remains friendly with someone he or she disagrees with.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). Three things are named: expressing an opinion of one's own, discussing it, and realising that not everyone has the same opinion. The statement names no topics and no form of discussion, and does not say how the opinion is expressed. It does not ask that children come to agree.

### Common mistakes

Children repeat the opinion of a friend or of the first speaker and present it as their own. They give an opinion as a fact ("that is just stupid") and give no reason. They take disagreement as unkindness or as a threat to the friendship.
