## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child collects packaging, leaflets and advertisements and points out what is meant to make someone want the product: a colour, a cartoon figure, a free gift, the words "new" or "now only". The child asks other children why they chose their shoes, bag or snack and tallies the reasons: a friend has it, seen in an advertisement, price, taste. A child of 7 tells an advertisement apart from a programme or story and says that it wants people to buy; a child of 10 names the means an advertisement uses and names other influences, such as friends, habit and price.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring, and two things are explored: influences on what people consume, and the role of advertising. Advertising is the one influence named; the statement names no other and no medium (such as television, a shop or the internet). It does not say whether the influence is good or bad.

### Common mistakes

Children think an advertisement is information and that what it says is true. They do not recognise advertising when it is not a separate block, such as a figure on a package, a product in a video or a person who is paid to show it. They think advertising works on other people and not on them.
