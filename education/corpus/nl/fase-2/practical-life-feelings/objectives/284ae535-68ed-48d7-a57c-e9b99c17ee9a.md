## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with a disability or illness (chronic) in yourself or others

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child with asthma, diabetes, an allergy or a hearing aid tells the group what he or she needs and does, such as using an inhaler before sport, and the others ask questions. A child plays with another child who uses a wheelchair or tires quickly, changes the rules so that both can take part, and asks "do you want help?" before helping. A child of 7 asks direct questions and helps when asked; a child of 10 takes account of the condition without being asked and does not treat it as the only thing about the person, and when the condition is his or her own, tells others what he or she needs.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names a disability or an illness, with "chronic" in brackets after illness, and two cases: in yourself or in others. It names no conditions and no ways of dealing with them, and does not say who the others are. It sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

Children think a disability or a chronic illness can be caught, and keep away. They help without asking and take over what the other child can do. They think a child who looks well is not ill, and take a condition that cannot be seen for an excuse.
