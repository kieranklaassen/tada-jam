## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing that situations call up feelings that are not experienced in the same way by everyone (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a shared event, such as a school trip, a thunderstorm, a test or a film, each child shows on a card or on a line how it felt, and the child sees how far apart the answers lie. The child asks a child with another answer what made it exciting, frightening or dull for him or her. A child of 7 notices that another child felt something else and is surprised by it; a child of 10 expects that feelings differ, asks before assuming, and can give a reason for the difference, such as an earlier experience.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The source prints *beleeft* where *beleefd* ("experienced") is plainly meant, and the gloss gives what is meant. The verb is experiencing: meeting the difference, not explaining or judging it. The statement names no situations and no feelings.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out that their own feeling is one of several: they assume that what frightens or pleases them does the same to everyone. They also call another child's feeling wrong or exaggerated ("that is not scary"), and they overlook feelings that are not shown.
