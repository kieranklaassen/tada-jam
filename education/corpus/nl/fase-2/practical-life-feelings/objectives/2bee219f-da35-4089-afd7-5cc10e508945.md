behaving from respect for generally accepted values and norms (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child waits for a turn, greets, says thank you, lets someone finish speaking, leaves other people's things alone and tells truthfully what happened, also when no adult is looking. In a group talk the child says why a rule exists, such as not laughing at a mistake, and which value is behind it. A child of 7 follows the known rules of class and home and can say what a rule is for; a child of 10 acts on the value where no rule was given, such as helping a new child, and notices when rules differ between places.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The statement is about behaving, and about where the behaviour comes from: respect for values and norms. It names no values or norms and does not say what "generally accepted" (*algemeen aanvaarde*) covers or who decides that.

### Common mistakes

Children think a rule holds only while the adult who made it is present. They apply a rule strictly to others and not to themselves. They follow a rule and cannot say what it is for, so they do not know what to do where no rule was given.
