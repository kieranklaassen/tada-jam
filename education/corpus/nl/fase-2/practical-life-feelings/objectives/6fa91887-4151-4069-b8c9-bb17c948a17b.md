## Official wording

bespreken dat verliefdheid kan verschillen in aspecten als uitingsvorm, leeftijd en geslacht maar altijd vertrouwen en respect als basis hebben

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discussing that being in love can differ in aspects such as form of expression, age and *geslacht* (sex or gender), but always has trust and respect as its basis

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk a child says what he or she thinks being in love is and hears that others describe it differently. The child talks about the aspects the statement names (form of expression, age and *geslacht*) and about trust and respect as the basis of being in love. A child of 7 says what he or she thinks when asked; a child of 10 also asks others what they mean.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The source prints the plural verb *hebben* where the singular *heeft* is plainly meant, so that "always has trust and respect as its basis" is said of being in love; the gloss gives what is meant. The verb is discussing. Form of expression, age and *geslacht*, a word that covers both sex and gender, are introduced by "such as" (*als*), so they are examples of aspects. The statement names no forms of expression and no ages, and sets no level of detail. It stands under the heading relationships and sexuality (*Relaties en seksualiteit*).

### Common mistakes

Children think there is one right way to be in love or to show it, and call another way strange. They think that if one person is in love, the other must be too.
