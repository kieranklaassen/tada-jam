## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child interviews classmates, relatives or neighbours about how they live: what they eat, which days they celebrate, which languages are spoken at home, who lives in the house. The group puts the answers side by side in a table or on a map, and the child points out what is shared and what differs. A child of 7 tells how his or her own home and a classmate's home are alike and how they differ; a child of 10 puts the questions to people outside the class, compares the answers without ranking them, and says what people with different ways of living do together in the street, at school or at a club.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is investigating. The brackets in *(samen)leven* join living and living together; the statement covers both. A second pair of brackets adds "multicultural society" without explaining it. The content-line document (the institute's PDF of the line Jezelf en de ander) underlines that term and refers for underlined terms to a separate elaboration of knowledge topics, which the pack does not hold. The statement is bounded to people in the Netherlands, and names no cultures, religions, groups or customs.

### Common mistakes

Children take what happens at home to be what everyone does, and call a different way "wrong" or "strange". They take one person's habit as that of a whole group ("they all eat that"). They notice differences and leave out what is shared.
