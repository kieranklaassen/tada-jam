## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising (*onderkennen*, seeing it for what it is) prejudices and expressions of discrimination in oneself and others (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads or hears statements such as "girls cannot play football" or "old people do not understand computers" and says which are a judgement about a whole group made before knowing the person. The child looks in books, advertisements and jokes for examples and brings them to the group. The child tells of a time when he or she thought something about a person in advance and found it was not so. A child of 7 recognises unequal treatment in a concrete case, such as a child who may not join in because of how he or she looks; a child of 10 recognises a prejudice in a remark or a joke, including one of his or her own, and says how it differs from discrimination.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is recognising, with two objects (prejudices, and expressions of discrimination) and two places (in oneself and in others). The statement names no groups of people and no examples, and does not mention acting against discrimination. The concepts themselves are the subject of `ojw/ds/1/06/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Children recognise a prejudice in others and not in themselves. They think a prejudice is always negative and do not count "all ... are good at ..." as one. They take a remark for true because they know one person whom it fits.
