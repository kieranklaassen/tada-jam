## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child draws himself or herself in the middle of a page with the groups he or she belongs to around it (family, class, sports team, club, street) and writes in each what he or she is there: oldest sister, pupil, goalkeeper, beginner. The child asks an adult he or she knows the same questions and draws that person's page. A child of 7 names the groups and what he or she is in each, such as child at home and pupil at school; a child of 10 says how he or she acts differently from role to role, and that the same person fills different roles.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is discovering. The statement says "everyone", so it covers the child and other people. It names no groups and no roles. How a role influences behaviour is the subject of `ojw/ja/3/10/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Children think a person is one thing only, and are surprised to meet the teacher as a parent or as a customer in a shop. They name a group and leave out the role: "I am in a team", and not what they are in it. They take a role as fixed, for example that the oldest child always decides, and do not see that roles change between groups.
