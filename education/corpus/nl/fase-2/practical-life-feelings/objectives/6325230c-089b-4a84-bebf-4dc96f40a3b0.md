## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks back at a moment when he or she slammed a door, said something unkind or did not dare to join in, and names the feeling that came before it. In a story or a short acted scene the child points out what a character did and says which feeling was behind it. A child of 7 makes the link where it is plain to see, such as hitting when angry; a child of 10 makes it where it is less obvious, such as being quiet or joking when nervous, and uses it to guess what another child feels.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising. The statement is general: it does not say whose feelings and behaviour, so it covers the child and other people. It names no feelings and no kinds of behaviour, and does not mention changing the behaviour.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out the feeling and explain behaviour by the person: "he pushes because he is mean". They also use the feeling as an excuse ("I was angry, so I could not help it"), and they read the same behaviour as the same feeling in everyone, so that a quiet child must be sad.
