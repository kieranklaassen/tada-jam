## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child hears or reads a short case, such as a child who may not join in because of where the family comes from, and says which of the words fits: prejudice, discrimination or tolerance. The child lays the five words out on cards and draws lines between words that have to do with each other, saying why. The child asks someone who was treated unequally, or reads that person's account, and tells what it did to that person. A child of 7 recognises unequal treatment in a concrete case and hears the word for it; a child of 10 says how a prejudice can lead to discrimination and names an effect on a person and on a group.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). Two things are named: taking note of five concepts (prejudice, discrimination, tolerance, emancipation and the freedom of expression) and of how they relate to one another, and investigating what the effects are. The statement does not say which effects or on whom, gives no definitions or examples, and names no groups of people. The content-line document (the institute's PDF of the line De samenleving) underlines *discriminatie* and refers for underlined terms to a separate elaboration of knowledge topics, which the pack does not hold. Recognising prejudices and expressions of discrimination in oneself and others is the subject of `ojw/ja/3/05/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Children use prejudice and discrimination as the same word, and do not separate thinking something about a group from treating someone unequally. They take freedom of expression to mean that anything may be said whatever it does to others. They take tolerance to mean agreeing with everything or having no opinion.
