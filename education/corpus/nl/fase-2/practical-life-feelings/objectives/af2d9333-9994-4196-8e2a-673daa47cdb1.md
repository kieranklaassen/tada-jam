## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

becoming aware that every person, and so every child too, has rights (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks at pictures of what children need, such as food, a home, school, play, care when ill and protection, and sorts them apart from wishes, such as a new toy. The child says that the same things hold for every child. A child of 7 says that every child has a right to food and a home; a child of 10 says that every child has a right to go to school, whoever and wherever the child is.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The source prints *elke kind* where *elk kind* ("every child") is plainly meant, and the gloss gives what is meant. The verb is becoming aware. The statement names no rights and no document or treaty, and does not mention duties.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often mix up a right and a wish: "I have a right to sweets". They also leave out that the same rights hold for other children, including a child they dislike, and they think a right means always getting what they want.
