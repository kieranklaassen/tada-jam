## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a quarrel, or with a quarrel from a story, a child tells what each side wanted, thought and felt, and puts each on its own card. The child says which kind of difference started it (both wanted the same ball, they disagreed about a rule, one felt left out) and proposes ways to go on: taking turns, sharing, talking about it, asking a third person. A child of 7 tells what each side wanted and proposes a way to go on; a child of 10 says whether it was interests, views or feelings that were opposed and proposes a way that fits.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is discussing, and two things are discussed: that conflicts can be caused by opposing interests, views and feelings, and how to deal with this. The statement names no kinds of conflict, no method for dealing with one and no persons. Putting into words how simple conflict situations in one's own surroundings arose is the subject of `ojw/ja/3/08/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Children think a conflict means the other child is mean, and do not see that both wanted something reasonable. They tell who started it and not what each side wanted. They think a conflict ends only when one side wins or an adult decides.
