## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discussing the impact, safety and dangers of technology (*techniek*) and how to deal with this (for example tools)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before working with a saw, hammer, hand drill or glue gun a child tells what the tool does, what can go wrong and how to hold and carry it. The child talks about a made thing from daily life, such as a bicycle, a kettle or a lift, and names what it makes possible and what can go wrong with it. A child of 7 talks about the danger of a tool in the hand, such as a sharp edge, and how to hold it; a child of 10 talks about a thing that is not in the room, and sets what it makes possible beside what can go wrong with it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing, and what is discussed is the impact, the safety and the dangers of *techniek* and how to deal with this. The Dutch *techniek* covers made things and the skill of making and using them. Tools are given as an example (*bijv.*), and no particular tool or machine is named. The statement asks for discussion, not for use of a tool, and states no safety condition such as adult supervision.

### Common mistakes

Children think a tool is dangerous only when it is sharp or when it has hurt them before. They recite a safety rule and do not follow it once the work begins. They take "technology" to mean only screens and things with a plug.
