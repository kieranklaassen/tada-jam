## English gloss

dealing with one's own emotions (positive and negative) in different situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child who loses, gets a low mark or is left out notices the feeling, names it and chooses something to do with it: counting to ten, stepping away for a moment, saying "I am disappointed", asking for help. A child who wins or is very excited shows it without mocking the loser or disturbing the group. A child of 7 calms down with a fixed step that was practised, once reminded of it; a child of 10 picks a way that fits the place, such as waiting until the break to talk about it, and can say afterwards what helped.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name positive and negative emotions, and the statement says "in different situations" without naming any. It names no emotions and no ways of dealing with them. It is about the child's own emotions; the feelings, wishes and views of others are the subject of `ojw/ja/1/07/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Children think dealing with an emotion means not showing it, and hide their anger and later shout at someone who had nothing to do with it. They use one way everywhere, such as shouting or walking off, whether or not it fits the situation. They do not count positive emotions, and are so excited after winning that others are hurt.
