exploring the relationship between nature and the environment (for example *Natuurmonumenten*, *Staatsbosbeheer*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child visits a wood, heath or dune area nearby, reads the sign at the entrance to see who manages it and which rules hold there, and looks for traces of management such as a fence, a mown path or felled trees. Afterwards the child tells what would change for the plants and animals there if the water or air became dirty or if a road were built. A child of 7 says that the area is looked after and names a rule that holds there, such as staying on the path; a child of 10 says why the area is protected and names something from outside the area that affects it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. The statement does not say what the relationship between nature and the environment is. The brackets give two examples (*bijv.*), Natuurmonumenten and Staatsbosbeheer; the pack adds that these are Dutch organisations that manage nature areas, which the statement itself does not say. The statement does not say what is explored about them. It names no area, plant or animal.

### Common mistakes

Children use "nature" and "environment" as the same word. They think a nature area looks after itself and that nobody works there. They think nature is only somewhere far away, and do not count the verge, the park or the ditch near school.
