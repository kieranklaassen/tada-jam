## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child sorts cards with things about a person (name, address, a photo, a diary, a password, a favourite colour) into what anyone may know, what only some people may know and what is the child's alone, and compares the piles with another child's. The child practises asking "may I take your photo?" and "may I show this to others?" and accepting a no. A child of 7 names things that are his or her own, such as a diary or a closed toilet door; a child of 10 applies it to photos, messages and passwords and can say that other people have the same claim to privacy.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. The brackets add "in the Netherlands"; the statement names no law, no right and no comparison with another country. It does not say which kind of privacy is meant (for example of the body, the home, letters or data).

### Common mistakes

Children think privacy means having a secret, and that secrets are wrong. They claim privacy for themselves and not for a sibling or classmate: they want their own diary left alone and read someone else's. They think a photo or message they send stays with the person they sent it to.
