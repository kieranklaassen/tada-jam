## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discussing experiences with different *samenlevingsvormen* (forms of living together, or forms of society), such as residential, living and working communities, and what this means for people (mutual dependence and influence) (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child tells about the communities he or she is part of or has visited: the household, the street or block of flats, a relative's care home or shared house, a parent's workplace, a club. The child says what people there do for each other and what one person's behaviour does to the others, such as noise on a shared staircase or a colleague who is ill. A child of 7 tells who lives in his or her home and who does what there; a child of 10 compares the communities he or she knows and says how the people in them depend on and influence one another.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is discussing, and two things are discussed: experiences with different *samenlevingsvormen*, and what this means for people. The Dutch *samenlevingsvormen* can mean forms of living together in a household or group, and can mean forms of society; the statement does not choose. Residential, living and working communities (*woon-, leef- en werkgemeenschappen*) are given as examples (*zoals*). The last brackets name mutual dependence and influence. The statement does not say whose experiences are discussed and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

Children assume that everyone lives as they do and call another form strange. They see what others do for them and leave out what they do for others. They think that depending on others is weakness, and that adults depend on nobody.
