## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that a combination of healthy food and hygiene promotes health and can prevent illnesses

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child rubs a little glitter on the hands, washes them and sees what is left, and then says how something that cannot be seen passes from hand to food to mouth. The child prepares a snack: washes hands and fruit first, uses a clean board and knife, and puts leftovers in the fridge. A child of 7 says that washing hands and eating fruit and vegetables help against being ill; a child of 10 says how germs spread, and that food and hygiene together lower the chance of illness without making illness impossible.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The source prints *bevorderd* where *bevordert* ("promotes") is plainly meant, and the gloss gives what is meant. The verb is realising. The statement names the combination of healthy food and hygiene; it says that this promotes health and "can" prevent illnesses, not that it always does. It names no foods, hygiene habits or illnesses. Posture, movement and rest are the subject of `ojw/pdm/2/04/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out the word "can": they think someone who eats well and washes never gets ill, so that an illness is the person's own fault. They also think illness comes only from cold or wet weather, and they think that what looks clean is clean.
