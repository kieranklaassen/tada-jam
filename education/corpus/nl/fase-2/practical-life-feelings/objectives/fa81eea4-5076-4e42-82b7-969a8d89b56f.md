## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising the (shared) responsibility for one's own health and that of others

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child with a cold does not drink from a friend's cup, coughs into the elbow and washes hands before handing out food, and tells an adult when another child has fallen or feels ill. The child takes account of another child's allergy when bringing a treat. The child says what he or she does for his or her own health, such as going to bed on time and drinking water. A child of 7 follows such habits as rules and tells an adult when someone is hurt; a child of 10 says how his or her own behaviour affects the health of others and acts on it without being told.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising. The brackets around "shared" (*mede-*) leave open whether the responsibility is one's own or one shared with others; the statement covers both. It covers one's own health and that of others. It names no behaviour and does not say with whom the responsibility is shared.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out other people: they see health as their own matter and do not link their cold or their unwashed hands to someone else falling ill. They also think health is the task of parents and doctors only, or they feel responsible for too much and feel guilty when someone near them is ill.
