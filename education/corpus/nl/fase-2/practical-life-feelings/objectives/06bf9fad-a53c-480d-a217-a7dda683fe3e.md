## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

learning, in a *zelfbewuste* (self-aware or self-assured) way, to make requests - to refuse them, to give compliments - to accept them, to give criticism - to receive it (an attitude of standing up for oneself, *weerbare houding*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In pairs a child practises each of the six: asking "may I borrow your pen?", saying "no, I do not want to" without an excuse, saying "I liked how you explained that", answering a compliment with "thank you", telling someone "I do not like it when you take my things", and listening to criticism without shouting back or crying. The child stands straight, looks at the other and speaks loudly enough to be heard. A child of 7 practises asking, refusing and saying thank you with fixed sentences; a child of 10 gives criticism of what someone did and not of the person, and can receive criticism, think about it and say what he or she will do with it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names three pairs joined by dashes: making and refusing requests, giving and accepting compliments, giving and receiving criticism. All are to be done in a way called *zelfbewust*, a word that can mean self-aware and can mean self-assured; the brackets add *weerbare houding*, an attitude of being able to stand up for oneself. The verb is learning. The statement names no situations and no persons to whom these are said.

### Common mistakes

Children refuse by giving an excuse or by walking away, or they say yes and do not do it. They dismiss a compliment ("it was nothing") or take it as teasing. They give criticism as a judgement of the person ("you are stupid") and hear criticism of what they did as dislike of themselves.
