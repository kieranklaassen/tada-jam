## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting an orientation on and discussing the elements of the traffic layout in one's own surroundings, such as traffic signs

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On a walk around the school a child photographs or draws every sign, road marking, traffic light, zebra crossing, cycle path and speed bump on the route and marks them on a simple map. Afterwards the child tells for each what it asks of a walker, cyclist or driver and why it stands at that spot. A child of 7 names signs and markings on the way to school and what to do there; a child of 10 sorts signs by shape and colour into prohibition, command and warning, and says why a crossing is laid out as it is.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The source prints *verkeerborden* where *verkeersborden* ("traffic signs") is plainly meant, and the gloss gives what is meant. Two verbs are named: getting an orientation on, and discussing. Traffic signs are given as an example (*zoals*) of elements of the traffic layout; no other element is named. The statement bounds the elements to those in one's own surroundings and names no number or kind of sign.

### Common mistakes

Children read a sign as a picture of what is there and not as an instruction: a sign with a bicycle means "bicycles here", whatever its shape and colour. They mix up a round sign with a red rim (not allowed) and a round blue sign (must). They think a zebra crossing or a green light makes crossing safe without looking.
