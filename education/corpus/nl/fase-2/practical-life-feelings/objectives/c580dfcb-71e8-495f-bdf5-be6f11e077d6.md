## English gloss

realising that human intervention on earth has an influence on the natural balance

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks at a ditch, pond or verge near school before and after it is mown or dredged and tells which plants and animals are gone and which have come. With picture cards of a food chain the child takes one animal away and says what happens to the animals that eat it and to what it eats. A child of 7 tells what changed at a place he or she has seen, such as fewer butterflies where the flowers were mown; a child of 10 follows the change further, to the animals that eat those butterflies, and names an intervention that restored something.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising, not explaining or acting. The statement names no particular kind of intervention, no place and no example, and does not say what the natural balance consists of. It does not say whether the influence is harmful or helpful. Environmental pollution is named, as an example of an aspect of the concept 'environment', in `ojw/rojh/6/02/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out the steps in between: they link an intervention to what it touches directly, and not to the plants and animals that depend on that. They also take "balance" to mean that nothing in nature ever changes, and they count only large interventions such as felling a forest.
