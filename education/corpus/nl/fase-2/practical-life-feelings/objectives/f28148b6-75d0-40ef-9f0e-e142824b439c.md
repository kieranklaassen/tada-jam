## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exchanging experiences in the role of consumer (for example with regard to advertising and product choice)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child tells about something he or she bought or asked for: what it was, why that one, and whether it was as expected. The child listens to another child's account and asks a question about it, and the group puts purchases that pleased and purchases that disappointed side by side. A child of 7 tells what was bought and whether he or she is glad of it; a child of 10 says what led to the choice, compares the product with what the advertisement or package promised, and says what he or she would do differently.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exchanging experiences, so what is described is telling and listening, not a level of knowledge. Advertising and product choice are given as examples (*bijv.*). The statement names no products or amounts and does not say with whom the experiences are exchanged.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out the reason for a choice: they tell what they bought and how much they like it, not why they chose it. They also tell of purchases their parents made as their own, and they do not connect a disappointment to what the advertisement or package promised.
