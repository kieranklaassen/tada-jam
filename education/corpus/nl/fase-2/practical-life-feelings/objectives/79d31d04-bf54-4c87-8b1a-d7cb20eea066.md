## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting (one's own) behaviour in traffic into words and thinking up alternative ways of acting

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child draws his or her route to school, marks a crossing or a bend and tells step by step what he or she does there: where the child stops, where the child looks, when the child goes. With a photo or a model of a traffic situation the child says what the walker or cyclist did and gives one or two other things that person could have done. A child of 7 describes his or her own behaviour on foot at a familiar spot and says what else he or she could do there; a child of 10 describes behaviour on the bicycle and says what another way of acting would lead to.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: putting behaviour in traffic into words, and thinking up alternative ways of acting. The brackets around "one's own" (*eigen*) leave open whether the behaviour described is traffic behaviour in general, the child's own, or both. The statement asks for words and ideas, not for acting in traffic; it names no situations and no rules, and states no safety condition such as adult supervision.

### Common mistakes

Children describe the rule and not what they did ("you have to look") when asked about their own behaviour. They judge behaviour by the outcome: nothing happened, so it was safe. They give "be more careful" as the only alternative, which names no other action.
