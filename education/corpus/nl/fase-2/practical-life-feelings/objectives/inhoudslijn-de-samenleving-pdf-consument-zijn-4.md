## Official wording

beseffen dat werken voor je geld een dienst voor een ander betekent en dat dit onafhankelijk maakt (inkomsten/uitgaven)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld: De samenleving, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that working for your money means a service for someone else and that this makes a person independent (income/expenses)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child does a job for someone, for example washes a neighbour's car or sells self-made cards at a class market, and says who was helped by the work and what they got for it. The child asks adults what work they do and for whom they do it. For some weeks the child notes the money that comes in and the money that goes out, and sees what they can pay for from what they earned. A child of 7 says that people get money for work they do for others; a child of 10 sets income beside expenses in a list and says that earning money lets a person decide on their own spending.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line De samenleving), and it has no code. The verb is realising (*beseffen*), so the goal is about an insight, not a skill. Two things are realised: that working for your money means a service for someone else, and that this makes independent; the Dutch does not say who becomes independent or of whom or what, and "a person" in the gloss is added for the English sentence. The brackets name income/expenses (*inkomsten/uitgaven*), printed with a slash and without "bijv."; the wording does not say how they relate to the two insights. The statement names no jobs, wages, amounts or pocket money. This goal states no number range. The role of money (buying, selling, paying, saving) is the subject of `ojw/ds/2/01/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Children think money comes from the bank, the cash machine or a card, and do not link it to work. They see work as something a person does for a boss or for the money, and do not say who is served by it. They take independent to mean being allowed to buy anything, and do not link what can be spent to what comes in.
