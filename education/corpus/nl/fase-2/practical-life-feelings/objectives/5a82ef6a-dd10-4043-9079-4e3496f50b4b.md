## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting into words how simple conflict situations in one's own surroundings arose and which solutions are possible (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a quarrel on the playground or at home a child tells in order what happened: what came first, what each one did and said next, and at which point it became a quarrel. The child then names two or three ways it could be solved and says what each would mean for both sides. A child of 7 tells his or her own side in order and says how it could be solved; a child of 10 tells it so that the other side agrees with the account too, and says what a solution would mean for each side.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is putting into words, with two objects: how the conflict situations arose, and which solutions are possible. The situations are bounded twice: they are simple, and they are in one's own surroundings. The statement asks for words, not for solving the conflict, and names no method. What causes conflicts and how to deal with them is the subject of `ojw/ja/1/06/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Children start the account at the moment the other child did something and leave out what they did before it. They tell who is to blame in place of what happened. They name as the only solution that the other child says sorry or is punished.
