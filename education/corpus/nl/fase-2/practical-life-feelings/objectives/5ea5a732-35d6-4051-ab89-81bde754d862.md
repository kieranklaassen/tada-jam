## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with simple 'health problems' such as a cold, flu, but also a small cut, a bruise, etc.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child with a cold blows the nose in a tissue, throws it away, washes hands, and says when he or she feels too ill to work. A child with a small cut rinses it under the tap, dries the skin around it and puts on a plaster, and a child who has bumped a knee holds something cold against it and rests it. The child says when it is more than he or she can handle and fetches an adult. A child of 7 tells an adult and helps with the plaster; a child of 10 treats a small cut or bruise without help and can say at which signs an adult is needed.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement puts 'health problems' in quotation marks and calls them simple. A cold, flu, a small cut and a bruise are given as examples (*zoals*), and "etc." shows that the list is open. It names no ways of dealing with them and states no safety condition such as adult supervision. First-aid skills are the subject of `ojw/pdm/2/09/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Children put a plaster on a cut without rinsing it first. They hide a small injury or feeling ill so as not to miss play, or they treat every scratch as serious. They think a cold comes from being cold and do not link it to hands and coughing.
