discussing what sexual abuse is and in what way help can be asked for

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk a child hears what sexual abuse is and says in his or her own words what he or she has understood. The child also hears in what way help can be asked for, and says that in his or her own words too. A child of 7 listens and answers when asked; a child of 10 also asks the questions he or she has.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing, and two things are discussed: what sexual abuse is, and in what way help can be asked for. The statement names no acts, persons or situations, names nobody to ask for help, and sets no level of detail. It stands under the heading relationships and sexuality (*Relaties en seksualiteit*).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the second part of what is discussed: they do not know that help can be asked for, or in what way. Many have not heard the words *seksueel misbruik* before and do not know what they mean.
