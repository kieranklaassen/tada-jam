## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring one's own characteristics, possibilities, limitations and preferences and developing a *zelfbewuste* (self-aware or self-assured) attitude (self-confidence)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child makes a page or box about himself or herself (what I look like, what I can do well, what I find hard, what I like and dislike) and adds to it during the year. The child tries something new, such as presenting to the group or a new sport, and says afterwards what went well. The child says "I am not good at this yet, I need help with ..." without shame. A child of 7 names what he or she likes and can do, and things about himself or herself that can be seen; a child of 10 names traits of character too, names a limitation without being upset by it, and chooses a task that fits what he or she can do.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: exploring four things about oneself (characteristics, possibilities, limitations and preferences), and developing an attitude. The Dutch *zelfbewust* can mean self-aware and can mean self-assured; the statement does not choose, and its brackets add self-confidence (*zelfvertrouwen*). It names no characteristics and no way of exploring.

### Common mistakes

Children describe themselves only by what can be seen and by what they own. They judge themselves by comparison with the best child in the group and conclude "I cannot do anything". They take one failure as a fixed trait ("I am bad at sums").
