## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising (*onderkennen*, seeing it for what it is) bullying and being aware of bullying behaviour

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With examples from stories or from the playground a child says which is teasing, which is a quarrel and which is bullying, and gives the reason: it is repeated, it comes from one side, the other cannot defend himself or herself. The child names the parts people have in it: the one who bullies, the one who is bullied, those who join in or laugh, those who watch, and the one who helps. The child says in which of these parts he or she has been. A child of 7 recognises open bullying such as hitting, name-calling and taking things; a child of 10 also recognises excluding, gossip and bullying by message, and recognises his or her own part as someone who laughs or watches.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: recognising bullying, and being aware of bullying behaviour. The statement does not say whose behaviour, so it covers that of others and the child's own. It names no forms of bullying, and does not mention stopping it, reporting it or helping.

### Common mistakes

Children call every unkind act bullying, or call bullying "just a joke" when they are the ones doing it. They do not count excluding and ignoring as bullying, because nothing is said or done. They think that someone who only watches or laughs has no part in it.
