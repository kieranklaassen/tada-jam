## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child collects litter along a fixed stretch of street or schoolyard, sorts it by material and counts each pile. The child wipes a white cloth over a windowsill or a leaf near a road and looks at the dust on it, and says where the dirty water from the classroom tap goes. A child of 7 names what is dirty and where it lies; a child of 10 names pollution of air, water and soil and says where each comes from.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. Environmental pollution is given as an example (*zoals*) of an aspect; no other aspect is named, and the statement does not say how many aspects are explored. It names no cause of pollution, no place and no measure against it.

### Common mistakes

Children count only pollution they can see, such as litter, and leave out what is in air and water. They think that what goes down the drain or into the bin is gone. They use "environment" and "nature" as the same word.
