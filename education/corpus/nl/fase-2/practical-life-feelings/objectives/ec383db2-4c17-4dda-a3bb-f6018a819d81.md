## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

working together in a group: dividing and carrying out tasks, making and keeping agreements, asking for and accepting help (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With a group a child makes something that no child could finish alone in the time, such as a poster, a model or a play. The group lists the tasks, each child takes one and does it, they agree who brings what and when it must be finished, and the child asks a group member for help and uses the help that is given. A child of 7 carries out the task he or she took and keeps to what was agreed; a child of 10 also has a say in how the tasks are divided, speaks to a member who does not keep an agreement, and asks for help when stuck.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). After the colon the statement names three pairs: dividing and carrying out tasks, making and keeping agreements, asking for and accepting help. It names no group size, no kind of task and no roles.

### Common mistakes

Children take over the whole task in place of dividing it, or leave it to the others. They agree to something to end the discussion and do not keep to it. They see asking for help as failing, or they accept help by letting the other child do the work.
