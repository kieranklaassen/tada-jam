## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child names places and moments where he or she feels safe and where not, and says what or who makes the difference: a parent, a teacher, a lock on the door, a rule. The child names people whose work is safety, such as police officers, firefighters and ambulance staff. A child of 7 links being safe to people and places close by; a child of 10 can also say that rules and laws protect people, and that being safe is not the same for everyone or everywhere.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising. The statement puts 'being safe' in quotation marks and adds "in the Netherlands"; it does not say what being safe covers (for example at home, in the street, online or from war) and names no comparison with another country. It names no persons, services or rules. The reasons why people flee their country are the subject of `ojw/ds/1/02/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out everything that is not physical: they link being safe to not getting hurt, and not to being free from threats, bullying or fear. They also take their own safety as how it is for every child, and they take a frightening news report as a sign that the same will happen to them.
