vinden en houden van een eigen plek binnen de (eigen) groep (zelfstandigheid)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

finding and keeping a place of one's own within the (own) group (*de (eigen) groep*) (independence)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child asks to join children who are already playing, proposes an idea in a group task and keeps to it when others want something else, and says "I was sitting here" when moved aside. The child finds something to do when the usual friend is absent, and takes a part in the group that fits, such as the one who draws or the one who keeps time. A child of 7 asks to join a game and plays along in it; a child of 10 keeps a place in a group task while holding to his or her own view.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: finding a place of one's own, and keeping it. The brackets around "own" leave open whether any group or the child's own group is meant, and the statement does not say which group that is. A second pair of brackets adds the word independence (*zelfstandigheid*). The statement names no situations and no behaviour.

### Common mistakes

Children keep their place by doing whatever the leading child wants, and give up their own view to belong. They take a place by having another child excluded. They withdraw after one refusal and conclude that nobody wants them.
