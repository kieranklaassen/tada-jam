## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that certain natural energy sources are finite (oil and gas) and others are infinite (sun, wind and water)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child sorts picture cards of energy sources into two groups, those that run out and those that do not, and says why for each. The child holds a small solar cell with a motor in the sun and in the shade, blows at a paper windmill and pours water over a water wheel, and sees that sun, wind and water keep coming. A child of 7 says where the heat and light at home come from and that gas can run out; a child of 10 sorts the sources the statement names and says for each why it does or does not run out.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising. The brackets name oil and gas as finite sources and sun, wind and water as infinite ones, and no other source is named. The statement's own words for the two groups are finite (*eindig*) and infinite (*oneindig*). It says nothing about how energy is generated or saved.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out that electricity has a source: they think it comes from the socket and is always there. They also take "infinite" to mean available at every moment, and forget that the sun does not shine at night and the wind does not always blow. They think oil and gas are made in a factory.
