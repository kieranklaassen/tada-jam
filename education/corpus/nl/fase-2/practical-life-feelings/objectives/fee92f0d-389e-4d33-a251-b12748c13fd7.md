## English gloss

discussing that people can choose different forms of relationship and that people think differently about that

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk a child tells which forms of relationship he or she knows from the adults around him or her, and hears from others about forms he or she does not know from home. The child hears that people hold different views about these forms and says what he or she thinks, with a reason. A child of 7 describes the forms he or she knows and notices that other homes differ; a child of 10 can say that the form is a choice people make and that views on it differ.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing, and two things are discussed: that people can choose different forms of relationship, and that views on this differ. The statement names no forms of relationship and no views, does not say which view is right, and sets no level of detail. It stands under the heading relationships and sexuality (*Relaties en seksualiteit*).

### Common mistakes

Children assume every household is built like their own and call another form "not real". They take a view they heard at home for a fact that everyone shares.
