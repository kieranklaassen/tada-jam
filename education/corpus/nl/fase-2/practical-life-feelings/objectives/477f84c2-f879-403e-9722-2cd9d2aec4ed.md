## Official wording

ontdekken dat de manier waarop je je gedraagt in een bepaalde rol sterk wordt beïnvloed door het verwachtingspatroon dat bij die rol hoort

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering that the way you behave in a particular role is strongly influenced by the pattern of expectations that belongs to that role

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child acts out the same person in two roles, such as a goalkeeper in a match and an older brother at home, and the group says what was different in voice, words and posture. The child lists what others expect of a class helper, a team captain, a guest and a host, and says what happens when someone does not do it. A child of 7 says what one is supposed to do in a familiar role, such as being quiet as a listener; a child of 10 says that the expectations come from other people, that they differ from group to group, and that he or she behaves differently because of them.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discovering. The statement says "strongly influenced", not determined, and speaks of "a particular role" without naming one. It does not say whose expectations make up the pattern, and does not say whether following them is good or bad. That everyone fills roles in different groups is the subject of `ojw/ja/3/09/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Children think they behave the same everywhere, and do not notice that they act differently at home, at school and at the club. They take the expectations of a role for rules that cannot be otherwise. They explain behaviour only by what a person is like and leave out the role that person is in.
