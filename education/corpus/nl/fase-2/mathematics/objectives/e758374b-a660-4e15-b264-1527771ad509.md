## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing some references for the standard measure gram and kilogram

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child picks up everyday things and learns which weigh about a gram and which about a kilogram, for example a paper clip for a gram and a full one-litre carton of milk or a pack of sugar for a kilogram, and checks them on scales. A child of 7 names, for the gram and for the kilogram, a thing it has held in its hand; a child of 10 names such a thing for each of the two units without the things at hand.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal asks for "some" references (*referenties*, here familiar things whose weight is known) for two units: gram and kilogram. Its own wording states no number of references and names none. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: a postage stamp, a tea bag weigh about a gram; a pack of sugar weighs (usually) a kilogram; it is an example, not a limit. The pack of sugar in the paragraph above is also in the official example; the paper clip and the carton of milk are the pack's examples. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child chooses as a reference a thing whose weight is far from the unit, most often a thing much heavier than a gram for the gram. The child chooses by size, for example a large empty box for the kilogram. The child treats the reference as exact and forgets that it is "about".
