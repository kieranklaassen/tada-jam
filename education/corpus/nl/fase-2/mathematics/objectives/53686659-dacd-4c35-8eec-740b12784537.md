## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding and subtracting by estimation up to at least 1000 in context situations and formal mathematical language and reasoning out whether the real outcome is (much) larger or smaller than the estimated outcome

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child rounds the numbers before adding or subtracting: for 289 + 412 the child says "about 300 and about 400, so about 700". The child then says whether the exact outcome will lie above or below the estimate, and by a little or by a lot, by looking at which way each number was rounded. In a situation the child uses the estimate to decide, for example whether 500 sheets are enough for 289 and 198 sheets. A child of 7 rounds each number with a number line and adds the round numbers; a child of 10 also reasons out, from the way each number was rounded, whether the real outcome is larger or smaller than the estimate.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The wording does not say whether the bound holds for the numbers in the calculation, for the outcome, or for both. The work is in context situations and in formal mathematical language. Two parts are named: adding and subtracting by estimation, and reasoning out whether the real outcome is larger or smaller than the estimate, and whether by much. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Janne buys a coat of 99 euros and shoes of 128 euros. About how much does that cost together?"; it is an example, not a limit. Neither the wording nor the example says to what the numbers are rounded; rw/gb/3/05/fase2 names rounding to tens or hundreds.

### Common mistakes

The child first works out the exact answer and then rounds it, in place of rounding first. The child rounds both numbers up and does not notice that the estimate of the sum is then too high. The child sees the estimate as a wrong answer and has no idea how far it may be from the real outcome.
