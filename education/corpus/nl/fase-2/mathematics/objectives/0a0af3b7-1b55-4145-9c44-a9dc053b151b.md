## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

maintaining the automatising (calculating fluently) of adding and subtracting up to at least 100 and of tens and hundreds up to at least 1000, analogous to calculating below 100

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works out 47 + 8, 63 − 20 or 36 + 27 quickly in the head, in a few known steps and without counting by ones. The child works out 300 + 400, 70 + 80 or 900 − 500 from the small fact it resembles: 3 + 4, 7 + 8, 9 − 5. The child says the answer and, when asked, the steps. A child of 7 says the steps aloud while working a sum out; a child of 10 gives the outcome fluently and names the small fact that a sum with tens or hundreds resembles.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is maintaining (*onderhouden*): keeping up what is already known. Two ranges are stated: adding and subtracting "up to at least 100" (*tot ten minste 100*), and adding and subtracting of tens and hundreds "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*), done by analogy with calculating below 100. For both ranges no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether the bound itself is included. Between 100 and 1000 the wording names only tens and hundreds, not all numbers. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: 350 + 200, by analogy with 35 + 20; it is an example, not a limit. The level named is automatising, which the goal itself explains as calculating fluently; rw/bew/1/04/fase2 names memorising for the facts below ten and across the first ten.

### Common mistakes

The child treats 300 + 400 as a new problem and does not use 3 + 4. The child loses or adds a zero in the analogy and gives 70 + 80 as 15 or as 1500. For a sum that passes a ten the child still counts on by ones, which is slow and gives answers that are one off.
