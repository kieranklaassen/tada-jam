## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

for a multiplying or dividing situation up to at least 1000, giving a formal multiplication or division and the reverse: thinking up fitting multiplying or dividing situations for a formal multiplication or division below 1000

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears a situation, such as 6 trays with 12 eggs each, or 96 children in teams of 8, and writes the formal multiplication or division that fits it: 6 × 12, 96 ÷ 8. The other way round, the child gets 5 × 30 or 60 ÷ 4 and makes up a story that fits it, and tells or draws the story. The child checks whether the story of another child fits. A child of 7 lays a situation out with objects before writing the multiplication or division; a child of 10 makes up both a sharing story and a grouping story for the same division.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two ranges are stated, one for each direction. The multiplying or dividing situations go "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The formal multiplications or divisions for which situations are thought up are "below 1000" (*onder 1000*), without "at least"; read strictly, *onder* (below) leaves 1000 itself out. The wording does not say whether these bounds hold for the numbers in the operation, for the outcome, or for both. The goal links a situation and an operation in both directions; the wording does not mention working out the outcome.

### Common mistakes

The child makes up an adding story for a multiplication, such as 5 boxes and 30 more for 5 × 30. The child's division stories are always about fair sharing, never about how many groups fit. The child writes a division with the two numbers the wrong way round.
