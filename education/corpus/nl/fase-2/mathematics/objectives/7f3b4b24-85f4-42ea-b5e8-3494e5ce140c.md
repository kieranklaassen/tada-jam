## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering how a ratio table (*verhoudingstabel*) is built up and can be used in solving simple quantitative ratios (*verhoudingen*, also: proportions)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child fills a table of two rows for a situation such as 1 bag holds 4 oranges: 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 bags in the top row and 4, 8, 12, 20 and 40 oranges below. The child sees that each column holds a pair that belongs together, and that a step taken in the top row, such as doubling, is taken in the bottom row too. The child uses the table to find a pair that is not yet in it, such as 6 bags from the columns for 1 and 5. A child of 7 fills the table by repeated adding, column after column; a child of 10 goes to a column by doubling, halving or multiplying and explains why that is allowed.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discovering: how a ratio table is built up, and how it can be used. The ratios are "simple" (*eenvoudige*) and "quantitative" (*kwantitatieve*); the wording does not say what counts as simple. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child adds the same number in both rows where both must be multiplied: goes from 2 bags and 8 oranges to 5 bags and 11 oranges. The child fills each row as a separate number sequence and loses the link inside a column. The child mixes up the rows and puts the bags where the oranges belong.
