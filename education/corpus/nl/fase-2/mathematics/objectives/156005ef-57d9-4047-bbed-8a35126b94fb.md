## Official wording

onderzoeken dat tijd zowel een lineair als een cyclisch karakter heeft (lineair: de tijd gaat steeds door, we worden ouder, gebeurtenissen zijn steeds langer geleden of komen steeds dichterbij; cyclisch: het terugkerend ritme van uren en dagdelen in een etmaal, de dagen van de week en de maanden en seizoenen in een jaar)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating that time has both a linear and a cyclical character (linear: time always goes on, we grow older, events are ever longer ago or come ever closer; cyclical: the recurring rhythm of hours and parts of the day in a 24-hour day (*etmaal*), the days of the week and the months and seasons in a year)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays events from its own life on a line, for example its birth, its first day at school, today and its next birthday, and sees that the line goes on in one direction. The child lays the parts of the day, the days of the week or the months and seasons in a circle and sees that after the last one the first one comes again. The child says what comes back, such as Monday or summer, and what does not, such as being six years old. A child of 7 puts events of its own life in order and knows that days and seasons come back; a child of 10 explains the difference, for example that every year has a January but that each January is a year later than the one before.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets say what is meant. Linear: time always goes on, we grow older, events are ever longer ago or come ever closer. Cyclical: the recurring rhythm of the hours and parts of the day in an *etmaal* (a full day of 24 hours), of the days of the week, and of the months and seasons in a year. The verb is investigating. The goal names no dates, and this goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that it will one day be as old as an older brother or sister. The child takes a day or season that comes back for the same time coming back, and does not see that a year has passed. The child cannot say which of two events is longer ago when both are more than a short time ago.
