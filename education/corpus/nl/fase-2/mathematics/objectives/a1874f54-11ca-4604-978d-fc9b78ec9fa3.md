## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

designing figures with the same area (for example on grid paper)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays or draws several different figures that each have the same number of squares, for example with twelve square tiles or by colouring twelve grid squares each time, and checks by counting. The child puts the figures side by side and sees that the shapes differ while the area is the same. A child of 7 makes different shapes with the same number of whole tiles; a child of 10 also uses half squares and notices that the perimeters of the figures differ.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Grid paper is given as an example ("bijvoorbeeld"). The goal names no kind of figure, no size of area and no unit. The number twelve in the paragraph above is the pack's example.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that a long thin figure has a larger area than a compact figure of the same number of squares. The child makes figures with the same perimeter instead of the same area. The child counts a half square as a whole square.
