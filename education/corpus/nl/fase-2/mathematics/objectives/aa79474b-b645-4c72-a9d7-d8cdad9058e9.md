## Official wording

begrijpen wat 'oppervlakte' is (wat er op past) en onderzoeken en aanwijzen wat de oppervlakte van driedimensionale voorwerpen, platte objecten en afbeeldingen is

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

understanding what 'area' (*oppervlakte*, which also means surface) is (what fits on it) and investigating and pointing out what the area of three-dimensional objects, flat objects and pictures is

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child moves a hand over the top of a table, the cover of a book or a sheet of paper to show its area, and covers it with tiles or cards to see what fits on it. With a box the child points to every face as the surface that could be painted or wrapped. In a picture, for example a drawing of a field, the child colours the area. A child of 7 points out and covers flat surfaces; a child of 10 points out the whole surface of a solid, with the faces that cannot be seen, and keeps the area apart from the edge around it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets explain area as "what fits on it". The goal names three kinds of things of which the area is pointed out: three-dimensional objects, flat objects and pictures. The verbs are understanding, investigating and pointing out. The goal names no unit and no measure; comparing areas with a measure is the subject of `rw/m/2/02/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

The child points to the edge of a figure when asked for its area, and so shows the perimeter. With a solid the child points only to the top face, or takes the area for how much fits inside. The child thinks that only flat things such as a floor have an area.
