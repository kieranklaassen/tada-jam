## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the concept 'scale' and maps and plans (*plattegronden*, floor plans or street maps) drawn 'to scale'

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures a table or a room and draws it smaller, with each real length made smaller in the same way, for example one centimetre in the drawing for every metre in reality. The child compares a plan drawn to scale with the real place and checks that a wall that is twice as long in reality is also twice as long on the plan. A child of 7 finds that everything on such a plan is made smaller by the same amount; a child of 10 uses a simple stated scale to say about how long something is in reality.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about investigating the concept "scale" and maps and plans drawn "to scale". It states no scale, prints no notation for a scale and names no calculation. The scale in the paragraph above is the pack's example.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that a drawing to scale only has to be smaller, and makes some things much smaller than others. The child gives a length measured on the map as the real length. The child thinks that things that are small on the map are small in reality, without looking at the scale.
