## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a clock with hands and says the time, and reads the time from a clock with digits. The child turns one into the other: it sets the hands of a practice clock to a time shown in digits, or writes in digits the time that a clock with hands shows. A child of 7 says the time on a clock with hands and picks the card that shows the same time in digits; a child of 10 writes the time of a clock with hands in digits and sets the hands for a time that is given in digits.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two kinds of clock, digital and analogue, and two activities: reading off times, and converting the two time notations into each other, in both directions. It does not say how exactly a time is read (to the hour, to five minutes or to the minute). It does not say whether the digital times are in 12-hour or 24-hour notation. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child mixes up the hour hand and the minute hand. The child reads the numeral the minute hand points to as the number of minutes, for example 4 minutes when the hand stands at the 4. In Dutch a half hour is named after the hour that is coming (*half vier*, literally half four, is 3:30), and the child writes 4:30 for it.
