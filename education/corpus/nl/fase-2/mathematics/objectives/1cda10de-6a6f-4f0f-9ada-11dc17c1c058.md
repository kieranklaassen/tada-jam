## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

determining/calculating the area of a square or rectangle on grid paper and roughly determining that of an irregular figure, expressed in the natural measure of the squares on the grid paper

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child counts the grid squares inside a square or a rectangle drawn on grid paper, or counts one row and multiplies by the number of rows, and says the area as a number of squares. For an irregular figure, for example the outline of a hand, the child counts the whole squares, puts parts of squares together to make whole ones, and gives the answer with "about". A child of 7 counts the squares one by one and marks each counted square; a child of 10 multiplies rows by columns for a rectangle and has a fixed way of dealing with the parts of squares.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. For a square or a rectangle on grid paper the area is determined or calculated; for an irregular figure (*grillig figuur*, a figure with an uneven outline) it is only determined roughly. The unit is the square of the grid paper (*hokje*), which the goal calls a natural measure; the goal names no standard measure such as cm² and does not say how large the grid squares are. The source leaves out the words "the area of" before the irregular figure; the gloss supplies "that of". This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child counts every square that the outline touches as a whole square, or leaves out every part of a square, and so finds much too many or too few. The child counts the grid lines or grid points along a side instead of the squares. The child counts the squares along the edge and so gives a perimeter instead of the area.
