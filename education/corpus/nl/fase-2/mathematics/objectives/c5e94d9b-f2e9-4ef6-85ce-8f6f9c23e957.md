## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading off and interpreting data in simple tables, picture charts (*beelddiagrammen*, charts built from small pictures) and bar charts

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds a value in a table by following a row and a column to where they meet. In a bar chart the child follows the top of a bar to the scale and reads the number; in a picture chart the child counts the pictures and uses what one picture stands for. The child then says what the data mean: which is most, which is least, and what that says about the situation. A child of 7 reads charts in which one picture or one square stands for one thing; a child of 10 reads scales that go in steps and values that fall between two marks.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The word "simple" (*eenvoudige*) stands before the list of tables, picture charts and bar charts; the wording does not say what counts as simple. Two actions are named: reading off and interpreting. This goal states no number range and no step size for a scale. Legends are named in rw/verb/1/05/fase2.

### Common mistakes

The child counts the squares of a bar and takes each for 1 when the scale goes in steps of 2 or 5. The child counts each picture as 1 when a picture stands for more. The child reads the wrong row or column of a table.
