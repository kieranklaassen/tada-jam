## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child uses these words when it tells about its own day and week: what happened yesterday evening, what comes in a while, what took long and what was over quickly. The child puts events of a day in order and says which belong to the morning, the afternoon and the evening. The child says how units of time relate, for example that a week has seven days and that an hour has four quarters of an hour. A child of 7 uses the words for the parts of the day and for yesterday and tomorrow and learns the units; a child of 10 uses the relations between hour, day, week, month, season and year when it tells about or plans something.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The concepts are given as examples ("zoals"), in a list of two parts separated by a semicolon, and the goal ends with "the relations between them". The units in the list are hour, quarter of an hour, half an hour, year, season, month, week, *etmaal* (a full day of 24 hours) and day. The minute and the second are not in this list; `rw/m/6/05/fase2` names minutes and `rw/m/6/07/fase2` names minutes and seconds. The goal prints no relation as a number; the relations in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. The source prints *dag* (day) twice, at the start and near the end of the list; the gloss keeps both.

### Common mistakes

The child mixes up yesterday and tomorrow, or earlier and later. The child takes "day" only for the light part and does not see that a day of the week also holds the night. The child takes a quarter of an hour for 25 minutes, or half an hour for 50 minutes, by analogy with a quarter and a half of 100.
