## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

determining amounts on the basis of given or pictured coins and banknotes and in doing so also handily (*handig*, in a clever and convenient way) grouping and adding up

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a handful of coins and banknotes, or looks at a picture of them, and sorts them by value. The child puts together coins that make a round amount, for example coins that together make one euro, adds the banknotes first and then the coins, and says the total. A child of 7 sorts the coins and banknotes by value and counts on from the largest; a child of 10 first puts together coins that make a round amount and then adds the round amounts.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The coins and banknotes are given or pictured, and the goal adds handily grouping and adding up; the Dutch leaves open whether *handig* also belongs to adding up. This goal states no number range and does not say which coins and banknotes are used. It does not name the currency; `rw/m/7/04/fase2` names the euro sign.

### Common mistakes

The child counts how many coins there are instead of adding their values. The child judges the value of a coin by its size, for example takes the 5-cent coin for more than the 10-cent coin because it is larger. The child adds euros and cents as if they were the same unit, for example makes 52 of 2 euros and 50 cents.
