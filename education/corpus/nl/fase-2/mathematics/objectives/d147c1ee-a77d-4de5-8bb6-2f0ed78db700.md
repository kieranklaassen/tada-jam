## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring and using, in concrete *verdeelsituaties* (situations of sharing something out, or of dividing something up), the concepts 'half/a quarter of a whole' (*de helft/kwart van een geheel*) and 'half/a quarter of a quantity' (*de helft/kwart van een hoeveelheid*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shares one thing, such as a pancake or a strip of paper, fairly between 2 or 4 children and names each part: half of the pancake, a quarter of the strip. The child shares a quantity, such as 12 strawberries, between 2 or 4 and says "half of 12 is 6" and "a quarter of 12 is 3". The child says that a half is a different amount when the whole or the quantity is different. A child of 7 finds a half or a quarter by folding or by dealing out and names each part; a child of 10 says beforehand how much a half or a quarter of a quantity is and checks by sharing.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verbs are exploring and using, in concrete *verdeelsituaties*: the Dutch *verdelen* covers sharing something out among people and dividing something up into parts, and the wording does not choose. Two fractions are named, half and quarter, each of a whole and of a quantity. The goal states no number range for the quantities, and the wording does not mention fraction notation.

### Common mistakes

The child shares into parts that are not equal and still calls them halves or quarters. For a quarter of a quantity the child takes 4 objects, not one of four equal groups. The child thinks that half is always the same amount, whatever it is half of.
