## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

solving context problems (*contextproblemen*, problems set in a described situation) below at least 1000 and explaining the way of solving, where a combination of operations is involved

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears or reads a situation such as: a school buys 3 boxes of 24 pencils and hands out 20; how many are left. The child decides which calculations are needed and in which order, works them out with notes or a sketch, and checks the answer against the situation. The child then tells how the problem was solved and why each step was taken. A child of 7 plays the situation out with objects or a sketch and tells what was done; a child of 10 writes the calculations down and explains why they are done in that order.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "below at least 1000" (*onder ten minste 1000*): numbers below 1000 are the least that is offered, and no upper bound is set; read strictly, *onder* (below) leaves the bound itself out. The wording does not say whether the bound holds for the numbers given in the problem, for the outcome, or for both. Two things are named: solving, and explaining the way of solving. The problems involve a combination of operations; the wording does not say which operations or how many steps. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Jip orders two mugs of chocolate milk of 3 euros each and two pieces of apple pie of 2 euros and 50 cents each. How much does he have to pay?"; it is an example, not a limit. The data gives this record a code that ends in "fase3"; the level link in the data and the content-line document, which prints the goal in the fase 2 column, agree on fase 2.

### Common mistakes

The child does only the first step and gives its result as the answer. The child picks the operation from a single word in the story, such as "left", without picturing the situation. The child gives an answer and cannot say how it was found.
