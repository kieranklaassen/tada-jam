## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating the perimeter of 1) a square and 2) a rectangle shown on grid paper, where the lengths of the sides are given, and roughly determining that of 3) irregular figures

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a square or a rectangle on grid paper with the lengths of its sides written beside it, and adds the four sides to find the distance all the way round. For an irregular figure, for example the outline of a leaf, the child lays a string along the edge and measures the string, or counts along the grid lines nearest to the edge, and gives the answer with "about". A child of 7 goes round the figure and counts the unit lengths one by one; a child of 10 calculates the perimeter by adding the given lengths of the four sides.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording numbers three cases: 1) a square and 2) a rectangle, for which the perimeter is calculated, and 3) irregular figures (*grillige figuren*, figures with an uneven outline), for which it is only determined roughly. The square and rectangle have side lengths that are given. The wording leaves open whether "shown on grid paper" also belongs to the square, and whether the irregular figures are on grid paper. The goal names no unit for the side lengths and states no number range. It names no particular way of calculating.

### Common mistakes

The child adds only two sides, the length and the width, and leaves out the other two. The child counts the squares inside the figure and so gives the area instead of the perimeter. When counting round a figure on grid paper the child counts squares along the edge instead of unit lengths, and counts wrongly at the corners.
