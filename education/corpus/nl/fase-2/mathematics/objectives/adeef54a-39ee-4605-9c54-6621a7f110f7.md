## English gloss

knowing and recognising (*(her)kennen*), understanding and using mathematical language for dividing such as 'sharing out' (*verdelen*), 'dividing into groups' (*opdelen*), 'dividing by' (*delen door*), 'divided by' (*gedeeld door*), 'repeated subtracting' (*herhaald aftrekken*), 'remainder' (*rest*) and the formal notation .. : .. = ..

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shares 20 cards fairly among 4 children and says that each gets 5, and makes bags of 4 from 20 marbles and says how many bags there are. The child writes both as a division, in Dutch notation 20 : 4 = 5, and reads it as "20 divided by 4 is 5". With 22 marbles the child names the 2 that are left as the remainder. A child of 7 shares and groups with objects and says in everyday words what happened; a child of 10 uses the terms, the notation and the word remainder without objects.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The words and the notation after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples: 'sharing out', 'dividing into groups', 'dividing by', 'divided by', 'repeated subtracting', 'remainder', and the formal notation, which is printed with a colon as the division sign ( .. : .. = .. ). Three things are named: knowing and recognising the language, understanding it, and using it. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child knows dividing only as sharing out and does not see "how many groups of 4 are in 20" as a division. The child writes the numbers in the wrong order, 4 : 20. The child ignores what is left over, or shares it out unfairly, and does not name it as the remainder.
