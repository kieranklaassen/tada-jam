knowing and recognising (*(her)kennen*), understanding and using mathematical language for subtracting ('taking away/removing/off' (*weghalen/wegnemen/eraf*) and 'determining the difference' (*verschil bepalen*) and the formal notation: .. - .. = ..)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears a story, such as 120 stickers of which 45 are given away, says that stickers are taken away and writes 120 − 45 = 75. The child lays two rows of blocks side by side or compares two heights, says the difference, and writes that as a subtraction too. The child reads a written subtraction aloud and tells a taking-away story and a difference story for it. A child of 7 uses the words for taking away while acting a story out with objects; a child of 10 also names a comparison of two amounts as a subtraction and writes it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name two meanings of subtracting, taking away (*weghalen/wegnemen/eraf*) and determining the difference (*verschil bepalen*), and the formal notation with the minus sign and the equals sign. Three things are named: knowing and recognising the language, understanding it, and using it. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child knows subtracting only as taking away and writes no subtraction for "how many more". The child writes the numbers in the order in which they come in the story, also when the smaller number comes first. The child reads "difference" as any way in which two things are unlike, not as how many more one has.
