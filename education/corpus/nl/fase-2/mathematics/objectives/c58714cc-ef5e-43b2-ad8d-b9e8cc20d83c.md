## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and experiencing how heavy different weights are

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lifts things of known weight one after another, for example a weight of 100 grams, a pack of one kilogram and a bag of several kilograms, and feels the difference in its hand and arm. The child orders a few closed bags from light to heavy by feel and then checks the order on scales. A child of 7 feels and orders bags that differ clearly; a child of 10 estimates the weight of a thing in grams or kilograms by feel before weighing it and compares the estimate with what the scale shows.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about investigating and experiencing how heavy weights are. The Dutch *gewichten* (weights), like the English word, can mean the pieces of known weight that are put on a balance or the weights of things in general; the goal does not say which. Its own wording names no unit and no weighing instrument and states no number range. The content-line document prints text in italics with this goal: an example, how heavy is 1 kg, 5 kg, 10 kg; 1 gram, 10 grams, 100 grams, and, after the bracket that closes it, the words "and on the basis of this indicating what, for example, weighs more or less than 1 kg/100 grams/5 kg", closed by a second bracket. The document sets all of this in italics, as it sets its examples, and the record holds it as accompanying text and not as part of the goal; the weights in it are examples, not limits. The weights in the paragraph above are the pack's examples; 100 grams and 1 kilogram are also in the official example.

### Common mistakes

The child judges by size instead of by feel and puts the largest bag last. Of two things that weigh the same, the child says that the smaller one is heavier, because it feels heavier in the hand. The child estimates in a unit it has no feeling for yet, and gives a number of grams where kilograms fit.
