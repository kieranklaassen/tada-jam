bij het onderzoeken van behoefte aan maatverfijning de rol van breuken verkennen (half blokje, half blaadje bij afpassen met een natuurlijke maat)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

when investigating the need for refining a measure (*maatverfijning*, using a smaller unit or a part of the unit), exploring the role of fractions (half a block, half a *blaadje* (a small sheet of paper, or a small leaf) when measuring off (*afpassen*, laying a unit end to end) with a natural measure (*natuurlijke maat*, an everyday object or body part used as unit))

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures the length of a table by laying blocks end to end and finds that it is more than 6 blocks and less than 7. The child sees that whole blocks give no good answer and says "6 and a half blocks", or folds the measuring unit in two to measure the last part. The child talks about what to do when the measure does not fit exactly. A child of 7 measures off with blocks and says "and a half" when the part that is left is about half a block long; a child of 10 measures off with sheets of paper and folds a sheet in two to measure the part that is left.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring, and the setting is investigating the need for a finer measure. The brackets name half a block and half a *blaadje* when measuring off with a natural measure; the Dutch word *blaadje* can mean a small sheet of paper or a small leaf, and the gloss gives both. Half is the only fraction named. This goal states no number range. It names no particular unit: the brackets name measuring off with a natural measure, and *maatverfijning* (refining a measure) is a general term. The wording does not mention fraction notation.

### Common mistakes

The child ignores the part that is left and gives only the whole number of units. The child calls every part that is left "a half", whatever its size. The child leaves gaps or overlaps between the units, so the number of units is wrong before any half is looked at.
