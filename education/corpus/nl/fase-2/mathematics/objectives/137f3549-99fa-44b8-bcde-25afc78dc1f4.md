## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child cuts an apple or folds a sheet of paper into two equal parts and calls each part a half, folds again and names a quarter, and puts the parts back into a whole. The child takes the half of 12 counters and doubles 6 back to 12, and says "the half of 12 is 6" and "double 6 is 12". The child uses the words for things and for amounts: half a glass, a quarter of the cake, half of the group. A child of 7 halves by sharing counters into two equal groups and doubles by laying the same amount again; a child of 10 halves and doubles a number without counters and names a quarter as the half of a half.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Six concepts are listed: whole, half, quarter, the half, halving and doubling; the list is not introduced by "such as". Half and quarter are the only fractions named. The goal states no number range for halving and doubling. The wording does not mention fraction notation; rw/bew/6/01/fase2 names work with named fractions without fraction notation.

### Common mistakes

The child calls any two parts halves, also when they are unequal. The child takes a quarter to be one of any four pieces and does not see it as the half of a half. The child halves an odd number by leaving one out, and gives the half of 7 as 3.
