## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a price tag written the Dutch way, such as € 3,25, and says it aloud as three euros and twenty-five cents. The child writes down an amount that someone says, with the euro sign and with the cents after the comma. A child of 7 reads a price tag aloud and lays the amount with coins to see what the digits after the comma stand for; a child of 10 writes down an amount that is said aloud.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three activities: reading, saying aloud and writing down amounts of money. The notation it names has a euro sign and a comma: in Dutch notation the comma is the decimal sign, which separates the euros from the cents (€ 2,50 where English notation has €2.50). The goal's own wording states no number range and does not say how many digits stand after the comma. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: € 4,95 (€4.95 in English notation), with two digits after the comma; it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child writes an amount of cents below ten as one digit, for example € 3,5 for three euros and five cents, or reads € 3,05 as three euros fifty. The child reads € 0,50 as fifty euros, or leaves out the zero before the comma. The child leaves out the euro sign, so that the number does not show that it is money.
