## Official wording

inhouden meten en aflezen in liters en milliliters met behulp van een maatbeker

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

measuring and reading off capacities (*inhouden*, also volumes) in litres and millilitres with the help of a measuring jug

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child pours water from a bottle or a cup into a measuring jug, puts the jug on a flat table, brings its eyes to the height of the water and reads the mark at the surface in millilitres or litres. The child also measures out an amount that is asked for by filling the jug up to a mark. A child of 7 reads the jug when the water stands at a numbered mark; a child of 10 first works out what each small mark stands for and then reads between the numbered marks, on jugs with different scales.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two units, litres and millilitres, and one instrument, the measuring jug (*maatbeker*). It states no precision for the reading and no number range. It names no other measuring instrument.

### Common mistakes

The child reads the jug from above or while holding it at a slant, and so reads the wrong mark. The child takes each small mark for 1 ml or 10 ml without checking the scale. On a jug with several scales the child reads the wrong scale, for example the scale for grams of sugar.
