## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering whole numbers up to at least 1000

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes two number cards, such as 487 and 478, and says which is larger. The child puts five or six cards in order from smallest to largest and puts a new card in its place in the row. The child builds two numbers with material for hundreds, tens and ones when unsure. A child of 7 builds two numbers with material and says which is larger; a child of 10 puts number cards in order from smallest to largest.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The numbers are whole numbers. The wording does not mention the symbols for greater and less, and states no number of numbers to order. rw/gb/1/02/fase2 names comparing and ordering in the counting sequence and placing on a number line.

### Common mistakes

The child looks at the size of single digits and calls 189 larger than 201 because 8 and 9 are large digits. The child compares only the first digits and cannot decide between 487 and 478. The child compares a number of two digits with a number of three digits by the first digit and calls 95 larger than 123.
