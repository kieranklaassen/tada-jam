volgen, beschrijven en tekenen van routes (van A naar B) met behulp van een eenvoudige kaart of plattegrond en daarbij ruimtelijke begrippen gebruiken zoals rechts, vooruit, rechtdoor, links, rechts

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

following, describing and drawing routes (from A to B) with the help of a simple map or plan (*plattegrond*, a floor plan or street map) and in doing so using spatial terms such as right, forwards, straight on, left

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds A and B on a simple plan, for example of the school building or of a few streets, and walks the route or follows it with a finger, turning the plan so that it lies in the direction of travel. The child tells another person the way with words such as "straight on, then left", and draws on the plan a route that someone else describes. A child of 7 follows and describes a route in a place it knows; a child of 10 describes a route on a map of a place it does not know, so that another person can follow it without pointing.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three activities for routes from one point to another: following, describing and drawing, with a simple map or plan. The spatial terms are given as examples ("zoals"): right, forwards, straight on, left. The source prints *rechts* (right) twice in this list; the gloss gives it once. The wording names no particular spatial term beyond its examples. Of the map or plan the goal says only that it is simple: it names no particular feature, such as a grid of letters and numbers, and does not say whether distances are part of a description.

### Common mistakes

The child gives left and right from its own position instead of from the position of the person who travels, most often when the route on the map runs towards the child. The child leaves out a turn when describing a route, or counts the side streets wrongly. The child keeps the map in one position and loses the direction after the first turn.
