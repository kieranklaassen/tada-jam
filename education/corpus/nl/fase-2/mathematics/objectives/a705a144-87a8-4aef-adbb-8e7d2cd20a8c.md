## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating what 'weight' means (how much something weighs) and thinking up situations in which weight plays an (important) role

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lifts two objects, one in each hand, says which is heavier and checks on a balance. The child thinks of situations in which weight matters, for example weighing ingredients for baking, a parcel at the post office, a lift with a maximum load or two children on a seesaw. A child of 7 compares by feel and names situations from its own life; a child of 10 says why the weight matters in a situation and what happens if it is not known.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets explain weight as "how much something weighs". The word "important" stands in brackets. The goal names no unit and no weighing instrument; kilogram and gram are named in `rw/m/4/03/fase2` and `rw/m/4/05/fase2`. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child takes weight for size and expects the larger thing to weigh more. The child describes weight only by what it can lift itself, not as something of the object that can be measured. The child names situations that are about size or about number instead of weight.
