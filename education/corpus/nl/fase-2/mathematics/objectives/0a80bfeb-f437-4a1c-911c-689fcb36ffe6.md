## English gloss

understanding and putting into words why we need and use standard measures such as kilogram and gram for dealing with weight

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child weighs the same bag on a balance against marbles while another child weighs it against blocks, and the two find numbers that cannot be compared. Both then weigh the bag in grams and find the same number. The child says in its own words why a recipe or a shop needs a measure that is the same for everyone. A child of 7 says that "ten blocks" depends on which blocks are used; a child of 10 explains that a standard measure lets people who never meet pass on and compare weights.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Kilogram and gram are named as examples of standard measures (the Dutch *als* here means "such as"). The goal is about the reason for standard measures: understanding it and putting it into words. Weighing is the subject of `rw/m/4/04/fase2`, and the relation between kilogram and gram of `rw/m/4/05/fase2`. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child gives a weight as a number without a unit. The child sees no problem in a measure of its own, because its own results agree with each other. The child says that a standard measure is needed because it is more exact, and does not say that it is the same for everyone.
