## English gloss

thinking critically and reasoning about time in problem situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child thinks about a question on time and gives reasons: whether it can be somewhere on time if the bus leaves at a given time, which of two activities takes longer, whether a clock that runs five minutes fast makes a person early or late. The child checks with a clock, a calendar or a timetable. A child of 7 reasons about before and after and about longer and shorter, and checks on a clock; a child of 10 plans back from a fixed ending time and judges whether a stated time or duration can be right.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is "problem situations", without the word "simple" that the goals of this kind for money and for length and perimeter have (`rw/m/7/06/fase2`, `rw/m/1/06/fase2`). The goal's own wording names no situations, no units of time and no instrument, and states no number range. The content-line document prints an example with this goal, in which Tara says "I am now two years younger than Liam, but next week I am only 1 year younger than Liam" and the question is whether what Tara says can be right; it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child judges how long something took by how it felt, and says that a pleasant activity was shorter. When planning back from an ending time, the child adds the duration instead of taking it away. The child calculates past the whole hour as with ordinary numbers, with 100 minutes in an hour.
