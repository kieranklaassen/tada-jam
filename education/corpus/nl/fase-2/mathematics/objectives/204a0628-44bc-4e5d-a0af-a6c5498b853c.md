geordend weergeven van ongeordende gegevens (uit tellingen) in een voorgestructureerde beeld- of staafdiagram

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

showing unordered data (from counts) in an ordered way in a pre-structured picture chart (*beelddiagram*, a chart built from small pictures) or bar chart

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child has counted something, such as the colours of the cars that pass, and holds a list or tallies in no order. The child gets a chart that is already prepared and fills it: colours each bar up to the right height, or puts the right number of pictures in each column. The child checks each bar against the count. A child of 7 puts one picture or one coloured square for each thing counted; a child of 10 fills a chart in which a square or a picture stands for more than one.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The data come from counts, as the brackets say, and are unordered at the start. The chart is "pre-structured" (*voorgestructureerd*): a picture chart or a bar chart that is prepared for the child to fill in; the wording does not say which parts are prepared. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child puts bars or pictures in the order in which the data came, not per category. The child miscounts the tallies or skips one, so a bar is one too high or too low. The child starts a bar above the base line or leaves unequal gaps between the pictures, so the heights cannot be compared.
