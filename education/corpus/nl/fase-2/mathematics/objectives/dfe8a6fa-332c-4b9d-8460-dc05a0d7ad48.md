## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about multiplying and dividing below 1000 in meaningful problem situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gets a problem for which no way is given, such as: 120 chairs must stand in equal rows; which rows are possible. The child tries, checks, and says why 7 rows cannot work and 8 rows can. The child judges whether an outcome makes sense in the situation and finds the mistake in the worked answer of another child. A child of 7 tries with objects or a drawing and shows which answers fit; a child of 10 reasons from the numbers and gives an argument that others can follow.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "below 1000" (*onder 1000*), without "at least"; read strictly, *onder* (below) leaves 1000 itself out. The wording does not say whether the bound holds for the numbers in the calculation, for the outcome, or for both. The problem situations are "meaningful" (*betekenisvol*); the wording names no kind of problem and no procedure. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Think up numbers that you can divide by 2 and 4 and 6. Can you think up a rule for this?"; it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child multiplies or divides the numbers that are given without asking whether the operation fits the situation. The child accepts an outcome that cannot be right in size, such as 6 × 48 = 2448. When asked why, the child repeats the calculation and gives no reason.
