converting units of time into each other (days, hours, quarters of an hour, minutes, seconds; years, months, weeks, days)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a time in another unit: 2 hours as 120 minutes, three quarters of an hour as 45 minutes, 3 weeks as 21 days, 2 years as 24 months. The child uses a clock face or a calendar to check. A child of 7 knows that an hour has 60 minutes and a week 7 days and converts whole numbers of them; a child of 10 converts in both directions, also when the result is not a whole number of the larger unit, for example 100 minutes as 1 hour and 40 minutes.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name two series of units, separated by a semicolon: days, hours, quarters of an hour, minutes, seconds; and years, months, weeks, days. The goal prints no relations between the units, unlike the goal for length (`rw/m/1/03/fase2`), and states no number range; the conversions in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. A month has no fixed number of weeks or days, and a year no whole number of weeks; the goal does not say how such conversions are handled.

### Common mistakes

The child converts as if an hour had 100 minutes, for example takes one and a half hours for 150 minutes or 100 minutes for 1 hour. The child takes a quarter of an hour for 25 minutes. The child takes a month for exactly four weeks and so finds 48 weeks in a year.
