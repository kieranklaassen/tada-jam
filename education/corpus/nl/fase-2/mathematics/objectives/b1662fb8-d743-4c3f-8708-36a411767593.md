understanding and explaining why we need and use standard measures such as the litre for dealing with capacity (*inhoud*, which also means volume)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures how much a jug holds with its own cup, another child measures the same jug with a different cup, and they find different numbers. Both then measure with a measure of one litre and find the same. The child explains why a milk carton or a recipe states litres and not cups. A child of 7 says that the other child's cup is bigger; a child of 10 explains that a standard measure is the same everywhere, so that amounts can be written down, bought and compared by people who use different cups.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The litre is named as an example of a standard measure (the Dutch *als* here means "such as"). The goal is about the reason for standard measures: understanding it and explaining it. Measuring with a measuring jug is the subject of `rw/m/3/06/fase2`. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child gives a capacity as a number without a unit. The child thinks that any cup will do as long as the counting is careful. The child says that a standard measure is needed because it is more exact, and does not say that it is the same for everyone.
