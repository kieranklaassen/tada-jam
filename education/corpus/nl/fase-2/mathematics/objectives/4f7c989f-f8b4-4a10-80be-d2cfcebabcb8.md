## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the determining of a viewpoint (*standpuntbepaling*, working out the place someone looks from): what do you see from other viewpoints, what not

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a group of objects on a table from one side, then walks to another side or bends down, and says what it sees now and what is hidden. The child looks at photographs or drawings of the group and says from which side each was made, and says what a person on the opposite side sees. A child of 7 finds the answer by going to the other place and looking; a child of 10 reasons it out from its own place, for example by saying that a tall object hides what stands behind it for a person on that side.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal asks two things about other viewpoints: what you see from there, and what you do not see. It names no objects, no setting and no number of viewpoints. It does not use a term such as sight line.

### Common mistakes

The child assumes that a person in another place sees the same as the child sees. The child knows that the view from the opposite side differs but does not swap left and right for that viewer. The child says that a hidden object can be seen from a place, because the child itself knows that the object is there.
