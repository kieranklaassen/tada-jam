## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about qualitative and quantitative ratios (*verhoudingen*, also: proportions) in problem situations, both roughly and precisely (ratio table)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child judges situations such as: which lemonade tastes stronger, 2 cups of syrup with 6 of water or 3 cups with 12; and which costs less per piece, 3 for 2 euros or 6 for 5 euros. The child first reasons roughly, "the second has much more water", and then checks precisely with a ratio table. The child says why a drawing looks right or wrong in its proportions. A child of 7 reasons roughly with words such as too much, twice as much and as much as; a child of 10 makes two pairs comparable in a ratio table and gives the argument.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Both qualitative and quantitative ratios are named, and reasoning both roughly (*globaal*) and precisely (*precies*); the brackets after "precisely" name the ratio table. The goal is about thinking critically and reasoning in problem situations; the wording names no kind of problem. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child compares one number of each pair only: 3 cups of syrup is more than 2, so that lemonade is stronger. The child compares by the difference and not by the ratio: 6 − 2 against 12 − 3. The child gives a rough judgement and sees no need to check it.
