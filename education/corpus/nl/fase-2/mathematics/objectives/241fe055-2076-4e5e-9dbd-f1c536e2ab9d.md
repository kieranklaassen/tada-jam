## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding and subtracting up to at least 1000 in context situations and in formal mathematical language; in doing so using standard procedures with insight, such as the stringing strategy (*rijgstrategie*, adding or taking away the second number in steps); the splitting strategy (*splitsstrategie*, splitting both numbers by place value); column-wise calculating (*kolomsgewijs rekenen*) (in which the adding or subtracting of hundreds, tens and ones is set out one under the other) and the standard written algorithm (*cijferen*, calculating digit by digit)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works out 348 + 235 with jumps on an empty number line: from 348 a jump of 200, then of 30, then of 5. The child works out the same sum by splitting, 300 + 200, 40 + 30 and 8 + 5, and joins the parts, or writes the hundreds, tens and ones one under the other. The child says what each step or digit stands for, for example that the 4 in 348 is 40. A child of 7 draws each step of a calculation as a jump on an empty number line; a child of 10 works without the line and explains why the procedure gives the right outcome.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The wording does not say whether the bound holds for the numbers in the calculation, for the outcome, or for both. The work is in context situations and in formal mathematical language. The procedures after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples: the stringing strategy, the splitting strategy, column-wise calculating and the standard written algorithm; the brackets explain column-wise calculating. The content-line document prints an example inside the sentence for two of the procedures: for the stringing strategy, 64 − 27 is 64 − 20 = 44 and then 44 − 7 = 37; for the splitting strategy, 34 + 27 is 30 + 20 = 50 and 4 + 7 = 11 and 50 + 11 = 61. These are examples, not limits; both use numbers below 100, and the document prints no example for column-wise calculating or for the standard written algorithm. The procedures are used "with insight" (*met inzicht*). The wording does not say which procedure belongs to which year.

### Common mistakes

When splitting a subtraction the child takes the smaller digit from the larger in each place: for 52 − 38 works out 50 − 30 = 20 and 8 − 2 = 6 and answers 26. In column-wise or written work the child forgets what was carried or exchanged, or writes a two-digit result in one place. The child follows the steps of a written procedure correctly but cannot say what a digit is worth.
