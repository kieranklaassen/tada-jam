## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring and investigating axes of symmetry and mirror lines (*spiegelassen*, literally mirror axes)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child folds a cut-out shape so that the two halves cover each other, marks the fold and tries other folds to find more such lines. The child stands a small mirror upright on a figure and moves it until the half in front of the mirror and its image together look like the whole figure. A child of 7 finds a fold line in a cut-out shape such as a heart by folding; a child of 10 says without folding whether a line is an axis of symmetry, for example why the diagonal of a rectangle is not.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two terms, *symmetrieassen* (axes of symmetry) and *spiegelassen* (mirror lines), and does not say whether it means two different things by them. Both terms are used for the line that divides a figure into two halves that are each other's mirror image; *spiegelas* is also used for the line in which a separate figure is mirrored. The goal names no figures and no number of axes. It does not mention symmetry by turning (rotational symmetry).

### Common mistakes

The child takes every line that divides a figure into two equal halves for an axis of symmetry, for example the diagonal of a rectangle. The child finds one axis and stops, and so misses the others (a square has four). The child looks only for upright axes.
