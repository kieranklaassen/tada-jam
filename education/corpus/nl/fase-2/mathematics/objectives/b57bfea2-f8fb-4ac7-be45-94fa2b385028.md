## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures a table in hand spans or a room in steps and compares the count with that of another child, which differs because the hands and steps differ. Both then measure with a metre rule or a tape measure and find the same length. The child says in its own words why buying a plank or a piece of cloth needs metres and centimetres. A child of 7 says that the other child's steps are bigger; a child of 10 explains that a measure that is the same for everyone lets people pass on a length to someone who was not there.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Metre and centimetre are named as examples of measures (the Dutch *als* here means "such as"). The goal is about the reason for such measures: discovering it and putting it into words. Measuring with instruments is the subject of `rw/m/1/02/fase2`, and the relations between the measures of `rw/m/1/03/fase2`. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child gives a length as a number without a unit. The child sees no problem in measuring with its own steps, because its own counts agree with each other. The child expects a person with larger steps to count more steps for the same length.
