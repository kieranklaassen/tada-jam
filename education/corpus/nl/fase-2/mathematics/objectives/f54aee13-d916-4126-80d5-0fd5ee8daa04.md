## Official wording

delen tot ten minste 1000 in contextsituaties en formele wiskundetaal waarbij een doelmatige oplossingsmanier wordt gekozen op basis van eigenschappen van bewerkingen en de structuur van getallen zoals herhaald aftrekken, splits- of verdeelstrategie gebruiken, compenseren, rekenen naar analogie en werken met inverse relatie tussen vermenigvuldigen en delen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dividing up to at least 1000 in context situations and formal mathematical language, in which an efficient way of solving is chosen on the basis of properties of operations and the structure of numbers such as repeated subtracting, using the splitting or distributing strategy (*splits- of verdeelstrategie*, splitting the number and dividing each part), compensating (*compenseren*), calculating by analogy and working with the inverse relation between multiplying and dividing

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at the numbers before calculating and picks a short way. For 84 ÷ 4 the child splits 84 into 80 and 4 and joins 20 and 1; for 150 ÷ 25 the child takes 25 away again and again, or asks how many times 25 makes 150; for 240 ÷ 6 the child uses 24 ÷ 6. The child says why this way fits these numbers and checks by multiplying back. A child of 7 takes equal groups away with objects and counts the groups; a child of 10 looks at the numbers first, chooses a way and gives the reason for the choice.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The wording does not say whether the bound holds for the numbers in the calculation, for the outcome, or for both. The work is in context situations and in formal mathematical language. The ways after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples: repeated subtracting, the splitting or distributing strategy, compensating, calculating by analogy, and working with the inverse relation between multiplying and dividing. The content-line document prints an example inside the sentence for each of the five, with the colon as the Dutch division sign: for repeated subtracting, 72 : 6, how many times can 6 be taken out of 72; for the splitting or distributing strategy, 72 : 6 = 60 : 6 and 12 : 6; for compensating, if 100 : 5 = 20, then 95 : 5 = 19, that is, one group of 5 fewer; for calculating by analogy, if 100 : 4 = 25, then 1000 : 4 = 250; for the inverse relation, working out 72 : 8 by finding how many times 8 is 72, .. × 8 = 72. These are examples, not limits, and every division in them comes out evenly. The wording itself states the range, and the cluster it sits in is about whole numbers; it does not say whether the divisions come out evenly. Divisions that do not come out evenly are the subject of rw/bew/3/09/fase2.

### Common mistakes

The child splits the divisor and not the number that is divided: works out 84 ÷ 4 as 84 ÷ 2 and 84 ÷ 2 joined. In repeated subtracting the child loses count of how many times the number was taken away. In an analogy the child loses a zero and gives 240 ÷ 6 as 4.
