## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and reasoning about nets (*bouwplaten*, flat sheets that fold into a solid) of three-dimensional figures

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at several flat patterns and says which can be folded into a cube or a box and which cannot, then cuts them out and folds them to check. The child points to two faces in a net and says whether they will lie next to each other or opposite each other, and gives the reason. A child of 7 matches a net to a solid by folding it; a child of 10 decides without folding, by reasoning about which edges meet.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names recognising nets and reasoning about them. Its own wording names no particular three-dimensional figures. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: a cube, a cuboid (*balk*), a pyramid, as figures whose nets are meant; it is an example, not a limit. Investigating and designing nets is the subject of `rw/mk/2/02/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

The child accepts every arrangement of six squares as a net of a cube, for example six squares in one row or in a block of two by three. The child counts the faces and does not check whether two of them would land on the same place. The child loses track of the faces while folding in thought and decides by how the pattern looks.
