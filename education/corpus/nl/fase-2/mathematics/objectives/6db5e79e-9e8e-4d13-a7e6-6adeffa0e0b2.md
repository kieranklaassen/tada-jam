## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking up and learning some reference measures (*referentiematen*, familiar things of a known size) for the measures millimetre, decimetre and kilometre

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks for things of about these sizes and checks them by measuring: for example the thickness of the wire of a paper clip for a millimetre, a rod of ten centimetre cubes for a decimetre, and a known walk of about a quarter of an hour for a kilometre. A child of 7 learns an example that is given and checks it by measuring; a child of 10 thinks up examples of its own and checks them by measuring.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three units: millimetre, decimetre and kilometre. Its own wording does not name the metre or the centimetre; it asks for "some" reference measures and names none. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the width of a finger is about a cm; "how far is a km about? From here to ….", a sentence the document leaves unfinished; it is an example, not a limit. The example gives a reference measure for the centimetre, a unit the wording does not name, and none for the millimetre or the decimetre. The things in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child chooses an example whose size is far from the unit. The child mixes up the decimetre with the centimetre or the metre, because the decimetre is little used in daily life. The child has no feeling for a kilometre, which cannot be seen at once, and takes the length of a street or a playground for it.
