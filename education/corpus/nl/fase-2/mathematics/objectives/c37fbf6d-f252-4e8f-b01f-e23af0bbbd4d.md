## Official wording

lezen, uitspreken en schrijven van hele getallen tot ten minste 1000

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading, saying aloud and writing whole numbers up to at least 1000

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a number such as 364 from a card and says it, and writes a number that is said aloud. The child takes care with numbers that hold a zero, such as 406 and 460. In Dutch the ones are said before the tens (64 is *vierenzestig*, "four-and-sixty"), so the child hears the ones first and still writes the tens first. A child of 7 lays a number that is said aloud with material for hundreds, tens and ones and then writes it; a child of 10 writes the number straight from hearing it, also when it holds a zero.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. Three actions are named: reading, saying aloud and writing. The numbers are whole numbers. The wording does not say whether writing means digits, number words or both.

### Common mistakes

The child writes the digits in the order in which they are heard: 46 for *vierenzestig* (64), or 346 for 364. The child writes a number in the parts in which it is said: 30064 or 3064 for 364. The child leaves out the zero and writes 46 for 406.
