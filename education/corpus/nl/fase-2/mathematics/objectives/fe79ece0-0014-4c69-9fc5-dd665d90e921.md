## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

choosing suitable informal measures for measuring capacity (*inhoud*, which also means volume) and understanding and explaining that the number of times the measure fits indicates the capacity

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child chooses a thing to measure with that suits the container: a spoon for a small cup, a cup for a pan, a bucket for a paddling pool. The child fills the container with it, counts the times, and says for example that the pan holds eight cups. A child of 7 measures by counting full cups; a child of 10 chooses the measure beforehand, says "a little more than seven" when the last cup does not go in completely, and explains that a smaller measure gives a higher count for the same container.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The measures are informal (not standard measures) and have to suit what is measured; the goal's own wording names none. The capacity is given by the number of times the measure fits. The goal does not say what is done when the measure does not fit a whole number of times. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Six small cups of water go into this bottle."; it is an example, not a limit. The goal's own wording states no number range; the example uses the number six.

### Common mistakes

The child chooses a measure that is much too small or too large for the container, for example a spoon for a bucket. The child counts cups that are not full. The child thinks that the same container holds more when the count is higher because the measure was smaller.
