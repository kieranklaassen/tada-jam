## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

looking for and learning reference measures (*referentiematen*, familiar things of a known size) for the measures of *inhoud* (capacity, volume or contents) litre and pointing out or naming objects that have an *inhoud* of about a litre or a millilitre

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks among everyday containers for ones that hold about a litre, for example a carton of milk, and checks by pouring the contents into a measuring jug. For the millilitre the child looks at very small things, for example a hollow cube of one centimetre each way or the amount in a medicine syringe up to the mark for 1 ml. The child points at or names such objects when asked for something of about a litre or about a millilitre. A child of 7 learns the litre carton as a fixed example; a child of 10 looks among other containers for one that holds about a litre and learns it as an example too.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names reference measures for the litre, and objects with an *inhoud* of "about" a litre or a millilitre. The Dutch *inhoud* can mean capacity (how much can go in), volume, or contents (how much is in it), and the wording does not choose. The source prints the plural *inhoudsmaten* (measures of *inhoud*) and then names only the litre; the second half of the goal names both the litre and the millilitre. The goal's own wording names no objects. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: a milk carton holds 1 litre, and eye drops or nose drops are expressed in millilitres; it is an example, not a limit. The carton of milk in the paragraph above is also in the official example; the other things there are the pack's examples. The goal's own wording states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child takes a millilitre to be much more than it is, for example a spoonful or a cupful. The child takes every carton or bottle for a litre, whatever its size. The child judges the capacity of a container by its height.
