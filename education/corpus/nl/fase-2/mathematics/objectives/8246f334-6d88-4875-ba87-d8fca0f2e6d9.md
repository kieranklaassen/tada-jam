## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing that capacity (*inhoud*, which also means volume or contents) indicates 'how much can go in', thinking and talking about the concept 'capacity' and naming situations in which capacity is involved (blocks in a box, lemonade in a glass)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child fills a box with blocks and counts how many go in, and pours a drink into a glass until it is full. The child talks about what capacity means and names other things that have a capacity, for example a bucket, a bath or a school bag. A child of 7 names things that can be filled and compares two of them by filling; a child of 10 keeps apart how much is in a container now and how much can go in when it is full.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal describes capacity as "how much can go in". The brackets name two situations: blocks in a box and lemonade in a glass. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Does a colander also have a capacity?"; it is an example, not a limit. The goal names no unit and no measuring instrument; litre and millilitre are named in `rw/m/3/04/fase2`. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child judges capacity by height alone and says that a tall narrow glass holds more than a low wide one. The child takes what is in a container now for its capacity, and says that a large bottle that is half full has less capacity than a small full one. The child thinks that capacity is only about liquids.
