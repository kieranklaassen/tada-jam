## Official wording

vermenigvuldigen tot ten minste 1000 in contextsituaties en formele wiskundetaal waarbij een doelmatige oplossingsmanier wordt gekozen op basis van eigenschappen van bewerkingen en de structuur van getallen zoals herhaald optellen, verwisselen, compenseren, verdubbelen, halveren, verdubbelen en halveren en rekenen naar analogie

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

multiplying up to at least 1000 in context situations and formal mathematical language, in which an efficient way of solving is chosen on the basis of properties of operations and the structure of numbers such as repeated adding, swapping (*verwisselen*), compensating (*compenseren*), doubling, halving, doubling and halving and calculating by analogy

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at the numbers before calculating and picks a short way. For 9 × 15 the child works out 10 × 15 and takes 15 off; for 4 × 26 the child doubles 26 twice; for 16 × 5 the child halves 16 and doubles 5 to get 8 × 10; for 7 × 30 the child uses 7 × 3. The child says why this way is shorter for these numbers. A child of 7 works a product out by repeated adding and then by doubling, and says which was shorter; a child of 10 looks at the numbers first, chooses a way and gives the reason for the choice.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The wording does not say whether the bound holds for the numbers in the calculation, for the outcome, or for both. The work is in context situations and in formal mathematical language. The ways after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples: repeated adding, swapping, compensating, doubling, halving, doubling and halving together, and calculating by analogy. The content-line document prints an example inside the sentence for each of the seven: for repeated adding, 3 × 46 = 46 + 46 + 46; for swapping, 46 × 7 = 7 × 46; for compensating, 7 × 198 = 7 × 200 − 7 × 2; for doubling, 4 × 48 = 192, so 8 × 48 is double that; for halving, 10 × 63 = 360, so 5 × 63 is half of 360; for doubling and halving, 12 × 25 = 6 × 50 = 3 × 100; for calculating by analogy, 7 × 5 = 35; 7 × 50 = 350. These are examples, not limits. The document prints "10 x 63 = 360" in the halving example; 10 × 63 is 630, so the example as printed holds an error. The outcome of the compensating example, 1386, lies above 1000. What the goal names is the choice of an efficient way, made on the basis of properties of operations and the structure of numbers.

### Common mistakes

When compensating, the child takes off the wrong amount: for 9 × 15 works out 10 × 15 and takes off 1 or 9, not 15. When doubling and halving, the child doubles both numbers or halves both. The child uses repeated adding for every product, also for a long one.
