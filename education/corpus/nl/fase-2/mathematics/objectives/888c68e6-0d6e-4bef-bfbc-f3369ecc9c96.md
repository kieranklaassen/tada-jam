## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

maintaining the automatising (calculating fluently) of all divisions from the division tables (*deeltafels*, the divisions that match the multiplication tables) up to and including 10 and of multiplying and dividing with multiples of 10

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child answers 56 ÷ 7, 45 ÷ 9 and 24 ÷ 6 quickly by thinking of the multiplication that matches. The child works out 6 × 40, 30 × 5, 240 ÷ 6 and 350 ÷ 50 quickly from the table fact each resembles, and says what happens with the zero. The facts come back in short rounds over a long time. A child of 7 finds a division by trying multiplications or by sharing objects; a child of 10 gives the table divisions fluently and works with multiples of 10 from the table fact.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is maintaining (*onderhouden*): keeping up what is already known. Two sets are named: all divisions from the division tables "up to and including 10" (*tot en met 10*), so the division table of 10 is included; and multiplying and dividing with multiples of 10 (*veelvouden van 10*), for which the goal's own wording states no number range. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: 6 × 80; 20 × 40; 50 : 5; 500 : 5; 500 : 50 (the colon is the Dutch division sign); it is an example, not a limit. The largest number printed in it is 500, and its largest outcome is 800, for 20 × 40. The level named is automatising, which the goal itself explains as calculating fluently; rw/bew/3/04/fase2 names memorising for the multiplications. Divisions that do not come out evenly are the subject of rw/bew/3/09/fase2.

### Common mistakes

With multiples of 10 the child leaves out or adds a zero: gives 6 × 40 as 24, or 30 × 50 as 150. The child does not link a division to the multiplication it comes from and starts sharing one by one. The child gives the divisor as the answer, 9 for 45 ÷ 9.
