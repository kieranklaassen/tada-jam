## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and recognising in reality geometric figures such as cube, cuboid (*balk*, the shape of a rectangular box), sphere, cylinder

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks for things that have the shape of a cube, a cuboid, a sphere or a cylinder, for example a die, a shoebox, a ball and a tin. The child picks them up, turns them, feels the faces and edges and tries whether they roll or can be stacked. A child of 7 says which shape an object has and sorts objects by shape; a child of 10 describes a shape by its faces, edges and corners and points out the shapes in a larger object such as a building.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names four figures as examples (the Dutch *als* here means "such as"): cube, cuboid, sphere and cylinder. All four are three-dimensional figures. They are investigated and recognised "in reality", in real things. The cone and the pyramid are not among the four examples. The goal states no number of faces, edges or corners.

### Common mistakes

The child uses the name of a flat shape for the solid: calls a cube a square or a sphere a circle. The child does not recognise a cylinder or a cuboid when its proportions are unusual, for example a coin or a long beam, or when it lies on its side. The child counts only the faces that can be seen from one side.
