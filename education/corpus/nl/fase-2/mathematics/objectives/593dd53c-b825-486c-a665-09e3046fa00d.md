## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking and reasoning about the phenomenon that something that is bigger does not also have to be heavier, and thinking up examples of this

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a large light thing and a small heavy thing, for example a cushion and a stone, says beforehand which is heavier and checks on a balance. The child then thinks up pairs of its own and says why the smaller thing is heavier: what each thing is made of, and whether it is hollow or solid. A child of 7 is surprised by such pairs and collects examples; a child of 10 explains that weight depends on the material as well as on the size.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states the phenomenon in one direction: something bigger does not also have to be heavier. The child thinks up examples; the goal gives none. It names no unit and no weighing instrument, and it does not use a term such as density.

### Common mistakes

The child predicts from size alone and keeps to the prediction after lifting both things. The child makes a new rule of it, that small things are always heavier. The child gives an example in which the bigger thing is also the heavier one.
