## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring joining together or determining the difference when dealing with named fractions (*benoemde breuken*, fractions said of a thing or a measure) without fraction notation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts half a cardboard pizza and a quarter of one on a plate and says how much pizza there is. The child pours half a litre and then another half litre into a jug and says "a whole litre", or lays half an apple beside a quarter of an apple and says how much more the half is. The child says and hears these amounts in words and does not write them as fractions. A child of 7 joins two halves into a whole; a child of 10 joins or compares halves and quarters of the same thing and says the result in words such as "three quarters".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring; the wording names no method and no level of fluency. The fractions are named fractions, said of a thing or a measure, and the work is without fraction notation, so no written form such as 1/2 is part of this goal. Two actions are named: joining together, and determining the difference. The goal's own wording states no number range and names no denominators; rw/gb/5/01/fase2 lists whole, half and quarter as concepts of this band. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "how many half loaves can you get out of a whole loaf? Can you get five half loaves out of two whole loaves?"; it is an example, not a limit. The half is the only fraction in the example; the quarter in the paragraph above is the pack's choice, taken from rw/gb/5/01/fase2.

### Common mistakes

The child counts the pieces and not their size, and says that a half and a quarter make "two". The child joins parts of different wholes, such as half of a small apple and half of a large one, and calls the result one whole. The child does not see that a half is the same as two quarters, so cannot say how much a half and a quarter differ.
