## English gloss

making the connection between reality and a drawn map of this reality

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands in a place it knows, for example a playground, with a drawn map of that place, points to a thing in the surroundings and then to its place on the map. The child also does the reverse: it takes a mark on the map and finds the real thing it stands for. The child turns the map until it lies the same way as the surroundings and marks where it stands itself. A child of 7 matches large fixed things such as a door or a climbing frame; a child of 10 also says from the map what lies between two things, which of them lie further apart, and what the map leaves out.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names one thing: connecting reality with a drawn map of that same reality. It names no kind of place, no size of area and no kind of map. Scale and legends are the subjects of `rw/mk/1/04/fase2` and `rw/mk/1/05/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

The child does not turn the map to match the surroundings, so that left and right on the map do not agree with what it sees. The child expects the map to look like the view from where it stands instead of the view from above. The child expects everything that is there in reality to be drawn on the map.
