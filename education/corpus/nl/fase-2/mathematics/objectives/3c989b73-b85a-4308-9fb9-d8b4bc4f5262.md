## Official wording

vergelijken en ordenen van kommagetallen in de context van geld (duurder/goedkoper; meer/minder)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster rw/gb/4; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 3c989b73-b85a-4308-9fb9-d8b4bc4f5262.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering decimal numbers (*kommagetallen*, numbers written with a decimal comma) in the context of money (more expensive/cheaper; more/less)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts price tags in order from the cheapest to the most expensive and says which of two things costs more. The child compares the euros first and then the cents, and checks with coins when unsure. The child uses the words more expensive, cheaper, more and less. A child of 7 compares prices by laying the amounts with coins; a child of 10 orders written prices such as €4.05, €4.50 and €4.15 by the euros first and then the cents.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The decimal numbers are compared and ordered in the context of money; the wording names no other context. The brackets name the word pairs more expensive/cheaper and more/less. The goal states no range for the size of the amounts, no number of decimal places and no number of amounts to order. The wording does not mention the symbols for greater and less.

### Common mistakes

The child compares the digits after the decimal mark as whole numbers and calls €4.5 less than €4.15. The child looks only at the cents and calls €3.90 more than €4.10. The child takes the amount with more digits for the larger amount.
