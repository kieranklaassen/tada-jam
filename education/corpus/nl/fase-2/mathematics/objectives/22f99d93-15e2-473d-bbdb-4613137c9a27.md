optellen en aftrekken van decimale getallen met twee decimalen in de context van geld

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding and subtracting decimal numbers with two decimal places in the context of money

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child adds the prices of two things, such as €2.75 and €1.50, with coins or by writing the amounts, and says the total. The child works out the change from an amount paid, or how much more one thing costs than another. The child keeps euros and cents apart and exchanges 100 cents for 1 euro when the cents pass 100. A child of 7 lays the amounts with coins and counts the euros and the cents; a child of 10 writes the amounts one under the other and adds or subtracts them.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The numbers are decimal numbers with two decimal places, and only in the context of money (*geld*). The wording names no currency; the amounts in euros and cents in these notes are the pack's examples. The goal states no range for the size of the amounts. It names no other context and no other number of decimal places. In Dutch the amounts are written with a decimal comma (€ 2,75). The data gives this record a code that ends in "fase1"; the level link in the data and the content-line document, which prints the goal in the fase 2 column, agree on fase 2.

### Common mistakes

The child adds the cents without exchanging 100 cents for a euro, and writes €2.75 + €1.50 as €3.125. The child reads €3.5 as 3 euros and 5 cents, not as 3 euros and 50 cents, and lines the amounts up wrongly. When subtracting, the child takes the smaller number of cents from the larger whichever amount it belongs to, and gives €5.20 − €1.50 as €4.30.
