## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child plays shop: one child pays with a banknote or coin that is worth more than the price, and the other works out the money to give back. The child checks that the price and the change together make the amount paid. A child of 7 counts on from the price to the amount paid while laying down coins; a child of 10 works the change out in writing or in the head, by counting on or by subtracting.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is three words long in Dutch and says only: calculating change. Its own wording states no number range, names no way of calculating (counting on, or subtracting) and does not say which coins and banknotes are used. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "It costs €28.95 (in Dutch notation € 28,95). How much do you get back if you pay with a banknote of 50 euros?"; it is an example, not a limit, and it uses a price in euros and cents and a banknote of 50 euros.

### Common mistakes

The child subtracts the euros and the cents separately and takes the smaller from the larger each time, for example pays 5 euros for something of 3 euros 75 and answers 2 euros 75. The child gives the price or the amount paid as the answer instead of the difference. The child does not check whether the change is less than the amount paid.
