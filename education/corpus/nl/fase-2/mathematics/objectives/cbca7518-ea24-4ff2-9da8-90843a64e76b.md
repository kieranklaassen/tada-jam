## Official wording

herkennen, verwoorden en voortzetten van eenvoudige verbanden en patronen in rijen getallen en (geometrische) figuren; zelf ontwerpen van patronen in getallenreeksen en (geometrische) figuren

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising, putting into words and continuing simple relations (*verbanden*) and patterns in rows of numbers and (geometric) figures; designing by oneself patterns in number sequences and (geometric) figures

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a row such as 3, 7, 11, 15, says the rule, "4 more each time", and writes the next numbers. The child looks at a row of figures, such as square, triangle, triangle, square, triangle, triangle, or a row of dot figures that grows, says what repeats or grows, and lays or draws the next ones. The child makes a pattern of their own with numbers or shapes for another child to continue. A child of 7 continues repeating patterns and number rows with a fixed step; a child of 10 describes patterns that grow and predicts a later term without drawing every step.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. This goal states no number range. The relations and patterns are "simple" (*eenvoudige*); the wording does not say what counts as simple. They are in rows of numbers and in figures, with "geometric" printed in brackets. Two parts are named: recognising, putting into words and continuing; and designing patterns oneself.

### Common mistakes

The child looks only at the last two terms and misses the rule of the whole row. In a repeating pattern the child goes on with a part that looks alike, not with the unit that repeats. The child can continue a pattern but cannot say its rule in words.
