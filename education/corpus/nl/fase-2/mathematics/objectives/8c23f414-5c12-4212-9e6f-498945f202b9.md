## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

measuring with measuring instruments for length that suit the context, such as a ruler or blackboard ruler (*(bord)liniaal*), a retractable tape measure (*rolmaat*), a measuring tape (*meetlint*), a soft sewing tape measure (*huishoudcentimeter*), and expressing the length to the nearest metre, decimetre, centimetre and/or millimetre

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child chooses the instrument that suits the thing to be measured: a ruler for a pencil, a retractable tape measure for a room, a soft tape measure round a head or a waist, a long measuring tape for a playground. The child puts the zero mark at one end, keeps the instrument straight or snug, reads the mark at the other end and says the length with its unit. The child gives the length as exactly as the situation asks, for example a playground to the metre and a pencil to the millimetre. A child of 7 measures with an instrument that is handed to it and says the length with its unit; a child of 10 chooses the instrument and the precision itself.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The instruments are given as examples ("zoals"): a ruler or blackboard ruler, a retractable tape measure, a measuring tape and a soft household tape measure; they have to suit the context. Four precisions are named, joined by "and/or": to the nearest metre, decimetre, centimetre and millimetre. The kilometre is not one of the four; it is named in `rw/m/1/03/fase2` and `rw/m/1/04/fase2`. This goal states no number range. The examples in the paragraph above of which precision suits which thing are the pack's.

### Common mistakes

The child starts at the end of the ruler or at the mark for 1 instead of at the zero mark. The child counts the marks instead of the spaces between them and finds one too many. The child lets the tape sag or lie at a slant, or moves a short ruler along and leaves gaps or overlaps.
