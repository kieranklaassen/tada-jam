## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

rounding off numbers up to at least 1000 to tens or hundreds

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds on a number line between which two tens a number lies, such as 347 between 340 and 350, and says which of the two is nearer. The child rounds 347 to a ten, 350, and to a hundred, 300. The child rounds a price or a count of things to say about how much or how many it is. A child of 7 looks on a number line for the nearer ten or hundred; a child of 10 rounds without a number line.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The rounding is to tens or to hundreds; the wording names no other rounding. It states no rule for a number that lies exactly in the middle, such as 350 rounded to hundreds.

### Common mistakes

The child always rounds down by cutting off the last digit, or always rounds up. Rounding to hundreds, the child rounds in two steps and gets a wrong result: 347 to 350 and then to 400. The child changes only the last digit and rounds 398 to tens as 390.
