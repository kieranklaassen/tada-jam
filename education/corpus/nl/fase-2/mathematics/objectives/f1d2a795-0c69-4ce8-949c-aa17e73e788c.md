## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating what 'disproportions' (*wanverhoudingen*, proportions that are wrong or distorted) are and in doing so thinking up or designing examples (distorting mirrors (*lachspiegels*), cuddly toys, caricatures)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a cuddly toy or a caricature and says what is out of proportion: the head is as large as the body, the eyes are far too big for the face. The child stands before a distorting mirror, or stretches a picture in one direction only, and says what changed and what stayed the same. The child draws or builds an example of their own, such as a person whose arms are as long as the legs. A child of 7 points at what looks wrong and says too big or too small; a child of 10 compares parts with each other and with the whole, and designs a disproportion on purpose.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is investigating, together with thinking up or designing examples. The brackets name distorting mirrors, cuddly toys and caricatures. This goal states no number range, and the wording does not mention calculating.

### Common mistakes

The child calls anything large or small "out of proportion", without comparing a part with the whole or with another part. The child thinks an enlargement in which everything grows equally is also a disproportion. The child sees that something looks wrong but cannot say which two parts do not fit each other.
