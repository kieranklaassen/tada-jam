de relaties ontdekken en verwoorden tussen de verschillende bewerkingen en deze gebruiken bij het rekenen (het gaat om relaties tussen optellen, aftrekken en splitsen, tussen (herhaald) optellen en vermenigvuldigen, tussen vermenigvuldigen en delen en tussen (herhaald) aftrekken en delen)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering and putting into words the relations between the different operations and using these when calculating (this concerns relations between adding, subtracting and splitting (*splitsen*, breaking a number into parts), between (repeated) adding and multiplying, between multiplying and dividing and between (repeated) subtracting and dividing)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out 4 rows of 6 counters and says it both as 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 and as 4 times 6. The child takes 6 counters away from 24 again and again, says that this is 24 divided by 6, and says that it belongs with 4 times 6. The child splits 15 into 8 and 7, reads from it 8 + 7 = 15 and 15 − 8 = 7, and uses a fact that is known to find one that is not. A child of 7 shows the link between repeated adding and a multiplication with counters; a child of 10 says the links in words and uses a multiplication to work out a division.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets say which relations are meant: between adding, subtracting and splitting; between adding (also repeated) and multiplying; between multiplying and dividing; and between subtracting (also repeated) and dividing. Three things are named: discovering the relations, putting them into words, and using them when calculating. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child treats each operation as a separate set of facts and works out 24 ÷ 6 from the start although 4 × 6 = 24 was just found. The child swaps the numbers of a subtraction or a division, as can be done in an addition or a multiplication. In a repeated subtraction the child counts the starting number as a step and gets an outcome that is one too high.
