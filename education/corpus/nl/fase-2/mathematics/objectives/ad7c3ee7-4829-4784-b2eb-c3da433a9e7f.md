## Official wording

omgaan met de telrij tot ten minste 1000, waaronder doortellen en terugtellen met sprongen, buurgetallen noemen van elk willekeurig getal

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with the counting sequence (*telrij*) up to at least 1000, including counting on and counting back in jumps, naming the neighbouring numbers (*buurgetallen*, the numbers just before and after) of any given number

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child counts on aloud from a given number, such as 385, and counts back from 702. The child counts in jumps of 10 from 46 or in jumps of 100 from 250, forwards and backwards, and goes on past each hundred. For any number that is named the child says the number before it and the number after it. A child of 7 makes the jumps on a number line and reads the neighbouring numbers from it; a child of 10 counts on and back in jumps and names the neighbouring numbers without a number line.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. After "including" (*waaronder*) the wording names counting on and counting back in jumps, and naming the neighbouring numbers of any number; "including" does not close the list. The wording does not state the size of the jumps. The Dutch leaves open whether "in jumps" (*met sprongen*) belongs to counting back alone or also to counting on.

### Common mistakes

The child goes wrong where a hundred is passed: counting back from 400 says 390, not 399. In jumps of 10 from a number such as 46 the child moves to the round tens and says 46, 56, 60, 70. The child names the number after a given number but hesitates on the number before it, most of all for a round number such as 500.
