## Official wording

onderhouden van het memoriseren (uit het hoofd kennen) van alle vermenigvuldigingen uit de tafels tot en met 10

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster rw/bew/3; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau d5ea4967-4ae9-4d17-ba3d-84ad6babf60f.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

maintaining the memorising (knowing by heart) of all multiplications from the tables up to and including 10

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child answers table facts such as 7 × 8, 6 × 9 and 4 × 7 at once and out of order, aloud or in writing. The child also names the fact that belongs to a product: "56 is 7 times 8". The facts come back in short rounds over a long time, because the goal is about keeping this knowledge up. A child of 7 says a fact and checks it from the fact that comes before it in the table, such as 6 × 8 from 5 × 8; a child of 10 gives the fact from memory, also when the facts are asked out of order.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is maintaining (*onderhouden*): keeping up what is already known. The range is "the tables up to and including 10" (*de tafels tot en met 10*), so the table of 10 is included, and "all" (*alle*) covers every multiplication in those tables. The wording does not say with which table the tables start or how far each table runs. The level named is memorising, which the goal itself explains as knowing by heart. The division tables are named in rw/bew/3/05/fase2.

### Common mistakes

The child recites a table from its start to reach one fact and loses count on the way. The child mixes up facts with products that lie close together, and gives 7 × 8 as 54 or 6 × 9 as 56. The child knows 3 × 8 but does not use it for 8 × 3.
