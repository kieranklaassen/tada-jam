## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding and subtracting up to at least 1000 in context situations and in formal mathematical language, in which an efficient way of solving is chosen on the basis of properties of operations and the structure of numbers, such as swapping (*verwisselen*) (in adding); compensating (*compenseren*); calculating by analogy; transforming (*omvormen*, changing both numbers so the outcome stays the same); filling up/determining the difference (*aanvullen/verschil bepalen*) and inverse relation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at the numbers before calculating and picks a short way. For 4 + 297 the child swaps to 297 + 4; for 298 + 145 the child adds 300 and takes 2 off; for 503 − 498 the child fills up from 498 to 503 and says 5; for 46 + 29 the child changes the sum to 45 + 30. The child says why this way is shorter for these numbers. A child of 7 works a subtraction out by compensating and then by filling up, and says which was shorter; a child of 10 looks at the numbers first, chooses a way and gives the reason for the choice.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The wording does not say whether the bound holds for the numbers in the calculation, for the outcome, or for both. The work is in context situations and in formal mathematical language. The ways after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples: swapping, which the text ties to adding; compensating; calculating by analogy; transforming; filling up or determining the difference; and the inverse relation. The content-line document prints an example inside the sentence for each of the six: for swapping, 99 + 236 = 236 + 99; for compensating, 367 − 99, where 99 is made into 100 and 367 − 100 = 267, after which the 1 too many that was taken off is added back, 267 + 1 = 268; for calculating by analogy, 40 + 50 = 90, so 400 + 500 = 900; for transforming, 367 − 99, where adding one to both numbers leaves the answer the same, so that it becomes 368 − 100 and the answer is 268; for filling up or determining the difference, 500 − 348, worked out by determining how much lies between 348 and 500; for the inverse relation, 500 − 348 = 152 and 152 + 348 = 500. These are examples, not limits. What the goal names is the choice of an efficient way, made on the basis of properties of operations and the structure of numbers.

### Common mistakes

When compensating, the child corrects in the wrong direction: for 145 − 98 takes 100 off and then takes 2 more off. The child swaps the numbers of a subtraction as if it were an addition. The child uses one fixed procedure for every sum, also for 503 − 498.
