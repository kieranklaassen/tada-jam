## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing (properties of) the geometric figures circle, rectangle, triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon, polygon and discovering the differences

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts cut-out shapes next to each other, counts their sides and corners, lays one shape on another to compare the lengths of sides and the corners, and says how the shapes differ. The child sorts a pile of shapes by a property, for example all shapes with four corners. A child of 7 tells the shapes apart by the number of sides and corners; a child of 10 also compares properties such as sides of equal length, and says what a square has that other rectangles do not have.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names seven figures, in a list without "such as": circle, rectangle, triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon and polygon. All seven are flat figures; polygon (*veelhoek*) is the general word for a figure with several corners. The words "properties of" stand in brackets, and the goal does not say which properties are compared. It states no number of figures and no size.

### Common mistakes

The child does not recognise a shape that is turned or has unusual proportions, for example a square that stands on a corner or a long thin triangle. The child treats the square and the rectangle as unrelated shapes, or calls every figure with four sides a square. The child counts a side or a corner twice when going round a figure with many sides.
