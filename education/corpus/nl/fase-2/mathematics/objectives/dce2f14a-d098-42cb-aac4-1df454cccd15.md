## Official wording

vergelijken en ordenen van verschillende oppervlaktes met een natuurlijke maat en uitleggen in eigen woorden waarom het gebruiken van één maat noodzakelijk is

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering different areas with a natural measure (*natuurlijke maat*, an everyday thing used as a unit) and explaining in their own words why using one measure is necessary

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child covers each of several surfaces, for example a table top, a mat and a book, with the same thing, such as sheets of paper or playing cards, counts how many fit and puts the surfaces in order of the count. The child also tries large sheets on one surface and small cards on another, finds that these counts cannot be compared and says why. A child of 7 covers and counts and learns to leave no gaps; a child of 10 chooses a measure that suits the surfaces, deals with places that are only partly covered and explains why one measure is needed.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The areas are compared and ordered with a natural measure; the goal names none. The child explains "in own words" why one measure has to be used. No goal of this cluster (rw/m/2) in this band names a standard measure for area such as cm² or m². This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child leaves gaps between the things it covers with, or lets them overlap. The child compares the counts of two different measures as if they were counts of the same measure. The child judges an area by its length alone and calls the longest surface the largest.
