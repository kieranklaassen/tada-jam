## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

changing amounts (*wisselen*, swapping money for the same value in other coins or banknotes) (e.g. 4 banknotes of 5 for 1 banknote of 20) and finding out which possibilities for changing there are and reasoning about this

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child swaps one banknote or coin for several of lower value that together are worth the same, for example a 10-euro note for five 2-euro coins, and does the reverse with a pile of small coins. The child looks for the ways to change one amount, for example a 20-euro note into two notes of 10, into four notes of 5 or into one note of 10 and two of 5, lays them side by side and checks each by adding up. The child says why a way is or is not possible and how it knows whether a way was missed. A child of 7 changes a coin or a banknote for coins of lower value and checks by adding up; a child of 10 lists the ways to change an amount in a fixed order and says how it knows that none was missed.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three things are named: changing amounts, finding out which possibilities for changing there are, and reasoning about this. The statement prints one example in brackets, after "bijv.": 4 banknotes of 5 for 1 banknote of 20; it is an example, not a limit, and it prints the two values without a currency sign. The goal does not explain *wisselen* further; calculating change after paying (*wisselgeld*) is the subject of `rw/m/7/05/fase2`. This goal states no number range. It does not say which amounts, coins or banknotes are changed, or whether every possibility has to be found. The open data stops before the example; the record holds the whole statement as the content-line document prints it.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks it has more money after changing one banknote for many coins, or less money after trading many coins for one. The child gives back a number of coins or banknotes that looks right without adding up their values. When looking for the possibilities, the child stops after one or two, or counts the same set of coins and banknotes twice in another order.
