## Official wording

onderzoeken en uitleggen dat de oppervlakte van een figuur niet verandert door omvormen (zoals een figuur verdelen in losse elementen of losse elementen van een figuur combineren tot een nieuw figuur)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and explaining that the area of a figure does not change through reshaping (such as dividing a figure into separate elements or combining separate elements of a figure into a new figure)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child cuts a paper rectangle into pieces and lays all the pieces, without overlap, into a new figure. The child says that the area is the same because nothing was added and nothing taken away, and checks by counting grid squares or by covering both figures with the same tiles. A child of 7 finds this by counting squares before and after; a child of 10 explains it without counting and uses it to find the area of an awkward figure by rearranging it into a rectangle.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal says that the area of a figure does not change through reshaping. Two ways of reshaping are given as examples ("zoals"): dividing a figure into separate elements, and combining separate elements of a figure into a new figure. The goal names no figures and no unit. It does not mention the perimeter.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that the new figure has a larger area because it is longer or looks bigger. The child thinks that the perimeter also stays the same. The child lets pieces overlap or leaves a piece out and concludes that the area did change.
