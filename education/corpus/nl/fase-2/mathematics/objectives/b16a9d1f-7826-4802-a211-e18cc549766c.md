## Official wording

de standaardmaten liter en de daarvan afgeleide maat milliliter kennen en weten dat 1 liter = 1000 ml

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing the standard measure litre and the measure derived from it, the millilitre, and knowing that 1 litre = 1000 ml

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child handles a carton or bottle of one litre and a medicine syringe or spoon marked in millilitres, and names the unit marked on each. The child pours a litre of water into a measuring jug and sees the water stand at the mark for 1000 ml. A child of 7 points to the mark for 1000 ml on a measuring jug and says that this is 1 litre; a child of 10 says without a jug that 1 litre is 1000 ml.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two units, litre and millilitre, and one relation: 1 litre = 1000 ml. The source prints the plural *standaardmaten* (standard measures) although it names one standard measure, the litre; the gloss gives the singular. No goal of this cluster (rw/m/3) in this band names the decilitre, the centilitre or a cubic measure such as cm³ or dm³. This goal states no number range beyond the relation.

### Common mistakes

The child takes 1 litre to be 100 millilitres, by analogy with 1 metre and 100 centimetres. The child thinks that 1000 ml is more than 1 litre because the number is larger. The child mixes up the abbreviations ml and mm.
