## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

solving simple quantitative ratio problems with the help of a pre-structured ratio model (recipes, prices) and in doing so using handy ways of calculating such as doubling, halving, multiplying, dividing

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gets a recipe for 4 people and a prepared table to fill, and works out the amounts for 8 or for 2 people by doubling or halving every ingredient. With prices, the child knows that 3 pens cost 6 euros and finds the price of 6 pens, of 1 pen and then of 5 pens. The child says which step was taken in the model. A child of 7 doubles or halves whole amounts in a prepared table; a child of 10 first finds the amount for 1, or joins two columns, to reach amounts such as 5 or 6 from 4.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The problems are "simple" (*eenvoudige*) and "quantitative" (*kwantitatieve*). A "pre-structured ratio model" (*voorgestructureerd verhoudingsmodel*) is used: a model that is prepared for the child; the wording does not say which model. The brackets name recipes and prices. The ways of calculating after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples: doubling, halving, multiplying, dividing. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child changes one amount and not the others: doubles the flour and leaves the eggs. The child adds a fixed number where multiplying is needed: for 2 people more, adds 2 to every ingredient. The child halves an odd amount and drops the half, and gives the half of 3 eggs as 1.
