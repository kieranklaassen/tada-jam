thinking critically and reasoning about area (*oppervlakte*, which also means surface) in problem situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child thinks about a question on area and gives reasons: which of two pieces of paper of different shape is larger, how many tiles cover a floor, whether a sheet of wrapping paper is enough for a box. The child says beforehand what it expects and checks by covering, by cutting and rearranging or by counting grid squares. A child of 7 compares by laying one surface on the other and by covering with tiles; a child of 10 reasons with rows and columns and keeps area and perimeter apart in its reasoning.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is "problem situations", without the word "simple" that the goals of this kind for money and for length and perimeter have (`rw/m/7/06/fase2`, `rw/m/1/06/fase2`). The goal's own wording names no situations and no unit. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Do two surfaces with an equally large area always look the same?"; it is an example, not a limit. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child judges an area by its length alone. The child thinks that two figures with the same perimeter have the same area. The child mixes up area and perimeter in a question, for example gives the amount of fence when asked for the amount of grass.
