## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating nets (*bouwplaten*, flat sheets that fold into a solid) of three-dimensional figures and designing nets oneself for three-dimensional objects

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child cuts a cardboard box open along its edges, lays it flat and folds it up again to see which faces meet. The child draws a net for a cube or a box, cuts it out, folds it and sees whether it closes without a gap and without two faces on top of each other. A child of 7 folds nets that are ready and unfolds boxes; a child of 10 designs a net for a given object and says before folding which faces will end up opposite each other.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two activities: investigating nets of three-dimensional figures, and designing nets for three-dimensional objects. Its own wording names no particular figure or object, no number of faces and no material. The content-line document prints an example inside the sentence at each of the two activities: at the figures whose nets are investigated, a cube, a cuboid (*balk*), a pyramid; at the objects for which nets are designed, a small box or a die. These are examples, not limits. Recognising nets and reasoning about them is the subject of `rw/mk/3/04/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

The child draws a net with a face missing or with a face too many, most often five squares for a closed cube. The child places two faces so that they fall on top of each other when the net is folded. The child thinks that a solid has only one correct net.
