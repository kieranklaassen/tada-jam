## English gloss

thinking critically and reasoning about weight in problem situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child thinks about a question on weight and gives reasons: whether a few parcels together stay under a maximum load, which is heavier of a kilogram of feathers and a kilogram of iron, whether a stated weight for a thing can be right. The child weighs, or estimates with things whose weight it knows, to check. A child of 7 reasons with heavier and lighter and checks on a balance; a child of 10 reasons with kilograms and grams and checks a claim by estimating.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is "problem situations", without the word "simple" that the goals of this kind for money and for length and perimeter have (`rw/m/7/06/fase2`, `rw/m/1/06/fase2`). The goal's own wording names no situations, no unit and no weighing instrument, and states no number range. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Name some examples in which you can better measure in grams than in kilograms, and explain why."; it is an example, not a limit, and it uses the units gram and kilogram. The content-line document prints the goal with one more word at its start, "kan kritisch denken en redeneren over gewicht in probleemsituaties" (*kan*, can); the record keeps the wording of the open data.

### Common mistakes

The child says that a kilogram of iron is heavier than a kilogram of feathers. The child compares two numbers that have different units, for example says that 800 grams is more than 1 kilogram. The child accepts a weight that cannot be right without questioning it.
