## Official wording

onderzoeken wat de meerwaarde kan zijn van het ordenen en schematiseren van losse gegevens in een tabel, staaf- of beelddiagram en vanuit een tabel in een staafdiagram

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating what the added value can be of ordering and schematising loose data in a table, bar chart or picture chart (*beelddiagram*, a chart built from small pictures) and from a table into a bar chart

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child has loose data, such as a pile of slips with one favourite fruit on each. The child first tries to answer "which fruit is chosen most" from the pile, then puts the data in a table with tallies, or builds a picture chart or a bar chart, and answers again. The child says what became easier to see, and what a bar chart made from the table shows faster than the table. A child of 7 sorts the slips into rows and sees the longest row; a child of 10 compares the table and the bar chart and says which suits which question.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is investigating, and what is investigated is the added value of ordering and schematising loose data. Three forms are named: a table, a bar chart and a picture chart; and one step from one form to another: from a table into a bar chart. This goal states no number range. The wording names no line graph and no pie chart.

### Common mistakes

The child treats making the chart as a drawing task and does not use it to answer a question. The child copies the data into the table in the order found, without putting the same answers together. The child thinks the chart holds other information than the loose data it was made from.
