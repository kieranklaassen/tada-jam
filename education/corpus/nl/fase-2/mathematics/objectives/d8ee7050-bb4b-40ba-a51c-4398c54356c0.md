## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about adding and subtracting up to at least 1000 in problem situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gets a problem for which no way is given, such as: two numbers add up to 500 and differ by 100; which numbers are they. The child tries, checks, and says why an answer can or cannot be right, for example that 380 + 250 cannot be 530 because 300 + 200 is 500 and 80 + 50 is more than 100. The child judges the worked answer of another child and finds the step that is wrong. A child of 7 tries with material and shows what was found; a child of 10 reasons from the numbers and gives an argument that others can follow.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The goal is about thinking critically and reasoning in problem situations; the wording names no kind of problem and no procedure. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Think up a way to add up the numbers 1 up to and including 1000 in a handy way"; it is an example, not a limit. In that example the numbers that are added go up to 1000, and their sum, 500,500, lies far above 1000.

### Common mistakes

The child gives an answer without checking whether it can be right for the situation. The child accepts an answer because a calculation was done, without asking whether the right operation was chosen. When asked why, the child repeats the calculation and gives no reason.
