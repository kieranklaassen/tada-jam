## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

understanding and explaining why choosing and agreeing on one fixed measure is a condition for fairly measuring, comparing and ordering capacities (*inhouden*, also volumes), and applying this knowledge in simple practical situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds out how much two jugs hold by filling them with cups, and sees that a large cup for one jug and a small cup for the other give numbers that cannot be compared. The child chooses one cup, agrees with the others to fill it to the rim each time, measures every container with it and puts the containers in order of the number of cups. A child of 7 sees that the comparison is unfair when both cups stand side by side and measures again with one cup; a child of 10 explains beforehand why one measure is needed and uses this when dividing a drink fairly.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal speaks of "one fixed measure" that is chosen and agreed on; it does not say that this is a standard measure. Three uses are named: measuring, comparing and ordering capacities. The knowledge is applied in "simple practical situations", which the goal does not name. The litre is named in `rw/m/3/04/fase2` and `rw/m/3/05/fase2`. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child compares the counts of two different measures as if they were counts of the same measure. The child does not fill the measure equally each time and counts a cup that is half full as a whole cup.
