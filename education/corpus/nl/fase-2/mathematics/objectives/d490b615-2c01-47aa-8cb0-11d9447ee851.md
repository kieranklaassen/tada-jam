## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child thinks about a question on money and gives reasons: whether the money in its hand is enough for two things together, which of two offers costs less for each piece, whether a pile with more coins is worth more. The child lays out coins or writes amounts down to support its reasoning, and checks a claim that someone else makes. A child of 7 lays coins out to reason about having enough and about more coins against more value; a child of 10 compares offers and checks by estimating whether an answer can be right.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The problem situations are "simple", and the subject stands in quotation marks: "money and value". The goal's own wording does not explain that subject, names no situations and states no number range. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Can you pay €4.95 (in Dutch notation € 4,95) with 6 banknotes/coins? What sorts of things can you buy for 100 euros and what certainly not?"; it is an example, not a limit, and the amounts in it are €4.95 and 100 euros.

### Common mistakes

The child judges by the number of coins or banknotes instead of by their value. The child compares only the prices of two offers and not how much each offer gives for the price. The child gives an answer without checking whether it can be right, for example change that is more than the amount paid.
