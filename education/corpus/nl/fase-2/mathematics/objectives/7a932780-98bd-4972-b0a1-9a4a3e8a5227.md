## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring the meaning, way of writing and pronunciation of decimal numbers (*kommagetallen*, numbers written with a decimal comma) in the context of amounts of money

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a price tag such as €3.45, says "three euros forty-five", and lays the amount with coins: 3 euros and 45 cents. The child writes an amount that is laid out or said aloud, and finds that the digits after the decimal mark are the cents. The child compares what is written on a tag with what people say. A child of 7 reads a price such as €2.50 aloud and lays it with coins; a child of 10 writes an amount that is said aloud and says what each digit after the decimal mark stands for.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. The decimal numbers are met in the context of amounts of money; the wording names no other context. Three aspects are named: meaning, way of writing and pronunciation. The goal states no range for the size of the amounts and no number of decimal places; rw/bew/2/01/fase1, a record of this band, names two decimal places in the context of money. In Dutch the amounts are written with a decimal comma (€ 3,45), and *kommagetal* means "comma number".

### Common mistakes

The child reads the digits after the decimal mark as a separate whole number and writes 3 euros and 5 cents as €3.5. The child takes €3.50 and €3.05 for the same amount. The child does not see the part after the mark as a part of one euro.
