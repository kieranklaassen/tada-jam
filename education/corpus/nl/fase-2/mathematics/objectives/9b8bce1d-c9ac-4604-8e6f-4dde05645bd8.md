## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience in meaningful situations with qualitative and quantitative ratios (*verhoudingen*, also: proportions) and describing these ratios in one's own words

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child mixes paint or lemonade, lays a table, builds with blocks or holds a toy beside the real thing, and says how the amounts or sizes go together. The child says it in their own words: "this chair is much too big for the doll", "one cup of syrup for every five cups of water", "each child gets two". The child notices when a relation stays the same while the amounts grow. A child of 7 uses words such as too big for, as many as and twice as much; a child of 10 says "1 for every 4" and tells when two situations have the same relation.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: gaining experience with qualitative and quantitative ratios in meaningful situations, and describing these ratios in the child's own words. The wording names no formal term and no notation for a ratio. The situations are "meaningful" (*betekenisvolle*); the wording names none. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "For 2 weeks you need twice as much as for one week"; it is an example, not a limit. The goal's own wording states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child describes a ratio as a difference: "there is 4 more water than syrup". The child names only one of the two amounts: "there is a lot of water". The child sees a relation in one situation and does not recognise the same relation with other amounts.
