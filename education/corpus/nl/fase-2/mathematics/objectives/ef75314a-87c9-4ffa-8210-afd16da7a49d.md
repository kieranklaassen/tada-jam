## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about whole numbers up to at least 1000 in problem situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gets a question for which no way is given, such as: which numbers can be made with the digits 3, 5 and 8, which is the largest, and which lies nearest to 500. The child tries, puts the numbers found in order, and says why none was missed. The child judges a claim such as "the number with the largest first digit is the largest" and gives a case for or against it. A child of 7 tries with digit cards and shows what was found; a child of 10 works in a fixed order and gives an argument that others can follow.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The numbers are whole numbers. The goal is about thinking critically and reasoning in problem situations; the wording names no kind of problem.

### Common mistakes

The child stops at the first answer found and does not look for others or check that all are found. The child gives one case as proof that something is always true. When asked why, the child says "I just see it" and gives no reason.
