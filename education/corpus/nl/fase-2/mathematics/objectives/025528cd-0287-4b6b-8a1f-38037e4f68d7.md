## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating properties of and relations between numbers up to at least 1000: even and odd, multiples, the role of 'zero' in numbers, sequences

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts numbers into even and odd, by pairing counters or by looking at the last digit, and tries out what happens when two odd numbers are added. The child colours the multiples of 3 or of 25 on a number chart and looks for what they share. The child compares 46, 406 and 460 and says what the zero does, and continues a sequence such as 150, 175, 200 and says its rule. A child of 7 sorts numbers into even and odd by pairing counters; a child of 10 gives a reason for what is found, for example why two odd numbers added together give an even number.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. After the colon the wording lists four subjects: even and odd, multiples, the role of 'zero' in numbers, and sequences; it does not say whether the list is complete. The verb is investigating. The wording singles out no property or relation beyond these four; divisors and prime numbers, for example, are not among them.

### Common mistakes

The child judges even or odd by the first digit or by all the digits, and calls 350 odd because of the 3 and the 5. The child takes zero for "nothing" in every place and treats 406 as 46. The child takes the rule of a sequence from its first step alone and does not check it against the other steps.
