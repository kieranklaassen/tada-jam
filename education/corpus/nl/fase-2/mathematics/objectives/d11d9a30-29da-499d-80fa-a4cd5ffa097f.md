## Official wording

onderhouden van het memoriseren (uit het hoofd kennen) van de splitsingen van getallen tot en met 10 en van de optellingen en aftrekkingen onder de tien en over het eerste tiental

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

maintaining the memorising (knowing by heart) of the splits (*splitsingen*, the ways to break a number into parts) of numbers up to and including 10 and of the additions and subtractions below ten and across the first ten (*over het eerste tiental*, where the calculation passes 10)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says at once, without counting, that 7 splits into 3 and 4 or into 5 and 2, and names the missing part when one part of 10 is given. The child answers 3 + 5, 9 − 4, 8 + 5 and 13 − 6 from memory, aloud or in writing. The facts come back in short rounds over a long time, because the goal is about keeping this knowledge up. A child of 7 says a fact from memory and checks it with counters; a child of 10 says a fact from memory and names the subtraction that belongs to an addition, such as 13 − 5 with 8 + 5.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is maintaining (*onderhouden*): keeping up what is already known. Three sets of facts are named: the splits of numbers "up to and including 10" (*tot en met 10*), so 10 is included; additions and subtractions "below ten" (*onder de tien*); and additions and subtractions "across the first ten" (*over het eerste tiental*), where the calculation passes 10, as in 8 + 5 or 13 − 6. The wording states no upper number for the facts across the first ten, and does not say where a fact with the outcome 10 exactly, such as 6 + 4, belongs. The level named is memorising, which the goal itself explains as knowing by heart.

### Common mistakes

The child still counts on by ones and gets an answer that is one off. The child knows an addition but not the subtraction that matches it: knows 8 + 5 = 13 and does not use it for 13 − 5. The child knows the facts when they are asked one by one but does not use them inside a larger calculation such as 48 + 5.
