## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with internal and external structures of numbers up to at least 1000 (internal structures: 1000 is 500 and 500; 1000 is 4 x 250; external structures: 998 lies very close to 1000; 489 lies closer to 500 than to 400)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a number apart in several ways: 1000 as 500 and 500 or as 4 times 250, and 360 as 300 and 60 or as 2 times 180. The child says where a number lies among other numbers, that 998 is very close to 1000 and that 489 is closer to 500 than to 400, and shows it on a number line. The child uses both when calculating, for example by treating 998 as 1000 minus 2. A child of 7 shows a split of a number with material and points to the place of the number on a number line; a child of 10 says without material how a number can be made and which round number it lies near, and uses this when calculating.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The brackets name, for internal structures, 1000 is 500 and 500 and 1000 is 4 × 250, and for external structures, 998 lies very close to 1000 and 489 lies closer to 500 than to 400. Three of these four use the number 1000 itself, and none uses a number above 1000. The wording does not define the two terms apart from these cases.

### Common mistakes

The child knows only the split by place value, 489 as 400, 80 and 9, and no other split. The child judges nearness by the first digit and says that 489 is closer to 400 because it starts with a 4. The child mixes up "and" and "times" in a split, and says that 1000 is 4 and 250.
