## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

working out divisions that do not come out evenly (*niet opgaande delingen*), determining the 'remainder' and interpreting the 'remainder' correctly within context situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shares 26 marbles among 4 children, gives each child 6, and names the 2 that are left as the remainder. In a situation the child decides what the remainder means: 26 children in cars of 4 need 7 cars, and 26 eggs fill 6 boxes of 4 with 2 eggs over. The child gives the answer in the words of the situation. A child of 7 shares objects and says how many are left; a child of 10 works out the division with numbers alone and decides from the situation what to do with the remainder.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording states no number range. Three parts are named: working out divisions that do not come out evenly, determining the remainder, and interpreting the remainder correctly within context situations. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: dividing 72 into groups of 5, that is 14, but then 2 are left over; it is an example, not a limit. For what is left over the wording names the remainder ('rest') and no other way of writing the outcome.

### Common mistakes

The child gives the remainder no meaning and answers "6 remainder 2 cars". The child always drops the remainder, or always rounds up, whatever the situation asks. The child leaves a remainder that is as large as the divisor or larger, such as 26 ÷ 4 = 5 remainder 6.
