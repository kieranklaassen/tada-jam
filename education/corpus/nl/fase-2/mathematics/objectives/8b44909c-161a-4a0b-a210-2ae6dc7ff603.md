using concepts around the calendar such as names (and order) of the days of the week, the numbers of the weeks, names (and order) of the months and the relations between them

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says the days of the week and the months of the year in order, starting from any of them, and says which comes before and which after. The child finds today on a calendar, reads the number of the week, and says in which month a birthday falls and which month follows it. A child of 7 knows the days in order and learns the months; a child of 10 goes forwards and backwards through the days and months past the end of the week or the year and uses the numbers of the weeks.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The concepts are given as examples ("zoals"): the names of the days of the week, the numbers of the weeks, the names of the months, and the relations between them. The words "and order" stand in brackets both times. The source prints a comma after *zoals*, which the gloss leaves out. Dates and the number of days in a month are not among the examples. Reading off a calendar and calculating with periods on it are the subject of `rw/m/6/06/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

The child can say the days or the months only from the start and cannot say what comes before a given one. The child does not go on past the end, for example does not know what follows December. The child thinks that every month has exactly four weeks.
