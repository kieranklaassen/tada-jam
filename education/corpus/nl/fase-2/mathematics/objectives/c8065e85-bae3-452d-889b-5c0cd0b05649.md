## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about capacity (*inhoud*, which also means volume) in problem situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child thinks about a question on capacity and gives reasons: whether the juice of one carton fits into a number of glasses, which of two vases of different shape holds more, how many cups can be filled from a jug. The child says beforehand what it expects and checks by pouring. A child of 7 predicts and then checks by pouring with cups; a child of 10 reasons with litres and millilitres before pouring and judges whether a stated capacity can be right.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is "problem situations", without the word "simple" that the goals of this kind for money and for length and perimeter have (`rw/m/7/06/fase2`, `rw/m/1/06/fase2`). The goal's own wording names no situations, no unit and no measuring instrument, and states no number range. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "How can we find out how much water you use in 1 minute of showering? Do you think that is a lot?"; it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child decides which container holds more by height alone. The child thinks that there is more or less liquid after it is poured into a container of another shape. The child compares two numbers that have different units, for example says that 750 ml is more than 1 litre.
