## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating the duration (in hours and minutes) between two points in time and working out at what time an event with a certain duration ends if it began at a given time

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a starting time and an ending time, for example from a timetable, and works out how long lies between them, for example by going first to the next whole hour and then on. With a starting time and a duration, for example a film of an hour and forty minutes, the child works out when it ends. A child of 7 turns the hands of a practice clock from the starting time to the ending time and counts on; a child of 10 calculates with the written times.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two calculations: the duration between two points in time, and the ending time of an event from its starting time and its duration. The brackets name the units of the duration: hours and minutes. The goal does not name working out a starting time from an ending time and a duration. It does not say whether the times lie on the same day, and it does not name seconds. The goal's own wording states no number range. The content-line document prints an example with this goal, in two parts: "The film starts at 15.15 and has ended at 17.00. How long does the film last?" and "The swimming lesson starts at 7.15 and lasts 45 minutes. At what time has the swimming lesson ended?"; it is an example, not a limit. The example writes the times with a point and the word *uur* (15.15 uur, 17.00 uur, 7.15 uur), and the times of the film are in 24-hour notation.

### Common mistakes

The child calculates with times as with ordinary numbers, with 100 minutes in an hour: from a quarter to three until a quarter past three it finds 70 minutes. When adding a duration the child writes a number of minutes above 59, for example 14:70. The child counts the starting hour as well when it counts on in hours, and finds one hour too many.
