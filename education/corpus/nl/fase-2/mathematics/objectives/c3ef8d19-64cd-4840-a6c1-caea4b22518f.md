## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the structure of the counting sequence (*telrij*) up to at least 1000 and reasoning about it

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a hundred chart, a number line or a long strip of numbers and says what repeats: after every 9 in the ones a new ten comes, after 99 a new hundred, and every hundred has the same order of tens inside it. The child predicts which number lies 10 or 100 further and checks it. The child explains why 400 comes straight after 399. A child of 7 points out on a number chart what repeats; a child of 10 explains without the chart how the next number can always be found.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The verbs are investigating and reasoning. The wording names no parts of the structure and no material. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "You can never remember all those number words by heart. How do you know the next number each time?"; it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child can say the numbers in order from memory but cannot say what changes at a ten or at a hundred. The child thinks the pattern of the tens ends at 100 and does not expect it to return between 100 and 200.
