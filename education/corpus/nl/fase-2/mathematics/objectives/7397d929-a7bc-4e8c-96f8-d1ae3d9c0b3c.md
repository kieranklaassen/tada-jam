## Official wording

hoeveelheden tot ten minste 1000 vergelijken en ordenen en aangeven of de hoeveelheden veel of weinig van elkaar verschillen in aantal

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering quantities up to at least 1000 and indicating whether the quantities differ much or little from each other in number

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child compares quantities, such as boxes with 250, 275 and 800 paper clips, and puts them in order. The child says whether two quantities differ by a little or by a lot: 250 and 275 are close, 250 and 800 are far apart. The child groups the objects in tens and hundreds, or uses the numbers that were counted, to decide. A child of 7 compares quantities by laying them out in groups side by side; a child of 10 compares them by the numbers that were counted and says whether they differ much or little.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "up to at least 1000" (*tot ten minste 1000*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 1000 itself is included. The difference is given as much or little; the wording does not mention the difference as a number. The goal is about quantities; rw/gb/3/03/fase2 names comparing and ordering whole numbers. The wording states no number of quantities to order.

### Common mistakes

The child judges by the space a quantity takes up and not by its number. The child calls a difference large because the numbers are large, as with 998 and 1000. When ordering several quantities the child compares each only with its neighbour and does not check the whole order.
