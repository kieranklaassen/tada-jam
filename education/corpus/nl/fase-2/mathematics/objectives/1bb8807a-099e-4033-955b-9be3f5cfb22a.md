## English gloss

explaining the difference between digits (*cijfers*) and numbers (*getallen*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says that there are ten digits, 0 to 9, and that every number is written with them. The child points at 364 and says "this is one number, made of three digits", and makes different numbers from the same digit cards, such as 46 and 64. The child says that 7 is a digit in 73 and is also a number when it stands alone. A child of 7 tells the two apart by pointing and by counting the digits of a number; a child of 10 explains the difference in words and uses both words correctly.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. This goal states no number range. What is named is explaining the difference; the wording gives no examples and no definition of either word.

### Common mistakes

The child calls every number a digit (*cijfer*), as everyday Dutch often does. The child thinks that a number of one digit, such as 7, is a digit only and not a number. The child does not count 0 as a digit.
