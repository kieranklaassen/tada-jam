## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

in simple meaningful situations calculating with combinations of quantities such as the price per kg, per metre, per litre and the speed in kilometres per hour

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a price per kilogram and works out what two kilograms or half a kilogram cost, for example by doubling and halving or with a table of matching amounts. In the same way the child works out the cost of three metres of ribbon from the price per metre, and how far a cyclist gets in two hours at a speed in kilometres per hour. A child of 7 finds the price of a number of kilograms by adding the price per kilogram again and again; a child of 10 doubles and halves in a table of matching amounts and explains each step.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The combinations of quantities are given as examples ("zoals"): the price per kg, per metre and per litre, and the speed in kilometres per hour. These four are the only combined quantities the goal names. The situations are "simple" and "meaningful"; the goal names none. This goal states no number range. It is the only goal of its cluster (rw/m/8) in this band.

### Common mistakes

The child takes the price per kilogram for the price of the thing, whatever it weighs. The child adds the two numbers, the amount and the price, instead of multiplying. With speed the child mixes up the distance and the time, or thinks that a higher speed means a longer time for the same distance.
