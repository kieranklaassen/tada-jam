## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

multiplying by estimation below at least 1000 in context situations and formal mathematical language and explaining why this estimate may be made

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child rounds before multiplying: for 4 × 198 the child says "about 4 × 200, so about 800", and for 6 boxes of 48 "about 6 × 50". The child says why the rounding is allowed here: the number lies close to a round number, and the situation asks only for about how many. The child says whether the exact outcome lies a little under or a little over the estimate. A child of 7 rounds the number in a situation and multiplies the round number; a child of 10 also explains why the rounding may be done for this question.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The range is stated as "below at least 1000" (*onder ten minste 1000*): numbers below 1000 are the least that is offered, and no upper bound is set; read strictly, *onder* (below) leaves the bound itself out. The wording does not say whether the bound holds for the numbers in the calculation, for the outcome, or for both. The work is in context situations and in formal mathematical language. Two parts are named: multiplying by estimation, and explaining why this estimate may be made. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: 8 × 39 is about 8 × 40; 19 × 18 is about 20 × 20; it is an example, not a limit. In its second part both numbers have two digits and both are rounded. Only multiplying is named; the wording does not mention dividing by estimation.

### Common mistakes

The child first calculates exactly and then rounds the answer. The child rounds so far that the estimate no longer helps, such as 4 × 149 as 4 × 100. The child gives "it is easier" as the only reason and cannot say whether the estimate is good enough for the question.
