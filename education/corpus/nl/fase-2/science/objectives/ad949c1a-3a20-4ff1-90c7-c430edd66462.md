## Official wording

waarnemen en op basis van uiterlijke kenmerken vergelijken van (delen van) verschillende planten (bijv. vorm, kleur, bouw, geur, bloeiwijze) en vergelijken van (delen van) verschillende dieren (bijv. vorm, kleur, bouw, beharing, gedrag)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

observing and, on the basis of outward characteristics, comparing (parts of) different plants (e.g. shape, colour, build, scent, *bloeiwijze*: the way the flowers are arranged on the plant) and comparing (parts of) different animals (e.g. shape, colour, build, hair covering, behaviour)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays leaves of several trees side by side and compares their edge, shape and veins, makes rubbings, and smells crushed leaves of herbs. The child compares two birds at a feeding table, or a snail and a slug, by shape, colour, covering and the way they move, then sorts a set into two groups and states the rule. A child of 7 compares the leaves of different trees by shape and colour; a child of 10 compares a snail and a slug by build and the way they move.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The comparing is on outward characteristics, of whole plants and animals or of parts. The characteristics in brackets are examples: shape, colour, build, scent and flower arrangement for plants; shape, colour, build, hair covering and behaviour for animals. No species are named.

### Common mistakes

Comparing on colour or size only. Noting one feature for the first plant or animal and a different feature for the second, so that nothing is compared. Calling two things the same kind because they have the same colour.
