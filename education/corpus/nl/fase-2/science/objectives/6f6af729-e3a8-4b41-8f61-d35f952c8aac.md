verkennen van eigenschappen en kenmerken van planten en dieren die passen bij de leefomgeving (levensgemeenschap)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring properties and characteristics of plants and animals that fit the environment they live in (*levensgemeenschap*: the community of plants and animals that live together in one place)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child visits a ditch or pond and a wood or meadow. The child nets small water animals into a tray and looks for swimming legs and breathing tubes, then compares them with the animals under fallen leaves. The child matches plants and animals to the place where they were found and says which feature fits that place. A child of 7 says who lives where; a child of 10 says which feature makes that possible and that the plants and animals of one place need each other.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. No environments or species are named. The word in brackets points to the community of living things in one place; the wording names no particular relations within it, and the term food chain is not used. Adaptations to temperature, drought, wind and enemies are named in `ojw/pdm/3/02/fase2`, not here.

### Common mistakes

Thinking an animal lives somewhere because it likes it there or chose it. Thinking any animal can live anywhere as long as it is fed. Matching on one feature only, for example placing every green animal in the grass.
