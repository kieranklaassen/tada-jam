## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering patterns and orderings, such as assigning shape properties to things around you and discovering patterns in the surroundings (e.g. in the paving tiles around the school, the bricks of brickwork, or in soap bubbles)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child walks around the school and draws, or rubs with a crayon on paper, how the paving tiles and the bricks of a wall are laid, and continues the pattern on squared paper. The child names the shapes of things nearby, such as round, square or six-sided. The child blows soap bubbles onto a wet plate and looks at how they join. A child of 7 finds and copies a repeating pattern; a child of 10 states the rule, for example that each row of bricks is shifted by half a brick.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two activities follow "such as": assigning shape properties to things, and discovering patterns in the surroundings. The three places in brackets are examples: paving tiles, brickwork and soap bubbles. No shapes or terms are named. The statement stands in the cluster on materials, substances and objects.

### Common mistakes

Copying a pattern without being able to say its rule. Seeing only the repetition of colour and missing the arrangement. Drawing brickwork as bricks stacked straight above each other, without the shift from row to row.
