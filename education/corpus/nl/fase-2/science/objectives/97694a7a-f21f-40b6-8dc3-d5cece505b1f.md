## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

seeing connections between observable properties of materials (whether visible directly, after some time or in another place)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tests one set of materials for several properties and puts the results in one table: which pass on heat, which make the bulb light, which had rusted after a week. The child notices that the metal objects end up in the same group on several tests. The child leaves pieces of paper, wood, iron and plastic outdoors for some weeks and compares them with pieces kept indoors. A child of 7 sees that the materials that pass on heat are also the ones that make the bulb light; a child of 10 sees in the table that the material that rusted is among those that make the bulb light.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. No properties or materials are named in this statement; conducting, insulating and rusting are the examples of `ojw/nattech/1/02/fase2`. The brackets say when or where a property shows: directly, after some time, or in another place. The wording does not explain these three further.

### Common mistakes

Looking only at what shows at once and not looking again later. Turning a link seen in two examples into a rule for all materials, such as "everything shiny lets electricity through". Treating a property of the object, such as its size or shape, as a property of the material.
