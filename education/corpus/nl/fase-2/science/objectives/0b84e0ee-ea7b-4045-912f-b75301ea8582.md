## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating how force and movement can be transmitted, such as force through a lever or pulley, and movement through a gear wheel or bicycle chain

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lifts a pile of books with a plank laid over a block and moves the block nearer to the books and further away. The child hoists a bucket with a rope over a pulley, turns one wheel in a set of gears and watches the next, and turns the pedals of a bicycle that stands upside down. A child of 7 tries and feels what is easier and sees which way things turn; a child of 10 counts teeth and turns and finds the rule about the distance to the turning point of a lever.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Four means follow "such as" and are examples: lever and pulley for force, gear wheel and bicycle chain for movement. *Kracht* is also the everyday word for strength or power; with lever and pulley beside it, the force of mechanics is meant. No calculation and no ratio is mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Expecting two gear wheels that mesh to turn the same way. Expecting a small wheel driven by a large one to turn more slowly. Believing that a single fixed pulley makes the load lighter.
