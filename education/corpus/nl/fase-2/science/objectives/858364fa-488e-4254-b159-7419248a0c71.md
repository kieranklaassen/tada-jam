## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

applying technical principles in solving a question or problem by using them functionally in one's own designs (investigating and designing)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a problem, such as lifting a load onto a shelf or crossing the gap between two tables, sketches a solution and picks a principle already met, such as a lever, a pulley, a triangle or a gear wheel. The child builds it, tries whether the problem is solved, improves the design and shows it. A child of 7 uses a lever in their own design to lift the load; a child of 10 uses a pulley in their own design to get the load onto the shelf.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The principles are not named in this statement; other statements in this cluster name structural principles (`ojw/nattech/3/01/fase2` and `ojw/nattech/3/02/fase2`), joins (`ojw/nattech/3/03/fase2`) and the transmission of force and movement (`ojw/nattech/3/04/fase2`). The designs are the child's own, and the brackets name the way of working: investigating and designing.

### Common mistakes

Adding a principle that does no work in the design, such as a gear wheel that drives nothing. Copying an example instead of solving their own problem. Not checking at the end whether the problem is solved.
