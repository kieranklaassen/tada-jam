## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child goes around the classroom and playground, touches, taps, lifts and scratches objects and names the material of each. The child sorts a box of objects by material and finds objects made of more than one, such as scissors or a pencil. A child of 7 sorts into wood, metal, glass and plastic; a child of 10 tells look-alikes apart, such as painted metal and plastic or concrete and natural stone, and gives a reason.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The nine materials in brackets are examples. The statement is about what objects are made of; the properties of materials are in `ojw/nattech/1/02/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Naming the object when asked for the material. Judging by the colour or the coating, so that a painted metal object is called plastic. Calling every metal iron.
