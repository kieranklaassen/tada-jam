maken van een object met behulp van eenvoudige constructieprincipes zoals profielen en driehoek-constructies (stabiele en stevige constructie)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making an object with the help of simple structural principles such as profiles and triangle constructions (a stable and sturdy construction)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds a bridge, a tower or a chair for a toy from paper, straws, strips of card or sticks. The child folds strips into an L or U shape, adds slanting strips that form triangles, loads and pushes the object and mends the place that failed. A child of 7 builds by trying and adds triangles after being shown; a child of 10 plans where the slanting strips go and tests with a counted load.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two principles follow "such as" and are examples: profiles and triangle constructions. The brackets name the result: a stable and sturdy construction. No object, size or material is named. Investigating such principles is in `ojw/nattech/3/01/fase2`; this statement is about making.

### Common mistakes

Making weak joins, so that the object fails where parts meet. Adding a slanting strip that is not fixed at both ends and so holds nothing. Using a flat strip where a folded one is needed.
