## Official wording

ervaring opdoen met en onderzoeken van het verschijnsel licht: lichtbronnen; doorlaten of terugkaatsen van licht (licht gaat alle kanten op); schaduwvorming; relatie met zien; relatie met verwarmen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with and investigating the phenomenon of light: light sources; letting light through or reflecting it (light goes in all directions); the forming of shadows; the relationship with seeing; the relationship with warming

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shines a torch at glass, tracing paper, cardboard, a mirror and foil and sorts them by what the light does. The child makes hand shadows on a wall and moves the hand towards the lamp and away from it. The child looks into a closed box through a small hole, with and without a second hole for light, and holds a hand in sunlight and then in shade. A child of 7 plays with shadows and sorts; a child of 10 predicts the size of a shadow and uses a mirror to send light to a chosen spot.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Five parts are named after the colon: light sources, letting through or reflecting, shadows, the link with seeing and the link with warming. The wording names no particular sources or materials; colour is not among the five parts, and no terms are prescribed.

### Common mistakes

Believing that we see because something leaves the eye. Calling the moon, a mirror or a shiny object a light source. Treating a shadow as a thing that belongs to the object and not as a place the light does not reach.
