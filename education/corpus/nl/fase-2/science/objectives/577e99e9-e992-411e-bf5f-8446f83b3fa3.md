## Official wording

herkennen van een geautomatiseerd systeem in een apparaat en uitzoeken welke actie of informatie leidt tot welk effect

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster ojw/nattech/3; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 577e99e9-e992-411e-bf5f-8446f83b3fa3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising an automated system in a device and finding out which action or information leads to which effect

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks for things that act without a person operating them: a sliding door, a lamp that switches on when someone passes, a traffic light with a push button, a heating thermostat. The child tries actions one at a time, such as walking up slowly, standing still or waving, and writes each finding as "if ..., then ...". A child of 7 notices that the thing acts by itself when they do something; a child of 10 tells apart what the device senses, the rule it follows and what it then does, and tests one condition at a time.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement speaks of an automated system in a device; none is named. What is to be found out is which action or which information leads to which effect. The wording does not ask for programming.

### Common mistakes

Saying that the device sees, knows or wants something, as in "the door knows I am coming". Trying only one action, so that it cannot be said which action caused the effect. Calling a remote-controlled device automatic.
