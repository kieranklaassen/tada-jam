## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring energy: energy sources (natural gas, geothermal heat, biomass, wood, oil, water, wind, sun, but also human and horse); relating them to forms of energy (heat, light, *kracht* (which means force and also strength or power) and sound); reflecting on the use of energy sources (availability, advantages and disadvantages)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child goes around the home or school and notes for each appliance what it gives (heat, light, movement or sound) and what it runs on. The child makes a paper windmill and a water wheel turn, feels a dark and a light surface that have lain in the sun, and turns the crank of a hand-powered torch. The child sorts cards of sources and talks about what is good and bad about each. A child of 7 finds what makes a thing warm, light or moving; a child of 10 follows the steps from source to use and compares the advantages with the disadvantages.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name ten sources: natural gas, geothermal heat, biomass, wood, oil, water, wind, sun, human and horse; the goal names these ten and no others. The brackets after forms of energy name four: heat, light, *kracht* and sound. *Kracht* means force and also strength or power, and the wording does not choose; electricity is not among the four. Reflection covers availability and advantages and disadvantages. The wording does not use the words renewable and fossil.

### Common mistakes

Believing that electricity comes from the wall socket, with no source behind it. Believing that energy is gone after use instead of changed into another form. Believing that sun and wind have no disadvantages.
