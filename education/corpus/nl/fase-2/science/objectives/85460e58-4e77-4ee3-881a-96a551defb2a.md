## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring forms of reproduction in plants (through seed that develops in a fruit, or through a tuber, a bulb or runners)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child cuts open an apple, a tomato and a bean pod and finds the seeds. The child plants a potato and a flower bulb, and pins a strawberry runner down in a pot of soil and watches it take root. A child of 7 finds, plants and watches; a child of 10 sorts plants by how they make new plants and notices that some do it in more than one way.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name four forms: seed that develops in a fruit, tuber, bulb and runners; the wording names no others. Pollination, a step on the way to seed and not a form of reproduction, is not named. How seeds spread and germinate is in `ojw/pdm/3/09/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Believing that every new plant comes from a seed. Using the kitchen meaning of fruit, so that a tomato, a pod or a nut is not seen as a fruit with seeds. Mistaking a bulb or a tuber for a seed.
