## Official wording

onderzoeken van constructies en constructieprincipes die zorgen voor stabiliteit en stevigheid in voorwerpen, gebouwen en kunstwerken zoals brede basis, driehoek-constructies, boog, profiel, buis (bijv. in kast, toren, piramide, brug)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating structures and structural principles that provide stability and sturdiness in objects, buildings and *kunstwerken* (the word means works of art and, in Dutch civil engineering, built structures such as bridges and viaducts), such as a wide base, triangle constructions, arch, profile, tube (e.g. in a cupboard, tower, pyramid, bridge)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds block towers on a narrow and on a wide base and pushes against them. The child makes a square and a triangle from strips and split pins and presses on a corner of each. The child lays a flat sheet of paper as a bridge between two piles of books and loads it with coins, then folds the sheet into a zigzag or rolls it into a tube and loads it again. A child of 7 builds, pushes and sees which one stays up; a child of 10 counts the load, names the principle and finds it in a crane, a bridge or a bicycle frame.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Five principles follow "such as" and are examples: wide base, triangle constructions, arch, profile and tube. The four places in brackets are examples too: cupboard, tower, pyramid and bridge. A profile here is a strip or sheet with a folded or shaped cross-section; the wording does not explain the word. No materials, loads or sizes are given.

### Common mistakes

Trying to make a structure stronger with more material or more tape instead of another shape. Building high on a narrow base. Expecting a frame of four strips to keep its shape as a triangle does.
