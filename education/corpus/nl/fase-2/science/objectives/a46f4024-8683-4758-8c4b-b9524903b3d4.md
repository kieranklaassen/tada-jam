## English gloss

investigating and observing the elementary stages in the growth and development of animals and of humans

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child keeps caterpillars, tadpoles or mealworms in the classroom and draws them every week. The child lays photos of themselves as a baby, a toddler and now in order, next to photos of a parent and a grandparent, and marks their own height on a wall strip through the year. A child of 7 puts pictures in order; a child of 10 names stages such as egg, larva, pupa and adult and compares animals whose young look like the adults with animals that change form.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. It covers animals and humans; the growth of a seed into a plant is in `ojw/pdm/3/09/fase2`. The stages are called elementary and are not listed. No species are named.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that a caterpillar and a butterfly, or a tadpole and a frog, are different animals. Thinking that growing is only getting bigger. Leaving out the egg as a stage.
