## Official wording

verkennen van manieren waarop dieren zich voortplanten en realiseren dat een levend wezen steeds voortkomt uit een ander levend wezen van dezelfde soort

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring ways in which animals reproduce and realising that a living being always comes from another living being of the same kind or species (*soort*, which means both)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts animal pictures into animals that lay eggs and animals that bear live young. The child looks at frogspawn, snail eggs or hatching chicks, and matches young to their parents, including young that look different from the adults, such as a tadpole and a caterpillar. A child of 7 matches young to parents; a child of 10 compares how many eggs or young different animals have and how the young are cared for.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The first part is about animals; reproduction in plants is in `ojw/pdm/3/08/fase2` and reproduction in humans in `ojw/pdm/3/11/fase2`. The ways are not listed. The second part is a general idea about all living beings: each comes from another of the same *soort*, a word that means both kind and species; the wording does not choose.

### Common mistakes

Believing that maggots, flies or worms arise from dirt, rotting food or rain. Thinking that only birds lay eggs. Failing to match young that look unlike their parents.
