## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and applying different types of joins, such as sewing, stapling, screwing, soldering, hinging, and joining with hook-and-loop fastener

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a piece of clothing, a book, a cupboard door and a bag and finds how the parts are joined. The child sews two pieces of felt together, staples paper, drives a screw into soft wood with a screwdriver, and makes a hinge from card and a split pin. A child of 7 staples, tapes and sews with a thick needle; a child of 10 screws, and chooses a join by the material and by whether the part must move or come apart again.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Six kinds of join follow "such as" and are examples: sewing, stapling, screwing, soldering, hinging and hook-and-loop fastener. Gluing and nailing are not among the examples. Soldering is named without any condition; the wording says nothing about tools, supervision or safety.

### Common mistakes

Choosing a join that does not suit the material, such as staples for thick wood. Making a fixed join where a part has to move, or not knowing which joins can be undone. Pushing on a screw instead of turning it.
