## Official wording

onderzoeken hoe zaden van planten zich verspreiden (wind, water, vogels) en volgen van de ontwikkeling van zaadje (ontkiemen) naar plant (zaaiproeven) bij verschillende (snelgroeiende) zaden

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating how the seeds of plants spread (wind, water, birds) and following the development from seed (germinating) to plant (sowing trials) with different (fast-growing) seeds

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child collects seeds outdoors, such as maple keys, dandelion fluff and berries, drops them from a height, blows on them and floats them in water. The child sows cress, beans and sunflower seeds on damp paper or in soil and draws them every day or two. A child of 7 sows and draws; a child of 10 measures each day and compares the kinds of seed in a table.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name three ways of spreading: wind, water and birds; the wording names no others. The sowing is done with different seeds, and the brackets add fast-growing ones; no species are named.

### Common mistakes

Believing that a seed needs soil or light to germinate. Expecting leaves to appear first, when the root comes out first. Thinking that seeds travel on purpose.
