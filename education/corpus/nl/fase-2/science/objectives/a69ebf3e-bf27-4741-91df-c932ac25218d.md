realising that the earth turns and moves relative to the sun (universe)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a ball as the earth and uses a lamp as the sun, turns the ball around and carries it in a circle around the lamp. The child puts a sticker on the ball for the place where they live and watches the sticker pass into and out of the lamplight. A child of 7 acts the two movements out with the body, turning on the spot and walking around a classmate who is the sun; a child of 10 can say that both movements happen at once.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names two things: the earth turns, and the earth moves relative to the sun. It gives no durations such as a day or a year and names no other planets; the word in brackets only places the subject in the universe. The source prints "t.o.v. van de zon" with a doubled word (*t.o.v.* already stands for *ten opzichte van*, relative to); the gloss reads it as "relative to the sun". How day and night come about is in `ojw/rojh/2/03/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Believing that the sun travels around the earth, because the sun is seen crossing the sky. Mixing up the earth's turning on the spot with its path around the sun. Expecting that we would feel the earth move if it really moved.
