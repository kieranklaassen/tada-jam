## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks for the moon on several evenings, or in the morning sky, over a few weeks and draws the shape seen each time. The child lays the drawings in order and says which looked like a thin curve, which like half a circle and which like a full circle. A child of 7 looks at the moon and draws the shape seen; a child of 10 keeps a moon diary and sees that the shape is not the same every evening.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about experiencing that the shape we see differs. It names no particular shapes or phases and does not ask for an explanation. The earth's turning and its movement relative to the sun are in `ojw/rojh/2/02/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the daytime sky: they look for the moon only at night and believe it is in the sky only then. Many believe that the moon itself changes shape or that parts of it are missing, or that clouds or the earth's shadow cover the part that is not seen.
