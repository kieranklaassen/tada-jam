## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring how simple (measuring) instruments and devices work (e.g. *huis-tuin-keukengereedschap*: ordinary tools from the house, garden and kitchen)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child uses kitchen scales, a measuring jug, a tape measure and a thermometer and finds which part moves and where the value is read. The child opens and closes a garlic press, a nutcracker, pliers, a clothes peg and a hand whisk with gears, and says which part does what. A child of 7 uses the tool and says what it is for; a child of 10 explains how it works, such as the lever in a nutcracker, and reads a scale between the numbered marks.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The instruments and devices are to be simple; "measuring" stands in brackets, so both measuring instruments and other instruments are covered. Ordinary household tools are given as an example. None is named.

### Common mistakes

Saying what a tool is for when asked how it works. Reading a scale without starting at zero, or misreading the marks between two numbers. Counting only things with a plug as devices.
