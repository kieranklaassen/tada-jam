## Official wording

ervaring opdoen met en onderzoeken van temperatuur (warmte): warmtebronnen; warm en koud is relatief; verandering van temperatuur verandert stoffen (vriezen/koelen leidt tot stollen/krimpen; verwarmen/koken leidt tot smelten/verdampen/uitzetten)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with and investigating temperature (heat): heat sources; warm and cold are relative; a change of temperature changes substances (freezing/cooling leads to solidifying/shrinking; heating/boiling leads to melting/evaporating/expanding)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds one hand in warm water and the other in cold, then puts both in lukewarm water and says how each hand feels. The child melts an ice cube, chocolate and butter and lets them set again, and watches a saucer of water dry up over some days. A child of 7 feels, melts and freezes; a child of 10 reads a thermometer, compares the reading with what the hand feels, and says that the changes can be undone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three parts are named: heat sources, warm and cold being relative, and substances changing with temperature. The brackets name solidifying and shrinking for freezing or cooling, and melting, evaporating and expanding for heating or boiling; the wording names no other changes. No temperatures or units are given and no thermometer is named. The wording does not mention that water expands when it freezes. Heating and boiling are named, and the text states no safety condition.

### Common mistakes

Trusting the hand as a measure of temperature. Believing that evaporated water has stopped existing. Thinking of cold as something that moves into an object.
