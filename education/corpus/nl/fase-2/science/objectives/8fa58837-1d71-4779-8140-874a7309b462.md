exploring and investigating the elementary parts of plants and animals (such as organs) and their function

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child pulls up a weed and lays out its root, stem, leaves and flower. The child stands a white flower or a celery stalk in coloured water and sees the colour rise. Looking at a fish, a bird or an insect, the child points to the parts for moving, eating and breathing. A child of 7 names the parts; a child of 10 says what each part does and tests it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. It covers plants and animals; the human body has its own cluster in this lane (`ojw/pdm/1/01/fase2` to `ojw/pdm/1/03/fase2`). The parts are called elementary, with organs given as an example; they are not listed.

### Common mistakes

Thinking roots only hold the plant in place, or that they take in food as a mouth does. Naming a part without saying what it does. Assuming every animal has the same organs as a human, for example that an insect has lungs and bones.
