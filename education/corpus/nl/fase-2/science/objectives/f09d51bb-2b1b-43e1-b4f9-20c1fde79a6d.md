## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring the functional value of plants and animals for humans, such as supplier of oxygen and food, and their role in healing

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child goes through a lunch box and says for each item which plant or animal it comes from. The child grinds wheat grains into flour, picks vegetables or herbs in a school garden, and makes tea from mint or camomile. A child of 7 traces familiar foods to a plant or animal; a child of 10 also knows that plants give off the oxygen we breathe and that some medicines come from plants.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three examples follow "such as": supplying oxygen, supplying food, and a role in healing. No plants or animals are named. The statement is about usefulness to humans; the beauty of nature is in `ojw/pdm/4/02/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Not knowing that made foods such as bread, pasta, sugar and chips come from plants. Stopping at the shop when asked where milk, eggs or meat come from. Thinking that only trees give oxygen, or that plants make it for our sake.
