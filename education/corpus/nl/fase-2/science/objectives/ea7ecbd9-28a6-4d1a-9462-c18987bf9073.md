## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with and investigating the phenomenon of force (*kracht*, which also means strength or power): sources of force; force from people (muscle power); the force of water (floating/suspended/sinking/flowing; suspended, *zweven*, is staying at one depth in the water without rising or sinking)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child pushes, pulls and lifts things and says how hard each is. The child puts objects in a tub of water and sorts them into floating, staying under without reaching the bottom, and sinking; pushes an empty closed bottle under and feels the water push back; and shapes a ball of clay that sank into a boat. The child lets a stream of water turn a water wheel. A child of 7 pushes and sorts; a child of 10 predicts, gives shape and material as reasons, and counts how many marbles a boat carries before it sinks.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three parts are named after the colon: sources of force, force from people, with muscle power in brackets, and the force of water, with floating, staying at one depth, sinking and flowing in brackets. Apart from people and water the wording names no particular source of force, and no units are given. *Kracht* means force and also strength or power: the goal calls it a phenomenon and stands in the cluster on physical phenomena, which points to the force of physics, while "sources" (*krachtbronnen*) and "muscle power" (*spierkracht*) also fit the reading of power; the wording does not choose. Magnetism has its own statement, `ojw/nattech/2/05/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Believing that heavy things sink and light things float whatever their size and shape, though a large log floats and a small stone sinks. Believing that an object floats better in deeper water. Thinking that only living things exert force.
