opzetten en uitvoeren van een eenvoudig experiment met behulp van een technisch principe

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

setting up and carrying out a simple experiment with the help of a technical principle

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a lever, a ramp, a pulley or a pair of gear wheels and asks a question about it, such as where the block must lie under the plank for one finger to lift the bag. The child says what they expect, moves the block a step at a time, counts weights or measures with a ruler and records each result. A child of 7 predicts and observes within steps an adult has set; a child of 10 plans the test, keeps the load the same and records the results in a table.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The principle is not named in this statement, and the wording does not say what makes an experiment simple. The clusters on materials and on physical phenomena each hold a statement on experimenting of their own: `ojw/nattech/1/08/fase2` and `ojw/nattech/2/08/fase2`.

### Common mistakes

Building something that works and calling it an experiment, with no question and no measurement. Changing two things at once, such as the load and the place of the block. Recording only the trial that went well.
