## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with and investigating the phenomenon of electricity: current goes round in a closed circuit; how static electricity arises; use by people and danger for people

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child connects a battery, wires and a small bulb until the bulb lights, opens the circuit and sees the bulb go out, and adds a switch made of a paper clip. The child rubs a balloon on a sleeve and picks up scraps of paper with it. The child lists what uses electricity at home and says why sockets are not to be touched and why appliances are kept away from water. A child of 7 connects the battery, the wires and the bulb until the bulb lights; a child of 10 opens the circuit with the paper-clip switch and sees the bulb go out.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three parts are named: current going round in a closed circuit, how static electricity arises, and use by and danger for people. The wording names no apparatus (such as a battery or a bulb), no particular kind of circuit, and no symbols or units. Danger is named, without safety rules.

### Common mistakes

Connecting one wire from the battery to the bulb and expecting light, as if electricity only needs a way there. Believing that the current is used up in the bulb, so that less comes back. Treating a small battery as being as dangerous as a wall socket, or a wall socket as being as safe as a small battery.
