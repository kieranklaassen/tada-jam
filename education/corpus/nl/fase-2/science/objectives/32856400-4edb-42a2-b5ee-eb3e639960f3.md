## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring observable properties of substances: dissolving (e.g. *keukenpoeders*, powders found in a kitchen); mixing and separating out again (the forming of layers)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stirs a spoonful of salt, sugar, flour and cocoa each into its own glass of water and watches which one is no longer seen, which makes the water cloudy and which sinks. The child shakes oil and water in a closed jar and watches the layers come back. A child of 7 stirs, watches and tells; a child of 10 changes one thing, such as warm or cold water, and counts the spoonfuls until no more dissolves.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two properties are named: dissolving, with kitchen powders as an example, and mixing and separating out again, with the forming of layers in brackets. The wording names no particular powders or liquids and no other way in which a mixture separates.

### Common mistakes

Believing that a dissolved substance is gone; tasting the salt water shows it is still there. Saying "melting" for dissolving. Believing that harder stirring will make flour or sand dissolve.
