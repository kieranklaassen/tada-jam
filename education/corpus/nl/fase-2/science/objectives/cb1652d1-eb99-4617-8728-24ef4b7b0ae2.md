## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with and investigating the phenomenon of magnetism: the attracting and repelling of magnets; magnetism as a property of materials

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child brings two magnets together end to end, turns one around and feels pull and push. The child tests objects around the room with a magnet and sorts them into attracted and not attracted, and moves a paper clip with a magnet held under paper, card and a table top. A child of 7 feels and sorts; a child of 10 finds that only some metals are attracted, that aluminium foil is not, and that the same two ends always push each other away.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two parts are named after the colon: magnets attracting and repelling, and magnetism as a property of materials. The wording names no particular magnets or materials, gives no terms for the ends of a magnet, and names no instrument or application of magnetism.

### Common mistakes

Believing that every metal is attracted by a magnet. Believing that a larger magnet is always stronger. Expecting a magnet to push away objects that are not magnets.
