ontdekken van relaties tussen de werking, de vorm en het gebruikte materiaal bij bekende voorwerpen (denken in vorm-functie van producten en materiaal-functie bij producten)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering relationships between how familiar objects work, their shape and the material used (thinking in terms of form and function of products, and of material and function in products)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child handles a familiar object, such as scissors, a clothes peg, a cup or an umbrella, takes it apart where that is possible, and says what each part does, why it has that shape and why it is made of that material. The child thinks through the same object in another material, such as a pan with a metal handle and one with a wooden handle. A child of 7 says what the object is for and what it is made of; a child of 10 gives reasons why shape and material fit the use and proposes an improvement.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 2 (*groep* 4 to 6, about ages 7 to 10), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The objects are to be familiar; none is named. Three things are related: how the object works, its shape and its material.

### Common mistakes

Answering only with the use ("it is for cutting") and saying nothing about shape or material. Explaining the choice of material by how it looks. Assuming there is one right material for an object.
