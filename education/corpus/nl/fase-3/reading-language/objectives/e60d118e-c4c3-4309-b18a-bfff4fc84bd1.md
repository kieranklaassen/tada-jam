## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adapting language use to the purpose of the conversation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

To persuade the group to choose a plan, the child gives reasons and says "I think ... because ..."; to find out what a visitor knows, the same child asks open questions and listens. In a talk meant to settle a disagreement the child chooses careful wording ("I saw it differently") and leaves out accusations. When the purpose is to decide quickly, the child speaks briefly and proposes a vote. A child of 10 changes wording when the purpose has been named beforehand; a child of 12 chooses words and sentence forms for the purpose without a prompt, and changes them when the purpose of the talk changes.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is one short phrase: language use is adapted to the purpose of the conversation. It names no purposes and no features of language use (word choice, sentence form, tone), and gives no examples. NE/MT/01/02/01/fase3 is the goal about recognising kinds of conversation and their purposes, and NE/MT/01/04/02/fase3 the goal about forms of address and formal and informal language use. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Speaking in the same way whatever the purpose, for example telling a long anecdote in a talk that is meant to reach a decision. Wanting to persuade and only repeating the wish, without reasons. Wanting information and asking closed questions that can be answered with yes or no.
