## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Speaking to the whole school in the hall, the child speaks louder and slower than in the classroom and pauses after each main point. Reading a story to children of five, the child uses a livelier voice and a different voice for each character; presenting to parents, a calmer one. Seeing that listeners at the back lean forward to hear, the child raises the volume. A child of 10 speaks loudly and slowly enough when reminded before the talk, and speaks faster as the talk goes on; a child of 12 adapts pace, intonation and volume to the room and the listeners during the talk and stresses the words that matter most.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three things are adapted: speaking pace, intonation and volume. They are adapted to the audience; the statement does not say to which features of the audience (size, age, distance, prior knowledge). NE/MT/03/06/02/fase3 is the goal about grammar and articulation, and NE/MT/01/06/02/fase3 the goal for conversations that names volume and speaking pace. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Speaking faster and faster from nervousness, most of all near the end. Saying the end of each sentence so softly that the last words are not heard. Speaking every sentence with the same intonation, as when reading a list aloud.
