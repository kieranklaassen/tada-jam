## Official wording

afstemmen van het doel tijdens het spreken (taalgebruik en non-verbale ondersteuning)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/03/03; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 5ecdf4f9-d4a6-4ea8-ac06-612774b89ab5.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adjusting (*afstemmen van*) the purpose while speaking (language use and non-verbal support)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a talk meant to convince the class to collect money for an animal shelter, the child uses wording that appeals ("imagine that ..."), stresses the main argument and looks at the listeners at that moment. In a talk meant to explain how the collection works, the same child speaks in short steps and points at each step on the poster. Halfway through a talk the child notices that they have started an anecdote that does not serve the purpose, and returns to the purpose. A child of 10 fits wording and gestures to a purpose that was fixed beforehand, in the parts that were practised; a child of 12 keeps to the purpose through the whole talk and fits words, voice and gestures to it also in parts that were not prepared.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The source prints *afstemmen van het doel* ("adjusting the purpose"), without the *op* ("to") phrase that this verb has in the neighbouring goals, so the wording can be read in two ways: the purpose itself is adjusted while speaking, or the two things in the brackets, language use and non-verbal support, are matched to the purpose while speaking. The wording does not choose, and the gloss keeps the printed form. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/03/03, titled *Spreekdoelen* (speaking purposes), with NE/MT/03/03/02/fase3, the goal about a structure that fits the speaking purpose. The statement names no purposes and does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Stating a purpose at the start and then speaking in the same way whatever the purpose, for example listing facts in a talk meant to convince. Using gestures and facial expression that do not fit the purpose, such as laughing while making a serious appeal. Changing to another purpose halfway without noticing, for example from explaining to telling about a holiday.
