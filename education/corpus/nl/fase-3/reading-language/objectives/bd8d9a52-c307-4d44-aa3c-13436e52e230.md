## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming different emotions in a story

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a passage and gives each feeling in it a name: not only angry or sad, but jealous, ashamed, relieved, disappointed, homesick. The child writes the name beside the passage and says what in the text shows it. A child of 10 names feelings that the text shows plainly, with more exact words than happy, angry and sad; a child of 12 names feelings that are not stated, two feelings at once, and a feeling that changes within a scene.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is naming: giving the emotions a name. The object is different emotions in a story; the goal names no emotions and sets no number, and the feelings above are the pack's examples. The wording does not say whose emotions are meant: those of the characters, or those the story raises in the reader. It names no kind of story and does not say whether the story is read by the child or read aloud. Explaining how a character feels is in NE/LE/07/01/01/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Reducing every feeling to happy, angry or sad. Saying how the child would feel and not how the character feels. Naming the event in place of the feeling: "his dog died" where grief is meant.
