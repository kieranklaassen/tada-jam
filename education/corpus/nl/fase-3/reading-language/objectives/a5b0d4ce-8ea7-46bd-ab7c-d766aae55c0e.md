## Official wording

schrijven: - verhalende teksten (bijv. verhaal, email of gedicht); - informatieve teksten (bijv. werkstuk of verslag); - instructieve teksten (bijv. een handleiding of een draaiboek); - betogende teksten (bijv. een boekbespreking, een recensie over een tv-programma, een ingezonden brief of reclame)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing: - narrative texts (e.g. story, email or poem); - informative texts (e.g. project paper (*werkstuk*, a school paper on a chosen subject) or report); - instructive texts (e.g. a manual or a running order (*draaiboek*, a step-by-step plan for an event)); - persuasive texts (e.g. a book review, a review of a TV programme, a letter to the editor or advertising)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a story or a poem, an email about a holiday, a *werkstuk* of several pages about a subject they chose, a manual for a game they invented, a running order for a class evening, and a book review that tries to get others to read the book. The child writes each text for a reader and gives it to that reader. A child of 10 writes a story with a beginning, a middle and an end, a short report and a row of steps in order; a child of 12 writes a *werkstuk* with chapters, and a review or a letter to the editor with an opinion and arguments.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is writing. Four groups of text are named: narrative, informative, instructive and persuasive (*betogende*) texts. Everything in brackets follows "bijv." and is an example; none is required. The source lists the email among its examples of narrative texts, and the book review and advertising among its examples of persuasive texts. The goal sets no text length, no number of texts and no level of spelling, and it does not say whether the texts are written by hand or on a computer.

### Common mistakes

In a *werkstuk*, copying sentences from the sources and not writing in their own words. In a manual, leaving out a step that the writer finds obvious. In a review, retelling the book or the programme and giving no opinion or reason.
