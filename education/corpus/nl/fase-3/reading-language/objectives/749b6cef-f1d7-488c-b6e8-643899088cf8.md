## Official wording

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making a distinction between informal and formal context and using frequently occurring standard formulations in (formal) letters

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes about the same matter twice: a message to a friend, and a letter to the municipality or a company, for example to ask whether the class may visit. In the letter the child uses fixed forms such as "Geachte heer, mevrouw," (Dear Sir or Madam), "u" (the polite you) and "Met vriendelijke groet," (Kind regards). A child of 10 chooses between "Hoi" (Hi) and "Beste" (Dear) and between "je" and "u"; a child of 12 writes a formal letter with a fixed opening and closing sentence and leaves out abbreviations and the language of chat messages.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has two parts: making a distinction between informal and formal context, and using frequently occurring standard formulations in letters. "(Formele)" (formal) stands in brackets: the wording leaves open whether standard formulations for informal letters are meant as well. The goal names no formulations; the Dutch phrases above are the pack's examples. "Veelvoorkomende" (frequently occurring) is not defined further. The second part names letters (*brieven*) and no other kind of text, and the goal says nothing about the layout of a letter.

### Common mistakes

Writing to an adult they do not know as to a friend: "Hoi", "jij", abbreviations. Mixing the two within one letter: "Geachte" at the top and "Groetjes" (an informal goodbye) at the end, or "u" and "je" by turns. Using a formal sentence they have learned in a place where it does not fit what they want to say.
