paying attention to grammar, articulation, volume and speaking pace

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk the child speaks loudly enough for the classmate at the far side of the table, says words in full and does not hurry through a long sentence. The child hears that a sentence came out wrongly ("the book what I read") and says it again correctly. When a partner asks "What?", the child repeats more slowly and more clearly, not only louder. A child of 10 attends to volume or pace when reminded; a child of 12 attends to how they speak while thinking about the content, and slows down for a difficult part.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Four things are named: grammar, articulation, volume and speaking pace. The verb is "paying attention to" (*aandacht hebben voor*): the statement sets no standard for any of the four and does not say which grammar. The open data lists the goal under conversations, in sub-cluster NE/MT/01/06, titled *Vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing* (fluency, intelligibility and grammatical control); NE/MT/03/06/02/fase3, a goal for speaking, names grammar and articulation, and NE/MT/03/06/01/fase3 names speaking pace, intonation and volume. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is the listener's side: they know what they said and so do not notice that a hurried or mumbled part was not understood. When the content becomes difficult or exciting, they speak faster and less clearly and do not notice it.
