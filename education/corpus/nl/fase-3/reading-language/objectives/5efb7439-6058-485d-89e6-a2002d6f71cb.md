## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child talks with others about how language works and how it is used: why a message to a friend is worded differently from a letter to a mayor, how the words of young people differ from those of their grandparents, where a loanword comes from, what a proverb means, and how the same thing is said in another language or dialect that someone in the group knows. The child compares two wordings of one message and says what each does to a reader. A child of 10 notices such differences and gives examples; a child of 12 gives reasons, says what a choice of words does, and talks about how language changes over time and from group to group.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two subjects of conversation, language and language use, and nothing more exact: no terms, no aspects of language and no rules. The subjects in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. The form is conversation; the goal does not mention written work and does not say with whom the conversations are held. Under the cluster heading the content-line document prints a sentence, no part of the goal and quoted in the lane's frame, which says that the terms the document lists matter for reflecting on language and language use "in gesprekken met kinderen" (in conversations with children).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often talk about the thing a word names and leave out the word itself: asked about the word "vrijheid" (freedom), they tell what freedom is. Many children believe there is only one right way to say something and call another dialect, another language or the speech of another group wrong.
