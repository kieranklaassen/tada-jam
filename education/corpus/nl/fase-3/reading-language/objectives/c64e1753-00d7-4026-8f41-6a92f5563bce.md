## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

regularly putting gestures, facial expression, eye contact and body posture to use to support a contribution to a conversation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Giving a view in a group talk, the child turns toward the group, looks at the partners in turn and shows with the hands how two things differ in size. The child's face shows that a remark is meant as a joke, or that the matter is serious. When disagreeing, the child keeps facing the other and does not turn away. A child of 10 looks at the partners and shows something with the hands when it has been practised; a child of 12 does so without a prompt and can say afterwards what a gesture or a look added.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Four means are named: gestures, facial expression, eye contact and body posture. Their role is to support a contribution to a conversation, and the wording says "regularly" (*regelmatig*) without saying how often. NE/MT/03/07/01/fase3, the matching goal for speaking, names body posture, eye contact and gestures, and says "consciously" where this statement says "regularly". The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Looking at the table, only at the adult or only at one friend while speaking to the group. Making movements that have nothing to do with what is said, such as fiddling with a pen, which draw attention away from the words. Showing a face or posture that contradicts the words, such as proposing something with arms crossed and eyes down, so that the partners do not take the proposal seriously.
