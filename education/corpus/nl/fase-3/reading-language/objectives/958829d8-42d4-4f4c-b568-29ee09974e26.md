## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using a structure that fits the speaking purpose (e.g. retelling a recipe: step by step)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Retelling how to make pancakes, the child gives the ingredients first and then the steps in order, with "first", "then" and "when that is done". For a talk meant to convince, the same child builds differently: opinion, arguments, conclusion. For a report on a test the child follows question, method, result. A child of 10 uses time order for an instruction or an event and a given frame for other purposes; a child of 12 chooses the structure from the purpose without a frame and tells the audience at the start how the talk is built.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The structure is one that fits the speaking purpose; retelling a recipe step by step follows "e.g." and is the only example. The statement names no other structures and no speaking purposes. NE/MT/03/02/01/fase3 is the goal about ordering information and adding structure by means of a *schema*, and NE/MT/03/03/01/fase3 the other goal in sub-cluster NE/MT/03/03, titled *Spreekdoelen* (speaking purposes). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Using time order for everything, also for a talk meant to convince, which then becomes a story. Leaving out a step in an instruction because the child knows it so well that it seems needless to say. Announcing a structure at the start and not following it.
