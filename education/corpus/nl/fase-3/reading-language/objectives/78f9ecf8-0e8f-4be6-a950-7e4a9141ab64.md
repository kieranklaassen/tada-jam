## Official wording

teksten schrijven met verschillende schrijfdoelen: plezier verschaffen, informeren, instrueren, betogen en beschouwen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/SCH/02/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 78f9ecf8-0e8f-4be6-a950-7e4a9141ab64.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing texts with different writing purposes: giving pleasure, informing, instructing, arguing (*betogen*, writing to persuade) and considering (*beschouwen*, looking at a matter from several sides)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a story to amuse younger pupils, an article that informs about a subject, the instructions for a game, a letter that argues with reasons for a longer break, and a short piece that looks from several sides at a question such as whether children should have a phone. Before writing, the child says what the text is for. A child of 10 writes to amuse, to inform, to instruct and to argue, and keeps to one purpose in a text; a child of 12 also writes a piece that gives several sides, and says how that differs from arguing.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is writing, and the colon is followed by five writing purposes: giving pleasure, informing, instructing, arguing and considering. The list carries no "bijv.". The wording does not explain *betogen* or *beschouwen*; the short explanations in the gloss are the pack's. The goal names no kinds of text for the purposes (kinds of text are in NE/SCH/02/02/01/fase3) and sets no text length. It does not say that the child names the purposes.

### Common mistakes

Writing a story whatever the purpose: asked to explain how to do something, the child tells about the time they did it. In a text that argues, stating the opinion without a reason, or with one reason said several times. In a text that is meant to look at several sides, giving only the child's own side.
