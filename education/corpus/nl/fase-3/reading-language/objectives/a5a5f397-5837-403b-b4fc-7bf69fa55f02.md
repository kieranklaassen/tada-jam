## Official wording

zich verplaatsen in een personage en uitleggen hoe een personage zich voelt

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/07/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau a5a5f397-5837-403b-b4fc-7bf69fa55f02.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting oneself in the place of a character and explaining how a character feels

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a chapter in which a character has to choose, for example whether to tell a secret, and says what they would see, think and want in that character's place. The child explains how the character feels at that point and shows what in the text supports it: what the character does, says or does not say. A child of 10 explains a feeling that the text shows plainly and gives a reason from the story; a child of 12 explains mixed or hidden feelings, also of a character who is unlike them or whom they dislike.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: putting oneself in the place of a character (*zich verplaatsen in*, imagining oneself in another's position), and explaining how a character feels. The wording says "een personage" (a character) both times and does not say whether the two parts are about the same character. It does not say whether the explanation is spoken or written, names no kind of story and no text length, and does not say whether the story is read by the child or read aloud. Its cluster is about fiction. Naming emotions in a story is in NE/LE/07/02/02/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Saying how the child would feel and not how this character, with its own past and wishes, feels. Naming the feeling without explaining it from the story. Judging the character ("that was stupid") in place of imagining its position.
