## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Telling classmates about a concert, the child uses the words they use among themselves; telling the same to the head teacher or a visitor, the child says it in standard, polite words and leaves the slang out. In a presentation the child does not say "nice" five times and uses "impressive", "pleasant" and "well made" where they fit. The child rewords a sentence when the first word chosen was too informal for the listeners. A child of 10 knows which words belong with friends and which with an adult they do not know, and changes when reminded; a child of 12 changes between the two without a prompt and varies words to be exact, not only to avoid repeating.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts: matching word choice to conversation partners, and varying in word choice. The brackets name youth language and formal language. The wording says "conversation partners" (*gesprekspartners*) in a goal that the open data lists under speaking, in sub-cluster NE/MT/03/05, titled *Woordgebruik en woordenschat* (word use and vocabulary); NE/MT/03/04/01/fase3 is the goal about adjusting to an audience. The source prints the first verb without *van* ("of") after it, unlike most neighbouring goals; the gloss reads it in the same form as theirs. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Using the words of friends among themselves with a listener who does not know them or finds them rude. Treating youth language as wrong in every situation after a correction. Varying by replacing a word with one that does not mean the same, so that the sentence says something else.
