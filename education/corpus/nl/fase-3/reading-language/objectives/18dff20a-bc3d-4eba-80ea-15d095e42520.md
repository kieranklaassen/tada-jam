## Official wording

lezen van: - informatieve teksten (bijv. formulieren, zaakvakteksten, naslagwerken, internetteksten, schematische overzichten); - instructieve teksten (bijv. een handleiding, routebeschrijving, draaiboek); - betogende teksten (bijv. een recensie over een tv-programma, ingezonden brief, pamflet)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading: - informative texts (e.g. forms, texts for the content subjects (*zaakvakteksten*; subjects such as geography and history), reference works, internet texts, schematic overviews); - instructive texts (e.g. a manual, route description, running order (*draaiboek*, a step-by-step plan for an event)); - persuasive texts (e.g. a review of a TV programme, letter to the editor, pamphlet)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a chapter of the geography textbook with its map and table, looks something up in an encyclopedia or on a website, and reads a form to see what is asked where. The child follows a manual to set up a device or a running order for a class party, and reads a review or a letter to the editor and says what the writer wants the reader to think. A child of 10 reads a route description and follows the route on a map; a child of 12 reads a pamphlet and says what it asks the reader to do.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is reading. Three groups of non-fiction text are named: informative, instructive and persuasive (*betogende*) texts. Everything in brackets follows "bijv." and is an example; none is required. Forms are named as texts to read; filling them in is not named. The goal sets no text length, no reading level and no reading speed. The content-line document's text features for the non-fiction texts of this fase, which give subjects, density of information, structure and style and no text length, are no part of the goal and are quoted in the lane's frame.

### Common mistakes

Skipping the diagram, the table or the caption and reading only the running text. Doing the steps of a manual out of order, or starting before reading what is needed. Taking a review or a letter to the editor for a neutral report and not seeing that it wants to persuade.
