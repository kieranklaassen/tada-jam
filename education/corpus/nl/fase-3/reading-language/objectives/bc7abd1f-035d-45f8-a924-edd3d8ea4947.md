## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know and using terms about language (*taalbegrippen*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While talking about a text or about their own writing, the child uses terms such as "persoonsvorm" (finite verb), "onderwerp" (subject of a sentence), "bijvoeglijk naamwoord" (adjective), "voltooid deelwoord" (past participle), "synoniem" (synonym) and "alinea" (paragraph), for example to say that the finite verb is singular because the subject is. The child marks kinds of words or parts of a sentence and says how they found them. A child of 10 uses terms for kinds of words and finds the finite verb and the subject; a child of 12 also uses terms for parts of a sentence and for texts, and uses them to explain how a word is spelled or why a sentence is worded as it is.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names no terms and sets no number; those above are the pack's examples, and nothing in the wording says which terms belong to this band. Two verbs are used: getting to know and using. The wording does not say for which part of language the terms are (words, sentences, texts, spelling or conversation). The cluster is named *Begrippenlijst* (list of terms), but no list is part of this record. The content-line document prints a list of terms by pair of *groepen*, each term offered from the pair it stands under; the list is no part of the goal, and its column for *groep* 7-8 is quoted in the lane's frame.

### Common mistakes

Taking the first word or words of a sentence for the subject. Mixing up kinds of words and parts of a sentence: calling the subject "the noun". Saying a term without being able to point to an example of it.
