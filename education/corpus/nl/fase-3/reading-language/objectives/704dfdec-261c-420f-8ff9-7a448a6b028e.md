## Official wording

herkennen van zelfstandige alinea's

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising independent paragraphs (*zelfstandige alinea's*, paragraphs that stand on their own)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a text, marks where each paragraph begins and ends, and says for each in a few words what it is about. The child cuts a text into its paragraphs, reads one of them alone, and says whether it can be understood without the others or whether it depends on the one before through words such as "this" or "that is why". A child of 10 sees the paragraphs by blank lines or indents and gives each a heading; a child of 12 says which paragraphs stand on their own and which words link a paragraph to the one before.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is recognising: the goal does not say that the child writes paragraphs or states what a paragraph is about. "Zelfstandige" (independent, standing on its own) is not explained. It can be read as paragraphs that each deal with one part of the subject and can be understood on their own, or simply as the separate paragraphs of a text; the wording does not choose. Subheadings and other features of a text are not named in this statement. It names no kind of text and no text length; its cluster is about non-fiction texts. The goal itself sets no features of the texts; the content-line document's text features for the non-fiction texts of this fase are quoted in the lane's frame, among them "tekst met meerdere eenvoudige, in alinea's beschreven inhoudselementen" (a text with several simple content elements described in paragraphs).

### Common mistakes

Taking every new line for a new paragraph. Seeing a paragraph only by its layout and not being able to say what it is about. Giving the first sentence as what the paragraph is about, also where the core stands elsewhere in it.
