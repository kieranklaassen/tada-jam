## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding coherence through the use of signal words (*signaalwoorden*, words that link sentences) and reference words (*verwijswoorden*, words that refer back, such as "he", "that") (using a memory aid if necessary/desirable)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Explaining how a dyke breach happens, the child says "first", "as a result", "however" and "to sum up", so that the listeners hear the order, the causes and the contrast. After naming "the water board" once, the child says "it" and "this organisation", and names it again after another party has come into the account. The child holds a small card with the parts of the talk and the linking words in the margin, and looks at it between parts. A child of 10 uses words for order and reason ("then", "because", "so"); a child of 12 also uses words for contrast, condition and conclusion, and announces the change from one part of the talk to the next.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Coherence is added by two kinds of words: signal words and reference words. The brackets add the use of a memory aid "if necessary/desirable" (*zo nodig/wenselijk*), printed with a slash; the wording does not say who judges that, or what the memory aid is. The statement names no words and no number of them. The open data lists it under speaking, in sub-cluster NE/MT/03/02, titled *Samenhang* (coherence). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Saying "he", "she" or "that" when the listener cannot know who or what is meant, most often with two persons or things in the account. Using "so" or "therefore" where no reason was given. Looking at the memory aid the whole time and reading it out, so that it replaces the talk instead of supporting it.
