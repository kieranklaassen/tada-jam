## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with texts as everyday means of communication and expression

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads the texts they meet during a day and acts on them: the timetable, a message in a group chat, the instructions for homework, a letter from the sports club, a news report, the rules of a game. The child also reads texts in which someone expresses something, such as a poem by another pupil, the words of a song or a column. A child of 10 uses such texts when they meet them; a child of 12 goes to a text on their own, for example checks a timetable or reads the news about something they heard of.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names no kinds of text; those above are the pack's examples. Texts are named as means of two things: communication and expression. "Omgaan met" (dealing with) is a general verb: the wording does not say what the child does with the texts. The source prints "dagelijks" where the plural noun would take "dagelijkse"; "everyday" is plainly meant. The goal stands under reading pleasure and reading motivation (NE/LE/01).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often see reading as something done with books at school and do not count a message, a timetable or a manual as a text. A child may read such a text correctly and not do what it asks.
