## Official wording

schrijven langere teksten met meerdere inhoudselementen en complexere tekststructuren (bijv. oorzaak-gevolg)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing longer texts with several content elements and more complex text structures (e.g. cause-effect)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a text of several parts about one subject, for example about a flood: what happened, why it happened, what followed from it and what people did afterwards. The child orders the parts so that a cause stands with its effect, and words the link between them. A child of 10 writes about several points and links a cause to its effect; a child of 12 builds the whole text on a structure, such as cause and effect, and keeps to it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. "Langere" (longer) and "complexere" (more complex) are comparatives: the wording does not say than what, and it sets no text length. "Meerdere" (several) sets no number of content elements, and the wording does not explain "inhoudselement" (content element); the pack reads it as one part of what a text says, such as one point or one event. Cause-effect follows "bijv." and is an example of a more complex text structure; no other structure is named. The goal names no kind of text; kinds of text are in NE/SCH/02/02/01/fase3. The source words this as a clause without its subject ("schrijven langere teksten"); writing such texts is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Listing facts one after another without saying how they are linked. Giving as the cause of something what only happened before it. Making the text longer by repeating points or by adding details that do not belong to the subject.
