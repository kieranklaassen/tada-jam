## Official wording

studerend lezen van een tekst om informatie te onthouden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/03/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau f9bf71be-c99d-4200-b7ad-10457de43a7b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading a text in a studying way (*studerend lezen*, reading as one does when studying) in order to remember information

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a text for a test, for example about the water cycle: reads a paragraph, covers it, says or writes what it said, and checks. The child underlines the key terms, makes notes or a small diagram, and asks themselves questions about the text a day later. A child of 10 reads the text again, underlines, and learns from questions that someone else asks; a child of 12 makes their own notes or diagram and tests themselves without being asked.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The purpose is stated: to remember information. The manner is named, *studerend lezen* (reading in a studying way), and not described: the goal names no study techniques, and covering, underlining, notes, diagrams and self-testing above are the pack's examples. It says "een tekst" (a text) and names no kind and no length, and it does not say how much information is to be remembered or for how long. Its cluster is about non-fiction texts. Summarising is in NE/LE/04/01/01/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Reading the text several times and taking the feeling that it is familiar for knowing it. Underlining almost every sentence. Learning sentences by heart without understanding them, so that a question in other words cannot be answered.
