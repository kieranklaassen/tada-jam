## Official wording

schrijven in toenemende mate zelfstandig meerdere tekstversies

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing several versions of a text, increasingly independently

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a first version of a text, hears a reader's questions about it or reads it again a day later, and writes a second version in which something has changed: a part added or removed, the order changed, the opening written anew. The child decides what the next version needs and keeps the versions, so that they can show what is different. A child of 10 writes a second version after comments from someone else and changes sentences and some content; a child of 12 decides alone that a text needs another version and changes structure and content before wording.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal says "meerdere" (several) versions and sets no number. "In toenemende mate zelfstandig" (increasingly independently) gives a direction, not a level: the wording does not say how independently, or by when. It does not say what is changed from one version to the next; what revising looks at is named in NE/SCH/02/05/01/fase3. It sets no text length and names no kind of text. The source words this as a clause without its subject ("schrijven in toenemende mate zelfstandig meerdere tekstversies"); writing several versions of a text is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Copying the first version out neatly and calling it the second. Changing only spelling and leaving content and order as they were. Starting a wholly new text and not improving the first.
