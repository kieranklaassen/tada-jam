## Official wording

schrijven langere zinnen, naast enkelvoudige ook samengestelde zinnen, grammaticaal complexer; maken weinig spelfouten en grammaticale fouten

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing longer sentences, besides simple ones also composite sentences (*samengestelde zinnen*, sentences of more than one clause), grammatically more complex; making few spelling errors and grammatical errors

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes sentences with a main clause and a subordinate clause, such as "Hoewel het regende, gingen we naar buiten." or "De jongen die naast mij zit, komt uit Groningen." The child reads each long sentence back, splits one that has become too long to follow, and checks the verb forms and the word order. A child of 10 writes sentences with "omdat", "toen" and "die" and finds errors when reading the text again; a child of 12 writes sentences of several clauses, also with "hoewel", "zodat" or "terwijl", and makes few errors.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. "Langere" (longer) and "complexer" (more complex) are comparatives: the wording does not say than what, and it sets no sentence length and no number of clauses. Two kinds of sentence are named, simple and composite; the goal names no conjunctions and no kinds of clause, and the Dutch sentences and words above are the pack's examples. After the semicolon it says few spelling errors and grammatical errors: "weinig" (few) sets no number or share, and the wording names no spelling categories and does not say whether this holds for a first version or for an edited text. Editing one's own text for spelling and punctuation is in NE/SCH/02/06/02/fase3. The source words both parts as clauses without their subject ("schrijven langere zinnen", "maken weinig spelfouten"); writing such sentences and making few errors is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Not finishing a long sentence in the way it was begun: a clause is opened and not closed, or the verb no longer fits its subject. Joining whole sentences with commas where a full stop is needed. Writing a form from speech that counts as an error in writing, such as "hun hebben" for "zij hebben" (the pack's example).
