## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child watches a news item about a plan for a wind farm near a village and says who is for, who is against and which reason each gives. The child listens to a part of a discussion programme and says what the question was, which speakers disagreed and about what. With a partner the child draws two columns and puts each speaker's main argument in one of them. A child of 10 tells what the item is about and names opposing points of view when they are stated plainly; a child of 12 also follows speakers who interrupt each other and tells an argument from a claim without a reason.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement lists two things to understand: news and societal subjects with different points of view, and discussion programmes. The wording leaves open whether "with different points of view" belongs to the societal subjects only or also to the news. The verb is "understanding" alone: the statement does not ask the child to judge the points of view or to choose one. The open data lists the goal under listening, in sub-cluster NE/MT/02/03, titled *Luisteren naar radio, televisie en gesproken tekst op internet* (listening to radio, television and spoken text on the internet). The statement sets no length and no amount of time with media, and does not mention help. The content-line document's text features for listening at this fase, which are no part of the goal and are quoted in the lane's frame, name the kinds of text ("informatieve, verhalende, instructieve en betogende teksten": informative, narrative, instructive and argumentative texts) and their length ("informatieve teksten: 10 tot 15 minuten", "verhalende teksten: tot 20 minuten": informative texts 10 to 15 minutes, narrative texts up to 20 minutes).

### Common mistakes

Taking the point of view of the last or the loudest speaker for the conclusion of the programme. Hearing a point of view as a fact when it is stated firmly. Remembering who argued with whom and not what the disagreement was about.
