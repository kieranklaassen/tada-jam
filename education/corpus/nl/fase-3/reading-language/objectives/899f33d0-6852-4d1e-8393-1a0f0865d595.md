## Official wording

uitwisselen van leeservaringen op basis van emotieve argumenten en reageren op leeservaringen van medeleerlingen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/07/03; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 899f33d0-6852-4d1e-8393-1a0f0865d595.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exchanging reading experiences on the basis of emotive arguments (*emotieve argumenten*, reasons from what the reader felt) and responding to the reading experiences of fellow pupils

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group of pupils, the child tells what reading a book was like: where they could not stop, where they were moved or laughed, where they lost interest. The child listens to another pupil's experience and responds to it: asks what made that part frightening, and says where they felt the same or something else. A child of 10 tells their own experience and says whether they had the same; a child of 12 asks further, compares the two experiences and says what in the text may explain the difference.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has two parts: exchanging reading experiences, and responding to the reading experiences of fellow pupils (*medeleerlingen*). The exchange is "op basis van emotieve argumenten" (on the basis of emotive arguments); the wording does not explain the term and gives no examples here. Examples of such arguments are given in NE/LE/07/02/03/fase3. Fellow pupils are named in the second part. The wording does not say whether the pupils have read the same text or whether the exchange is spoken or written, and it names no kind of text; its cluster is about fiction.

### Common mistakes

Waiting for their own turn and not responding to what the other pupil said. Retelling what happens in the book in place of telling what reading it was like. Calling another pupil's experience wrong ("that was not scary at all") and not asking what caused it.
