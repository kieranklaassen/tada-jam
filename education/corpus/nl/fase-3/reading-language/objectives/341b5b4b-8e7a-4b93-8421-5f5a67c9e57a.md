## Official wording

vertellen voor bekend en onbekend publiek: - over een verhaal, gebeurtenis, activiteit of ervaring; - een boekbespreking houden; - uitleg geven aan medeleerlingen; - een presentatie houden (bijv. over een actueel onderwerp); - een voorbereid betoog houden met eigen mening en argumenten

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

telling to a familiar and unfamiliar audience: - about a story, event, activity or experience; - giving a book talk (*boekbespreking*, presenting a book one has read); - giving explanation to fellow pupils; - giving a presentation (e.g. about a topical subject); - giving a prepared argued talk (*betoog*, a talk that argues for a view) with one's own opinion and arguments

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands before a class of younger pupils they do not know and explains how to borrow a book from the school library. On another day the child gives a presentation to their own class about a subject from the news and answers questions. In a prepared argued talk the child states an opinion, "Our school should start an hour later", gives three arguments and ends with a conclusion. A child of 10 gives a prepared talk with a key-word card and keeps to the card; a child of 12 puts the arguments of an argued talk in a chosen order and departs from the card when needed.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The audience is "familiar and unfamiliar" (*bekend en onbekend*). Five kinds of telling are listed: telling about a story, event, activity or experience; giving a book talk; giving explanation to fellow pupils; giving a presentation; and giving a prepared argued talk with one's own opinion and arguments. A topical subject follows "e.g." and is an example of a subject for the presentation; "prepared" is printed with the argued talk only. The statement sets no length and no size of audience, and does not mention help. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/03/01, titled *Een monoloog houden* (holding a monologue).

### Common mistakes

In an argued talk, giving the opinion several times in other words instead of arguments. Assuming that an unfamiliar audience knows what the child's own class knows. Reading a written-out text aloud word for word without looking at the audience.
