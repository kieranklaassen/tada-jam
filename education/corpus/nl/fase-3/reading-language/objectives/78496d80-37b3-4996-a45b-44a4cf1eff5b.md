## Official wording

benoemen hoofdgedachte en selecteren belangrijke informatie uit de tekst

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming the main idea and selecting important information from the text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a spoken text about why bees are disappearing, the child says in one sentence what the text was mainly about and what it said about that. From the notes made while listening the child underlines the three points that a classmate who missed the text would need, and crosses out the examples and side matters. The child compares the choice with a partner's and gives a reason for each point kept. A child of 10 names the main idea when the text states it plainly at the start or the end, and selects information with a guiding question; a child of 12 words a main idea that the text does not state in one sentence, and tells main points from examples and repetitions.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts: naming the main idea, and selecting important information from the text. It does not say what makes information important, how much is selected or what kind of text is meant. The content-line document's text features for listening at this fase, which are no part of the goal and are quoted in the lane's frame, name the kinds of text ("informatieve, verhalende, instructieve en betogende teksten": informative, narrative, instructive and argumentative texts) and their length ("informatieve teksten: 10 tot 15 minuten", "verhalende teksten: tot 20 minuten": informative texts 10 to 15 minutes, narrative texts up to 20 minutes). "Text" here is a text that is listened to, since the open data lists the goal under listening, in sub-cluster NE/MT/02/05, titled *Begrijpen en interpreteren* (understanding and interpreting). The source prints both verbs without *van* ("of") after them and "main idea" without an article, unlike most neighbouring goals; the gloss reads them in the same form as theirs. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Naming the topic ("it is about bees") where the main idea is asked for. Selecting what was striking or funny and not what the main idea needs. Taking the first sentence of every text for its main idea.
