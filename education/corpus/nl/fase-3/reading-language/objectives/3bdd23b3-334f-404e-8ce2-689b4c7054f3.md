## Official wording

kritisch kijken naar eigen en andermans teksten om te bepalen of schrijfdoelen zijn behaald

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/SCH/02/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 3bdd23b3-334f-404e-8ce2-689b4c7054f3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

looking critically at one's own and other people's texts to determine whether writing purposes have been achieved

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gives their written instructions for a game to another child and watches whether the game can be played from them, and reads their own persuasive letter again a day later and asks whether it would convince the person it is for. With another pupil's text, the child first says what the text is for and then marks the places where it does and does not reach that purpose. A child of 10 says whether a text was clear and points to a place; a child of 12 names the purpose, checks the text against it and proposes a change.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The texts are the child's own and those of others; "andermans" (someone else's) does not say whose. The looking has one stated aim: to determine whether writing purposes have been achieved; purposes are named in NE/SCH/02/01/02/fase3. The wording mentions no help. It does not say that the text is rewritten afterwards; revising is in NE/SCH/02/05/01/fase3 and writing several versions in NE/SCH/02/05/03/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Judging the text by its length, its handwriting or its spelling and not by its purpose. Finding their own text clear because they know what they meant. Saying only "good" about another's text, or only pointing out mistakes.
