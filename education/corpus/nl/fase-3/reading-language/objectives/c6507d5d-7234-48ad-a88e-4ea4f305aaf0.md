## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and understanding signal words that indicate cause and effect

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a text that explains why something happens, for example why a dike breaks, and circles words such as "doordat" (because), "daardoor" (because of that), "zodat" (so that), "dus" (so) and "omdat" (because). For each word the child says what the cause is and what the effect is, and draws an arrow from the cause to the effect. A child of 10 does this within one sentence with common words such as "omdat" and "dus"; a child of 12 follows a chain of causes over several sentences, with words such as "daardoor" and "als gevolg van" (as a result of).

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. One function of signal words is named: indicating cause and effect. The goal names no words; the Dutch words above are the pack's examples. Signal words for other relations, such as contrast, order in time or a list, are not named in this statement. Two verbs are used: recognising and understanding. Its cluster is about non-fiction texts. The goal itself sets no features of the texts; the content-line document's text features for the non-fiction texts of this fase are quoted in the lane's frame, among them "verwijzingen staan niet altijd dichtbij" (references do not always stand close by).

### Common mistakes

Reversing cause and effect, most often where the sentence states the effect first: "the road was closed because the river flooded". Taking whatever stands first in the sentence as the cause. Reading past the signal word and remembering the two statements as separate facts.
