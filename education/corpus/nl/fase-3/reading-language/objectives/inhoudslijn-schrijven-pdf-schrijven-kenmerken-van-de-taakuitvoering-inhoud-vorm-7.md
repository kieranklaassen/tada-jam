## Official wording

plaatsen bij elkaar horende informatie in één alinea

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

placing information that belongs together in one paragraph

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes the notes for a text on cards and sorts them on the table by what they are about: for a text about an animal, everything about its food in one pile and everything about where it lives in another. Each pile becomes one paragraph, which begins on a new line. On reading the text again, the child moves a sentence that stands in the wrong paragraph. A child of 10 makes a paragraph for each point when the points are given; a child of 12 decides on the points and splits a paragraph that holds two of them.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. One feature of a text is named: information that belongs together stands in one paragraph (*alinea*). The goal does not say what makes information belong together. It sets no number of paragraphs and no length for a paragraph, and it does not say how a paragraph is marked on the page, for example by a new line or a blank line; the new line in the note above is the pack's example. The source words this with the verb first and without a subject ("plaatsen bij elkaar horende informatie"); placing such information in one paragraph is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Writing the whole text as one block. Beginning a new paragraph where the page or the line seems full, and not where a new point begins. Leaving a sentence at the place where they thought of it and not with the point it belongs to.
