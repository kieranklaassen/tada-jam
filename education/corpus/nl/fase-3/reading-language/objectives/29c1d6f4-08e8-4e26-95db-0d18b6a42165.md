## Official wording

herkennen van verschillende gesprekssoorten met bijbehorende doelen (bijv. discussie over stelling of informatie uitwisselen in interview met deskundige)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising different kinds of conversation with the purposes that belong to them (e.g. a discussion about a proposition (*stelling*, a statement to argue for or against), or exchanging information in an interview with an expert)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to two short recorded conversations, a discussion about a proposition and an interview with a vet, and says which is which and what each is for: to weigh views, or to find something out. Before a group talk starts, the child says "this is a talk to plan something, so at the end we must have a decision". During a talk the child notices that planning has turned into a discussion about opinions and says so. A child of 10 names the kind of conversation from clear features, such as the questions and answers of an interview; a child of 12 also names the purpose and says what that purpose asks of those who take part.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "recognising": the child tells kinds of conversation apart and links each to its purpose; adapting language use to the purpose is the subject of NE/MT/01/02/02/fase3. What follows "e.g." is two examples: a discussion about a proposition, and exchanging information in an interview with an expert. The statement does not list the kinds of conversation or say how many there are. It does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Naming the topic when asked for the kind of conversation ("it is about the school yard"). Taking every talk in which people disagree for a quarrel, and not seeing a discussion as a way to weigh views. Recognising the kind and not the purpose: knowing that it is an interview, and asking questions without knowing what information is wanted.
