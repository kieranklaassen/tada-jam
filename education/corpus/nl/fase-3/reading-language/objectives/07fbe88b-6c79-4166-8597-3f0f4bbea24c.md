## Official wording

maken gebruik van context (kennis van de wereld) bij het opvullen van hiaten in de tekst

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/02/05; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 07fbe88b-6c79-4166-8597-3f0f4bbea24c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making use of context (knowledge of the world) when filling in gaps in the text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears "She looked at the sky, went back inside and came out with an umbrella" and says that she expected rain, though the text does not say so. In a spoken news item that says only "the match was stopped because of the smoke", the child says, from what they know about football matches, that spectators probably lit fireworks. The child says which part of the answer was in the text and which part they added from their own knowledge. A child of 10 fills a gap about everyday matters; a child of 12 fills gaps about less familiar matters, names the knowledge used and checks the inference against the next part of the text.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The means is context, and the brackets name knowledge of the world. The statement does not say what kinds of gaps are meant (something left unsaid, a part not heard, an unknown word) and gives no examples. "Text" here is a text that is listened to, since the open data lists the goal under listening, in sub-cluster NE/MT/02/05, titled *Begrijpen en interpreteren* (understanding and interpreting). The source prints the verb in the finite form (*maken gebruik van*), where most neighbouring goals print an infinitive; the gloss reads it in the same form as theirs. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Filling a gap from the child's own experience in a way that the text contradicts. Not noticing that there is a gap, and so being unable to answer a question whose answer the text does not state. Being unable to tell afterwards what the text said from what they added themselves.
