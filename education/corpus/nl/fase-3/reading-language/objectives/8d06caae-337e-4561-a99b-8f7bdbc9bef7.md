## Official wording

relaties leggen tussen een verhaal en de abstractere werkelijkheid

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/07/02; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 8d06caae-337e-4561-a99b-8f7bdbc9bef7.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

drawing relations between a story and the more abstract reality

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story about a child who flees from a war, the child says what the story shows about things that exist outside it and cannot be pointed at: injustice, loyalty, freedom, what a war does to a family. The child links a passage to something known from the news, from a history lesson or from the lives of other people, and says what the link is. A child of 10 links the story to events and people they know of; a child of 12 names a general matter, such as discrimination or power, and says how the story shows it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The two sides of the relation are a story and "de abstractere werkelijkheid" (the more abstract reality). "Abstractere" is a comparative: the wording does not say more abstract than what, or how abstract, and it does not explain what the more abstract reality holds. The pack reads it as reality beyond what a child can see or has lived through, such as ideas, society and other times; that reading and the subjects above are the pack's. The goal names no kind of story and does not say whether the story is read by the child or read aloud. It does not ask for a judgement of whether the story is realistic. Its cluster is about fiction.

### Common mistakes

Staying with an experience of their own ("I also have a brother") where a wider link is possible. Naming a large word, such as war or friendship, without saying what the story shows about it. Taking everything in a realistic story as having really happened.
