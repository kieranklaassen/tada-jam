## Official wording

deelnemen aan geplande en ongeplande gesprekken: - om informatie uit te wisselen (bijv. in gesprek of interview met deskundigen); - voor discussie en overleg (bijv. over een actueel onderwerp of om plannen te maken voor activiteiten)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking part in planned and unplanned conversations: - to exchange information (e.g. in a conversation or interview with experts); - for discussion and consultation (*overleg*, talking over what to do) (e.g. about a topical subject or to make plans for activities)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child interviews a beekeeper who visits the class: asks the questions prepared beforehand, listens to the answers and asks a further question about an answer that was not expected. In a group of five the child discusses a subject from the news, gives a view with a reason and responds to a classmate's reason. With classmates the child talks over the plan for a sports day and helps the group decide who does what. A child of 10 keeps to the prepared questions; a child of 12 departs from the list when an answer invites it, weighs the reasons of others in a discussion and words what the group has agreed.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two purposes are named: exchanging information, and discussion and *overleg*. What follows "e.g." in each pair of brackets is an example: a conversation or interview with experts for the first purpose; a topical subject and making plans for activities for the second. The statement covers planned and unplanned conversations alike, and sets no group size, no length and no number of turns. It does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

In an interview, reading out the next prepared question without listening to the answer just given. In a discussion, repeating one's own view more loudly instead of responding to the other's reason. When planning, agreeing with the first proposal so that the talk ends, without checking that everyone knows what was decided.
