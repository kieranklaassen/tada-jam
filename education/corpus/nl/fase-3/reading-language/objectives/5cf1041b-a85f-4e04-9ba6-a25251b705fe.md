## Official wording

oordeel geven over een tekstdeel of tekst

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

giving a judgement about a part of a text or a text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After reading an informative page or a persuasive text, the child says what they think of it, or of one part of it, and why: the explanation is clear, the examples help, the second paragraph is hard to follow, the writer gives only one side. The child points to the place the judgement is about. A child of 10 says whether the text was clear or interesting and names a part; a child of 12 says what they judge by, such as whether the text does what it is meant to do or whether the arguments are sound, and gives places in the text as evidence.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The object is "een tekstdeel of tekst" (a part of a text or a text). The wording does not say what the judgement is based on and does not mention giving reasons; what the child judges by in the paragraph above is the pack's example. It does not say whether the judgement is spoken or written, and it names no kind of text; its cluster is about non-fiction texts. Evaluating with emotive arguments is named in NE/LE/07/02/03/fase3, in a cluster about fiction.

### Common mistakes

Judging the subject and not the text: "I do not like spiders". Saying "good" or "boring" with no reason. Judging a text only by its length or its pictures.
