## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

paying attention to the reliability of sources

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

When the child looks something up, they ask who made the source, when, and for what purpose: an encyclopedia, a news site, the page of a shop, someone's own video channel. The child compares what two sources say about the same thing and notes where each piece of information came from. A child of 10 asks who wrote something when reminded, and sees that an advertisement is not a neutral source; a child of 12 checks a surprising claim in a second source and looks at the maker and the date without being asked.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is "aandacht hebben voor" (paying attention to): it asks for attention and names no way of checking and no signs of reliability; the questions above are the pack's examples. "Bronnen" (sources) is general: the wording names no kinds of source and does not limit them to the internet. Its cluster is about non-fiction texts. Search engines are named in NE/LE/04/02/01/fase3 and relating sources to one another in NE/LE/03/02/01/fase3.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out asking who made a source and why: they take the first search result, or a page that looks well made, as reliable. Many take a claim for true because it stands in several places that copy one another.
