## Official wording

gebruikmaken van (tussen)kopjes

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making use of (sub)headings

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gives each part of a longer text a short heading that says what the part is about, for example "Voedsel" (food) and "Leefgebied" (habitat) in a text about wolves. The child checks that everything under a heading belongs to it and moves what does not. A child of 10 writes a word or a question above each part; a child of 12 chooses headings of one kind for the whole text and uses them to check the order of the parts.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. The verb is making use of. "(Tussen)kopjes" is printed with its first part in brackets: *kopjes* are headings and *tussenkopjes* are headings between the parts of a text; the wording does not tell levels of headings apart. The goal names no kinds of text, sets no number of headings and no text length, and does not say whether the text is written by hand or on a computer. The Dutch headings above are the pack's examples. Headings are also an example of layout in NE/SCH/02/06/01/fase3 and are named in the brackets of NE/SCH/02/05/04/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Writing a heading that does not match what stands under it. Mixing kinds of heading in one text: a question, then a single word, then a whole sentence. Beginning the part under a heading with "Dit" (this) or "Dat" (that), so that the first sentence cannot be understood without the heading.
