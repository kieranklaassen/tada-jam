## Official wording

(met hulp) oriënteren en reflecteren op verschillende aspecten van de uitvoering van de leestaak

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) getting an orientation on and reflecting on various aspects of carrying out the reading task

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before a reading task the child says, with help, what the task asks, what kind of text it is, how much time there is and how they will go about it. Afterwards the child looks back: whether that way worked, what was hard (the words, the length, the question), and what they would do differently next time. A child of 10 says, with help, that the words of a text were hard and what they will do about that next time; a child of 12 says, with help, that they read a study text too fast and what they will change.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help), which the content-line document prints after the two verbs ("oriënteren en reflecteren (met hulp) op ..."); it does not say from whom. Two verbs are used, both about "verschillende aspecten van de uitvoering van de leestaak" (various aspects of carrying out the reading task). The wording names none of the aspects; those above are the pack's examples. The goal is about looking at how one reads, not about understanding a particular text. Its cluster is about the approach to non-fiction texts. Applying reading strategies independently is in NE/LE/05/01/01/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Telling what the text was about when asked how the reading went. Saying "it went well" or "it was hard" and naming no aspect. Looking only at the text ("it was boring") and not at their own way of reading.
