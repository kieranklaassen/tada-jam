## Official wording

initiëren, leiden en afsluiten van een gesprek in kleine groep

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

initiating, leading and closing a conversation in a small group

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group of four the child opens the talk: says what it is about and what must be reached, and asks the first question. During the talk the child gives turns, asks a quiet classmate for a view, brings the group back when it leaves the topic and watches the time. At the end the child says what was agreed, asks whether everyone can accept it and closes the talk. A child of 10 leads with a card that lists the steps and gives turns; a child of 12 leads without a card, summarises between parts and closes with a decision or a clear statement of what remains open.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three acts are named: initiating, leading and closing. The setting is a small group; the statement sets no number for it and does not say what kind of conversation. It does not say whether one child does all three in one conversation. It does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Leading by giving the most opinions and deciding alone, instead of letting the others speak. Starting without saying what the talk is for, so that the group does not know when it is finished. Stopping when the time is over without saying what was decided.
