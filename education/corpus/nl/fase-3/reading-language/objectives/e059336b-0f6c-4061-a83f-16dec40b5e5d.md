correcting one's own errors and falterings

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

During a presentation the child says "in 1845 ... no, 1945", and goes on. The child no longer knows what comes next, looks at the card, says "I will go back one step" and continues. After halting on a difficult word, the child says it again slowly. A child of 10 corrects an error they notice by starting the sentence again; a child of 12 corrects briefly and calmly, uses the card to find the place, and does not stop for a small slip that does not hinder the audience.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are corrected: one's own errors and falterings. The statement does not say which errors (of grammar, of word choice, of content) and gives no examples. NE/MT/01/06/01/fase3 is the matching goal for conversations, which prints "own" in brackets. The open data lists this goal under speaking, in sub-cluster NE/MT/03/06, whose title names fluency, intelligibility and grammatical control. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Stopping completely after an error, or starting the whole talk again from the beginning. Apologising at length or laughing after each slip, which draws more attention than the slip did. Not noticing an error of content, such as a wrong year, because the attention is on remembering the text.
