## Official wording

afleiden van de belangrijkste boodschap uit een tekst en geven deze kort weer

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/02/07; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 21f71924-31a9-470b-a226-481e4e9a4e32.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

inferring the most important message from a text and stating it briefly

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a spoken fable the child says in one or two sentences what the story wants to tell: "If you always let others do your work, nobody helps you when you need it". After a spoken text about sleep the child tells a partner in a few sentences what the speaker most wanted the listeners to know. The child compares this with the partner's version, and the two agree on the shortest wording that still covers it. A child of 10 states the message of a text that nearly says it outright; a child of 12 infers a message that the text does not state and words it themselves.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts: inferring the most important message from a text, and stating it briefly. The verb of the first part is "inferring", so the message need not be stated in the text; "briefly" is not given a length. The open data lists the goal under listening, in sub-cluster NE/MT/02/07, titled *Samenvatten* (summarising), so the text is one that is listened to. The source prints the second verb in the finite form (*geven deze kort weer*) after an infinitive in the first part; the gloss reads both in the same form. NE/MT/02/05/01/fase3 is the goal about naming the main idea and selecting important information. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Retelling the text from the beginning in order, instead of stating the message. Giving the topic ("it was about sleep") or a striking detail as the message. Giving their own opinion about the subject in place of the message of the text.
