## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

correcting (one's own) errors and falterings

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk the child says "he have ... he has seen it", or stops halfway through a sentence that went wrong and begins it again. The child says the wrong name, notices it and says "no, I mean Samira". After halting to search for a word, the child describes the thing in other words and goes on. A child of 10 corrects a wrong word or a wrong name when a partner looks puzzled; a child of 12 hears the error while speaking, corrects it briefly and continues, and does not stop for a small slip that does not hinder understanding.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are corrected: errors and falterings. The source prints "own" (*eigen*) in brackets, so the wording leaves open whether the errors and falterings of others are also meant; NE/MT/03/06/03/fase3, the matching goal for speaking, prints "own" without brackets. The statement does not say which errors (of grammar, of word choice, of content) and gives no examples. The source prints the verb without *van* ("of") after it, unlike most neighbouring goals; the gloss reads it in the same form as theirs. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Not hearing one's own error, and going on while the partners have understood something else. Starting the same sentence again several times, so that the correction hinders the partners more than the error did. Correcting a classmate's slip aloud during the conversation, in a way that stops that classmate from speaking.
