## Official wording

samenvatten van gedichten en verhaalfragmenten in eigen woorden

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

summarising poems and story fragments in one's own words

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a poem, or a passage of a few pages from a story, closes the book, and tells or writes in a few sentences what it is about. For a story fragment the child says who does what, where and why; for a poem the child says what the poem speaks of and what thought or feeling it carries. A child of 10 retells the main events of a fragment in order and shortened; a child of 12 gives only the core and, for a poem, says what it is about where the poem does not say so directly.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two kinds of text are named: poems and story fragments (*verhaalfragmenten*, parts of a story). Whole stories and whole books are not named. "In eigen woorden" (in one's own words) is the one stated condition. The goal sets no length for the fragment or for the summary and does not say whether the summary is spoken or written. Its cluster is about fiction; summarising with the help of strategies is in NE/LE/04/01/01/fase3, in a cluster about non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Retelling everything in the same order, a little shorter. Copying lines from the poem or sentences from the story and not using their own words. For a poem, listing what is in it (a bird, a window, rain) without saying what the poem is about.
