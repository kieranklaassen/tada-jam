## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising elements of structure, such as changes of time and place, rhyme and verse form

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a story that goes back to an earlier time (a flashback) or moves to another place, marks each spot where the time or the place changes, and says what shows it: a blank line, a new chapter, a date, another verb tense. In a poem the child marks the words that rhyme, writes the rhyme scheme with letters (a b a b), and counts the lines and stanzas. A child of 10 finds changes that the text marks plainly and rhyme at the ends of lines; a child of 12 follows a flashback that is not announced and says how a poem is built, also when it does not rhyme.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is recognising. "Zoals" (such as) introduces examples of elements of structure: changes of time and place, rhyme and verse form. The wording does not say which of them belong to stories and which to poems, and it does not say that the child uses terms for them; "flashback", "rhyme scheme" and "stanza" above are the pack's words. The goal names no kind of text and no text length; its cluster is about fiction. The goal itself sets no features of the texts; the content-line document's text features for the fiction of this fase are quoted in the lane's frame, among them "teksten met wisselende vertelperspectieven die duidelijk aangegeven zijn" (texts with changing narrative perspectives that are clearly marked).

### Common mistakes

Reading on past a change of time that is not marked and taking the earlier events as happening now. Finding rhyme only where the endings are spelled the same and missing words that rhyme by sound, such as "trein" and "fijn". Taking each line of a poem for a sentence and stopping at every line end.
