## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using abstract words of school language and subject language (*school- en vaktaalwoorden*: general lesson words and subject terms)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk after a history lesson the child says "cause", "consequence" and "development", and not only "that happened and then that". Comparing two solutions of a sum with a partner, the child says "ratio", "on average" and "it depends on". The child hears the adult rephrase "it gets less and less" as "it decreases gradually" and uses "decrease" in a later contribution. A child of 10 uses abstract school words that have just been offered, with the meaning roughly right; a child of 12 uses them without a prompt in a conversation about another subject and can say what a word means.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names two kinds of words in one compound and defines neither: *schooltaal* is commonly understood as the language of lessons and tasks across subjects, and *vaktaal* as the terms of one subject. The words are called abstract; the statement does not say what makes a word abstract, and names no words, no subjects and no number of words. The source prints the verb without *van* ("of") after it, unlike most neighbouring goals; the gloss reads it in the same form as theirs. The open data lists the goal under conversations, in sub-cluster NE/MT/01/05, titled *Woordgebruik en woordenschat* (word use and vocabulary); NE/MT/03/05/02/fase3 is the matching goal for speaking.

### Common mistakes

Understanding an abstract word in an instruction and still using an everyday phrase when speaking ("what came out of it" for "consequence"). Using an abstract word with only part of its meaning, such as "cause" for anything that happened earlier. Using the word in the lesson where it was taught and not in a conversation about another subject.
