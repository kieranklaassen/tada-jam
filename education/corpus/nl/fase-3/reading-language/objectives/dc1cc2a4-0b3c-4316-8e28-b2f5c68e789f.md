## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Phoning a museum to ask about a class visit, the child says *goedemiddag*, gives their name, says *u* and *mevrouw* or *meneer*, and closes with thanks. An hour later the child says *hoi* and *je* to a classmate about the same visit. In an interview with the mayor the child keeps saying *u* through the whole conversation and says "Could you tell us ..." rather than "Tell us ...". A child of 10 uses *u* and a formal greeting where it has been practised; a child of 12 judges from the partner and the situation which form fits, keeps to it, and also adapts word choice and sentence form.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three things are to be applied: forms of address, formal language use and informal language use. The source prints "correctly" (*correct*) at the end of the phrase, where it can be read with all three. The statement does not say what counts as correct, names no situations and no forms of address, and gives no examples. NE/MT/03/04/01/fase3 and NE/MT/03/05/03/fase3 are goals for speaking that name formal situations and formal language. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Starting with *u* and changing to *je* after a few sentences. Taking formal language to be *u* only, and keeping the word choice used among friends. Treating informal language as wrong language, and speaking stiffly to classmates after a lesson about formal language.
