## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

applying simple and more complex conversation skills (e.g. summarising)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk the child waits for a turn, listens and responds to what was said (the skills in this note other than summarising are the pack's examples). Halfway through, the child says "So far we have two ideas: ... and ...", and asks whether that is right. The child asks a further question about a vague answer and asks a silent classmate what they think. A child of 10 takes turns, responds to the content and asks a further question; a child of 12 also summarises what several speakers said and checks whether they understood a speaker correctly.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names two levels of conversation skills, simple and more complex, and does not say which skills belong to which; summarising follows "e.g." and is the only example. The verb is "applying". NE/MT/01/03/03/fase3 is the goal about initiating, leading and closing a conversation, and NE/MT/01/08/01/fase3 gives asking follow-up questions and asking for clarification as examples of conversation rules. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Summarising by repeating the last speaker's words, or by giving one's own opinion as the summary. Asking a further question that was already answered, because the child was preparing the question instead of listening. Applying a skill when it is the task of the lesson and not in an ordinary conversation.
