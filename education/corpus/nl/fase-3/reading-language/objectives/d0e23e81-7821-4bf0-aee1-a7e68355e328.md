## Official wording

eigen mening geven over de inhoud en vorm van een tekst en daarbij verwijzen naar voorbeelden in een tekst

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/02/08; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau d0e23e81-7821-4bf0-aee1-a7e68355e328.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

giving one's own opinion about the content and form of a text and, in doing so, referring to examples in a text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a classmate's presentation the child says "I found the part about the training clear, because you showed each step with a photo, but the ending was sudden: you stopped after the last fact". After a radio item the child says whether the reasons convinced them and names one reason that did and one that did not. The child keeps two things apart: what was said, and how it was built and presented. A child of 10 gives an opinion about the content with an example from the text and says of the form whether it was nice or dull; a child of 12 gives an opinion on both, points to places in the text, and says what the form did to the content.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The opinion is about two things, the content and the form of a text, and the child refers to examples in a text while giving it. The statement does not say what belongs to form (structure, word choice, delivery) and sets no number of examples. The text is one that is listened to, since the open data lists the goal under listening, in sub-cluster NE/MT/02/08, titled *Evalueren* (evaluating). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Giving an opinion without an example from the text ("it was good"). Giving an opinion about the speaker as a person, or about the subject, when the text is asked about. Leaving out the form, or taking form to be only the pictures or the speaker's voice.
