## Official wording

schrijven in toenemende mate zelfstandig teksten waarin woorden, formuleringen en stijlmiddelen worden gebruikt die zijn afgestemd op het schrijfdoel

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing, increasingly independently, texts in which words, formulations and stylistic devices (*stijlmiddelen*) are used that are matched to the writing purpose

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before writing, the child says what the text is for and chooses the wording to fit it: in a letter that is meant to persuade the head teacher, polite and firm formulations and a question that makes the reader think; in the instructions for a game, short commands; in a story, a comparison or an exaggeration. The child reads the text again and replaces words and formulations that do not fit the purpose. A child of 10 does this when reminded of the purpose; a child of 12 does it alone and says why a formulation fits.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. "In toenemende mate zelfstandig" (increasingly independently) gives a direction, not a level: the wording does not say how independently, or by when. Three things are matched to the writing purpose: words, formulations and stylistic devices. The goal names no purposes (they are named in NE/SCH/02/01/02/fase3), no stylistic devices and no list of words or formulations; the devices above are the pack's examples. It sets no text length and names no kind of text. The source words this as a clause without its subject ("schrijven in toenemende mate zelfstandig teksten"); writing such texts is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Writing every text in the same way, often in the words of speech or of chat messages, whatever the text is for. Using a stylistic device because it has been learned, in a text where it does not serve the purpose, such as an exaggeration in a text that informs. In a text meant to persuade, putting strong words in the place of arguments.
