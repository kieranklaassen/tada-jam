## Official wording

begrijpen van de bedoeling van schrijvers en aan elkaar relateren van verschillende bronnen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

understanding the intention of writers and relating different sources to one another

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads two or three sources on one subject, for example a news report, a page of an action group and an encyclopedia entry about wolves in the Netherlands, and says what each writer wants: to inform, to persuade, to amuse or to sell. The child lays the sources side by side and marks what they agree on, where one adds something, and where they contradict each other. A child of 10 says what each text is for and finds something that the sources agree on; a child of 12 explains a difference between sources from what each writer wants.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two parts are joined by "en" (and): understanding the intention of writers, and relating different sources to one another. The wording does not say how the two parts belong together. It names no intentions, no kinds of source and no number of sources; those above are the pack's examples. It mentions no help. Its cluster is about non-fiction texts. The reliability of sources is in NE/LE/03/03/03/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Naming the subject of the text when asked what the writer wants. Reading each source on its own and not noticing that two sources contradict each other. When sources differ, taking the one read last as the right one without a reason.
