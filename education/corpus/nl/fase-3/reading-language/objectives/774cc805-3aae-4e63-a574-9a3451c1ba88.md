## Official wording

(met hulp) identificeren van het beoogde publiek van een tekst

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/03/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 774cc805-3aae-4e63-a574-9a3451c1ba88.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) identifying the intended audience of a text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gets texts on one subject written for different readers, for example a leaflet about dental care for young children, one for parents and a page for dental assistants, and says, with help, for whom each is written. The child points to what shows it: the words used, how the reader is addressed ("je" or "u"), the pictures, and where the text was published. A child of 10 tells a text for children from a text for adults; a child of 12 names a narrower audience, such as beginners or the members of a club, and gives signs from the text.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help), which the content-line document prints at the end ("... van een tekst (met hulp)"); it does not say from whom. The verb is identifying and the object is the intended audience of a text. The goal names no kinds of text and no signs that show an audience; those above are the pack's examples. It does not ask the child to judge whether the text suits that audience. Its cluster is about non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Naming the subject of the text when asked for whom it is written. Answering "for everyone" or "for people who want to know this". Deciding by the pictures alone, so that every text with drawings is said to be for young children.
