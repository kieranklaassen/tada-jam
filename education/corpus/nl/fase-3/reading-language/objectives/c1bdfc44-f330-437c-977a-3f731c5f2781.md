## Official wording

vragen stellen over woordbetekenis wanneer dat het luisteren/begrip belemmert

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

asking questions about word meaning when that hinders listening/understanding

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

During a spoken explanation about parliament the child hears "coalition" several times, cannot follow without it and asks what it means at the first pause. Listening to a recording, the child writes the unknown word down and asks about it afterwards, with the sentence it was in. The child does not ask about an unknown word that does not hinder understanding, such as the name of a rare mineral in a list. A child of 10 asks "What does that mean?" about a word that stops them from following; a child of 12 first judges whether the word is needed and whether the text will explain it, and asks an exact question ("Does 'scarce' mean that there is little of it?").

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The condition is part of the statement: the question is asked when not knowing what a word means hinders listening or understanding, so the child is not asked to ask about every unknown word. The wording's "that" points back loosely to the word meaning, and the source prints "listening/understanding" with a slash. NE/MT/02/06/02/fase3 is the goal about inferring the meaning of unknown words. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Noticing the unknown word and staying silent, so as not to be the only one who asks. Not noticing that a known word is used with another meaning, and so not asking. Interrupting for every unknown word, also where the next sentence explains it.
