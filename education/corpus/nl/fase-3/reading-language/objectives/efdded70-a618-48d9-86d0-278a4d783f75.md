## Official wording

zich oriënteren voorafgaand aan het gesprek op soort gesprek en gespreksregels (bijv. doorvragen of vragen om verduidelijking)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting an orientation, before the conversation, on the kind of conversation and on conversation rules (e.g. asking follow-up questions (*doorvragen*, asking further about an answer) or asking for clarification)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before an interview with a guest, the child says what kind of conversation it will be and what it is for, and reads through the prepared questions. The child agrees with the group on the rules for this conversation: who asks which question, that a vague answer gets a further question, and that anyone who does not understand asks for clarification. The child writes one rule on a card and keeps the card on the table. A child of 10 names the kind of conversation and a rule that fits it; a child of 12 also says why the rule fits this kind of conversation and prepares the wording of a follow-up question.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The orientation takes place before the conversation and covers two things: the kind of conversation and conversation rules; asking follow-up questions and asking for clarification follow "e.g." and are examples of such rules. This statement does not carry "with help", which NE/MT/01/08/02/fase3 in the same sub-cluster does. NE/MT/01/02/01/fase3 is the goal about recognising kinds of conversation, and NE/MT/01/08/02/fase3 the goal about reflecting on one's own part in a conversation.

### Common mistakes

Beginning on the content at once when asked what kind of conversation it will be. Naming the topic instead of the kind of conversation. Saying the rule correctly beforehand and not applying it some minutes later, for example accepting a vague answer in the interview.
