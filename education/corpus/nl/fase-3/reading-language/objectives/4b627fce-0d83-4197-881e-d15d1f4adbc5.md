## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

For a presentation about the solar system the child makes a few slides, each with one picture and a few key words, and gets help with choosing what goes on a slide and with the working of the program. While presenting, the child moves to the next slide at the right moment, points at the part of the picture they are speaking about and keeps facing the audience. The child removes a slide that is full of text after a partner says they could not read and listen at the same time. A child of 10 makes slides with help and reads from them; a child of 12 gets help with choices, uses a slide to show what words cannot and tells the content in their own words.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording prints "with help" (*met hulp*) after the phrase and without brackets, where other goals of this lane carry it in brackets, and the brackets that follow name two presentation programs, PowerPoint and Prezi. The wording can be read in two ways: visual support is put to use with help from a person, or with the help of such programs; it does not choose, and it does not say whether the two programs are examples. The content-line document sets "(PowerPoint, Prezi)" in italics, as it sets the examples in the brackets of other goals, which favours reading the two programs as examples. The statement does not say who helps or what the visual support is for. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/03/08, titled *Visuele ondersteuning* (visual support).

### Common mistakes

Putting the whole text on the slides and reading it out with the back to the audience. Spending the preparation time on colours and effects and not on what to say. Showing a picture and saying nothing about it, or leaving a slide in view that no longer fits what is being said.
