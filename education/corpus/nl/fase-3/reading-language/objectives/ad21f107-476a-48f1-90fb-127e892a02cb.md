## Official wording

onderscheiden en vergelijken in een tekst van feiten, meningen en argumenten

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/03/03; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau ad21f107-476a-48f1-90fb-127e892a02cb.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing and comparing facts, opinions and arguments in a text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a letter to the editor, for example about a ban on phones at school, and marks in three colours what can be checked, what the writer thinks, and the reasons the writer gives for it. The child compares them: which argument rests on a fact, which on another opinion, and which opinion has no argument at all. A child of 10 tells facts from opinions and finds the reason that follows "because"; a child of 12 sets the arguments side by side, says which are supported by facts, and finds an opinion that is worded as a fact.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two verbs are used, distinguishing and comparing, and three things are named: facts, opinions and arguments. The wording says "in een tekst" (in a text): one text. It does not say what is compared with what: the three kinds with one another, or several facts, opinions or arguments among themselves. It does not say that the child checks whether a fact is true. Giving a judgement about a text is in NE/LE/03/03/02/fase3 and the reliability of sources in NE/LE/03/03/03/fase3. The goal names no kinds of text; its cluster is about non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Calling a statement a fact because the child agrees with it, and an opinion because the child does not. Calling a false statement an opinion, although a statement that can be checked and is wrong is not an opinion. Taking an argument for a fact because it is stated firmly: "everyone knows that ...".
