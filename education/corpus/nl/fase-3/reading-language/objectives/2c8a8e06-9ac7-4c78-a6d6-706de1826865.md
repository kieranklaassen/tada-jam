## Official wording

opzoeken van informatie met behulp van zoekmachines en het lezen van schema's, grafieken en tabellen en relaties leggen binnen een tekst

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/04/02; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 2c8a8e06-9ac7-4c78-a6d6-706de1826865.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

looking up information with the help of search engines and the reading of diagrams (*schema's*), graphs and tables and drawing relations within a text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child has a question, for example how many people live in a province, types search words into a search engine, chooses a result from the list by its title and web address, and finds the answer on the page. The child reads a table, a graph or a diagram: first the title, the labels of the axes or columns and the unit, then a value or the way a line rises or falls. Within a text the child links one part to another, for example a sentence to the graph it refers to, or a cause in one paragraph to an effect in a later one. A child of 10 searches with words from the question and reads off a value; a child of 12 changes the search words when the results do not fit, compares values and links what the text says to what the graph shows.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three things are joined by "en" (and): looking up information with the help of search engines; the reading of diagrams, graphs and tables; and drawing relations within a text. The Dutch can be read with the reading of diagrams, graphs and tables as a second aid for looking up information, or as an activity of its own; the gloss leaves this open. The wording does not say what kinds of relations within a text are meant; those above are the pack's examples. It names no search engine and no kinds of graph, sets no number range for the graphs and tables, and does not mention judging the results of a search; the reliability of sources is in NE/LE/03/03/03/fase3. Its cluster is about study skills with non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Searching with a whole question or with one very broad word, and taking the first result. Reading a value from a graph or table without looking at the labels and the unit, for example reading a number that stands for thousands as that number of ones. Reading the text and the table apart and not noticing that they are about the same thing.
