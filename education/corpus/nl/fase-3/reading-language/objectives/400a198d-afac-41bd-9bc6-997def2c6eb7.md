## Official wording

houden rekening met verschillen tussen gesprekpartners (bijv. rekening houden met verschil in tempo)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking differences between conversation partners into account (e.g. taking a difference in pace into account)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk the child notices that one classmate needs more time to word a thought, waits and does not finish the sentence for them. Speaking with a child who is learning Dutch, the child uses shorter sentences and checks whether a word was understood. With a partner who speaks fast and much, the child asks for a moment to think. A child of 10 adapts to a difference that is plain or has been pointed out, such as slower speech; a child of 12 notices differences in pace, knowledge, age and ease of speaking without a prompt, and adapts without drawing attention to the difference.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The differences between partners are not listed; a difference in pace follows "e.g." and is the only example, and the statement does not say whether pace means the pace of speaking, of thinking or of working. The source prints the verb in the finite form (*houden rekening*), where most neighbouring goals print an infinitive, and prints *gesprekpartners* without the linking *s*; the gloss reads the verb in the same form as the neighbouring goals. NE/MT/01/04/03/fase3 is the goal about matching a contribution to the partners' interest and prior knowledge. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Filling every silence, so that a slower partner does not get a turn. Adapting in a way that belittles the partner, such as speaking very loudly and slowly or explaining what the partner already knows. Taking a partner who says little for a partner who has nothing to say.
