## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Practising a presentation aloud, the child hears that a long sentence went wrong halfway and makes two short sentences of it. The child practises the difficult words of the subject, such as "photosynthesis", until they can say them clearly, and says word endings in full. During the talk the child says a wrong verb form, hears it and says it again correctly. A child of 10 attends to clear speech in the parts that were practised and makes errors in long sentences; a child of 12 builds sentences that they can finish correctly, also when speaking without a text, and articulates clearly while thinking about the content.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: grammar and articulation. The verb is "paying attention to" (*aandacht hebben voor*): the statement sets no standard for either and does not say which grammar. NE/MT/03/06/01/fase3 is the goal about speaking pace, intonation and volume, and NE/MT/03/06/03/fase3 the goal about correcting one's own errors and falterings; NE/MT/01/06/02/fase3 is the matching goal for conversations, which names four things. The open data lists this goal under speaking (NE/MT/03). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is checking their own sentences while speaking: their attention goes to the content and to remembering the order. Before an audience many speak in the long sentences of their written text and cannot finish them correctly, and mumble the difficult words they have not practised aloud.
