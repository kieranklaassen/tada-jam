## Official wording

ordenen van informatie en structuur aanbrengen m.b.v. een schema

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/03/02; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 32bb2d25-d75b-4dd0-bb78-38c86b8b0aad.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

ordering information and adding structure by means of a *schema* (a diagram, or an outline)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Preparing a presentation about volcanoes, the child writes what they found on slips and lays them in a diagram on a large sheet: the subject in the middle, branches for kinds, causes, consequences and one well-known eruption. The child numbers the branches in the order of the talk, removes two slips that fit nowhere and writes an opening and a close. The child then gives the talk from the diagram. A child of 10 fills in a diagram whose form is given and follows it in order; a child of 12 chooses a form that fits the content, such as a timeline for events or two columns for arguments for and against, and uses it to say at the start how the talk is built.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts, ordering information and adding structure, and one means: a *schema* (the source prints the abbreviation *m.b.v.*, for *met behulp van*, "with the help of"). *Schema* can mean a diagram or an outline, and the wording does not choose; it names no form. The open data lists the goal under speaking, in sub-cluster NE/MT/03/02, titled *Samenhang* (coherence), so the information is for a spoken text. The statement sets no length and does not mention help from a person.

### Common mistakes

Filling the diagram with whole sentences and reading them out. Making the diagram and then telling things in the order in which they come to mind. Putting everything that was found into the diagram, without leaving out what does not serve the talk.
