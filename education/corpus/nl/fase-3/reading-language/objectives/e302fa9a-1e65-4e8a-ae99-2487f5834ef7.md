## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

matching a contribution to the interest and prior knowledge of the conversation partner(s)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Telling a grandparent about a computer game, the child first says what kind of game it is and leaves out the names of levels; telling a classmate who plays it, the child begins at the new part. Before explaining, the child asks "Do you know how a lock in a canal works?" and tells more or less after the answer. Seeing that a partner looks away, the child shortens the account and asks what the partner wants to know. A child of 10 adapts after the partner shows that something is unknown or dull; a child of 12 judges beforehand what each partner knows and cares about, and adapts for a group in which the partners differ.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The contribution is matched to two things: the partner's interest and the partner's prior knowledge. The wording prints "partner(s)", so it covers one partner and several. It gives no examples and does not say how the child finds out what a partner knows or finds interesting. NE/MT/01/04/01/fase3 is the goal about taking differences between partners into account. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Assuming that the partner knows the people, places and terms the child knows. Explaining at length what the partner already knows, after having been told to explain more. Noticing a lack of understanding and not a lack of interest, because a bored partner often stays silent.
