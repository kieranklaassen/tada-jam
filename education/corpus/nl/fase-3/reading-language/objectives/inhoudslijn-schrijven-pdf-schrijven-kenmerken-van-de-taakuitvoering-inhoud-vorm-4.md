## Official wording

aanbrengen van samenhang door de relatie tussen zinnen (en passages) expliciet te maken m.b.v. verschillende taalmiddelen: >verwijswoorden (deze, die..) >voegwoorden (als, hoewel..) >woorden die volgorde aanduiden (eerst, toen, daarna) >woorden die verbanden uitdrukken (bijv. oorzaak-gevolg: daardoor..) >argumentatieve indicatoren (daarom, ten eerste, namelijk, bovendien)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding coherence by making the relation between sentences (and passages) explicit with the help of various language means: >reference words (*deze* "this", *die* "that"..) >conjunctions (*als* "if", *hoewel* "although"..) >words that indicate order (*eerst* "first", *toen* "then", *daarna* "after that") >words that express connections (e.g. cause-effect: *daardoor* "because of that"..) >argumentative indicators (*daarom* "that is why", *ten eerste* "firstly", *namelijk* "namely", *bovendien* "moreover")

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a text in which a word shows how each sentence follows from the one before: "Eerst ... Daarna ..." for the order of steps, "Daardoor ..." for an effect, "Ten eerste ... Bovendien ..." for arguments, and "deze" or "die" for something just named. The child underlines these words in the first version and checks for each one whether it says the link that is meant. A child of 10 uses words of order and "omdat" or "daarom" for a reason; a child of 12 also uses "hoewel", "daardoor", "namelijk" and "bovendien", and chooses between them by the link between the sentences.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. The goal is adding coherence by making the relation between sentences explicit; "(en passages)" (and passages) stands in brackets. After the colon it lists five kinds of language means, each with Dutch words in brackets: reference words; conjunctions; words that indicate order; words that express connections, with cause-effect after "bijv." as an example of a connection; and argumentative indicators. Three of the brackets end with "..", which shows that their words are not the whole list; for the other two (order, argumentative indicators) the brackets name words without "bijv." and without "..", and the pack claims neither that those lists are complete nor that they are only illustrations. The goal sets no number of such words to be used and no text length, and it does not say that the child knows the names of the five kinds. "Omdat" in the note above is the pack's example; the other Dutch words there are from the wording. The record prints each sub-point with ">", the mark the PDF uses.

### Common mistakes

Using a reference word when the reader cannot tell what it refers to. Choosing a word that states another link than the one meant, such as "daarom" (that is why) where the sentence gives the reason and "namelijk" (namely) fits. Opening every sentence with a linking word, "Toen ... Toen ... Daarna ...", also where the order is clear without one.
