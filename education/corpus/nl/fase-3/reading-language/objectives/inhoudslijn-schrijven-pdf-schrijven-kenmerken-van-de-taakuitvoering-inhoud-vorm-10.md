## Official wording

gebruikmaken van spreekwoorden, gezegden en uitdrukkingen in de eigen teksten

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making use of proverbs, sayings and expressions in one's own texts

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child uses a fixed phrase in a text where its meaning fits: in a story, "Hij viel met de deur in huis." for someone who says at once what he has come for; at the end of a text about saving, the proverb "Wie het kleine niet eert, is het grote niet weerd." Before using a phrase, the child asks or looks up what it means and how its words go. A child of 10 uses a common expression such as "de kat uit de boom kijken" in a story; a child of 12 chooses a proverb for the title or the conclusion of a text and writes it in its fixed words.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. The verb is making use of, and the place is the child's own texts. Three kinds of fixed phrase are named: proverbs (*spreekwoorden*), sayings (*gezegden*) and expressions (*uitdrukkingen*). The wording does not explain how the three differ and lists none; the Dutch phrases above are the pack's examples. The goal sets no number of them, names no kind of text and does not say that the child explains what a phrase means.

### Common mistakes

Taking the words of an expression in their literal sense and using it where its meaning does not fit. Changing the fixed words, or joining the halves of two expressions. Putting several expressions in a short text because they have just been learned.
