## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using content words (*inhoudswoorden*, words that name things, actions, properties) and function words (*functiewoorden*, grammatical words such as conjunctions and prepositions) that are specifically related to the topic

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a presentation about the heart the child says "ventricle", "valve", "artery" and "circulation" and says in a few words what each means. Explaining the route of the blood, the child says "via", "towards", "as soon as" and "through which" (the pack's examples of function words that belong to a topic). Before the talk the child lists the words the topic needs and afterwards checks with a partner which were used and which were explained. A child of 10 uses the topic's main terms and explains those the audience does not know; a child of 12 uses them exactly and consistently, and uses the linking words that a description of a process, a comparison or an argument needs.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two kinds of words are named, content words and function words, and both are to be specifically related to the topic. The statement does not say how a function word is related to a topic, and names no words, no topics and no number of words. The open data lists it under speaking, in sub-cluster NE/MT/03/05, titled *Woordgebruik en woordenschat* (word use and vocabulary); NE/MT/03/05/02/fase3 is the goal about abstract school language and subject language. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Using "thing", "that" and "do" where the topic has a word of its own. Using a term the audience does not know without explaining it. Using two words for the same thing, or one word for two things, so that the audience cannot tell which is meant.
