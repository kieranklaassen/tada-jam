## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

consciously putting body posture, eye contact and gestures to use to support the speaking

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before a presentation the child decides where to stand, when to look up from the card and which gesture goes with which point, and practises this for a partner. During the talk the child stands upright facing the audience, looks at listeners in different parts of the room and counts three arguments on the fingers. Afterwards the child says which of the planned means they used and what each did. A child of 10 carries out what was planned at the marked moments; a child of 12 chooses posture, eye contact and gestures for their effect on the audience, and uses them also at moments that were not planned.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three means are named: body posture, eye contact and gestures. Facial expression is not named here; NE/MT/01/07/01/fase3, the matching goal for conversations, names it. The wording says "consciously" (*bewust*), and the role of the means is to support the speaking. The statement does not say how often and does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Looking at the card, the slides or one familiar face the whole time. Swaying, turning or fiddling with the card without being aware of it. Making a planned gesture at the wrong moment, or so stiffly that it distracts from what is said.
