## Official wording

reflecteren in tekstbesprekingen op het schrijfproces (oriënteren, formuleren en reviseren) en het schrijfproduct (structuur, samenhang, stijl, doel, publiek, lay-out, register)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reflecting, in discussions of texts, on the writing process (orienting, formulating and revising) and the written product (structure, coherence, style, purpose, audience, layout, register)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a small group the child lays their text on the table and the group talks about it: whether the structure is clear, whether the sentences and paragraphs link up, whether the tone and the choice of words fit the reader, and whether the layout helps. The child also tells how they went about it: how they prepared, where finding the words was hard, and what they changed when revising and why. A child of 10 says what was hard and hears from the group what is strong in the text and what can be better; a child of 12 asks the group a question about their own text, talks about structure and audience, and says what they will do differently in the next text.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording mentions no help. The setting is discussions of texts (*tekstbesprekingen*); the wording does not say with whom or whose texts. Two things are reflected on. For the writing process the brackets name orienting, formulating and revising; for the written product they name structure, coherence, style, purpose, audience, layout and register. Neither bracket carries "bijv.": the pack does not claim that the lists are complete or that they are only illustrations. None of the terms is explained, register (commonly the kind of language that fits a situation, such as formal or informal) included, and the goal does not say that the child uses the terms.

### Common mistakes

Talking about what the text is about and not about how it is written. Defending the text ("but I meant ...") and not hearing what a reader did not understand. Commenting only on spelling and layout when discussing another's text.
