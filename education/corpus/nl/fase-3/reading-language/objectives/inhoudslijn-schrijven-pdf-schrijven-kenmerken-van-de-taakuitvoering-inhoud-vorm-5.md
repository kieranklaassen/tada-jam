## Official wording

toepassen van de basisstructuur inleiding-kern-slot in eigen teksten

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

applying the basic structure introduction-body-conclusion (*inleiding-kern-slot*) in one's own texts

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before writing, the child plans the text in three parts: an introduction that names the subject, a body with the content in an order, and a conclusion that closes the text. The child writes the three parts and checks afterwards whether each one does what it is for. A child of 10 writes a short introduction and a short conclusion around the body; a child of 12 fits the parts to the kind of text, for example in a letter that argues: the opinion in the introduction, the arguments in the body and a request in the conclusion.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The institute's open data lacks this goal: the record is read from the content-line document (the PDF of the line Schrijven), and it has no code. The verb is applying, and it is applied in the child's own texts. One structure is named, with three parts: introduction, body and conclusion; the wording calls it the basic structure. The goal does not say what each part holds or how long it is; what the parts do in the note above is the pack's description. It sets no text length and no number of paragraphs, names no kinds of text, and mentions no help.

### Common mistakes

Writing an introduction that only repeats the title, such as "Ik ga het hebben over ..." (I am going to talk about ...; the pack's example). Bringing a new point in the conclusion, or stopping without one. Writing the body as one block in which the points are not ordered.
