## Official wording

afstemmen op publiek door: - aanpassen van woordgebruik aan formele en informele situaties, rekening te houden met voorkennis en waarnemen van signalen van het publiek; - publiek boeien (bijv. met anekdotes, ervaringen); - vragen stellen en te beantwoorden; - passend omgaan met commentaar/feedback

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adjusting to the audience by: - adapting word choice to formal and informal situations, taking prior knowledge into account and perceiving signals from the audience; - holding the audience's interest (e.g. with anecdotes, experiences); - asking and answering questions; - dealing fittingly with comments/feedback

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Presenting to parents at an information evening, the child greets them formally, explains the terms that only the class knows and leaves out words used among friends. The child opens with a short experience of their own, asks the audience a question halfway and, seeing puzzled faces, says a part again in other words. Afterwards the child answers questions and, when a listener says that a part went too fast, thanks them and says what they will change. A child of 10 adapts the greeting and explains terms as prepared, and answers questions briefly; a child of 12 watches the audience while speaking, changes the talk at that moment and responds to criticism without defending at once.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Four ways of adjusting are listed: adapting word choice to formal and informal situations, together with taking prior knowledge into account and perceiving signals from the audience; holding the audience's interest; asking and answering questions; and dealing fittingly with comments or feedback. Anecdotes and experiences follow "e.g." and are examples of how to hold interest. The statement does not say whose questions are asked and answered, or what counts as fitting. The source prints a stray *te* in two places (*rekening te houden*, *te beantwoorden*) and "comments/feedback" with a slash; the gloss reads the two verbs in the same form as the others in the list. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/03/04, titled *Publiek* (audience). The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Seeing a puzzled or bored audience and continuing as planned. Telling an anecdote that amuses and has no link with the subject, and spending most of the time on it. Hearing feedback as an attack and defending, or answering another question than the one that was asked.
