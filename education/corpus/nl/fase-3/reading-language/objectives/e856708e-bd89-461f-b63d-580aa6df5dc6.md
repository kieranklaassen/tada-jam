## Official wording

belangstelling hebben voor verhalende teksten (fictie, waaronder poëzie) en informatieve teksten (zakelijke teksten)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster NE/LE/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau e856708e-bd89-461f-b63d-580aa6df5dc6.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having an interest in narrative texts (fiction, including poetry) and informative texts (non-fiction texts, *zakelijke teksten*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a novel for young readers, a book of poems and a book or magazine about a subject such as space travel from the shelf, leafs through them and starts to read one. The child asks for more texts on a subject, looks something up because they want to know it, and tells others what they read. A child of 10 chooses by subject and by series; a child of 12 also follows a writer or a kind of book, and reads informative texts on a subject of their own choice, for example in a newspaper for young readers.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about interest, in two groups of texts: narrative texts, which the brackets describe as fiction including poetry, and informative texts, which the brackets describe as *zakelijke teksten* (non-fiction texts). It names no texts and sets no reading level, no amount of reading and no text length. It does not say whether the child reads the texts or hears them.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often keep to one kind of text, for example only one series or only texts about one sport, and leave out the other group. A child may be interested in a subject and still not pick up a text about it.
