## Official wording

schrijven ook over abstractere onderwerpen die niet direct gerelateerd zijn aan eigen leefwereld en betrekking hebben op verleden en toekomst

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/SCH/02/03; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 0dfca415-52ec-464a-9ab3-23b2f2caf781.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing also about more abstract subjects that are not directly related to one's own world (*leefwereld*, the surroundings a child lives in) and relate to past and future

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes about what freedom meant to someone in an occupied country, about how a town got its rights in the Middle Ages, about what fairness is, or about how people may travel in fifty years. The child uses what they read, heard or looked up, because the subject cannot be written about from their own experience, and explains terms for the reader. A child of 10 writes about another time or place from what they read and adds a thought of their own; a child of 12 writes a whole text about an idea, or about something in the past or the future, in their own words.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "Ook" (also) says that these subjects come in addition to others; the wording does not name the others. The subjects are described in one clause: "abstractere" (more abstract, a comparative; the wording does not say more abstract than what), not directly related to the child's own world, and relating to past and future. The Dutch joins these with "en" (and) and does not say whether every such subject has to do with the past and the future. The goal sets no text length and names no kind of text. The source words this with the verb first and without a subject ("schrijven ook over"); writing also about such subjects is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Copying sentences from a source text and not writing in their own words. Describing the past with things from the present, for example a child in the Middle Ages who goes to school every day. Using an abstract word such as "freedom" without explaining it or giving an example, so the reader does not learn what is meant.
