## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

editing one's own text for spelling and punctuation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

When the content of the text is finished, the child reads it again slowly, sentence by sentence, and checks each verb form, the capital letters and the words they find hard. The child reads the text aloud to hear where a sentence ends and puts in missing full stops, commas, question marks and quotation marks. A child of 10 corrects words from the spelling categories they have learned and the marks at the ends of sentences; a child of 12 also checks the spelling of verbs by rule, commas, and the punctuation of direct speech.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The text is the child's own; editing other people's texts is not named. Two things are edited: spelling and punctuation. The goal names no spelling categories and no punctuation marks, and sets no share of errors to be found; the marks and categories above are the pack's examples. The content-line document adds a cross-reference to the statement, "(zie Taalbeschouwing: begrippenlijst en taalverzorging)" (see Taalbeschouwing: list of terms and language care), which the record's wording lacks; the lists it points to, with the punctuation marks and spelling categories offered from *groep* 7-8, are quoted in the lane's frame. It does not mention help, or aids such as a dictionary or a spelling checker. Looking again at content and at the build-up of sentences is in NE/SCH/02/05/01/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Reading what they meant to write and not what stands on the page, so errors go unseen. Checking only the words the child was already unsure of. Passing over verb forms whose ending cannot be heard: "hij word" for "hij wordt", "het is gebeurt" for "het is gebeurd".
