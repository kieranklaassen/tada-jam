## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gives a text the parts that fit its kind: a *werkstuk* (a school paper on a chosen subject) gets a title page, a table of contents, chapters with headings and pictures with captions; a manual gets numbered steps and a drawing with a step; a letter gets none of these. The child checks that the headings match the table of contents and that each picture stands with the text it belongs to. A child of 10 puts a title, headings and a picture in a report; a child of 12 chooses the layout by the kind of text and the reader and makes a table of contents that matches the text.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is using. "Passende" (appropriate, fitting) is not explained: the wording does not say what the layout has to fit, such as the kind of text, the purpose or the reader. Everything in brackets follows "bijv." and is an example of layout: titles, headings, illustrations, table of contents. The goal names no kinds of text and does not say whether the texts are written by hand or on a computer. Editing for spelling and punctuation is in NE/SCH/02/06/02/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Writing a title that does not say what the text is about, or headings that do not match what stands under them. Choosing pictures as decoration, with no link to the text and no caption. Making a table of contents that does not match the headings or the page numbers.
