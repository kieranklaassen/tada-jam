## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking note of and investigating aspects of human reproduction and sexuality, such as the passing on of hereditary characteristics

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads and hears plain information about how a human life begins and develops before birth, uses the correct words and puts questions. The child investigates hereditary characteristics in photographs of a family, such as eye colour, hair colour and curly or straight hair, and notes who shares them. A child of 10 asks mostly factual questions, such as how a baby grows before birth; a child of 12 also asks questions that connect to the changes in their own body.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two verbs are used: taking note (*kennisnemen*) and investigating. The subject is aspects of human reproduction and sexuality; "such as" (*zoals*) introduces one example, the passing on of hereditary characteristics, and no other aspect is named. The text sets no level of detail and names no body parts or acts. Bodily changes in puberty are in `ojw/pdm/3/16/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Thinking a child inherits characteristics from one parent only. Thinking features gained during life, such as a scar or dyed hair, are passed on. Knowing only family words or street words for the body and for reproduction, and not the correct words.
