## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating how an automated system in a device works

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a device that acts without a person operating it, such as a heating thermostat, an automatic door, a lamp that switches on at dusk or a washing machine, and finds what it senses, what rule it follows and what it then does. The child tests one condition at a time, for example covering the light sensor or walking up slowly, and draws the working as a chain from what goes in, through the rule, to what the device does. A child of 10 writes the working as "if ..., then ..." rules; a child of 12 draws the whole chain, including how the result comes back to the sensor, as when a thermostat switches the heating off once the room is warm.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement speaks of one automated system in one device; none is named. What is investigated is how it works. The wording does not use the words sensor, input or output, and making or programming such a system is not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Saying that the device sees, knows or wants something, as in "the door knows I am coming". Calling a remote-controlled device automatic, though a person operates it. Describing only what the device does and leaving out what it senses and the rule in between.
