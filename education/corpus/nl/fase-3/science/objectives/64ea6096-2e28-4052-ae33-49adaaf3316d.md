## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing organs of humans with those of animals and (seed) plants

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays a drawing of the human lungs beside a picture of the gills of a fish and says that both take in oxygen, one from air and one from water. The child compares the human skin with fur, feathers and scales. The child takes a seed plant apart into root, stem, leaf and flower and asks which task of a plant organ is also a task of a human organ, such as carrying water in the stem and carrying blood in the blood vessels. A child of 10 matches organs that look alike; a child of 12 matches organs by their task, also when they look different.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The comparison starts from human organs. No organs, animals or plants are named. The source writes *(zaad-)planten*: plants, with the part of the word that narrows it to seed plants in brackets. Another statement, `ojw/pdm/5/06/fase3`, states the comparison starting from the organs of animals.

### Common mistakes

Assuming every animal has the same organs as a human, for example that an insect has lungs and bones. Thinking plants have no organs because they have no heart or stomach. Matching organs by shape and not by task.
