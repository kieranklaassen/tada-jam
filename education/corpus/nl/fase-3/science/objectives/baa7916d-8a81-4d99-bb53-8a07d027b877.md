## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the form and function of external and internal body parts (organs) of humans and systems of the human body (breathing, blood circulation, locomotion and metabolism)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child counts the pulse at the wrist for a minute at rest and again after running on the spot, and counts breaths in the same way. The child takes a torso model apart and puts it back, points to the heart, lungs, stomach and intestines, and says what each organ does. The child bends and stretches an arm and feels which muscle hardens each time. A child of 10 names an organ and its job; a child of 12 links organs into a system, for example that the lungs take oxygen into the blood and the heart pumps that blood around the body.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The first brackets add the word organs to the body parts. The second brackets name four systems: breathing, blood circulation, locomotion and metabolism; the wording does not say whether other systems are meant too. The statement covers the human body only, names no single organ and sets no level of detail. Another statement, `ojw/pdm/3/08/fase3`, lists parts and systems for animals and humans.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the heart makes the blood or cleans it, instead of pumping it. Thinking breathed-in air stays in the lungs and has nothing to do with the blood. Thinking food and drink go down two separate tubes, one for solids and one for liquids.
