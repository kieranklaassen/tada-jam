verkennen van overeenkomsten en verschillen van onderdelen bij (zaad‑)planten en vergelijken van (inwendige) organen van (zaad-)planten met die van dieren en de mens

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring similarities and differences of parts in (seed) plants and comparing (internal) organs of (seed) plants with those of animals and humans

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays the roots, stems, leaves, flowers and seeds of several seed plants side by side, such as a grass, a bean plant and a dandelion, and compares each part across the plants. The child cuts a stem across and along, looks at the vessels with a magnifying glass, and compares them with the blood vessels in a drawing of an animal or a human: both carry fluid through the body, but a plant has no heart that pumps. A child of 10 compares the outward parts of plants; a child of 12 compares by task and says where the comparison stops, for example that a plant makes its own food in the leaves and an animal has to eat.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts: exploring similarities and differences of parts in plants, and comparing organs of plants with those of animals and humans. The source writes *(zaad-)planten* twice: plants, with the part of the word that narrows it to seed plants in brackets; it also puts the word internal in brackets before organs. No parts, organs or species are named.

### Common mistakes

Thinking plants have no organs because they have no heart, lungs or stomach. Treating a likeness as complete, for example saying that the sap of a plant is pumped round as blood is, or that roots eat as a mouth does. Comparing by shape and not by task.
