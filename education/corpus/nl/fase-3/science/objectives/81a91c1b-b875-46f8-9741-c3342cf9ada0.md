## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating food relations and food chains (or food cycles) with the role of plants, animals, fungi and bacteria (life cycle)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays cards in a chain and draws each arrow from what is eaten to what eats it: oak leaf, caterpillar, great tit, sparrowhawk. The child links several chains from one place into a web, and adds the fungi and bacteria that break down dead leaves and dead animals into substances that plants take up again. Outdoors the child looks for the signs: eaten leaves, droppings, a mushroom on dead wood. A child of 10 builds a chain that starts with a plant; a child of 12 builds a web, closes the cycle with fungi and bacteria, and predicts what happens when one link becomes rare.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names food relations and food chains, and adds "or cycles" in brackets. Four groups are named with a role: plants, animals, fungi and bacteria; the roles are not described, and the words producer, consumer and decomposer are not used. The second brackets add the single word life cycle. No species or places are named.

### Common mistakes

Drawing the arrows the wrong way round, from the eater to what it eats. Starting a chain with an animal and leaving out the plant. Ending the chain at the largest animal and leaving out the fungi and bacteria that break down what dies.
