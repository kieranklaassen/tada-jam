## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

seeing the connection between the position of the sun, the length of shadows and the length of the day in summer and winter (and linking this to climate zone)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures the shadow of the same upright stick at midday in different months and records the length beside the times of sunrise and sunset for that day. The child compares the records: in summer a high midday sun, a short shadow and a long day; in winter a low sun, a long shadow and a short day. With a globe and a lamp the child compares how steeply the light falls near the equator and near the poles. A child of 10 measures and states the pattern; a child of 12 links the pattern to how high the sun stands in different parts of the earth and so to the climate zones.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three things are connected: the position of the sun, the length of shadows and the length of the day, compared for summer and winter. The brackets add linking this to climate zone; the wording uses the singular and names no zone. The statement does not mention the tilt of the earth's axis or any other cause of the seasons, and it names no place, instrument or unit.

### Common mistakes

Believing that it is summer because the earth is then closer to the sun; the cause is the tilt of the earth's axis, and the distance to the sun has almost no effect. Expecting the midday shadow to be longer in summer than in winter. Believing that the day is equally long everywhere on earth on the same date.
