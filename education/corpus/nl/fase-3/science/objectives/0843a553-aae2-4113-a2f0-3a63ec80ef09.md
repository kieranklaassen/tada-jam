## Official wording

onderzoeken hoe planten en dieren zich aanpassen aan de seizoenen en leefomgevingen (bijv. groeien of leven in 'sociaal verband')

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating how plants and animals adapt to the seasons and to living environments (e.g. growing or living in a 'social group' or 'social connection': *sociaal verband* means both)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child visits the same tree, hedge or ditch in autumn, winter and spring and notes what has changed: leaves dropped, buds closed, birds gone or arrived, insects no longer seen. The child compares a plant that grew in shade with a plant of the same species that grew in full light. The child watches animals that live in a group, such as ants or a flock of starlings, and plants that grow close together, and asks what living or growing together gives them. A child of 10 describes what changes with the season; a child of 12 gives a reason, such as a tree losing less water without leaves in the season when its roots take up little water from cold ground.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things to adapt to are named: the seasons and living environments. The brackets give one example, growing or living in a 'sociaal verband'; the quotation marks are the source's, and *verband* can mean a group or a connection. No plants, animals, seasons or environments are named. The statement does not use the word evolution; that concept is named in `ojw/pdm/5/10/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Believing that one plant or animal changes itself on purpose because it needs to. Thinking that all birds leave in winter, or that every animal that is not seen in winter is asleep. Thinking that a tree without leaves in winter is dead.
