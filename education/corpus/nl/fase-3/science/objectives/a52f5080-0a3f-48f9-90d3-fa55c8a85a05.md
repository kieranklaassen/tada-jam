## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the 'social group' or 'social connection' (*sociaal verband* means both), the interaction between plants, animals, humans and surroundings

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child chooses a place near the school, such as a ditch, a park or the edge of a field, and maps who depends on whom there: which plants grow, which animals eat them or shelter in them, and what people do there, such as mowing, feeding ducks or leaving litter. The child draws arrows between them and says for each arrow what passes or changes. The child goes back after people have changed something, such as mowing the verge, and notes what else has changed. A child of 10 names pairs that influence each other; a child of 12 follows an effect through several steps, for example fewer flowers after mowing, fewer insects that visit them and fewer birds that eat those insects.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The source prints *het 'sociale verband' de wisselwerking* with no word or punctuation mark between the quoted expression and what follows; the gloss puts a comma there and reads the interaction as an explanation of the expression. *Verband* can mean a group or a connection. Four parties are named: plants, animals, humans and surroundings. No place, plants or animals are named. Food relations have their own statement, `ojw/pdm/5/13/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Seeing only what plants and animals do for people, and not what people change for them. Seeing each influence as going one way, when the parties influence each other. Leaving out the surroundings that are not alive, such as water, soil and light.
