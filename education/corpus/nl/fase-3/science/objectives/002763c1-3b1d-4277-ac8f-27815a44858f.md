## Official wording

uitvoeren van eenvoudige experimenten met planten (bijv. factoren die van invloed zijn op de groei) en met dieren (bijv. factoren die van invloed zijn op de voorkeuren van gronddieren zoals regenwormen); waarbij de zorg voor levende organismen niet uit het oog wordt verloren

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

carrying out simple experiments with plants (e.g. factors that influence growth) and with animals (e.g. factors that influence the preferences of ground animals such as earthworms); in which care for living organisms is not lost sight of

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child grows cress or beans in several pots that differ in one thing only, such as the amount of light or water, measures the height every few days and records it in a table. The child lays a few earthworms in a tray with damp soil on one side and dry soil on the other, or with one side covered, and notes after some minutes where they are. The child keeps the worms damp and out of the sun, handles them briefly and puts them back in the soil afterwards. A child of 10 follows a set plan and measures; a child of 12 chooses the factor, keeps everything else the same and repeats the test before drawing a conclusion.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The experiments are to be simple. Both pairs of brackets give examples: for plants, factors that influence growth; for animals, factors that influence the preferences of ground animals, with earthworms as an example of such animals. The statement sets one condition: care for living organisms must not be forgotten. No factors, plants or other animals are named, and the statement does not say what that care consists of.

### Common mistakes

Changing several conditions at once, so that it cannot be said which one made the difference. Using one plant or one animal per condition and treating the result as certain. Forgetting the animals during the test: letting worms dry out, or keeping them out of the soil too long.
