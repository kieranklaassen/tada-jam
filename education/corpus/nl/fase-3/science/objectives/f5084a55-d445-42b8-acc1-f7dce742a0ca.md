## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the cycle of material by means of familiar objects

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a familiar object, such as a glass jar, a newspaper, a drinks can or a plastic bottle, and finds out what raw material it comes from, how it is made, where it goes when it is thrown away and what is made of it afterwards. The child sorts a bag of clean waste into the kinds collected separately where they live and draws the path of one object, as a circle or as a line that ends. A child of 10 follows one object through the steps; a child of 12 compares materials, such as glass, which can be melted again and again, with paper, whose fibres get shorter each time, and says where material is lost.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The cycle is studied through familiar objects; no object or material is named. The wording names no steps of the cycle and does not use the words waste, recycling or reuse.

### Common mistakes

Believing that an object that is thrown away is gone. Believing that everything put in a bin for recycling becomes the same object again, with nothing lost. Mixing up using an object again as it is with making its material into something new.
