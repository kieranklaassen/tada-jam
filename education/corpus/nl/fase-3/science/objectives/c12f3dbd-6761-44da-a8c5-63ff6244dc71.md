## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring how simple (measuring) instruments and devices work (e.g. microscope)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks through a magnifying glass and a microscope at a hair, a leaf or a grain of salt, turns the focus wheel, changes the lens and says what each part does: the lenses, the stage, the lamp or mirror. The child takes a simple measuring instrument, such as a spring balance, a thermometer or kitchen scales, finds the part that moves and explains how the movement becomes a reading. A child of 10 uses the instrument correctly and names its parts; a child of 12 explains how it works, for example that two lenses in a row enlarge more than one, and judges how exact a reading is.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. The instruments and devices are to be simple; "measuring" stands in brackets, so both measuring instruments and other instruments are covered. The microscope follows "bijv." and is an example; no other instrument or device is named, and no magnification or unit is given.

### Common mistakes

Saying what an instrument is for when asked how it works. Turning to the highest magnification at once and then finding nothing, instead of first focusing at the lowest. Expecting the image in the microscope to move the same way as the slide, when in most school microscopes it moves the opposite way.
