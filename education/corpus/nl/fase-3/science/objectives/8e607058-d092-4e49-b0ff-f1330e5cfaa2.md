## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a product apart, such as a bicycle bell, a stapler, a ballpoint pen or a pair of pliers, and says for each part what it does, why it has that shape and why it is made of that material. The child compares several versions of one product, such as three bottle openers or three cups, and says what the differences in shape and material mean for the use. A child of 10 explains single parts; a child of 12 explains how the parts work together, compares one choice with another, such as a stronger part with a lighter one, and proposes an improvement.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement relates four things: the form of a product, its function, the way it works and the materials used. It speaks of products in general and names none; it does not say that they are familiar or simple. Designing and making an object is in `ojw/nattech/1/06/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Answering only with the use ("it is for cutting") and saying nothing about shape or material. Explaining a shape or a material only by how it looks, when it is there for grip, strength or weight. Assuming that there is one right shape or material for a product.
