## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing a *weerbeeld* (the overall picture of the weather) for each season, and differences in weather across space (places in the world) and over time (historical)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child describes what the weather is usually like in each season where they live, in temperature, precipitation, wind and cloud, from weather records. The child compares this with the same months in other places in the world, for example a place near the equator and a place in the southern half of the earth, where the seasons fall in the opposite months. The child looks up weather of the past in old records, pictures or accounts, such as a very cold winter or a great storm, and compares it with recent years. A child of 10 describes from tables and pictures; a child of 12 reads climate graphs of several places and describes the differences in figures.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is describing; an explanation is not asked. Three things are described: a picture of the weather for each season, differences between places, for which the brackets name places in the world, and differences over time, for which the brackets say historical. The statement names no place, no period and no aspect of the weather; `ojw/rojh/7/01/fase3` lists weather phenomena.

### Common mistakes

Assuming that the seasons fall in the same months everywhere, so that July is summer all over the world. Assuming that every place has four seasons, though many places near the equator have a wet and a dry season instead. Taking one remembered extreme, such as one severe winter, for how the weather used to be.
