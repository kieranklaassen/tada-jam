## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

finding out the working and functions of the senses or sense organs (*zintuigen*, which means both), skin, immune system, muscular system, skeleton, system of blood vessels, respiratory system, digestive system and reproductive organs in animals and in humans

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes one of the named parts, such as the skin or the digestive system, finds out from books, models and film how it works and what it is for, and presents it with a drawing or a model. The child follows the path of a bite of food on a drawing of the human body: mouth, gullet, stomach, small intestine, large intestine. The child compares the same system in an animal, for example the stomach of a cow, which has four compartments, with the human stomach. A child of 10 names the parts of a system and says what the system is for; a child of 12 explains in order how it works and how two systems work together, such as breathing and the blood vessels.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Nine parts or systems are listed, each for animals and for humans. The verb is finding out (*uitzoeken*), and the statement asks for both the working and the functions; it names no method and no animals. For the reproductive organs, as for the other eight, the text sets no level of detail and names no parts.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the skin is only a wrapping, and not an organ with tasks of its own, such as protecting, feeling and cooling the body. Thinking digestion happens in the stomach only, and leaving out the intestines, where most of the food is taken up into the blood. Describing each system on its own and missing that the systems work together.
