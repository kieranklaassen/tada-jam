## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and observing the difference between reproduction through seed and through tuber, bulb and runners

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sows beans, plants a potato and a tulip bulb, pins a strawberry runner down in a pot of soil, and follows all four. The child cuts a soaked bean and an onion open: in the seed a tiny plant with its food store, in the bulb thick layers around a bud. The child notes which new plants come from a flower and its seed and which grow straight from a part of the parent plant. A child of 10 sorts plants by the way they make new plants; a child of 12 states the difference: a seed forms in a flower after pollination and the new plant differs a little from its parents, while a tuber, bulb or runner gives a plant with the same characteristics as the one parent plant.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The comparison has two sides: reproduction through seed, and reproduction through tuber, bulb and runners. The verbs are investigating and observing. No plants are named, and the wording does not use the terms sexual and asexual reproduction. Cuttings and spores are not named.

### Common mistakes

Believing that every new plant comes from a seed. Mistaking a bulb or a tuber for a large seed. Thinking a potato plant or a tulip makes no flowers and no seeds because it is grown from a tuber or a bulb.
