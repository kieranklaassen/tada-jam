## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds real fossils or casts, such as an ammonite, a shark's tooth or the print of a fern in stone, and draws them. The child lays each beside a picture of a living plant or animal that resembles it, the ammonite beside a nautilus and the fern print beside a fern leaf, and notes what is the same and what differs. The child places pictures of fossils on a time line. A child of 10 knows that a fossil is the remains or the print of something that lived long ago; a child of 12 uses the word evolution for the slow change of species over very many generations and knows that many fossil species no longer exist.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts, joined by a semicolon: getting to know fossils and the concept of evolution, and comparing fossils with the plants and animals of today. The verb of the first part is getting to know: a first meeting. No fossils, species, periods or ages in years are named, and the statement sets no level of detail for the concept of evolution.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that one animal changes during its life and passes that change on, instead of species changing over many generations. Thinking that a fossil is the bone or shell itself, when most are stone that took the shape of the remains, or a print. Placing everything from long ago at one moment, so that people and dinosaurs lived at the same time.
