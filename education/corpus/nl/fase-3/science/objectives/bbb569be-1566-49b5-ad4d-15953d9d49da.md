## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating which parts of plants provide functions such as firmness, reproduction, metabolism and transport

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands a celery stalk or a white flower in coloured water, cuts the stalk across after a day and sees the coloured dots of the vessels that carried the water up. The child bends a woody twig and a soft stem and compares their firmness, and sees a plant that was not watered for some days go limp and stand up again after watering. The child takes a flower apart and finds the stamens with pollen and the pistil. A child of 10 links one part to one function; a child of 12 names the leaf as the place where the plant makes its food with light, and says that one part can serve several functions, as the stem does for firmness and for transport.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "Such as" (*als*) introduces four functions as examples: firmness, reproduction, metabolism and transport. The parts of the plant are not named: finding them is the investigation. The statement names no plants and sets no level of detail for any of the functions.

### Common mistakes

Thinking plants take their food from the soil through the roots, instead of making it in the leaves. Thinking the roots only hold the plant in place. Thinking flowers are there for decoration and not for reproduction.
