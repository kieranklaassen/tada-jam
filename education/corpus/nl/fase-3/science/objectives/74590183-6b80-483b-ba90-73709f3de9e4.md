## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a graph of the average temperature over many years and at photographs of one glacier taken decades apart, and says what has changed. The child lists where greenhouse gases come from, such as the burning of coal, oil and gas for power, heating and transport, and what follows from a warmer climate, such as melting ice, a rising sea level and more heat waves. The child sorts cards into causes and consequences and draws arrows between them. A child of 10 names causes and consequences one at a time; a child of 12 follows a chain of several steps and tells apart what has been measured and what is expected.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. The statement names causes and consequences and gives no example of either; it does not say whether natural or human causes are meant, and it names no gas, place or period. What people can do about climate change is not mentioned in this statement.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up weather and climate, so that one cold winter counts as proof that the climate is not warming. Believing that the hole in the ozone layer causes the warming. Believing that the greenhouse effect is wholly man-made, when a natural greenhouse effect keeps the earth warm enough to live on and the gases people add make it stronger.
