opzetten en uitvoeren van een vergelijkend experiment met een natuurkundig verschijnsel

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

setting up and carrying out a comparative experiment with a physical phenomenon

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child asks a question that compares, such as on which of four surfaces a block needs the largest pull before it slides, or which length of string gives the highest note, and writes down what they expect. The child decides what is changed, what is measured and what stays the same, measures each case in the same way several times and records the results in a table. The child draws a conclusion from the figures and compares it with what was expected. A child of 10 needs help to keep the other things the same; a child of 12 plans the comparison alone, repeats it, draws a graph and names what could have made it unfair.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The experiment is comparative; the wording does not say what that means and names no steps of a method. The phenomenon is not named in this statement; the other statements of this cluster name light, sound, temperature, force, magnetism, electricity and energy. The clusters on materials and on technical principles each hold a statement on a comparative experiment of their own, `ojw/nattech/1/07/fase3` and `ojw/nattech/3/08/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Changing several things at once, such as the surface and the weight of the block. Measuring in a different way each time, for example from a different distance. Writing down the expected result in place of the one observed, or leaving out a measurement that does not fit.
