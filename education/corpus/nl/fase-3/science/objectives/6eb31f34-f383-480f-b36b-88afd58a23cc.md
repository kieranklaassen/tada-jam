investigating the influence of weather and climate on humans, animals, plants and landscape

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes one place and finds out how its weather and climate show in the houses, clothes and work of the people, in the plants that grow and are farmed there, in what the animals do through the year and in the land itself. The child looks for traces of weather in a landscape, such as a tree bent by the wind, a gully cut by rain or a dune shaped by wind. The child compares the place with a place in another climate. A child of 10 collects examples for each of the four; a child of 12 separates the effect of a few days of weather from the effect of the climate over many years.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two influences are named, weather and climate, and four things they act on: humans, animals, plants and landscape. The statement gives no example and names no place, climate or effect. The influence of people on the climate is not the subject of this statement; causes and consequences of climate change are in `ojw/rojh/7/03/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Treating weather and climate as the same, so that the effect of one storm is given as an effect of the climate. Seeing the influence on people only in clothing and comfort and leaving out farming, building and water. Leaving out the landscape, or thinking that the land does not change because the change is too slow to see.
