onderzoeken van het verschijnsel geluid: geluidsbronnen; eigenschappen van geluid (geluidstrilling, geluidssterkte, toonhoogte, factoren die de klank bepalen en voortplanting door materiaal); relatie met horen (waarnemen van geluid in bijv. verkeer) en gehoorschade

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the phenomenon of sound: sound sources; properties of sound (sound vibration, loudness, pitch, factors that determine how a sound sounds (*klank*), and propagation through material); the relationship with hearing (perceiving sound in e.g. traffic) and hearing damage

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child plucks a ruler held over the edge of a table at different lengths, stretches strings tighter and looser, and links length, tightness and thickness to pitch and the force of the stroke to loudness. The child listens to a tapped table with an ear against the wood, speaks through a string telephone, and compares how a ticking clock sounds through air, through wood and through a layer of foam. The child stands with closed eyes and points to where a sound comes from, and names sounds that can harm hearing. A child of 10 tests and describes high and low, loud and soft; a child of 12 explains that sound is a vibration passed on through air, water or a solid to the ear, and changes one factor at a time.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. After the colon the statement names sound sources; properties of sound, for which the brackets name vibration, loudness, pitch, the factors that determine how a sound sounds, and propagation through material; the relationship with hearing, with perceiving sound in traffic as an example after "bijv."; and hearing damage. No unit is named: the decibel and the hertz are not mentioned. The statement does not say which factors those are.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up high and low with loud and soft, or expecting a harder stroke to give a higher note. Believing that sound travels only through air; it also goes through water and solids, and it does not go through empty space. Believing that hearing damaged by loud sound recovers.
