## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays picture cards in order for several animals: egg, tadpole, frog; egg, caterpillar, pupa, butterfly; egg, chick, hen; and for a mammal: fertilised egg cell, young growing in the womb, birth, young animal, adult. The child compares the rows and marks where the young develops in an egg outside the mother's body and where inside the mother. A child of 10 orders the stages of one animal; a child of 12 compares the rows and names the stage with which every one of them starts, a fertilised egg cell.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is *kennisnemen*, taking note: becoming informed, not investigating. The stages are not listed, and no animals or groups of animals are named. The text sets no level of detail on mating or fertilisation. Human reproduction is in another statement, `ojw/pdm/3/15/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Thinking a caterpillar and a butterfly, or a tadpole and a frog, are different animals. Thinking that only birds lay eggs. Leaving out the first stage, the fertilised egg cell, and starting with the egg that is laid or the young that is born.
