## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring similarities and differences in the functioning of the respiratory system, the locomotor apparatus and the senses or sense organs (*zintuigen*, which means both) of humans and certain groups of animals

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child compares how a human, a fish and an insect take in oxygen: with lungs, with gills, and with small air tubes that open on the sides of the body. The child compares walking on two legs with the swimming of a fish and the flying of a bird, from film, pictures or animals outdoors, and names the body parts that do the moving. The child compares their own hearing and smell with those of a dog or a bat. A child of 10 says what each animal has; a child of 12 says how the part works differently and what that lets the animal do.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three things are compared: the respiratory system, the locomotor apparatus and *zintuigen*, a word that means both the senses and the sense organs. The comparison is of how they function. The statement does not say what the locomotor apparatus (*voortbewegingsapparaat*) consists of: in a human it is the bones, joints and muscles, which is the pack's example of the human case, and the term also covers animals that have no bones. "Certain groups of animals" is not filled in: no group is named.

### Common mistakes

Thinking fish do not breathe, or that they use the water itself and not the oxygen dissolved in it. Thinking whales and dolphins breathe with gills because they live in water. Assuming that animals sense the world just as people do.
