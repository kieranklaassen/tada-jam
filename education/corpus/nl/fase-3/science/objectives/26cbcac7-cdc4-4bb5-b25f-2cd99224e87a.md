## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With a lamp as the sun and their own head as the earth, the child holds a small ball at arm's length as the moon, turns slowly on the spot and watches the lit part of the ball grow from a thin curve to a full circle and shrink again. The child draws the moon on the days of a month when it can be seen and matches each drawing to a place of the ball. The child turns a globe in the lamplight for day and night and carries it around the lamp for the earth's path around the sun. A child of 10 acts the movements out and lays the drawings in order; a child of 12 explains that half of the moon is always lit and that the shape seen from the earth depends on where the moon is in its path around the earth.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names the turning of the earth, the movement of earth and moon relative to each other, and their movement relative to the sun. The brackets name the phases of the moon and the universe. The statement gives no durations (a day, a month, a year), does not mention eclipses or tides, and names no model or instrument. Summer and winter are in `ojw/rojh/2/02/fase3`, and the other planets in `ojw/rojh/2/03/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Believing that the phases of the moon come from the shadow of the earth falling on the moon; that shadow causes an eclipse of the moon, not the phases. Believing that the sun goes around the earth once a day, because the sun is seen crossing the sky. Believing that the moon is in the sky only at night.
