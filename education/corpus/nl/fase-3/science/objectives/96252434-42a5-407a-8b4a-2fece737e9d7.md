## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring outward similarities and differences between parts of people, people in their surroundings and elsewhere in the world, as well as between people and animals (mammals)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child compares hands, ears, hair and eye colour with those of classmates and notes what is the same in everyone and what differs. The child does the same with photographs of people from several parts of the world. The child lays their own hand beside a picture of the front foot of a cat, a horse or a bat and looks for what is alike and what differs from outside. A child of 10 lists differences first; a child of 12 also names what all people share, and what people share with other mammals, such as hair and four limbs.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The comparison is of outward characteristics only: what can be seen from outside. The Dutch leaves the grouping of the first part open; it can be read as three comparisons: between parts of people, between people in their surroundings and people elsewhere in the world, and between people and animals. The source writes *(zoog-)dieren*: animals, with the part of the word that narrows it to mammals in brackets. No characteristics, peoples or animals are named, and the statement attaches no value to any difference.

### Common mistakes

Not counting humans as animals or as mammals. Listing only differences and leaving out what is the same. Drawing conclusions about what a person is like from one outward feature.
