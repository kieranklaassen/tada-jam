## Official wording

reflecteren op betekenissen van dieren en planten in relatie tot voor mensen relevante technologische ontwikkelingen (bijv. op het gebied van voeding, medicijnen, apparatuur)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reflecting on meanings of animals and plants in relation to technological developments that are relevant for humans (e.g. in the field of food, medicines, equipment)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes one product from each named field and finds out what part a plant or an animal has in it: wheat that was bred to give more grain, a painkiller developed from a substance in willow bark, hook-and-loop fastener copied from a burr. The child gives an opinion with reasons on a question such as whether people may change a plant or an animal by breeding so that it yields more, and listens to other opinions. A child of 10 finds the plant or animal behind a product; a child of 12 weighs what the development gives people against what it asks of the plants or animals.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is reflecting: thinking about meanings, not learning a list of inventions. The brackets give three fields as examples: food, medicines and equipment. No plants, animals or developments are named, and the statement does not say which meanings are meant and gives no judgement on them.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often stop at naming a useful product and leave out the reflection: what the use means for people and for the plants and animals. Many think of plants and animals as raw material only and leave out that a way of working can be copied from them.
