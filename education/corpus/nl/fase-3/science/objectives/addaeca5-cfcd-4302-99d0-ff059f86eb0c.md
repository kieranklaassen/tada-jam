## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the phenomenon of magnetism: the attracting and repelling of magnets (the relationship with the terms north pole/south pole and how a compass works); magnetic properties of materials

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child brings the ends of two bar magnets together in every combination and records which pairs pull and which push. The child hangs a bar magnet on a thread and sees it come to rest along the north-south line, compares it with a compass and moves a magnet near the compass. The child tests a set of materials with a magnet and sorts them. A child of 10 finds the rule that like poles repel and unlike poles attract; a child of 12 uses the rule to explain why a compass needle points north and what disturbs it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. After the colon two parts are named: the attracting and repelling of magnets, with the terms north pole and south pole and how a compass works in brackets, and the magnetic properties of materials. The statement names no material and no measure of how strong a magnet is. Electromagnets are in `ojw/nattech/2/06/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Believing that a magnet attracts every metal; it attracts iron, nickel and cobalt, and not aluminium, copper or brass. Believing that one end of a magnet attracts and the other end repels, when that depends on which pole of the other magnet it meets. Mixing up the poles of a magnet with the plus and minus of a battery.
