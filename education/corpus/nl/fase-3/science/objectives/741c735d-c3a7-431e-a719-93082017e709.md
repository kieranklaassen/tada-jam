exploring physical changes that occur in humans during puberty

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads and hears plain information about how the body changes in puberty and sorts statements about those changes into what happens in girls, in boys and in both, such as growing taller quickly, which happens in both. The child puts questions and uses the correct words. A child of 10 mostly hears about changes that have not started yet; a child of 12 may notice some in their own body or in classmates, and sees that they start at different ages in different children.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. The statement is limited to physical changes in humans during puberty. It names no change and no body part, sets no level of detail, and does not say whether changes in girls and in boys are treated together. Feelings and relationships are not mentioned; sexuality is named in `ojw/pdm/3/15/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that puberty starts at the same age for everyone, so that being early or late means something is wrong. Thinking the changes happen all at once and not over several years. Thinking only the changes that can be seen count, and leaving out changes inside the body.
