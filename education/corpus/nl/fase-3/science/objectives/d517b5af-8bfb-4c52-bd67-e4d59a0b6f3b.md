## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating changes to observable properties of materials: with regard to heat, sound and electricity; the relationship between weight and volume; the influence of moisture (rusting); the influence of temperature (expanding/shrinking); the repelling and attracting force of magnetic materials

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child weighs blocks of the same size made of wood, iron, aluminium and plastic and compares weight with volume. The child leaves one iron nail dry and one in damp air for a week, watches the liquid in a thermometer rise in warm water and fall in cold water, and tests which materials pass on heat, sound or the current of a battery. The child feels two magnets pull and push and tests which materials a magnet attracts. A child of 10 tests and sorts; a child of 12 measures, keeps all but one thing the same and states a rule, such as that a block of iron is always heavier than a block of wood of the same size.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. After the colon five parts are named: properties with regard to heat, sound and electricity; the relationship between weight and volume; the influence of moisture, with rusting in brackets; the influence of temperature, with expanding and shrinking in brackets; and the repelling and attracting force of magnetic materials. The properties are to be observable. The word density is not used, and no unit, instrument or material is named. The text states no safety condition for work with heat or electricity.

### Common mistakes

Believing that a larger object is always heavier, without looking at the material. Believing that all metals rust and that a magnet attracts all metals; rust forms on iron and steel, and a magnet attracts iron, nickel and cobalt but not aluminium or copper. Believing that metal in a room is colder than wood in the same room, because it feels colder to the hand.
