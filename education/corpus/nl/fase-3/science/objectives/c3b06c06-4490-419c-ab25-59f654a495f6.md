## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating properties of substances: dissolving; mixing and separating out again (watery and fatty layers); settling (soil in water)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stirs salt, sugar, sand and flour each into a glass of water and records which dissolve, then adds salt spoon by spoon until no more dissolves. The child shakes oil and water in a closed jar and times how long the layers take to come back. The child shakes garden soil with water in a tall jar, lets it stand and draws the layers that settle, with the coarsest grains at the bottom. A child of 10 tests and describes; a child of 12 compares amounts and changes one thing at a time, such as the temperature of the water.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three properties are named after the colon: dissolving; mixing and separating out again, for which the brackets name watery and fatty layers; and settling, for which the brackets name soil in water. No substance is named for dissolving. Filtering and evaporating are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Believing that a dissolved substance is gone, though the water has become heavier by the weight of the salt. Saying "melting" for dissolving. Taking cloudy water, as with flour or soil, for a solution; a solution is see-through, even when it is coloured, and nothing settles from it.
