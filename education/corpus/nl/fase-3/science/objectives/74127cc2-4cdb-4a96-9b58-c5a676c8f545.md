## English gloss

encountering, wondering at and realising the great variety of plants, animals and mushrooms (fungi)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child marks out a square metre of road verge, hedge bottom or woodland floor and records every different kind of plant, small animal and mushroom found in it, with drawings or photographs. The child compares the count with that of another place, such as a mown lawn. In autumn the child looks at mushrooms on dead wood and on the ground without picking them. A child of 10 is surprised by how many kinds one small place holds; a child of 12 compares places and asks why one holds more kinds than another.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three groups are named: plants, animals, and mushrooms, with fungi in brackets. The verbs are encountering, wondering and realising; ordering and naming are asked in other statements, `ojw/pdm/5/01/fase3` and `ojw/pdm/5/08/fase3`. No numbers or names of species are given.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the small and plain living things: insects, worms, mosses, grasses and mushrooms. Many count mushrooms as plants, although fungi are a group of their own.
