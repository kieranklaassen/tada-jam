investigating why people keep animals and manage crops

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lists animals that people keep, such as cows, hens, sheep, bees, dogs, horses and goldfish, and sorts them by reason: food, wool or leather, work, company, sport. The child visits a farm or a vegetable garden, or interviews someone who keeps animals or grows crops, and asks what the animals or plants give and what work they ask for: sowing, weeding, watering, feeding, protecting against pests. A child of 10 names what each animal or crop gives; a child of 12 gives several reasons for one animal or crop and sees that a crop is tended because it would yield less if left alone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The question is why: the reasons people have for keeping animals and for managing crops. No animals, crops or reasons are named. Animal welfare and the environment are not mentioned in this statement; treating plants and animals with care is in `ojw/pdm/4/03/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Thinking of pets only and leaving out farm animals, or the other way round. Naming the shop when asked where milk, eggs, bread or meat come from. Thinking crops grow by themselves once sown, with no work between sowing and harvest.
