## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At the same time each day the child reads a thermometer in the shade, a barometer, a rain gauge and a wind vane, estimates the wind force from what flags and branches do, and notes how much of the sky is covered and what the clouds look like. The child keeps the readings in a table for some weeks, draws graphs and looks for patterns, such as whether rain follows a fall in air pressure. A child of 10 reads the instruments and keeps the table; a child of 12 sets a question of their own, compares the readings with the weather report and draws a conclusion from the graphs.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name six things to study: wind direction, wind force, air pressure, temperature, the type and amount of precipitation, and the amount and kind of cloud cover. No instrument, unit or scale is named; *windkracht* (wind force) is the everyday Dutch word for how hard the wind blows. The statement names no place and no length of time, and weather maps and forecasting are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Naming the wind by where it blows to instead of where it comes from. Reading a thermometer that hangs in the sun, so that the reading is higher than the temperature of the air. Thinking that air weighs nothing and so cannot press on anything.
