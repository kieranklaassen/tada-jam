## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the way in which the senses or sense organs (*zintuigen*, which means both) perceive phenomena such as light, colour, scent or sound

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child watches a partner's pupils grow smaller when the hand that shaded the partner's eyes is taken away. The child looks at coloured paper in dim light and finds the colours hard to tell apart, points with closed eyes to where a sound comes from, first with both ears and then with one ear covered, and finds that a scent in the room is no longer noticed after some minutes. A child of 10 carries out the test and says what happened; a child of 12 explains it with the parts involved, for example that light enters the eye through the pupil and falls on the retina, and that sound makes the eardrum vibrate.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "Such as" (*als*) introduces four phenomena as examples: light, colour, scent or sound. The statement is about the way in which *zintuigen*, a word for both the senses and the sense organs, perceive them; it names no sense organ and no part of one. The wording names only these four phenomena, and the list after *als* is not given as complete. The statement does not say whether human senses, animal senses or both are meant, and it states no safety condition.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that seeing happens because something goes out from the eye to the object. Thinking the sense organ does all the perceiving, with no part for the nerves and the brain. Thinking colour belongs to the object alone and looks the same in any light.
