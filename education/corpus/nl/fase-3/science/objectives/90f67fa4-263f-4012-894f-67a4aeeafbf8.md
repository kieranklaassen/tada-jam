## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring the use of physical phenomena in different technologies

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a device and finds which physical phenomenon it uses: mirrors in a periscope, a lens in a camera, a vibrating cone in a loudspeaker, a magnet and a coil in a bicycle dynamo, a liquid that expands in a thermometer, friction in a brake. The child opens a device that is safe to open, such as a torch without its batteries or a wind-up toy, and points to the part where the phenomenon does its work. A child of 10 matches a device to one phenomenon; a child of 12 explains the working in steps and finds the same phenomenon in two different devices.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. The statement names no phenomenon and no technology; the other statements of this cluster name light, sound, temperature, force, magnetism, electricity and energy. Making a device is not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Saying what a device is for when asked which phenomenon makes it work. Thinking of technology only as things with a screen or a plug, so that a brake, a vacuum flask or a pair of glasses is not counted. Naming electricity as the phenomenon for every device with a plug, without saying what the electricity is turned into: light, sound, heat or movement.
