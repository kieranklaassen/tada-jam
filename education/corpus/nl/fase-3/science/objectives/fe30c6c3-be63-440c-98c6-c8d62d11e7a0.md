## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

setting up and carrying out a comparative experiment with materials, substances or objects

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child asks a question that compares, such as which of four fabrics keeps a cup of warm water warm longest or which kitchen paper takes up the most water, and writes down what they expect. The child decides what is changed, what is measured and what stays the same, tests each material in the same way, repeats the test and records the results in a table. The child draws a conclusion from the figures and compares it with what was expected. A child of 10 needs help to see everything that must stay the same; a child of 12 plans the comparison alone, repeats the measurements and names what may have made the test unfair.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The experiment is comparative; the wording does not say what that means and names no steps of a method. Its subject is materials, substances or objects. The clusters on physical phenomena and on technical principles each hold a statement on a comparative experiment of their own, `ojw/nattech/2/08/fase3` and `ojw/nattech/3/08/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Changing more than one thing at a time, such as the material and the amount of water, so that the comparison shows nothing. Measuring each material once and treating a small difference as a result. Reporting what was done instead of what the comparison showed.
