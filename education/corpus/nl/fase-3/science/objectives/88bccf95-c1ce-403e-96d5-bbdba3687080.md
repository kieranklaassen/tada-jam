## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child feels their own skull, ribs, backbone and knuckles through the skin and compares them with a model skeleton or a drawing. The child moves the elbow, the knee and the shoulder and finds that the elbow and the knee bend like a hinge, while the shoulder lets the arm turn in all directions. The child says what the skull and the ribs protect. A child of 10 names the large bones and points to joints; a child of 12 gives three jobs of the skeleton (firmness, protection, and movement together with the muscles) with an example of each.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement stands in the cluster on the human being and names the skeleton only, with its build and its function. It names no bones or joints and no number of bones. The muscular system is not named here; it is listed in `ojw/pdm/3/08/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Thinking bones are dead, dry things, as in a picture of a skeleton, and not living parts that grow and heal. Thinking a bone itself bends at a joint, instead of two bones meeting there. Thinking the skeleton only holds the body up, and leaving out that it protects organs.
