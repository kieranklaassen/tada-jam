## Official wording

verkennen van overeenkomsten en verschillen tussen onderdelen van verschillende plantensoorten en tussen onderdelen van verschillende diersoorten

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster ojw/pdm/5; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 834cf6ad-8a3d-4264-94d2-fdf3a46c9ab3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring similarities and differences between parts of different plant species or kinds and between parts of different animal species or kinds (*soort* means both kind and species)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays the leaves, flowers or seeds of several plant species side by side and compares the same part across them: the leaf edge and veins, the number of petals, the way the seed is carried. The child compares the same part in several animals: the beaks of a duck, a finch and a bird of prey, or the feet of a duck, a hen and a woodpecker. The child draws the parts next to each other and marks what is the same and what differs. A child of 10 describes the differences; a child of 12 also finds what the parts have in common under the differences, such as the same upper-arm and forearm bones in the wing of a bird and the front leg of a mammal.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two comparisons are named: between parts of different plant species, and between parts of different animal species. A comparison between plants and animals is not asked here; it is in `ojw/pdm/5/05/fase3` and `ojw/pdm/5/06/fase3`. No parts or species are named, and the statement does not say whether outward or inner parts are meant.

### Common mistakes

Comparing a different part in each plant or animal, so that nothing is compared. Comparing on colour or size only. Listing differences and leaving out what the parts have in common.
