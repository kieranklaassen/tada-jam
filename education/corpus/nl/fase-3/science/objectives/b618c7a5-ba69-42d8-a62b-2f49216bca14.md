## Official wording

opzetten en uitvoeren van een vergelijkend experiment met behulp van een technisch principe

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

setting up and carrying out a comparative experiment with the help of a technical principle

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a principle, such as a belt drive, a profile or a pair of syringes, and asks a question that compares, such as which of three folded shapes of the same sheet of card carries the most weight as a bridge. The child writes down what they expect, decides what is changed, what is measured and what stays the same, tests each case in the same way several times and records the results in a table. The child draws a conclusion from the figures. A child of 10 needs help to keep the span, the card and the way of loading the same; a child of 12 plans the comparison alone, repeats it and names what could have made it unfair.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The experiment is comparative, and it is done with the help of a technical principle; the principle is not named, and the wording does not say what comparative means. The clusters on materials and on physical phenomena each hold a statement on a comparative experiment of their own, `ojw/nattech/1/07/fase3` and `ojw/nattech/2/08/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Building something that works and calling it an experiment, with no question and nothing compared. Changing two things at once, such as the shape and the span. Recording only the trial that went well.
