## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the phenomenon of electricity: materials that let current through (conducting) or do not (insulating); circuits; how an electromagnet works (coil, dynamo); generating static electricity; reflecting on the use and danger of electricity

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds a circuit of a battery, wires and a small bulb, puts objects of different materials in a gap in the circuit and sorts them into conductors and insulators. The child winds insulated wire around an iron nail, connects it to the battery and counts how many paper clips it lifts with the current on and off, and turns a bicycle dynamo by hand to light its lamp. The child rubs a balloon on wool and picks up scraps of paper with it, lists where electricity is used at home and says what makes the electricity from a wall socket dangerous. A child of 10 builds the circuit and sorts the materials; a child of 12 draws the circuit, finds a fault by reasoning about the closed loop and explains why the coil attracts only while the current flows.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. After the colon five parts are named: materials that conduct current or insulate; circuits; how an electromagnet works, with coil and dynamo in brackets; generating static electricity; and reflecting on the use and the danger of electricity. No material, kind of circuit, symbol or unit is named: the volt and the ampere are not mentioned. The source puts the dynamo in the brackets after the electromagnet; a dynamo makes current from movement with a magnet and a coil, and an electromagnet makes magnetism from current. Danger is named as a subject for reflection; the text states no safety condition for the child's own work with electricity.

### Common mistakes

Believing that the current is used up in the bulb, so that less comes back to the battery; the current is the same all round the loop, and what the bulb takes out is energy. Believing that only metals conduct, though the graphite of a pencil and salt water conduct too. Treating a small battery as being as dangerous as a wall socket, or a wall socket as being as safe as a small battery.
