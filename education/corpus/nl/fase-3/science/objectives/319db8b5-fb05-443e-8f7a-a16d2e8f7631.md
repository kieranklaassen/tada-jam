## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

designing and making an object with the help of structural principles such as profiles and triangle constructions (a stable and sturdy construction)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child designs a bridge, a tower, a crane or a chair to a stated demand, such as spanning half a metre and carrying a bag of sugar, and draws it before building. The child folds or rolls strips of card into L, U and tube shapes, joins them with slanting struts that form triangles, loads the object and strengthens the place that failed. A child of 10 draws a simple plan and adds triangles where the object wobbles; a child of 12 plans where the profiles and the struts go before building, tests with a measured load and explains why the object held or failed.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two principles follow "zoals" and are examples: profiles and triangle constructions. The brackets name the result: a stable and sturdy construction. A profile here is a strip or sheet with a folded or shaped cross-section; the wording does not explain the word. No object, size, material or load is named. Joins are in `ojw/nattech/3/02/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Making the structure stronger with more material or more tape instead of with another shape. Adding a slanting strut that is fixed at one end only, so that it holds nothing. Building first and drawing the design afterwards.
