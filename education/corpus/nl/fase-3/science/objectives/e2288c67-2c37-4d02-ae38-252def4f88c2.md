## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks through a microscope at a thin piece of onion skin or a leaf of waterweed, or at a photograph taken through one, and draws the cells as small compartments side by side. The child sorts cards into three sets: whole living things (an oak, a snail, a human), organs (a leaf, a heart, a lung) and cells. The child uses the three words in sentences that link them: a plant or an animal is an organism, it has organs, and its organs are built of cells. A child of 10 learns the words and sees cells for the first time; a child of 12 puts the three in order of size and knows that some organisms consist of one cell.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is getting to know: a first meeting with three concepts. The source prints organism and organ in the singular and cells in the plural. The statement stands in the cluster on plants and animals; it names no parts of a cell and no kinds of cell, and it does not mention tissues.

### Common mistakes

Thinking cells are separate things inside the body, such as blood cells only, and not that the whole body is built of cells. Mixing up the order of size, for example calling a leaf an organism or a heart a cell. Thinking plants are not organisms, or that only animals are made of cells.
