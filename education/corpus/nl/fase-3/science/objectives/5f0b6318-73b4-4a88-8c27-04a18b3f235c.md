## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the phenomenon of force: gravity; friction; the force of air; the force of water (upward force)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hangs objects on a spring balance and compares how far it stretches, and pulls a block over wood, carpet and sandpaper to compare the friction. The child drops a flat sheet of paper and a crumpled sheet side by side, and runs with a large sheet of card held in front to feel the air push back. The child hangs a stone on the spring balance in air, lowers it into water and sees the pull become less, and pushes an empty closed bottle under water. A child of 10 feels and compares the forces; a child of 12 measures with the spring balance, names the forces that act on one object and says in which direction each works.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. After the colon four forces are named: gravity, friction, the force of air and the force of water, with the upward force in brackets; that is the push of water on an object in it. No unit or instrument is named: the newton is not mentioned. *Kracht* is also the everyday word for strength; with gravity and friction beside it, the force of physics is meant. Magnetism is in `ojw/nattech/2/05/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Believing that a heavy object falls faster than a light one because it is heavy; where two objects fall differently, it is the air that slows one more than the other. Believing that a moving object stops by itself, and not through friction. Believing that gravity does not act on an object that floats, when gravity and the upward force of the water are in balance.
