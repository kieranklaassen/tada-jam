## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out the sun and the eight planets with balls or cards and names them from the sun outwards: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, with the earth as the third. On the playground the child paces out the distances on a scale, with a few steps from the sun to the earth and many more to the outer planets. A child of 10 learns the order and the names; a child of 12 works with a scale for the distances and finds that the gaps between the outer planets are far larger than those between the inner ones.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb, *kennis nemen van*, asks for taking note, not for investigating or explaining. The statement names the earth, other planets and the sun; it does not say how many planets or which, and it gives no distances, sizes or times for a path around the sun. The movement of earth and moon is in `ojw/rojh/2/01/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Imagining the planets at equal distances from each other and about the same size, as most pictures show them. Believing that the planets stand in a fixed row on one side of the sun. Mixing up planets and stars, or counting the sun or the moon as a planet.
