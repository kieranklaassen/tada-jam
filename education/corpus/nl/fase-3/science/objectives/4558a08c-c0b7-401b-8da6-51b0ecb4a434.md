## Official wording

toepassen van technische principes bij het oplossen van een vraag of probleem door deze functioneel te gebruiken in eigen ontwerpen (onderzoeken en ontwerpen)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

applying technical principles in solving a question or problem by using them functionally in one's own designs (investigating and designing)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a question or problem, such as moving a load up a slope with little force or making a model bridge that opens, and writes down what the solution must do. The child chooses principles already met, such as a belt drive, a triangle construction, a bolted join or a pair of syringes, sketches a design in which each has a job, builds it and tests whether the problem is solved. The child improves the design after the test and explains the choices. A child of 10 uses one principle and revises after a test; a child of 12 combines principles, gives a reason for each choice beforehand and judges the result against what was written down at the start.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The principles are not named in this statement; other statements of this cluster name structural principles, joins, principles of movement and transmission, and pneumatics. The designs are the child's own, and the brackets name the way of working: investigating and designing. No problem, material or tool is named.

### Common mistakes

Adding a principle that does no work in the design, such as a wheel and belt that drive nothing. Starting to build without stating what the solution must do, so that it cannot be judged afterwards. Changing many things at once after a failed test.
