## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child catches a ball and says afterwards what took part: eyes that followed the ball, arms that reached, fingers that closed, legs that kept the body in balance. The child ties a shoelace with closed eyes, writes their name with the other hand, or stands on one leg with eyes open and then closed, and notices which parts have to work harder. A child of 10 names the parts that can be seen moving; a child of 12 also names parts that are not seen, such as the sense of balance and the brain that steers the muscles.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experiencing consciously: the wording describes an experience of how body parts work together, not knowledge of anatomy. It is about human acting and moving; no body parts and no actions are named. The statement does not ask for an explanation of how the cooperation comes about.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often name only the limb that visibly moves and leave out the eyes, the balance and the parts of the body that hold still to steady it. Many call an everyday movement simple because they no longer notice the parts that take part in it.
