## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the 'social group' or 'social connection' (*sociaal verband* means both) in which animals live

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child watches animals that live together, such as ants on a trail, hens in a run, jackdaws or a herd of cows, and notes who does what: who eats first, who keeps watch, who follows whom. The child compares this with an animal that lives alone for most of the year, such as a hedgehog. The child lists what living together gives the animals and what it costs them, for example more animals that watch for danger and more animals that share the same food. A child of 10 describes what the animals in a group do; a child of 12 names roles and rank order and gives advantages and disadvantages of living in a group.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The quotation marks around 'sociale verband' are the source's; *verband* can mean a group or a connection, and here it is the one in which animals live. No animals and no forms of living together are named. The expression is also used in `ojw/pdm/3/02/fase3` and `ojw/pdm/5/11/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Describing the animals as people, with a "king", "friends" or "a family that loves each other". Thinking a group of animals always has one leader that decides everything. Thinking animals of one species live the same way all year, always in a group or always alone.
