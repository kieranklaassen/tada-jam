## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

ordering and classifying plants and animals into main groups and kinds or species (*soorten*, which means both) (plant kingdom and animal kingdom)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts cards of animals first into animals with and without a backbone, then sorts the animals with a backbone into fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals, and names the feature used at each step. The child sorts plants into groups such as plants that make flowers and seeds, and mosses and ferns, which make spores. The child places difficult cases and gives a reason: a bat and a whale with the mammals, a spider outside the insects. A child of 10 sorts into the well-known groups; a child of 12 gives the deciding feature, such as hair and milk for the young in mammals or six legs in insects.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two levels of grouping are named: main groups and *soorten*, a word that means both kinds and species. The brackets name the plant kingdom and the animal kingdom. The main groups are not listed, and no features for classifying are given. Fungi are not named in this statement; mushrooms are named in `ojw/pdm/5/08/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Sorting by where an animal lives or how it moves, so that a whale goes with the fish and a bat with the birds. Counting only large or furry animals as animals and leaving out insects, worms and snails. Counting spiders as insects.
