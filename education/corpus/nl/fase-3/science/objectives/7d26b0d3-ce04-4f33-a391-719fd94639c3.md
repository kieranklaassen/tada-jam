## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

consciously experiencing the importance and the function of even the smallest parts of a human being

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tapes one thumb against the hand and tries to button a coat or open a jar. The child tries to pick a coin up from a flat table with the fingertips alone, without using the nails, and stands on one leg with bare feet and feels the toes work to keep balance. The child says afterwards what the small part turned out to be needed for. A child of 10 is surprised by the effect and tells about it; a child of 12 also says what the part does, for example that the eyelashes and eyelids keep dust out of the eye.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experiencing consciously: the wording describes an experience, not knowledge to be repeated. It does not say which parts are meant by the smallest parts and gives no example; whether cells are included is not said (the concepts organ and cells are in `ojw/pdm/5/03/fase3`). The source prints *zelfs kleinste onderdelen* without an article; the gloss reads it as "even the smallest parts".

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often think only of large organs such as the heart and the brain and leave out small parts such as a nail, the eyelashes, a toe or the skin of the fingertips. Many name the part without saying what would go wrong without it.
