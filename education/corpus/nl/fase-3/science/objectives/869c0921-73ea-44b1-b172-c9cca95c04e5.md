## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

describing different climates in Europe and linking climates to landscapes/landscape zones in Europe

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On a map of Europe the child colours regions by climate, such as the mild and wet sea climate of the west, the dry hot summers around the Mediterranean, the cold winters and warm summers further inland, and the cold of the far north and the high mountains. The child reads climate graphs of a few cities and describes each climate in temperature and precipitation through the year. The child matches photographs of landscapes, such as olive groves, deciduous forest, conifer forest, steppe and tundra, to the climates. A child of 10 describes two or three climates and matches clear examples; a child of 12 reads the graphs unaided and explains why a landscape fits a climate.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is limited to Europe, for the climates and for the landscapes. It names no climate, landscape or country and does not say how many climates. The slash leaves open whether single landscapes or landscape zones are meant. The verbs are describing and linking; explaining why the climates differ is not asked.

### Common mistakes

Assuming that it only gets warmer towards the south, and overlooking the difference between places near the sea and places far inland. Believing that the lands around the Mediterranean are hot and dry all year, though their winters are mild and wet. Linking a landscape to a country instead of to a climate, as if the landscape changed at the border.
