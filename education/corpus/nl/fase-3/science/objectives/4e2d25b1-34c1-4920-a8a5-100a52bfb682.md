## Official wording

ervaren dat voor dieren en voor de mens voedsel en water noodzakelijk zijn voor groei en ontwikkeling (opbouw, handhaving, herstel en energie)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing that for animals and for humans food and water are necessary for growth and development (building up, maintenance, repair and energy)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child notes for a day what they ate and drank and how the body felt before a meal, after a meal and after sport without a drink. The child follows young animals kept in the classroom, such as caterpillars or stick insects, measures their length each week and notes what they eat and drink. The child says which of the four named uses is meant in an example: growing taller, keeping the body working, healing a graze, having energy to run. A child of 10 links food to growing and to energy; a child of 12 also names maintenance and repair, for example that skin and hair are renewed all the time.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experiencing. The statement names animals and humans, not plants, and two needs: food and water. The brackets name four things under growth and development: building up, maintenance, repair and energy. No foods, nutrients or amounts are named. The code of this record ends in `fase1`, while the record's level is fase 3 and the other codes of this cluster at this level end in `fase3`; the code is given as the data has it.

### Common mistakes

Thinking food is only fuel for moving, and not also the material the body is built from. Thinking only children need food for building, because adults have stopped growing. Thinking an animal that lies still, or that sleeps through the winter, uses no energy.
