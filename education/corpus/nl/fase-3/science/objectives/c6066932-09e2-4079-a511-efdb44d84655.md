## Official wording

beseffen dat micro-organismen betrokken zijn bij de bereiding van voedsel (bijv. gisting), bij de voedselvertering (in de darmen), bij voedselbederf en bij het vergaan van (delen van) dode organismen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that micro-organisms are involved in the preparation of food (e.g. fermentation), in the digestion of food (in the intestines), in food spoilage and in the decay of (parts of) dead organisms

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees bread dough with yeast rise and hears that yoghurt and cheese are also made with the help of micro-organisms. The child looks at a mouldy piece of fruit or bread in a closed see-through bag, and outdoors at the half-decayed leaves under freshly fallen ones. The child hears that bacteria in the intestines help to digest food. A child of 10 sees the changes and learns that very small living things cause them; a child of 12 names the four places where micro-organisms are involved: making food, the intestines, spoiled food and dead plants or animals.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising: the statement asks for an awareness, not for an experiment. Four places are named where micro-organisms are involved: the preparation of food, with fermentation as an example; the digestion of food, with the intestines in brackets; food spoilage; and the decay of dead organisms or parts of them. No micro-organisms are named, and illness caused by micro-organisms is not mentioned. The text sets no level of detail and states no safety condition for spoiled food.

### Common mistakes

Thinking all micro-organisms are harmful and cause illness. Thinking food spoils and leaves rot by themselves, with nothing living involved. Thinking micro-organisms are found only in dirty places.
