## Official wording

onderzoeken van energie: voorbeelden van energiebronnen (aardgas, aardwarmte, olie, biomassa, hout, kernenergie, water, wind, zon, maar ook mens en paard); energiebronnen kunnen verschillende vormen van energie leveren; energievormen kunnen in elkaar overgaan waarbij geen energie verloren gaat (bijv. licht in warmte, elektriciteit in warmte en licht, spierkracht in beweging); reflectie op gebruik van energiebronnen (beschikbaarheid, voor- en nadelen)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating energy: examples of energy sources (natural gas, geothermal heat, oil, biomass, wood, nuclear energy, water, wind, sun, but also human and horse); energy sources can supply different forms of energy; forms of energy can change into one another, with no energy being lost (e.g. light into heat, electricity into heat and light, muscle power into movement); reflecting on the use of energy sources (availability, advantages and disadvantages)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child follows the energy in a few things step by step and writes each as a chain: a hand-cranked torch (muscle power, movement, electricity, light and heat), a solar cell that drives a small motor, a dark stone warmed by the sun. The child feels that a charger and a running motor get warm and counts that heat as part of the energy that came out. The child sorts cards of energy sources and sets out for each whether it can be used up, when it is available and what its advantages and disadvantages are. A child of 10 names sources and single changes of form; a child of 12 draws chains of several steps, accounts for the heat at each step and compares the sources with each other, giving reasons.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. After the colon four parts are named. The first says "examples of energy sources", and its brackets name eleven: natural gas, geothermal heat, oil, biomass, wood, nuclear energy, water, wind, sun, human and horse. The second and third say that sources can supply different forms of energy and that forms change into one another with no energy lost; the three changes after "bijv." are examples. The fourth is reflection on the use of sources, with availability and advantages and disadvantages in brackets. Coal is not in the list, the words renewable and fossil are not used, and no unit of energy is named.

### Common mistakes

Believing that energy is gone after use, when it has changed into another form, mostly heat that spreads into the surroundings. Believing that electricity is an energy source, when it has to be made from a source. Believing that sources that cannot be used up, such as sun and wind, have no disadvantages.
