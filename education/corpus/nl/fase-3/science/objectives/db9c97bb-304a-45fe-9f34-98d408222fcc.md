## Official wording

aan de hand van een werktekening iets construeren dat aan vooraf geformuleerde bedoelingen beantwoordt

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

constructing something, on the basis of a working drawing, that meets intentions formulated beforehand

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes down beforehand what the thing must do, such as a box with a lid that closes and holds a set of pencils, and works from a working drawing with views, sizes and a list of parts. The child measures and marks the parts from the drawing, cuts and joins them in order and checks the sizes against the drawing while building. At the end the child tests the thing against each of the written demands. A child of 10 follows a drawing with sizes in whole centimetres; a child of 12 reads a drawing to scale with several views and checks the result against every demand.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two conditions are stated: the child works from a working drawing, and what is made meets intentions that were formulated beforehand. The wording does not say who makes the drawing or who formulates the intentions, and it names no object, material, tool or scale.

### Common mistakes

Reading a size from the drawing without using the scale, or measuring from the end of the ruler instead of from zero. Building a part mirrored or turned compared with the drawing. Stating what the thing is for only afterwards, so that there is nothing to test it against.
