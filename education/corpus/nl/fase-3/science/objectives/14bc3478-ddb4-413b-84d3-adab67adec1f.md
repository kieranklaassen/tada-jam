## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the connection between form and function in (parts of) plants and in (parts of) animals

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tries to pick up seeds, to pull a piece of string out of a narrow hole and to scoop floating crumbs from water, using tweezers, pliers and a sieve, and matches each tool to a bird's beak of that shape. The child drops a maple fruit with its wing and one with the wing cut off and compares how they fall. The child looks at the webbed foot of a duck, the digging claws of a mole or the long root of a dandelion and predicts from the shape what the part is used for. A child of 10 matches a shape to a use; a child of 12 explains why the shape suits the use and tests the explanation with a model.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The subject is the connection between form and function, in plants and in animals, as wholes or as parts: the brackets add "parts of" for both. No plants, animals, parts or functions are named. Humans are not named here; form and function in the human body are in `ojw/pdm/1/01/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Saying that a plant or animal grew a part on purpose because it needed it. Naming the form and the function next to each other without saying how the one makes the other possible. Assuming every feature has a use that can be read from its shape.
