comparing organs of animals with those of humans and of (seed) plants

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes one task, such as taking in oxygen or carrying fluid through the body, and looks up which part does it in an animal, in a human and in a seed plant. The child fills in a table, for example for gas exchange: gills in a fish, lungs in a human, and tiny openings in the leaves of a plant. The child compares a drawing of the heart of a fish, with two chambers, with one of the human heart, with four. A child of 10 fills in the table for the animal and the human; a child of 12 adds the plant and names tasks that a plant carries out with quite different parts or not at all, such as moving from place to place.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The comparison starts from the organs of animals and goes to those of humans and of plants. The source writes *(zaad-) planten*, with a space after the closing bracket: plants, with the part of the word that narrows it to seed plants in brackets. No organs or animals are named. Another statement, `ojw/pdm/1/05/fase3`, states the comparison starting from human organs.

### Common mistakes

Assuming an animal has the same organs as a human, for example that a fish breathes with lungs or that an insect has bones. Thinking a plant exchanges no gases with the air because it has no lungs. Matching organs by shape and not by task.
