## Official wording

ordenen en op naam brengen van, planten (en paddenstoelen) en dieren uit de omgeving met behulp van passende determineersleutels (op kaart en computer)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

ordering and identifying by name plants (and mushrooms) and animals from the surroundings with the help of suitable identification keys (on card and computer)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a twig with leaves, a wild flower or a small water animal from near the school and works through an identification key step by step: leaves opposite each other or alternating, leaf edge smooth or toothed, six legs or more. The child arrives at a name, checks the description and picture against the real thing, and goes back a step when they do not match. The child does the same with a key on a computer and compares the two results. A child of 10 follows a short key with pictures; a child of 12 uses a key with more steps and the terms it needs, and sorts the named finds into groups.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The plants, mushrooms and animals are those from the surroundings; mushrooms are added in brackets after plants. The keys are to be suitable, and the brackets name two forms: on card and on computer. The source prints a comma after *van*, before *planten*; the gloss leaves it out. No species or groups are named, and the statement does not ask for names known from memory.

### Common mistakes

Going straight to the picture that looks most alike without following the steps of the key. Deciding on colour or size, which vary within one species, and not on the feature the key asks for. Stopping at the first name reached without checking the other features.
