## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a model, a drawing or a film of the inside of an animal, such as a fish, a bird or a rabbit, and points to the heart, the stomach, the intestines and the lungs or gills. The child lays a drawing of the organs of the animal beside one of the human organs and finds the organs with the same name. A child of 10 names a few organs and where they lie; a child of 12 says what each one does and notices organs that people do not have, such as the swim bladder of a fish or the gizzard of a bird.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is getting to know: a first meeting. The statement is about internal organs of animals; it names no organs and no animals. It names no method: it does not say whether real organs, models or pictures are used. Human organs are in the cluster on the human being, for example in `ojw/pdm/1/01/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Assuming every animal has the same organs as a human, for example that an insect has lungs and bones. Thinking small animals such as insects and worms have no organs inside. Naming an organ without saying what it does.
