## Official wording

verklaren dat het gedrag van iemand het gevolg kan zijn van emoties, gevoelens, opvattingen en wensen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

explaining that someone's behaviour can be the result of emotions, feelings, views and wishes

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads a story or watches a scene in which a character slams a door, stays silent or gives something away, and says which emotion, view or wish could be behind it, giving more than one possibility. After an incident the child tells what each person may have felt, thought or wanted that led to what he or she did. A child of 10 gives one cause for a behaviour; a child of 12 gives several possible causes, tells emotions, views and wishes apart, and says that he or she cannot be sure without asking.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is explaining. Four sources of behaviour are named: emotions, feelings, views and wishes; the statement lists emotions and feelings separately and does not say how they differ. It says behaviour "can be" the result, not that it always is. It says "someone", so it does not limit whose behaviour is explained, and it names no behaviour.

### Common mistakes

Children explain behaviour by what kind of person someone is ("he is just mean") and not by what that person felt, thought or wanted. They give one cause and treat it as certain. They explain their own behaviour by the situation and another's by character.
