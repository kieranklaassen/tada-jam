## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads a receipt, a guarantee slip and the returns notice of a shop or web shop, and finds out what a buyer may do when a product breaks or is not what was promised. The child practises returning a faulty product: says what is wrong, shows the receipt, says what he or she asks for, and writes a short complaint. A child of 10 knows to keep the receipt and to go back to the shop with a faulty thing; a child of 12 looks up what the rule is for a concrete case, such as a purchase online, and words the complaint.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is investigating, and two things are investigated: the rights as a consumer, and how to deal with them. The statement names no rights, laws, organisations or kinds of purchase, and does not say whose rights are meant or whether the child is the buyer.

### Common mistakes

Children think a shop decides for itself whether it takes back a faulty product. They think that throwing away the packaging ends every right. They mix up a faulty product with regret about a purchase, for which other rules hold.
