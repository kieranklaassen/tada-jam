## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child practises on another child or on a manikin: checking whether the place is safe, speaking to the person and checking for a response, cooling a burn under running water, pressing on a bleeding wound and bandaging it, putting on a sling, and laying someone on his or her side. The child practises the call to the emergency number: who, where, what happened, and staying on the line. A child of 10 carries out a few fixed steps in order and gives a clear report by telephone; a child of 12 chooses the fitting steps in a practice case and tells a bystander to fetch help.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is practising, which describes doing and repeating, not only a first meeting. The word "simple" (*eenvoudige*) stands in brackets, so the statement covers first-aid skills and simple ones; it names none, names no injuries or situations, and states no safety condition such as adult supervision. Dealing with 'simple health problems' is the subject of `ojw/pdm/2/05/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Children go straight to the hurt person without first looking whether the place is safe for themselves. They are busy with the wound and nobody calls or fetches an adult. On the telephone they tell what happened and leave out where they are.
