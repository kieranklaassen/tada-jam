## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising (*onderkennen*, seeing it for what it is) bullying and being aware of bullying behaviour and working on adequate solutions

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With examples from stories or from the group a child says which is teasing, which is a quarrel and which is bullying, and names the parts people have in it, his or her own included. With the group the child proposes what can be done about a case, agrees who does what, and looks later at whether it worked. A child of 10 recognises open bullying and excluding, and proposes a solution for a case that is given; a child of 12 also recognises gossip and bullying by message, and works on a solution in a case in which he or she has a part as a bystander.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three things are named: recognising bullying, being aware of bullying behaviour, and working on adequate solutions. The statement does not say whose behaviour, so it covers that of others and the child's own. It names no forms of bullying, no solutions and no situations, does not say what makes a solution adequate, and does not say who works on the solutions with whom.

### Common mistakes

Children call bullying "just a joke" when they are the ones doing it or laughing. They think that someone who only watches has no part in it, and so no part in the solution. They take a solution to mean punishing the one who bullies, and leave out what the group changes.
