## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks at a traffic situation, in the street, on a photo or in an account, and names what the road user could do there: wait or go, ride side by side or one behind the other, use the telephone or put it away, take the short route or the quiet one. The child says for each choice what could follow from it, and says which he or she finds right and why. A child of 10 names the choices in a situation he or she knows and picks one with a reason; a child of 12 does so also where the rule allows more than one choice or where friends choose otherwise, and defends the position in a discussion.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: distinguishing choices of behaviour in traffic, and taking positions on them. The statement names no situations, choices or kinds of road user, and does not say which position is right. It is about distinguishing and judging; acting in traffic is the subject of `ojw/ds/4/02/fase3`. It states no safety condition such as adult supervision.

### Common mistakes

Children see only what the rule says, and not that a road user who has priority still chooses whether to take it. They take a position from what they are allowed to do and leave out what others on the road can see or expect. They state a position in a talk and choose differently when cycling with friends.
