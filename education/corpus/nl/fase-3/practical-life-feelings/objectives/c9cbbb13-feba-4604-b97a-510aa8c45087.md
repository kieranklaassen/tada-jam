## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring characteristics and similarities of and differences between *samenlevingsvormen* (forms of living together, or forms of society), such as residential, living and working communities (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child visits or reads about several communities, such as a household, a shared house for students or elderly people, a monastery, a farm where people live and work, or a parent's workplace, and notes for each who belongs to it, what is shared and who decides. The child puts the notes in a table and marks what the communities have in common and where they differ. A child of 10 describes two communities he or she knows and names differences; a child of 12 compares several on the same points, including ones he or she knows only from sources.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is exploring, and what is explored is characteristics, similarities and differences. The Dutch *samenlevingsvormen* can mean forms of living together in a household or group, and can mean forms of society; the statement does not choose. Residential, living and working communities (*woon-, leef- en werkgemeenschappen*) are given as examples (*zoals*). The statement names no characteristics and no points of comparison.

### Common mistakes

Children assume that everyone lives as they do and call another form strange. They list characteristics of each form and do not compare them point by point. They see only differences and leave out what the forms have in common.
