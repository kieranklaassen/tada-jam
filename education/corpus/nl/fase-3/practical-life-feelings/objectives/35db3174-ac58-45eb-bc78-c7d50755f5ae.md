## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child lists what others can know about him or her (name, address, photos, where he or she is, what he or she wrote in a chat) and sorts it by who may know it: everyone, friends, family, nobody. The child looks at the settings of an app or account he or she uses, or at a form that asks for data, and finds out who can see what and what is kept. The child asks an older person how such things were known and kept when that person was young. A child of 10 says what he or she wants to keep to himself or herself, and finds out who can see a photo once it has been shared; a child of 12 finds out what a service collects, and compares that with what he or she gets for it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is investigating, and two things are investigated: what privacy is, and how to deal with it in a changing society. The statement does not say what is changing in society. It names no technology, rule, law or situation and no way of dealing with privacy, and sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

Children think privacy matters only to people who have something to hide. They think that what they share with a friend or in a closed group stays there. They think of privacy as something of their own, and not of the photos or messages of others as something those others may want to keep to themselves.
