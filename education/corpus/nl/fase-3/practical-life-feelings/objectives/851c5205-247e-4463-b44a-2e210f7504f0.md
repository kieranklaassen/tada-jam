## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child receives tasks for a week, estimates how long each takes, puts them in an order, starts without being told and works on when nobody checks. When stuck, the child rereads the instruction, tries another way or asks someone nearby before asking an adult; at the end the child checks the work and records what is done. A child of 10 plans and carries out the tasks of a day or a week; a child of 12 plans work that lasts several weeks, such as a project or homework, and changes the plan when the work is late.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement gives two phrasings joined by a slash: carrying out tasks independently, and working independently; the brackets name attitude to work (*werkhouding*). It names no tasks, no length of time and no degree of help, and does not say what independent (*zelfstandig*) rules out. It stands under the heading living together and working together (*Samen leven en samenwerken*).

### Common mistakes

Children start with the task they like and have no time left for the others. They estimate too little time for a large task and begin it late. They take working independently to mean never asking anything, and go on with a wrong approach.
