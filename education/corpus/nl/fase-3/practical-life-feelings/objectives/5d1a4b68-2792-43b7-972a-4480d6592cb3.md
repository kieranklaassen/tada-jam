## English gloss

discussing that there are different forms of expression of and views about sex

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk a child hears that there are different forms in which sex is expressed and that people hold different views about it. The child says in his or her own words what he or she has understood and asks the questions he or she has. A child of 10 mostly listens and notes that views differ; a child of 12 can say that people differ in this, and can repeat a view he or she has heard without calling another view strange.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing, and what is discussed is that there are different forms of expression of sex and different views about it. The statement names no forms of expression and no views, does not say which view is right, names no acts or situations and sets no level of detail. It stands under the heading relationships and sexuality (*Relaties en seksualiteit*).

### Common mistakes

Children take what they heard at home or from other children for what everyone thinks. They take what they have seen in media for how it is for everyone. They laugh at or reject a view that differs from the one they know.
