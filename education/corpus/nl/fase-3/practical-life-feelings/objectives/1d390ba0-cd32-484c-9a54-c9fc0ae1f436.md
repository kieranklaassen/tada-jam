## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering that many personal characteristics and skills are not fixed, but can be developed (identity)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child puts work from the start of the year beside work from now (a text, a drawing, a recorded reading) and says what changed and what he or she did for that. The child chooses one thing to get better at, such as speaking before a group or keeping patience, practises it for some weeks and records what happens. The child asks an adult what that adult could not yet do at the same age. A child of 10 sees it for skills that can be practised, such as a sport or handwriting; a child of 12 sees it also for traits such as shyness or a quick temper, and says which things about himself or herself change little.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discovering. The statement says "many" personal characteristics and skills, not all, and says they "can be" developed; it does not say which, or by what. The brackets name identity. The statement names no characteristics or skills.

### Common mistakes

Children take one failure as a fixed trait ("I am just bad at sums"). They think that being good at something means it costs no effort, so that needing practice shows a lack of talent. They read "many" as "all" and expect that everything about a person can be changed by trying.
