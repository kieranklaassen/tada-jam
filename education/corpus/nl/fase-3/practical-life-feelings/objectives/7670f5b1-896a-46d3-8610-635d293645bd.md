## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks in advertisements, series, shop shelves and schoolbooks at what boys and girls, men and women are shown doing, counts it, and compares it with what the people he or she knows do. The child asks a grandparent or another older person what was expected of boys and girls when that person was young. A child of 10 says where such expectations come from, such as home, friends, media and shops; a child of 12 says how they are passed on, by praise, teasing and example, and that they change over time.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is seeing in the sense of understanding (*inzien*), and its object is how gender roles arise and how they are learned. The Dutch *sekserollen* is literally "sex roles": the behaviour expected of someone as a boy or a girl, a man or a woman. The statement names no roles and nothing from which they are learned, and does not say whether the roles are good or bad.

### Common mistakes

Children think that what boys and girls usually do is fixed by the body and cannot be otherwise. They think they chose every preference themselves and that nobody influenced them. They correct or tease a child who does not follow a role, and do not see that this is one of the ways in which the role is taught.
