realising the importance of (sexual) *integriteit* (a person's inviolability, or acting uprightly)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child hears or reads two short stories: one in which a person's body and person are treated as his or her own, and one in which a person acts uprightly although nobody would have noticed otherwise. The child says what this meant for the people in each story, and what would have been lost for them if it had gone differently. A child of 10 says that it matters; a child of 12 gives reasons why it matters, for the person and for the people around that person.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising. The Dutch *integriteit* can mean that a person's body and person are not to be violated, and can mean acting uprightly; the statement does not choose. The word "sexual" stands in brackets, so the statement covers integrity in general and sexual integrity. It names no parts of the body, situations or acts and sets no level of detail. It stands under the heading relationships and sexuality (*Relaties en seksualiteit*).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out the other person: they see why it matters for themselves and do not see that it matters as much for another. They also think it counts for less when the other person is older or in charge.
