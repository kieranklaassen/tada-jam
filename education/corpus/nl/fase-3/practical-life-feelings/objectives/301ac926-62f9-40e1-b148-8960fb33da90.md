## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring with regard to *het voorkomen* (the occurrence, or the preventing), the danger and the use of bacteria and viruses in relation to health (infection, flu, vaccinating)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks, without opening them, at closed containers with slices of bread that were touched with a hand, a door handle and a telephone, and compares what has appeared on each. The child makes yoghurt, or finds out how cheese is made, and so meets bacteria that people use. The child reads or hears how an infection passes from one person to another, what happens in a body with flu, and what a vaccination does. A child of 10 says that bacteria and viruses are too small to see and are in many places, and that some make people ill; a child of 12 tells useful from harmful ones with examples, and explains in a few steps how a vaccination prepares the body.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The source prints "exploring with regard to" (*verkennen m.b.t.*) without a word for what is explored, and the gloss keeps that. The verb is exploring. Three things about bacteria and viruses are named in relation to health: *het voorkomen*, which can mean their occurrence and can mean preventing them; their danger; and their use. The brackets name infection, flu and vaccinating, without *bijv.* or *zoals*. The statement names no kinds of bacteria or viruses and no illness other than flu, does not say how bacteria and viruses differ, sets no level of detail and states no safety condition.

### Common mistakes

Children think all bacteria make people ill, and do not know of those in food and in the gut. They treat bacteria and viruses as the same thing. They think a vaccination is a medicine that cures someone who is already ill.
