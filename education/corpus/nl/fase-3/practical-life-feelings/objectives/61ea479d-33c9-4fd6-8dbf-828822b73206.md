## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads a child-friendly text of the rights of the child, chooses one right, and finds out what it means in daily life and where it is not met. The child presents this to the group and says what would be different for him or her without that right. A child of 10 names several rights and gives an example of each; a child of 12 says why rights are laid down for everyone, and looks at a case in which two rights, or the rights of two people, conflict.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is going more deeply into (*verdiepen in*), which describes more than a first meeting. The slash joins children's rights and human rights; the statement also names their importance. It names no rights, no treaty or declaration and no country.

### Common mistakes

Children mix up a right and a wish: "I have a right to a telephone". They think rights must be earned by good behaviour and can be taken away as a punishment. They think children's rights concern only children in poor or distant countries.
