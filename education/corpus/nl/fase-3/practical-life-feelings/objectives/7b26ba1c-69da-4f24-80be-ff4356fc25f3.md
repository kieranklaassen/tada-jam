## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with one's own emotions (positive and negative) in different situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child who is nervous before a presentation, disappointed by a mark or angry after a match notices the emotion, names it and chooses something to do with it: breathing slowly, stepping away for a moment, saying what is the matter, asking for help. A child who is proud or very excited shows it without mocking someone who did less well. A child of 10 picks a way that fits the place, such as waiting until the break to talk about it; a child of 12 also foresees a situation that will be hard, such as a test or a farewell, and decides beforehand what he or she will do.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name positive and negative emotions, and the statement says "in different situations" without naming any. It names no emotions and no ways of dealing with them. It is about the child's own emotions; the feelings, wishes and views of others are the subject of `ojw/ja/1/06/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Children take dealing with an emotion to mean not showing it, and later show it to someone who had nothing to do with it. They use one way everywhere, such as joking or walking off, whether or not it fits the situation. They do not count positive emotions as something to deal with, and are so excited that others are hurt.
