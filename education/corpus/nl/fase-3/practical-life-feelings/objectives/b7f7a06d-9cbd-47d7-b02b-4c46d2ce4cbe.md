## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking note of important laws and regulations concerning traffic and acting in accordance with them

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child studies the priority rules, the main traffic signs and road markings, and the rules for cyclists and pedestrians such as lights, place on the road and signalling, with pictures of situations and with questions. The child then applies them on a walked or cycled route and says at a crossing who goes first and why. A child of 10 knows the rules for situations on his or her own routes; a child of 12 applies them in a situation he or she has not seen before.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: taking note of important laws and regulations concerning traffic, and acting in accordance with them. The verb *kennisnemen van* is "taking note of", not knowing by heart. The statement calls the laws and regulations important without naming any, names no signs, rules or kinds of road user, and mentions no test. It states no safety condition such as adult supervision.

### Common mistakes

Children think that traffic from the right always has priority, also where signs or markings say otherwise. They know a rule on paper and do not apply it when cycling. They think a cyclist or pedestrian is always in the right against a car, and so do not look.
