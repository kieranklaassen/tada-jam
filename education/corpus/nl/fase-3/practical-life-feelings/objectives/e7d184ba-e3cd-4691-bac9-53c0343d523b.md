## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child gives an opinion in a class meeting or school paper that differs from that of most, and listens to an opinion he or she dislikes without interrupting or mocking. The child reads or hears about a place or time in which people were punished for what they said or wrote, and says what people there could not do. A child of 10 says that everyone may say what he or she thinks and that others may answer; a child of 12 gives reasons why this matters for a group or a country, such as that mistakes of those in charge can be named.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The open data carries the marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*), on this goal, and the content-line document does not print it there. The verb is realising: the statement is about awareness of why free expression of opinion (*vrije meningsuiting*) matters. It does not say why it matters, names no law or country, and does not mention limits on what may be said. Discrimination is the subject of `ojw/ds/1/07/fase3`, and children's rights and human rights of `ojw/ds/1/08/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out that the freedom holds for opinions they dislike: they claim it for themselves and want others silenced. They also take criticism of what they said for an attack on their freedom to say it.
