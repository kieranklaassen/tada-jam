dealing with 'simple health problems' and realising consequences or possibilities for recovery

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child with a cold, a headache, a small cut, a sprain or a blister does what fits: rests, drinks water, rinses and covers the cut, cools the ankle and keeps weight off it. The child says what can happen if the problem is ignored, what helps it to heal, and at which signs an adult or a doctor is needed. A child of 10 treats a small cut or bruise without help and tells an adult about anything more; a child of 12 also judges whether he or she can go on with sport or work, and says what could follow from going on.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement puts 'simple health problems' in quotation marks and names none. Two things are named: dealing with them, and realising consequences or possibilities for recovery; the word is "or" (*of*), and the statement does not say consequences of what. It names no ways of dealing with them and states no safety condition such as adult supervision. First-aid skills are the subject of `ojw/pdm/2/09/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Children go on with sport on a sprained ankle because the pain has eased. They hide a small injury or feeling ill so as not to miss something, or they treat every scratch as serious. They put a plaster on a cut without rinsing it first.
