## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discussing the influence of different fields of application of technology (*techniek*) and technical systems on the safety of the living environment

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child takes a field in which technology is applied, such as traffic, building, energy, food or communication, and lists things from it in his or her surroundings: traffic lights, a smoke alarm, a dyke with a pumping station, a fuse box, a camera. For each the child says what it does for safety and what danger it brings or would bring if it failed, and brings this to a group talk. A child of 10 does this for single things he or she can see; a child of 12 does it for a system of connected parts, such as the electricity supply or a level crossing, and compares fields.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing, and what is discussed is an influence on the safety of the living environment: that of different fields of application of *techniek* and of technical systems. The Dutch *techniek* covers made things and the skill of making and using them. The statement names no fields, systems or dangers and does not say whether the influence makes the living environment safer or less safe. It is about discussing, not about using a device, and states no safety condition such as adult supervision.

### Common mistakes

Children take technology to mean only screens and things with a plug, and leave out dykes, locks and brakes. They see a technical thing as either safe or dangerous, and not as something that lowers one risk and brings another. They name single devices and do not see how the parts of a system depend on each other.
