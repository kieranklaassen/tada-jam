## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that there are different *gewoonten* (customs or habits) and views about nudity

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child notices that homes, changing rooms and countries differ in what is usual about being undressed, and tells in a group talk what is usual where he or she lives or has been. The child hears that others are used to something else, and says what he or she is comfortable with. A child of 10 names differences between homes or places; a child of 12 can say that these are *gewoonten* and views that differ between people, and that his or her own is one among them.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising. The Dutch *gewoonten* can mean customs of a group and can mean habits of a person; the statement does not choose. What exists in different forms is *gewoonten* and views about nudity (*bloot*, being undressed). The statement names no customs, habits, views, places or situations, does not say which is right and sets no level of detail. It stands under the heading relationships and sexuality (*Relaties en seksualiteit*).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out that what they know is one way among several: they take what is usual at home for normal and call another way strange or dirty. They also tease a child who is more covered or less covered than they are used to.
