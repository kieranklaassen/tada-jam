## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(consciously) dealing with (pocket) money

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child keeps a record for some weeks of the money he or she gets and spends, in cash or on an account, and adds it up per kind of spending. The child sets a saving goal, works out how many weeks it takes, and decides at each purchase whether it is worth reaching the goal later. A child of 10 keeps the record and saves for a goal a few weeks away; a child of 12 draws up a budget for a month, compares prices before buying, and plans for a larger cost such as a telephone subscription or a gift.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two parts stand in brackets: "consciously" (*bewust*) and "pocket" (*zak-*), so the statement covers dealing with money in general and dealing consciously with pocket money. This goal states no number range and no amounts, and names no ways of dealing with money. It does not say whether the child has money of his or her own. The function of a bank and the working of bank accounts are the subject of `ojw/ds/2/05/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Children spend the money as soon as they have it and have nothing left for what they wanted more. They do not count small purchases, and so cannot say where the money went. They treat money on a card or in an app as less real than coins.
