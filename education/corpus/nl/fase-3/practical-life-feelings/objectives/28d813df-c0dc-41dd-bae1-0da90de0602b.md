## Official wording

bewust omgaan met eigen gevoelens en opvattingen en deze voor anderen begrijpelijk uiten

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing consciously with one's own feelings and views and expressing them in a way others can understand

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child who is angry about a decision in a group task says what he or she feels and what he or she thinks about the decision, and gives a reason for each. The child writes a note or message about something that upset him or her, rereads it, and asks the reader whether it was understood as meant. A child of 10 separates what he or she feels from what he or she thinks about it; a child of 12 also chooses the words, the moment and the person so that the other can follow, and waits before reacting in order to do so.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: dealing consciously with one's own feelings and views, and expressing them so that others understand. The statement does not say how they are expressed (in words, in writing or otherwise) and does not say who the others are. It names no feelings, views or situations.

### Common mistakes

Children state a view as a fact ("that is just stupid"), so that the listener cannot tell that it is a view. They hide a feeling in front of a group and show it later in a way the other does not understand, such as ignoring someone. They mix up a feeling and a view: "I feel that it is unfair".
