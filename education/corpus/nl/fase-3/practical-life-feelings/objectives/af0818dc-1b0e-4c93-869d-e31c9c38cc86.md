## Official wording

ervaren wat het belang is van aspecten als gezondheid, veiligheid en stevigheid (maar ook duurzaamheid) bij de keuze van een product

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster ojw/ds/2; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau af0818dc-1b0e-4c93-869d-e31c9c38cc86.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing what the importance is of aspects such as health, safety and sturdiness (but also *duurzaamheid*, sustainability or durability) in the choice of a product

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child compares three versions of one product, such as a bicycle helmet, a school bag, a snack or a toy: reads the label and the safety marks, pulls at seams and straps, and looks at what it is made of and whether it can be repaired. The child chooses one and tells which aspect decided the choice and what he or she gave up for it. A child of 10 tests and compares on aspects that are given; a child of 12 decides which aspects count for this product, finds the information for them, and compares them with the price.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experiencing. Health, safety and sturdiness are given as examples of aspects ("aspects such as", *aspecten als*), and the brackets add *duurzaamheid*, a word that can mean sustainability and can mean durability; the statement does not choose. It names no products, and it speaks of the choice of a product without saying that the child buys one. The list is open: the wording names these four aspects and does not give them as complete.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out everything except looks, brand and price when they choose. They also take a claim on the packaging, such as "healthy" or "green", for a tested fact.
