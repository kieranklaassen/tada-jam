## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having an awareness of how addictive habits arise and of the influence of this on functioning as a person

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child describes a habit of his or her own that is hard to stop, and notes for some days when it happens and what comes before it. In a group talk the child hears how a habit can become addictive and what that does to a person's days, and says in his or her own words what he or she has understood. A child of 10 says that something done often becomes hard to leave; a child of 12 describes in steps how a habit can grow into one that a person cannot easily leave, and names effects on what the person does with others and for himself or herself.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement speaks of having an awareness (*besef hebben*), not of a skill. Two things are named: how addictive habits arise, and the influence of this on functioning as a person (*het functioneren als mens*). The content-line PDF prints an example in brackets after the goal (*bijv.*), which the open data leaves out: smoking, alcohol, drugs, gambling, computer; it is an example, not a list of what is treated. The statement names no situations and sets no level of detail. It does not say what functioning as a person covers.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out the beginning: they think addiction is something that is there at once, and not something that grows from a habit. They also think a person can stop at any moment he or she decides to.
