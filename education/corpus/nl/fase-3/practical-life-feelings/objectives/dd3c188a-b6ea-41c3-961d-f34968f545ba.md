## Official wording

bewust worden van de (eigen) bijdrage aan een veilige leefomgeving

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

becoming aware of the (one's own) contribution to a safe living environment

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child walks through the school and the street around it, notes what makes the place safe or unsafe (a blocked emergency exit, a bicycle lying on the pavement, a broken lamp, broken glass) and marks which of these he or she can change. The child lists who else contributes, such as neighbours, a crossing patrol or the police, and what each does. A child of 10 names things he or she does or leaves undone, such as clearing a passage or reporting broken glass; a child of 12 also counts how he or she treats others, such as not passing on a threatening message, as part of the contribution.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is becoming aware. The word "own" (*eigen*) stands in brackets, so the statement covers the contribution to a safe living environment in general and the child's own. It does not say what makes a living environment safe (traffic, fire, crime or how people treat each other), names no place and no actions, and states no safety condition such as adult supervision. Recognising dangerous situations and dealing with them is the subject of `ojw/ds/1/02/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out their own part: they see safety as the work of the police, the fire service and adults. They also count only what they must not do, and leave out what they can add, such as reporting something or helping someone.
