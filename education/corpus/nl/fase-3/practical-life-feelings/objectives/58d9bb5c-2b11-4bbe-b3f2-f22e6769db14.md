discussing different forms in which discrimination is expressed and through what discrimination can arise (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk a child looks at cases from the news, from stories or from his or her own experience and says in what form the discrimination shows: a remark or joke, excluding someone, a rule that treats a group differently, violence. For each case the child says what could lie behind it, such as fear of what is unknown, a prejudice passed on by others, or wanting to belong to a group. A child of 10 names forms and one possible cause in a case that is given; a child of 12 recognises less open forms, such as not being invited or not being chosen, and names several causes that work together.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is discussing, and two things are discussed: different forms in which discrimination is expressed, and through what it can arise. The statement names no forms, no causes and no groups of people, says that discrimination "can" arise without listing from what, and sets no level of detail. Recognising prejudices and discrimination in oneself and others, and discussing how to deal with this, is the subject of `ojw/ja/3/05/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Children count only open forms, such as name-calling, and not excluding or a joke. They think discrimination is done only by bad people and on purpose. They mix up prejudice (a judgement in advance) and discrimination (treating unequally).
