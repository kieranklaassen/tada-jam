## Official wording

verlenen van eenvoudige verkeershulp aan jongeren en ouderen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

giving simple traffic help to *jongeren en ouderen* (the young and the elderly, or younger and older people)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child walks with a younger child to a crossing, waits there, says when the road is clear and crosses together with him or her. The child asks an elderly person who waits at the kerb whether help is wanted, and offers an arm or carries a bag across. A child of 10 helps a younger child he or she knows on a known route; a child of 12 also offers help to someone he or she does not know, and judges first whether he or she can give it safely.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about giving help in traffic and calls the help simple. Those who are helped are named as *jongeren en ouderen*, which can be read as the young and the elderly or as people younger and older than the child; the statement does not choose. It names no kinds of help and no situations, and states no safety condition such as adult supervision.

### Common mistakes

Children help without asking and take hold of someone who did not want it. They watch the person they help and stop watching the traffic. They pull a younger child along at their own speed.
