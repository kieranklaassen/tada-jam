## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising (*onderkennen*, seeing it for what it is) prejudices and discrimination in oneself and others and discussing how to deal with this (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads or hears remarks, jokes and news items and says which contain a judgement about a whole group made before knowing the person, and which describe unequal treatment. The child tells of a time when he or she thought something about a person in advance and found it was not so. In a group talk the child says what can be done when one hears such a remark or sees such treatment, and listens to what others propose. A child of 10 recognises a prejudice in a remark or joke and says how it differs from discrimination; a child of 12 recognises one of his or her own, and compares several ways of dealing with it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The first verb is recognising, with two objects (prejudices, and discrimination) and two places (in oneself and in others); the second is discussing how to deal with this. The statement names no groups of people, no examples and no ways of dealing with it, and sets no level of detail. Forms in which discrimination is expressed and how it can arise are the subject of `ojw/ds/1/07/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Children recognise a prejudice in others and not in themselves. They do not count a favourable judgement about a whole group ("all ... are good at ...") as a prejudice. They think that dealing with it means only that an adult punishes someone, and leave out what they can say or do.
