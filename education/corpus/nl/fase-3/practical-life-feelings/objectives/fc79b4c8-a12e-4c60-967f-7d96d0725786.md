## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks, without opening them, at two closed containers, one with a slice of bread that was touched with unwashed fingers and one with a slice that was touched with washed fingers, and compares what has grown on each. The child lists the moments in a day at which hygiene counts (after the toilet, before eating, after sport, when preparing food, when coughing) and says for each what could be passed on. A child of 10 links washing hands and clean food to not getting ill; a child of 12 says how something that cannot be seen passes from one person to another, and why hygiene protects others as well as oneself.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising: the statement is about awareness of why good hygiene matters, not about carrying it out. It does not say what good hygiene is or whose hygiene is meant, and names no habits, illnesses or parts of the body. The text states no safety condition. The combination of healthy food, movement, hygiene and rest is the subject of `ojw/pdm/2/03/fase3`, and bacteria and viruses of `ojw/pdm/2/10/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out other people: they see hygiene as something for their own sake, and not as protection of those they touch or prepare food for. They also think that what looks clean is clean, and that a quick rinse without soap is washing.
