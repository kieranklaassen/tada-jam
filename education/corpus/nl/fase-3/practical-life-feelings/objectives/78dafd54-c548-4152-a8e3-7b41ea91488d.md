## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a group talk a child tells which medicines he or she knows of, such as a painkiller, an inhaler for asthma or an antibiotic, and what he or she knows they do. With the group the child reads the package leaflet of a common medicine and finds what it is for and which side effects are listed. A child of 10 says what a medicine he or she knows is for; a child of 12 tells apart a medicine that eases a complaint and one that takes away its cause, and says that a medicine can have effects that are wanted and effects that are not.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing, and what is discussed is the effect of medicines. The statement names no medicines, illnesses or doses, does not say whether wanted or unwanted effects are meant, and sets no level of detail. It is about discussing, not about handling or taking a medicine, and states no safety condition such as adult supervision.

### Common mistakes

Children think a medicine cures at once, and that a larger dose works better. They think antibiotics help against every illness, a cold included. They think a medicine that is sold without a prescription, or a remedy called natural, has no unwanted effects.
