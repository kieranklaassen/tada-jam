## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

developing an attitude of standing up for oneself (*weerbare houding*) and discovering the importance of this (in a *zelfbewuste* (self-aware or self-assured) way making and refusing requests, giving and accepting compliments, giving and receiving criticism)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In practice pairs and in real situations a child asks for something plainly, says "no, I do not want that" without an excuse when pressed, gives a compliment that names what the other did, answers a compliment with "thank you", tells someone what bothers him or her about what that person did, and listens to criticism without shouting back. Afterwards the child says what the result was of speaking up, or what happened when he or she did not. A child of 10 does this with children he or she knows; a child of 12 also refuses when a group presses, gives criticism of what someone did and not of the person, and says why being able to do this matters.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: developing an attitude called *weerbaar* (able to stand up for oneself), and discovering why it matters. The brackets name three pairs: making and refusing requests, giving and accepting compliments, giving and receiving criticism, all in a way called *zelfbewust*, a word that can mean self-aware and can mean self-assured. The statement names no situations and no persons to whom these are said.

### Common mistakes

Children take standing up for oneself to mean being hard or loud, and attack where a plain "no" would do. They say yes under pressure from a group and then do not do it, or refuse with an excuse. They hear criticism of what they did as dislike of themselves.
