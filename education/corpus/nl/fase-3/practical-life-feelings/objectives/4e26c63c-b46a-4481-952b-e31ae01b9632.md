## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring what society offers for people with a disability or (chronic) illness

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child goes through the school or the streets around it in a wheelchair, or with a blindfold and a guide, and notes what helps and what hinders: ramps, lifts, guiding lines in the pavement, sound at traffic lights, a toilet with room. The child asks someone with a disability or chronic illness, or reads an account, about the help, aids and arrangements that person uses. A child of 10 finds and names provisions that can be seen in the surroundings; a child of 12 also finds out about provisions that cannot be seen there, such as adapted transport, sign-language interpreters, special schools and help at home, and names what is still lacking.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring, and the question is what society offers for people with a disability or illness. The word "chronic" (*chronische*) stands in brackets, so the statement covers illness in general and chronic illness. It names no disabilities, illnesses, provisions or organisations, and does not say who in society offers something.

### Common mistakes

Children think of a disability as a wheelchair only, and leave out what cannot be seen, such as deafness or a chronic illness. They look at what a person cannot do and leave out what the person can do with the right aid. They think that a ramp or lift solves everything, and do not ask the person what he or she needs.
