## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring possibilities people have to contribute to preserving/restoring the natural balance (water management)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child builds a model river in a sand tray, lets water run through a straight channel and through one with bends and room at the sides, and compares how fast and how high the water comes. The child visits or studies a place where water is managed, such as a pumping station, a fish passage, a water-storage area or a restored stream, and notes what was done there and what it is for. A child of 10 describes one such measure and what it changes for the water; a child of 12 explains how the measure helps plants, animals or people, and names what it costs or whom it hinders.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring, and what is explored is possibilities people have to contribute to preserving or restoring the natural balance; the slash joins preserving and restoring. The brackets name water management, without *bijv.* or *zoals*. The statement does not say what the natural balance is, names no measures and no areas, and does not say that the child carries out a measure.

### Common mistakes

Children think that the natural balance returns by itself as soon as people stop doing harm, or that nothing people do can restore it. They take water management to mean only keeping water out with dykes, and leave out storing water and giving it room. They look at one kind of animal or plant and do not see that a measure can help one kind and harm another.
