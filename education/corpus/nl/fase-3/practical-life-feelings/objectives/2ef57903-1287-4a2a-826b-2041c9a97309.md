dealing with conflict situations in one's own surroundings by finding solutions that suit all parties (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a quarrel a child lets each side tell what happened and what it wants, repeats both, and looks with the others for something each can accept: taking turns, sharing, swapping or a new rule. The child checks with each side whether the solution is acceptable, and looks later at whether it was kept. A child of 10 does this in a quarrel between two with fixed steps; a child of 12 leads the steps as a mediator for others, and looks for the wish behind a demand when the two demands cannot both be met.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The statement names conflict situations in the child's own surroundings (*eigen omgeving*) and one way of dealing with them: finding solutions that suit all parties. It does not say which surroundings, names no kinds of conflict, and does not say whether the child is a party or helps others. Dealing with conflict situations, without a way being named, is the subject of `ojw/ja/1/07/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Children take a solution to be a decision on who was right. They accept a solution to end the talk and do not keep to it. They split everything in half, also where neither side is helped by half.
