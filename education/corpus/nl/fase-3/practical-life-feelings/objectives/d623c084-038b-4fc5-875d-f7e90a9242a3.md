## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating one's own characteristics, possibilities, limitations and preferences and developing a *zelfbewuste* (self-aware or self-assured) attitude (self-confidence)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child draws up a profile of himself or herself (what I am like, what I can do, what I find hard, what I prefer) and tests it: asks two others how they see him or her, tries a task he or she expects to be hard, and compares the outcome with what he or she wrote. The child presents the result and says "I am good at ..., I am not good at ... yet". A child of 10 names skills, preferences and some traits of character; a child of 12 uses what he or she found to make a choice, such as a part in a project, and names a limitation without being upset by it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two things are named: investigating four things about oneself (characteristics, possibilities, limitations and preferences), and developing an attitude. The Dutch *zelfbewust* can mean self-aware and can mean self-assured; the statement does not choose, and its brackets add self-confidence (*zelfvertrouwen*). It names no characteristics and no way of investigating.

### Common mistakes

Children judge themselves by comparison with the best of the group and conclude that they can do nothing well. They describe who they would like to be, or who the group expects them to be, and not what they find when they look. They take one failure as a fixed trait.
