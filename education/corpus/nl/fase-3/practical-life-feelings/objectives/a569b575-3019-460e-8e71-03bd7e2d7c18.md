## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

feeling jointly responsible for the right to one's own development and that of others (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child makes room for another child to practise, try and show what he or she can do: gives a turn, does not laugh at a first attempt, and passes on what he or she knows. The child claims the same for himself or herself, for instance by asking for time or help to work on something he or she wants to learn. A child of 10 does this in a group when it is pointed out; a child of 12 notices without being told when someone gets no chance, says so, and can say that a right to develop holds for everyone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The statement is about a feeling: feeling jointly responsible (*medeverantwoordelijk*), which is responsibility shared with others. Its object is the right to one's own development (*ontplooiing*, developing one's abilities) and that of others. It does not say who the others are, where this right is laid down or what the child does out of this feeling. Children's rights and human rights are the subject of `ojw/ds/1/08/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out one side: they claim room for their own development and do not see what that takes from others, or they always let others go first and claim nothing. They also think the right to develop belongs only to those who are already good at something.
