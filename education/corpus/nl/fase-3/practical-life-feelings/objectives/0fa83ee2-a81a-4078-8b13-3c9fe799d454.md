## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that traffic depends on human action and as such can be changed

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child observes a crossing or the street by the school at a busy time, counts who passes and how, and notes where it goes wrong and what people did there. The child compares an old photo or map of the street with the present one and says what people changed: a cycle path, a speed bump, a rule. The child draws a proposal for another layout of the street by the school and presents it. A child of 10 says that a traffic jam or a dangerous moment came from what people did; a child of 12 says that the layout, the rules and the habits were all made by people and can be made differently.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising. Two things are realised: that traffic depends on human action, and that as such it can be changed. The statement does not say whose action (that of road users, of those who make the rules or of those who lay out roads) and names no changes. It does not say that the child changes anything, and states no safety condition such as adult supervision.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out that roads and rules were decided by people: they take the street as it is for given. They also see dangerous traffic as the fault of "bad drivers" only, and leave out their own behaviour and the layout.
