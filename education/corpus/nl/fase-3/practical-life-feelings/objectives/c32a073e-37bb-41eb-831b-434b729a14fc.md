## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

responding to or anticipating (*inspelen op*) the line of thought of others from one's own position (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a discussion a child listens to how another person reasons, repeats that reasoning in his or her own words, and then answers it from what he or she thinks: "you say ..., because ...; I see it differently, because ...". Before asking for something, the child thinks about what the other will object and prepares an answer. A child of 10 answers the point the other made and does not only repeat his or her own; a child of 12 follows several steps of another's reasoning, says where he or she agrees and where not, and keeps his or her own position apart from the other's.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The Dutch *inspelen op* can mean responding to something and can mean anticipating it; the statement does not choose. Two things are fixed: what is responded to is the line of thought (*gedachtegang*) of others, and the child does so from his or her own position. The statement does not say who the others are or in what kind of contact this happens, and does not say what "position" covers (a point of view, a role or a place in a group).

### Common mistakes

Children answer what they expected the other to say, not what was said. They repeat their own view more loudly in place of answering the other's reasoning. They take following another's line of thought for agreeing with it, and so either refuse to follow it or give up their own position.
