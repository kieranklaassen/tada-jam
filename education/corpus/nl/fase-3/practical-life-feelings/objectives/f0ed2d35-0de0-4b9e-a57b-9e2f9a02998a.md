## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads a bank statement, a real one or a specimen, points out the account number, the money coming in, the money going out and the balance, and works out the balance after a payment. The child follows a payment by card or telephone step by step: from whose account the money goes, to whose, and what the bank does in between. A child of 10 says what a bank keeps and does for an account holder and tells a current account from a savings account; a child of 12 carries out the steps of a transfer in a practice setting, and says what a PIN or code is for.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. Two things are explored: the function of a bank, and the working of three things: bank accounts, internet banking and mobile banking. This goal states no number range and no amounts, names no kinds of account, and does not say that the child has an account or carries out a payment. Dealing with money is the subject of `ojw/ds/2/01/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Children think the bank keeps each person's money apart as notes in a safe. They think money on a card is without limit as long as the card works. They do not link a payment by telephone to the balance of an account.
