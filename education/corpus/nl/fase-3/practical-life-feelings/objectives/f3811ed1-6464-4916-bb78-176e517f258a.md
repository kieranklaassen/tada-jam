## English gloss

seeing the connection between integration and keeping your own identity (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child tells what he or she kept and what he or she changed on joining a new group, such as a new class, a club or a new country: habits, words and ways of doing things. The child asks someone who moved to another place or country what that person took over from the new surroundings and what he or she kept from before. A child of 10 names things kept and things taken over in a concrete case; a child of 12 says how the two are connected in that case, and where the one made the other easier or harder.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is seeing a connection (*samenhang zien*). The two things connected are integration and keeping your own identity; the statement does not say what the connection is, and says neither that the two go together nor that they conflict. It does not define integration, does not say who integrates into what, and names no groups, countries or cultures.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out one of the two sides: they take integration to mean becoming the same as everyone else, or take keeping an identity to mean changing nothing. They also see identity as one fixed thing, and not as made up of several parts of which some change and some stay.
