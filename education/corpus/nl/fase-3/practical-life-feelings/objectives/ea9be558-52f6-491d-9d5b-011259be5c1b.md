## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

For a group task, such as a presentation, a party or a stall at a market, a child writes down with the group what must be done, in what order, by whom and by when, and what is needed. During the work the child checks the list, speaks to someone who is late, and takes over or swaps a task when needed. A child of 10 does this for a task of a few lessons with a plan form that is given; a child of 12 draws up the plan with the group for work that lasts several weeks, and changes it when something goes wrong.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The statement is about carrying out tasks in a group, and after the colon it names two parts: making plans, and organising the execution. It names no tasks, no size of group and no length of time, and does not say who leads. Carrying out tasks independently is the subject of `ojw/ja/3/06/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Children start doing before anything is agreed, so that two do the same part and one part is left undone. They make a plan and do not look at it again. One child decides and does most of the work, and the others wait.
