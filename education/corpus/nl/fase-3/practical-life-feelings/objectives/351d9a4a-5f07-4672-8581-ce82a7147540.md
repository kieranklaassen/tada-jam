## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating what the influence of the human pattern of consumption on the environment is (for example the waste problem)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child keeps and weighs the waste of his or her household or class for a week, sorts it by kind, and works out what that comes to in a year. The child follows one product, such as a plastic bottle or a pair of jeans, from raw material to waste with the help of sources, and notes what each step takes from the environment or puts into it. A child of 10 measures and sorts the waste and links it to what was bought; a child of 12 compares two ways of consuming, such as buying new and repairing, and reasons about what each does to the environment.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is investigating, and the question is what influence the human pattern of consumption has on the environment. The waste problem is given as an example (*bijv.*); nothing else is named. The statement names no products and no scale (a household, a country or the world), and does not say that the child changes what he or she consumes. Taking care of the environment is the subject of `ojw/rojh/6/04/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Children think waste is gone once it is in the bin or has been collected. They count only the packaging they throw away and leave out what making and transporting the product cost. They think that separating waste undoes the effect of buying more.
