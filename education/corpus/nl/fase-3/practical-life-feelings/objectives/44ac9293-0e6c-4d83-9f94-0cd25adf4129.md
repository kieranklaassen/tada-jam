## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

developing a place of one's own within the group (independence)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child takes a task or part in the group that fits him or her, such as keeping time, explaining to others, drawing or settling quarrels, and says what he or she adds to the group. The child gives an opinion that differs from that of friends, chooses an activity that his or her friends do not choose, and stays in the group while doing so. A child of 10 holds a place in a group he or she knows well; a child of 12 finds a place in a new or changed group, and keeps to his or her own choice when the group presses for another.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is developing. The statement speaks of "the group" and does not say which group; the brackets name independence (*zelfstandigheid*). It does not say what a place of one's own consists of, and names no roles or situations.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out the word "own": they take a place in the group to mean doing what the most popular children do. They also take independence to mean needing nobody, and withdraw from the group.
