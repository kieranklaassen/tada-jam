## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing and using some much-used reference measures (*referentiematen*, familiar things of a known weight) for weight

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child knows the weight of a few familiar things, for example a pack of sugar of 1 kilogram and a paper clip of about 1 gram, and lifts them to feel the difference. The child uses them to estimate: a bag that feels about as heavy as three packs of sugar weighs about 3 kg. A child of 10 uses a reference measure to estimate a weight; a child of 12 uses a reference measure to judge whether a stated weight can be right, for example that a dog of 300 kg cannot be right.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording asks for "some" much-used reference measures and names none. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: in a kg of apples there are about 5, and adults mostly have a weight between 60 and 100 kg; it is an example, not a limit. The things in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. The goal's own wording names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range; the example uses the kilogram, and its numbers belong to the example. The verbs here are knowing and using; the goals of this kind for length and area (`rw/m/1/07/fase3`, `rw/m/2/04/fase3`) say collecting and using, and the one for *inhoud* (`rw/m/3/07/fase3`) says thinking up and using.

### Common mistakes

The child estimates a weight from the size of a thing and gives a large, light thing too high a weight. The child has a reference for the kilogram and none for the gram or for very heavy things, so that its estimates of very small and very large weights are far from the real weight. The child gives an estimate as a number without a measure.
