## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

collecting and ordering data, arguing which graphic representation fits them best and working the data into a graphic representation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child collects data with a question in mind, for example counts for a week how many children come to school by bicycle each day, and orders them in a table. The child says which display fits the data best and why, for example a bar chart for numbers per kind and a line graph for something measured over time, and then makes that display with a title, labelled axes and a legend where needed. A child of 10 chooses between displays that are offered and makes the display on a grid that is given; a child of 12 chooses a kind itself, argues for the choice and sets up the axes itself.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three steps: collecting and ordering data, arguing which graphic representation fits them best, and working the data into a graphic representation. It names no kind of graphic representation; the kinds in the paragraph above are the pack's examples, and `rw/verb/1/03/fase3` names tables, pie, picture and bar charts and line graphs. It names no kind of data, no way of collecting and no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child chooses the display it likes or knows best and gives no reason from the data. The child draws a line graph for separate kinds that have no order, and joins points between which nothing lies. The child collects data without a question and cannot say afterwards what the display must show.
