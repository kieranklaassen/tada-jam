## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and using formal mathematical language for displaying relations in tables, charts (pie, picture, bar) and line graphs such as axes, system of axes (*assenstelsel*, the two axes together), horizontal axis, vertical axis, x-axis, y-axis, legend, rising, falling, increase, decrease, constant, steep and flat

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child points to the parts of a graph and names them: the two axes that together form the system of axes, the horizontal axis or x-axis, the vertical axis or y-axis, and the legend. The child describes the course of a line with the words for it: where it rises or falls, where the increase or decrease is large, where it stays constant, where it is steep and where it is flat. The child understands these words when another person uses them, and finds the place in the graph that is meant. A child of 10 describes the course of a line with the terms; a child of 12 also corrects a description in which a term is used wrongly, for example steep where high is meant.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two activities, recognising and using, for formal mathematical language for displaying relations. It names the displays: tables, three kinds of chart in brackets (pie chart, picture chart and bar chart) and line graphs; it names no other kind of chart or graph. The terms are given as examples ("zoals"), fourteen of them: axes, system of axes, horizontal axis, vertical axis, x-axis, y-axis, legend, rising, falling, increase, decrease, constant, steep and flat. The goal names no particular terms beyond its examples; coordinates are named in `rw/verb/1/04/fase3`. The goal names no formula and states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child mixes up the x-axis and the y-axis, or horizontal and vertical. The child uses "steep" for "high": it calls the highest part of a line the steepest. The child takes "constant" for "nothing" and reads a level line as a value of zero.
