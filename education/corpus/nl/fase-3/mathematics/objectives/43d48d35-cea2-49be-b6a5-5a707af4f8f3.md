## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child compares three boxes of paper clips labelled 1,000, 850 and 1,250 and puts them in order from fewest to most. The child reads the numbers of visitors of four museums in a table and orders the museums, and for two piles of paper that cannot be counted quickly compares the heights of the piles. A child of 10 compares quantities that are shown, such as the boxes or the piles; a child of 12 compares quantities known only from numbers in a text or a table.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two actions on quantities: comparing and ordering. This goal states no number range. It does not say how the quantities are given (as objects, pictures or numbers) or how many quantities are ordered at one time. Comparing and ordering whole numbers by place value is in rw/gb/3/02/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child judges a quantity by the space it takes up and calls a loosely filled large box more than a tightly filled small one. The child compares quantities given in different units, such as boxes and single pieces, without making the units equal. The child orders the numbers by their first digit.
