## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works 3,002 − 1,998 in columns, with borrowing across two zeros, and by counting on from 1,998, first 2 and then 1,002, gets 1,004 both times and says which way gives fewer chances of an error. The child looks at a worked subtraction in which 524 − 268 came to 344, sees that the smaller digit was taken from the larger one in every column, and explains what went wrong. A child of 10 compares two ways and says which was quicker; a child of 12 also explains why each way gives the right outcome.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names critical thinking and reasoning about standard procedures and strategies for adding and subtracting with whole numbers. Its own wording names no procedure and no strategy; rw/bew/1/05/fase3 and rw/bew/1/06/fase3 name examples for their own content. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "When adding several numbers you can put the numbers one under the other and then add. Why is that not handy when subtracting several numbers?"; it is an example, not a limit. This goal states no number range, and the example holds no numbers.

### Common mistakes

Believing that the written algorithm is always the best way. Not noticing an outcome that cannot be right, such as a difference larger than the number subtracted from. Carrying out the steps of borrowing without being able to say what happens to the tens and hundreds.
