## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking in ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) when enlarging and reducing (photos, lengths, areas and volumes)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child enlarges a photo of 10 cm by 15 cm so that every length doubles, measures 20 cm by 30 cm and finds, by laying the small photo on the large one, that the area is 4 times as large. The child builds a cube with an edge of 2 blocks and a cube with an edge of 4 blocks and counts 8 and 64 blocks, so 8 times the volume. A child of 10 enlarges a drawing on squared paper and compares the measurements before and after; a child of 12 says beforehand what the enlarging does to them, and checks it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name photos, lengths, areas and volumes as what is enlarged or reduced. This goal states no number range and names no factors of enlargement. The text does not say that areas and volumes change by another factor than lengths do; that they do is the pack's reading of why lengths, areas and volumes are each named. The gloss gives *inhouden* as volumes; the word also covers capacity, how much a container holds. Calculating with scale is in rw/verh/2/08/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that the area and the volume double when the lengths double. The child enlarges by adding the same amount to each side, which changes the shape: 10 by 15 becomes 15 by 20. The child enlarges one side of a photo and not the other.
