weten dat voor inhoud twee standaardmaten gebruikt worden: de liter en de kubieke meter

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing that two standard measures are used for *inhoud* (volume or capacity): the litre and the cubic metre

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says that the amount a bottle, a bucket or a tank holds is usually given in litres, and the size of a room, a shipping container or a load of sand in cubic metres. The child stands in or next to a frame of metre rules that marks a cube of 1 metre in each direction, and holds a litre carton next to it. A child of 10 knows the two names and where each is used; a child of 12 also knows that both measure the same quantity, and says so when an amount of water is given in cubic metres, as on a water meter.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two standard measures: the litre and the cubic metre. It names no relation between them; `rw/m/3/05/fase3` prints 1 l = 1 dm³ and other relations. It names no measure derived from the two, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range. The verb is knowing.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that litres and cubic metres measure different things, litres for liquids and cubic metres for solid things. The child confuses the cubic metre with the square metre or with the metre. The child does not know how large a cubic metre is and takes it for about a bucket, where it holds 1000 litres.
