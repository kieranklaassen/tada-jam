## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shares 3 litres of juice equally over 4 jugs and writes the amount in each jug as 3 ÷ 4 and as 3/4 litre. The child works out 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 and marks 3/4 and 0.75 at the same point on a number line between 0 and 1. A child of 10 links the fraction to a sharing that can be acted out; a child of 12 reads any fraction as its numerator divided by its denominator and finds 1/8 as 1 ÷ 8 = 0.125.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about insight: the fraction is taken as a formal number ("formeel getal"), and the goal says that this number is a division. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "3/4 is 3 ÷ 4" (in Dutch notation 3/4 is 3 : 4); it is an example, not a limit. The text does not explain "formeel getal"; the pack reads it as the fraction seen as a number, apart from any pizza or strip. The example shows the division as the numerator divided by the denominator. The goal's own wording states no number range and names no denominators. It does not say how the division is carried out. Converting fractions into decimal numbers is in rw/gb/5/07/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child divides the denominator by the numerator and reads 3/4 as 4 ÷ 3. The child sees a fraction only as a part of a pizza or a strip and does not accept that 3/4 is one number with a place on the number line. The child thinks that a smaller number cannot be divided by a larger one.
