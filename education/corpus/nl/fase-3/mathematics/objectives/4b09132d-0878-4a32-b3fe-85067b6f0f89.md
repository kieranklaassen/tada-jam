## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking up and using some reference measures (*referentiematen*, familiar things of a known size) for *inhoud* (volume or capacity)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child thinks of containers whose capacity it knows, for example a milk carton of 1 litre, a drinking glass of about 2 dl and a bucket of about 10 litres, and checks them by pouring. The child uses them to estimate, for example that a watering can holds about as much as five milk cartons, so about 5 litres. A child of 10 uses a reference measure to estimate an amount; a child of 12 uses a reference measure to judge whether a stated amount can be right.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording asks for "some" reference measures and names none. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: a carton of milk holds a litre, and a glass holds about 200 to 250 ml; it is an example, not a limit. The milk carton in the paragraph above comes from that example, and the glass of about 2 dl is at the lower end of the example's 200 to 250 ml; the bucket and the watering can are the pack's. The verbs are thinking up and using. The goal's own wording names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range; the example uses the litre and the millilitre.

### Common mistakes

The child takes every carton or bottle to be a litre, whatever its size. The child judges the capacity of a container by its height. The child has a reference for the litre only, so that its estimates of very small and very large amounts are far from the real amount.
