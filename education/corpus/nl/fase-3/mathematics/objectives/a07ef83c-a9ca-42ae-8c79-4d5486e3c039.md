## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

rounding whole numbers within the meaning of the given context

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads that 18,742 people came to a match and says "about 19,000" for a headline, and "about 18,700" where more precision is wanted. The child rounds the 1,480 euro needed for a school trip up to 1,500 euro, because an amount rounded down would not be enough. A child of 10 rounds to the nearest ten, hundred or thousand when told which; a child of 12 decides whether to round up, down or to the nearest, and to which place, from what the number is used for.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about rounding whole numbers in a way that fits the meaning of the given context; it does not say how the context decides. This goal states no number range and names no place to round to (such as tens, hundreds or thousands). Rounding decimal numbers is in rw/gb/4/05/fase3, which names hundredths, tenths and whole numbers as its own places.

### Common mistakes

The child rounds to the nearest round number in every situation, and rounds down where the situation needs rounding up. The child rounds in steps, from 18,449 to 18,450 to 18,500 to 19,000, where rounding to the nearest thousand in one step gives 18,000. The child gives a rounded number that is more exact than the situation can use, or so rough that it no longer informs.
