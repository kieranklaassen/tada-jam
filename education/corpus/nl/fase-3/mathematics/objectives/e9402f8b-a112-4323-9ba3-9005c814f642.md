## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about length and perimeter (*omtrek*, also circumference) in problem situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works on a question about length or perimeter and gives reasons: whether the world record for the long jump would fit in the school hall, first estimated and then marked out with a tape measure; how much fencing a garden of a given shape needs; whether a stated length can be right. The child estimates with lengths it knows, measures or calculates, and compares the result with the estimate. A child of 10 estimates and checks by measuring; a child of 12 also reasons with scale, conversions and the formula for the perimeter of a rectangle and says which data are missing.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording says "problem situations" and names none. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question whether you can mark the world record for the long jump (*verspringen*) in the *aula* (the hall of the school), with the task to estimate first and then find out; it is an example, not a limit. The long jump in the paragraph above comes from that example; the tape measure, the fencing and the stated length are the pack's. Neither the wording nor the example names a unit, a measuring instrument, a conversion or a formula, and the goal states no number range. The goals of this kind for area, *inhoud* and weight are `rw/m/2/10/fase3`, `rw/m/3/10/fase3` and `rw/m/4/07/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

Asked how much fencing a garden needs, the child calculates the area. For a garden that is not a rectangle the child leaves out the sides whose length is not given directly. The child accepts a result that cannot be right and does not check it against a length it knows.
