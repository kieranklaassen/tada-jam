## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering and reasoning about the effect of enlarging on both the area (*oppervlakte*) and the *inhoud* (volume or capacity) of objects

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds a cube from centimetre cubes with edges of 1 and one with edges of 2 and counts: 1 cube against 8 cubes, and 1 square on each face against 4 squares. The child makes a box twice as high only and finds twice the volume, then makes it twice as long, twice as wide and twice as high and finds 2 x 2 x 2 = 8 times the volume and 2 x 2 = 4 times the area of each face. A child of 10 discovers this by building and counting; a child of 12 reasons it out for other factors, for example that 3 times as long, wide and high gives 9 times the area and 27 times the volume.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording names no factor of enlargement and no object, and it does not say in how many directions the object is enlarged. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: a pack of *hagelslag* (chocolate sprinkles) is made twice as high, and the questions are what can then be said about its *inhoud* and how many times as large the *inhoud* becomes; it is an example, not a limit. The example enlarges in one direction, by a factor of 2, and asks about the *inhoud* only; the box made twice as high in the paragraph above follows it, and the cubes and the other factors are the pack's. The goal names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range. The effect of enlarging on area alone is the subject of `rw/m/2/09/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that an object that is twice as long, wide and high has twice the area and twice the volume. The child uses the factor for area for the volume too and says 4 times where it is 8 times. Where only one measurement is doubled, the child says that the volume becomes 8 times as large.
