## Official wording

omzetten van echte breuken en samengestelde breuken naar decimale getallen (en andersom)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster rw/gb/5; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 6557225c-f4e8-4ecf-ba37-bf2e21e3c271.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

converting proper fractions and compound fractions (*samengestelde breuken*: mixed numbers, or fractions within fractions) into decimal numbers (and the other way round)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes 3/4 as 0.75, 3/8 as 0.375 and 2 1/2 as 2.5, by rewriting the fraction in tenths, hundredths or thousandths or by dividing the numerator by the denominator. In the other direction the child writes 0.6 as 6/10 and simplifies it to 3/5, and writes 1.25 as 1 25/100 and then as 1 1/4. A child of 10 converts with money or a measuring tape at hand; a child of 12 converts without them.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names both directions: from fractions to decimal numbers and, in brackets, the other way round. It names proper fractions (the numerator is smaller than the denominator) and *samengestelde breuken*. The text does not define that term: it mostly means mixed numbers (such as 2 1/2) and is also used for fractions with a fraction in the numerator or denominator; the wording does not choose, and the examples above use mixed numbers. This goal states no number range: it names no denominators and no number of decimal places. It does not say how a fraction whose decimal form does not end, such as 1/3, is written as a decimal number. The goal rw/gb/4/01/fase3 names four place values (wholes, tenths, hundredths and thousandths).

### Common mistakes

The child puts the numerator and the denominator on either side of the decimal point and writes 3/4 as 3.4 or 0.34. The child writes 1/8 as 0.8 or 0.18. Going back, the child reads the digits after the point over the wrong denominator and writes 0.05 as 5/10.
