## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

interpreting, comparing and calculating (converting, *herleiden*) with price or number per unit of length, per unit of area, per unit of capacity (*inhoud-eenheid*; *inhoud* also means volume), per unit of weight, per unit of time

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a price or a number "per" something and says what it means: a price per metre of rope, per square metre of floor, per litre of juice or per kilogram of apples, a wage per hour, or a number of plants per square metre. The child compares by bringing both to the same unit: 1.5 litres for 1.80 euros is 1.20 euros per litre, and 2 litres for 2.20 euros is 1.10 euros per litre, so the second is cheaper per litre. The child converts a price to another unit: 0.90 euros per 100 grams is 9 euros per kilogram, and 12 euros per hour is 0.20 euros per minute. A child of 10 works a price per unit out in a table of matching amounts; a child of 12 works without the table and explains which pack is cheaper per unit.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three activities, interpreting, comparing and calculating (with *herleiden*, converting, in brackets), with a price or a number per unit. It names five kinds of unit and no others: a unit of length, of area, of capacity or volume (*inhoud*), of weight and of time. It names no particular unit (no metre, litre, kilogram or hour), no currency and no formula; the units and amounts in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. This goal states no number range. The source prints a space before the comma after *oppervlakte-eenheid*. Speed and population density have their own goals, `rw/m/8/02/fase3` and `rw/m/8/04/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

The child compares the prices of two packs without looking at how much each holds. The child divides the wrong way round, the amount by the price, and gets the amount per euro where the price per unit is asked. The child converts a price per 100 grams to a price per kilogram by dividing by 10 where it must multiply by 10.
