## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

multiplying with whole numbers in context situations and in formal mathematical language according to standard procedures such as splitting (*splitsen*, splitting a number into parts), repeated adding, forms of column-wise multiplying (*kolomsgewijs*, whole place values, written in columns) and of multiplying with the written algorithm (*cijferend*, digit by digit in columns)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works 6 × 347 by splitting: 6 × 300 = 1,800, 6 × 40 = 240 and 6 × 7 = 42, together 2,082. The child works 34 × 27 with the written algorithm: 7 × 34 = 238 and 20 × 34 = 680, together 918. The child works out how many pencils there are in 24 boxes of 36 and finds 864. A child of 10 carries out a procedure and writes every partial product down; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "zoals" (such as) introduces the procedures as examples: *splitsen*, repeated adding, and forms of *kolomsgewijs* and *cijferend* multiplying; the goal does not ask for all of them and does not prescribe one. The numbers are whole numbers, in context situations and in formal mathematical language. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "6 × 4983; 23 × 456; 9 m² at 2068 euros per m², how many euros is that in total?" (in Dutch notation 6 x 4983 and 23 x 456); it is an example, not a limit. The goal's own wording states no number range and no number of digits; the example multiplies a number of one digit by a number of four digits, and a number of two digits by a number of three digits. The source prints "kolomsgewijs - en cijferend" with a loose hyphen; it is glossed as plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Not shifting the second partial product, so that 34 × 27 becomes 238 + 68 = 306. When both numbers are split, multiplying only tens by tens and ones by ones, so that 34 × 27 becomes 600 + 28 = 628. Forgetting to add a carried digit.
