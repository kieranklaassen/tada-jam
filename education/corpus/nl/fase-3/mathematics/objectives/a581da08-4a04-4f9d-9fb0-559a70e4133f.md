## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

multiplying with decimal numbers in context situations and in formal mathematical language according to standard procedures such as splitting (*splitsen*, splitting a number into parts), repeated adding, forms of column-wise multiplying (*kolomsgewijs*, whole place values, written in columns) and of multiplying with the written algorithm (*cijferend*, digit by digit in columns)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works 6 × 3.75 by splitting: 6 × 3 = 18, 6 × 0.7 = 4.2 and 6 × 0.05 = 0.3, together 22.5. The child works 2.4 × 3.6 with the written algorithm as 24 × 36 = 864, and places the decimal point so that the outcome is 8.64. The child works out the price of 3.5 kg of cheese at €12.40 per kg: 3 × 12.40 = 37.20, half of 12.40 is 6.20, together €43.40. A child of 10 carries out a procedure and writes every partial product down; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "zoals" (such as) introduces the procedures as examples: *splitsen*, repeated adding, and forms of *kolomsgewijs* and *cijferend* multiplying; the goal does not ask for all of them and does not prescribe one. The numbers are decimal numbers, in context situations and in formal mathematical language; the goal does not say whether one or both of the numbers multiplied are decimal numbers. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "8 × 12.75; 23 × 4.56; For 15 shirts you pay €397.50, how many euros is that per shirt?" (in Dutch notation 8 x 12,75; 23 x 4,56; €397,50); it is an example, not a limit. In the two bare multiplications of the example a whole number is multiplied by a number with two decimal places; its question about the shirts is a division (397.50 ÷ 15 = 26.50), printed with this goal about multiplying. The goal's own wording states no number range and no number of decimal places. The open data lists this goal under the cluster for multiplying and dividing with whole numbers (rw/bew/3); the content-line document prints it under multiplying and dividing with decimal numbers (rw/bew/4), and the pack follows the document. The code is kept as the data has it, so it begins with rw/bew/3.

### Common mistakes

Placing the decimal point of the outcome under the points of the two numbers, as in adding, so that 2.4 × 3.6 becomes 86.4. Multiplying the whole parts and the decimal parts separately, so that 2.4 × 3.6 becomes 6.24.
