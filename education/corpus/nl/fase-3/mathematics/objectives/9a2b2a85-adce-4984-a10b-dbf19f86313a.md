## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with and reasoning about viewpoints (*standpunten*, places someone looks from) in a space (*ruimte*, also: a room)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a scene of objects, for example a few buildings of blocks on a table, and says or draws what a person on the other side sees. The child gets photos or drawings of the scene, says from which place each one was made, and marks that place on a plan seen from above. The child gives reasons: which object stands in front of which, and what is on the left and on the right as seen from that place. A child of 10 walks to the place to check; a child of 12 works it out without moving, also for a place it cannot go to, such as a high point above the scene.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two activities, dealing with and reasoning about viewpoints, and one setting: "in een ruimte", which can be read as in a space or in a room. *Standpunt* is here the place from which someone looks. The goal's own wording names no number of viewpoints, no kind of space and no aid. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: reasoning out where a photographer stood when taking photos of situations, and marking viewpoint(s) on a map or *plattegrond* (a plan seen from above); it is an example, not a limit. The photos and the plan in the paragraph above come from that example; the buildings of blocks on a table are the pack's. Sight lines are the subject of `rw/mk/1/02/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

The child draws what it sees itself when it is asked what a person at another place sees. The child swaps left and right for a viewpoint opposite its own. The child forgets that an object in front hides an object behind it as seen from that viewpoint.
