## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding and subtracting with decimal numbers by estimation (*schattend*, with rounded numbers), in context situations and formal mathematical language, and reasoning whether the actual outcome is (much) larger or smaller than the estimated outcome and on that basis possibly applying a correction

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child has €20, picks things of €3.95, €6.49 and €7.85, rounds them to 4, 6.50 and 8 and gets about €18.50. The child reasons that all three prices were rounded up, so the actual total is a little lower, and that €20 is enough. The child estimates 15.2 − 6.9 as 15 − 7 = 8 and reasons that the actual outcome is a little more. A child of 10 rounds the numbers and says whether the actual outcome is a bit more or a bit less; a child of 12 also says why the estimate is off in that direction and corrects it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has three parts: adding and subtracting by estimation, reasoning whether the actual outcome is (much) larger or smaller than the estimate, and possibly ("eventueel") applying a correction; the correction is not asked for every time. The numbers are decimal numbers, in context situations and in formal mathematical language. This goal states no number range, and does not say how to round or how close an estimate must be.

### Common mistakes

Working out the exact answer first and then rounding it, instead of rounding first. Rounding all the numbers the same way and not noticing that the estimate is then too high or too low. Rounding a price by dropping the digits after the point, so that €7.85 becomes €7.
