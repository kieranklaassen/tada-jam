## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works out the cost of a trip for 27 children with tickets of €7.50 each and a bus that costs €385. The child first writes the plan, 27 × 7.50 + 385, then types 27 × 7.50, notes 202.50, adds 385 and reads 587.50. A child of 10 writes down every in-between result; a child of 12 enters the whole chain, using brackets or the memory where the calculator has them, and keeps the written plan next to it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names using the calculator correctly, and context problems with several operations ("meer bewerkingen", more than one). The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "What are you going to type in if you want to work out how much it costs to order 3 cups of coffee and three pieces of pie" (3 kopjes koffie en drie stukken vlaai); it is an example, not a limit. The goal's own wording names no operations, no kinds of number and no keys, and states no number range; the example gives the numbers of cups and pieces and no prices.

### Common mistakes

Typing the numbers in the order of the story without a plan. Not noting an in-between result and having to start again. Entering a chain into a calculator that works step by step as if it applied the precedence rules.
