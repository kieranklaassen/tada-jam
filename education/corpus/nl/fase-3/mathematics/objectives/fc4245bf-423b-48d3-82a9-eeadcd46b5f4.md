## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about the effect of enlarging on the area (*oppervlakte*) of figures

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child draws a rectangle of 3 by 2 squares on grid paper and beside it one that is twice as long and twice as wide, 6 by 4, and counts 6 squares and 24 squares, which is 4 times as many. The child reasons why: the first rectangle fits twice along the length and twice along the width, so 2 x 2 times. The child tries a factor of 3 and finds 9 times the area. A child of 10 finds the effect by drawing and counting; a child of 12 says beforehand what it will be for any factor, also for a figure that is enlarged in one direction only.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording names no factor of enlargement and no figure, and it does not say whether a figure is enlarged in one direction or in both. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: if an object becomes 2 times as long and 2 times as wide, then the area becomes 4 times as large; it is an example, not a limit. The example has the factor 2 in both directions; the rectangles on grid paper and the factor 3 in the paragraph above are the pack's. The verb is reasoning. The effect of enlarging on both the area and the *inhoud* of objects is the subject of `rw/m/3/09/fase3`. The goal names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that a figure that is twice as long and twice as wide has twice the area. Where only the length is doubled, the child says that the area becomes 4 times as large. The child thinks that the perimeter becomes larger by the same factor as the area, and says 4 times where the perimeter becomes 2 times as long.
