## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays 2.5, 2.05 and 2.50 in a place-value chart and finds that 2.5 and 2.50 are the same number and that 2.05 is a different, smaller one. The child says what the zero does in each number: in 2.05 it holds the tenths place, in 2.50 it adds nothing to the value, and in 0.4 it shows that there are no wholes. A child of 10 lays the numbers in a place-value chart and says what each zero does; a child of 12 says it from the written numbers, without the chart.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The source prints "de specifieke rol van de '0' decimale getallen", with a word missing before "decimale getallen"; the gloss reads it as the role of the 0 in decimal numbers. The goal is about investigating how decimal numbers are built and what the digit 0 does in them. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "In which of the following numbers is the '0' necessary: 0.7; 0.07, 0.70?" (in Dutch notation 0,7; 0,07, 0,70); it is an example, not a limit. The goal's own wording states no number range and no number of decimal places; the example uses numbers with one and two decimal places. rw/gb/4/01/fase3 names four place values (wholes, tenths, hundredths and thousandths).

### Common mistakes

The child drops a zero that holds a place and treats 2.05 as 2.5. The child thinks that a zero at the end makes the number ten times as large, as it does in a whole number, so that 2.50 is more than 2.5. The child calls 0.5 smaller than 0.35 because 5 is smaller than 35.
