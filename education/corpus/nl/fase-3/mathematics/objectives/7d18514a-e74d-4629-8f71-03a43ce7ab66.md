## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring and using the relation between measuring the temperature above and below zero and the number line

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a thermometer upright beside a number line that runs through zero and sees that the two have the same order: the marks above zero are the positive numbers and the marks below zero the negative numbers. The child uses the line to compare and to count steps: -5 is colder than -2; from -5 a rise of 6 degrees goes 5 steps to 0 and 1 more, to 1 degree above zero; from 3 a fall of 7 degrees ends at -4. A child of 10 places temperatures on the line and compares them; a child of 12 finds differences and changes that cross zero by steps on the line.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names temperature above and below zero and the number line. Its own wording names no unit of temperature (`rw/m/5/02/fase3` names degrees and °C) and no measuring instrument. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: that the temperature below zero indicates freezing, that -5 is colder than -2, and that when the temperature rises by 6 degrees from -5 it is 1 degree above zero; it is an example, not a limit. The comparison of -5 with -2 and the rise of 6 degrees from -5 in the paragraph above come from that example, which says degrees (*graden*) and names no scale; the fall of 7 degrees from 3 is the pack's. The goal does not say how far below or above zero the temperatures go, and it states no number range; the numbers of the example run from -5 to 1. It names no rule for calculating with negative numbers, no conversion and no formula. The source joins the two parts with "tussen ... met" (between ... with); the gloss reads "between ... and".

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that -5 is warmer than -2, because 5 is more than 2. To find a difference that crosses zero the child subtracts the two numbers without their signs, for example says that from -3 to 4 is 1 degree where it is 7. The child counts the marks and not the steps between them, and so miscounts by one.
