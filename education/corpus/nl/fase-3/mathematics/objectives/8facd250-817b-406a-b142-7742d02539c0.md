## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating numbers, among them also prime numbers, square numbers and triangular numbers, and calculating and explaining whether numbers are divisible by a requested number

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays 12 counters in every possible rectangle (1 by 12, 2 by 6, 3 by 4), then tries 13, which makes only a single row, and calls 13 a prime number. The child lays squares of 1, 4, 9 and 16 counters and triangles of 1, 3, 6 and 10 counters and says how each row of numbers grows. The child works out that 138 can be divided by 6 without a remainder, 138 ÷ 6 = 23, and that 140 cannot, because 2 is left over, and explains how they know. A child of 10 finds these out by laying counters and dividing; a child of 12 gives a reason from the number itself, such as "it is even, so it is divisible by 2".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "waaronder ook" (among them also) names three kinds of number, prime numbers, square numbers and triangular numbers, as part of the numbers investigated, not as the full set. The second part is calculating and explaining whether numbers are divisible by a requested number; the goal names no divisors and no divisibility rules. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Are there prime numbers between 50 and 60? Explain" (Zitten er priemgetallen tussen 50 en 60? Leg eens uit); it is an example, not a limit. The goal's own wording states no number range; the example asks about the numbers between 50 and 60.

### Common mistakes

Calling 1 a prime number, or calling every odd number prime, such as 9 and 15. Judging divisibility from the last digit for every divisor, as works for 2, 5 and 10, and so calling 26 divisible by 3. Mixing up squaring and doubling, and calling 8 the square of 4.
