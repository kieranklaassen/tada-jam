## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering objects by length by estimating or on the basis of given indications

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts things in order of length, by looking where it can see them and by the stated lengths where it cannot. Where the lengths are given in different measures, for example one person who is 1.72 m tall and another who is 169 cm tall, the child converts both to one measure (172 cm and 169 cm) and says who is taller. A child of 10 writes stated lengths such as 0.8 m, 75 cm and 9 dm in one measure before ordering them; a child of 12 orders the same lengths without writing them out.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about objects and names length only, not perimeter. It names two bases for comparing and ordering: estimating, or given indications. Its own wording does not say what the given indications are. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: Dirk says that he is 1.72 m tall (printed "1,72 m") and Sami says that he has a height of 169 cm, and the question is who is taller; it is an example, not a limit. The two heights in the paragraph above come from that example; 0.8 m, 75 cm and 9 dm are the pack's. The wording says objects (*voorwerpen*), and the example compares two people. Measuring is the subject of `rw/m/1/04/fase3`. The goal's own wording names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range; the example uses the metre and the centimetre.

### Common mistakes

The child compares the numbers and ignores the measures, for example says that 169 cm is taller than 1.72 m. The child compares decimal numbers by the digits after the point, for example takes 1.75 m to be more than 1.8 m because 75 is more than 8. When estimating, the child judges things that do not lie side by side wrongly, for example takes an upright thing to be longer than one of the same length that lies flat.
