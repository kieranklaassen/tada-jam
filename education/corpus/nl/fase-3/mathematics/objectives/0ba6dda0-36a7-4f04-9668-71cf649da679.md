## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dividing with decimal numbers in context situations and in formal mathematical language according to standard procedures such as multiplying up (*opvermenigvuldigen*, building up multiples of the divisor), repeated subtracting, splitting (*splitsen*, splitting a number into parts), a form of column-wise dividing (*kolomsgewijs delen*, multiples of the divisor subtracted in a column) and long division (*cijferend delen*, the written algorithm, digit by digit)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works 7.2 ÷ 6 by splitting 7.2 into 6 and 1.2, which gives 1 + 0.2 = 1.2. The child finds how many glasses of 0.25 litre come out of 4.5 litres by multiplying up: 16 glasses for 4 litres and 2 more for the half litre, 18 glasses. The child works 86.4 ÷ 24 in a column by taking away 3 × 24 = 72, which leaves 14.4, and then 0.6 × 24 = 14.4, and finds 3.6. A child of 10 carries out a procedure and writes every step down; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "zoals" (such as) introduces the procedures as examples: *opvermenigvuldigen*, repeated subtracting, *splitsen*, a form of *kolomsgewijs delen* and *cijferend delen*; the goal does not ask for all of them and does not prescribe one. The numbers are decimal numbers, in context situations and in formal mathematical language; the goal does not say whether the number divided, the divisor or both are decimal numbers. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "3825 ÷ 1.5; 365 ÷ 13; 2665 ÷ 31; You have to pay €10,500 in 18 months, how many euros is that per month, rounded to two decimal places?" (in Dutch notation 3825 : 1,5; 365 : 13; 2665 : 31; € 10.500, where the point marks the thousands); it is an example, not a limit. In the example every number divided is a whole number; one divisor is a decimal number, 1.5, and in the other three divisions both numbers are whole and the outcome is not. The goal's own wording states no number range and no number of decimal places, and does not name rounding; the last question of the example asks for an outcome rounded to two decimal places, and rounding is named in rw/bew/3/18/fase3. The open data lists this goal under the cluster for multiplying and dividing with whole numbers (rw/bew/3); the content-line document prints it under multiplying and dividing with decimal numbers (rw/bew/4), and the pack follows the document. The code is kept as the data has it, so it begins with rw/bew/3.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that dividing always gives less, and so rejecting 18 as the outcome of 4.5 ÷ 0.25. Making only one of the two numbers 10 or 100 times as large before dividing. Misplacing the decimal point in the outcome, so that 86.4 ÷ 24 becomes 36.
