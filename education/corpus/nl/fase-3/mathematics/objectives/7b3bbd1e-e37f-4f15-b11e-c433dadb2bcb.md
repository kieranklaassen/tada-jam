## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

interpreting and knowing different notations for representing ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) in everyday situations and representations such as counts (*tellingen*), named fractions (*benoemde breuken*, fractions of a named thing or unit), percentages (also above 100%), scale notations and compound quantities (*samengestelde grootheden*, such as speed or price per kilogram)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child collects notations from daily life: "7 of the 28 children" from a count, 3/4 litre on a measuring jug, 120% in a report about growth, 1 : 25,000 on a walking map and 50 km/h on a road sign. The child says for each which two things are compared, such as kilometres and hours on the road sign, and works out for the map that 1 cm stands for 250 metres. A child of 10 reads a notation aloud and says where it is used; a child of 12 says which things it compares.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two actions, interpreting and knowing, for notations of ratios in everyday situations and representations. After "zoals" it gives five examples: counts, named fractions, percentages, scale notations and compound quantities. The brackets include percentages above 100% and set no upper limit. The content-line document prints an example with this goal, at its end, after the compound quantities: "km/u" (kilometres per hour; English writes km/h); it is an example, not a limit. The text does not define *tellingen* (counts or tallies), *benoemde breuken* (read here as fractions that name what they are a fraction of, such as 3/4 litre) or *samengestelde grootheden*; these readings are the pack's, and so is price per kilogram in the gloss, while speed is the quantity that the example km/u notates. This goal states no number range and names no scales. Compound quantities have their own cluster in this lane, rw/m/8.

### Common mistakes

The child reads a scale such as 1 : 25,000 as two separate numbers and not as a comparison of a length on the map with a length in reality. The child cannot say what is compared with what in a notation with "per" or a slash, such as 50 km/h. The child takes a percentage above 100% for a printing error.
