## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

interpreting ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) (including percentages) in pie charts and strips

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a pie chart of how the children of a class come to school, sees that the sector for cycling covers half of the circle and the sector for walking a quarter, and says 50% and 25%. The child reads a strip divided into 10 equal parts with 3 parts shaded as 3 in 10, or 30%, and compares it with a second strip. A child of 10 reads the share of a part from the chart or the strip; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about interpreting ratios shown in two forms: pie charts and strips; the brackets say that percentages are included. Making a pie chart or a strip is not named in this goal. This goal states no number range: it names no percentages and no number of sectors or parts. Tables, diagrams and graphs are the subject of the cluster rw/verb/1 of this lane.

### Common mistakes

The child reads the size of a sector as a number of things and not as a part of the whole. The child compares sectors of two pie charts as amounts, although the totals behind the charts differ. The child judges a sector by its place in the circle or its colour and not by the part of the circle it covers.
