## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a flat figure, for example a rectangle, and says what is true of it: how many sides and corners it has, which sides are equally long and which are parallel. The child takes a solid, for example a cube, turns it in its hands and counts and names the faces, the edges and the corner points. The child sorts figures by a property and says which property it used. A child of 10 names the numbers of sides, corners and faces; a child of 12 also names properties such as parallel sides, right angles and equal faces, and says which properties two figures share.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names one activity, naming properties, for geometric figures of two kinds: two-dimensional and three-dimensional. It names no figure and no property; the figures and properties in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. The source prints a closing bracket at the end with no opening bracket before it; the gloss leaves it out. Symmetry in shapes and figures is the subject of `rw/mk/3/03/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

The child uses the words for flat figures for solids, for example calls a cube a square or a face a side. The child counts the edges or corner points of a solid twice, or misses the ones at the back. The child takes a property of one drawn example for a property of the figure, for example thinks that a triangle must have a horizontal side at the bottom.
