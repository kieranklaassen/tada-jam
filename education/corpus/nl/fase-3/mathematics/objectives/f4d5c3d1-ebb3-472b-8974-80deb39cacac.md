onderzoeken van 'gelijkwaardigheid van breuken' en redeneren over de verhouding als getallenpaar met onbeperkt veel gelijkwaardige varianten

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating 'equivalence of fractions' and reasoning about the ratio (*verhouding*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) as a pair of numbers with an unlimited number of equivalent variants

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes 2/3 as 4/6, 6/9 and 20/30 and lays fraction strips under each other to see that they cover the same length. The child writes the ratio 2 to 3 as the pairs 4 to 6, 6 to 9 and 20 to 30 in a ratio table and says that the list can go on without end. A child of 10 finds further pairs by doubling and by multiplying both numbers by 3 or by 10; a child of 12 explains why multiplying or dividing both numbers by the same number gives an equivalent pair, and tests whether 14 to 21 belongs with 2 to 3.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has two parts: investigating the equivalence of fractions, which the source puts in quotation marks, and reasoning about a ratio as a pair of numbers that has an unlimited number of equivalent variants. This goal states no number range and names no fractions or ratios. Rows of equivalent fractions are in rw/gb/5/05/fase3, and equivalent number pairs that are thought up by the pupils are in rw/verh/2/05/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child makes a new pair by adding the same number to both numbers and thinks that 2 to 3 equals 3 to 4. The child multiplies only one of the two numbers. The child thinks that a pair with larger numbers stands for a larger ratio.
