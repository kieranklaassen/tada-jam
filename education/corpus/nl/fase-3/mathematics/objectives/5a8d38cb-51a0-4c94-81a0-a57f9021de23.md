## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering *inhouden* (volumes or capacities) by estimating or on the basis of given indications

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts a few bottles, cartons and jars in order of how much they hold, first by looking at them and then by reading the amounts printed on them. Where the amounts are given in different measures, for example 33 cl on one bottle and 300 ml on another, the child converts both to one measure (330 ml and 300 ml) and then says which bottle holds more. A child of 10 writes stated amounts such as 0.5 l, 33 cl and 300 ml in one measure before ordering them; a child of 12 orders the same amounts without writing them out.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two bases for comparing and ordering: estimating, or given indications. Its own wording does not say what the given indications are. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: in one small bottle there is 0.33 cl (printed "0,33 cl") and in the other 300 ml, and the question is in which bottle there is more; it is an example, not a limit. As printed, 0.33 cl is 3.3 ml; the source prints it that way, and the pack does not correct it. The paragraph above uses 33 cl beside 300 ml, which is the pack's number and not the example's. Measuring is not part of this goal; it is the subject of `rw/m/3/01/fase3`. The goal's own wording names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range; the example uses the centilitre and the millilitre.

### Common mistakes

The child compares the numbers and ignores the measures, for example says that 300 ml is more than 33 cl because 300 is more than 33. The child judges by height and takes a tall, narrow bottle to hold more than a low, wide one. The child orders decimal numbers by the number of digits, for example puts 0.25 l above 0.5 l.
