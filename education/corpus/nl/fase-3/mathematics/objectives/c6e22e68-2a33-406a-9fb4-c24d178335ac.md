## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing, saying aloud and writing down various area measures (*oppervlaktematen*): km², m², dm², cm², mm², are (*are*) (a), hectare (*hectare*) (ha)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads areas aloud, "twelve square metres" for 12 m² and "three hectares" for 3 ha, and writes an area it hears with the measure and the raised 2. The child draws a square of 1 cm by 1 cm and one of 1 dm by 1 dm, marks out 1 m by 1 m on the floor, and walks around a square of 10 m by 10 m on the playground for one *are*. A child of 10 says and writes the measures; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. After the colon the goal names seven area measures: km², m², dm², cm², mm², the *are* (a) and the *hectare* (ha). The wording names no others; the *are* and the *hectare* are as large as the square decametre and the square hectometre. The goal does not print the sizes of the *are* and the *hectare*: an *are* is 100 m² (a square of 10 m by 10 m) and a *hectare* is 100 *are*, which is 10,000 m² (a square of 100 m by 100 m). The goal is about knowing, saying and writing: it names no conversion (converting is the subject of `rw/m/2/02/fase3`) and no formula, and it states no number range. The source prints the exponents as plain digits ("km2", "m2"); the gloss writes km², m² and so on.

### Common mistakes

The child gives an area in a measure of length and writes m where m² is meant. The child takes 3 m² for a square of 3 m by 3 m, which is 9 m². The child confuses the *are* and the *hectare*, or does not know what the abbreviations a and ha stand for.
