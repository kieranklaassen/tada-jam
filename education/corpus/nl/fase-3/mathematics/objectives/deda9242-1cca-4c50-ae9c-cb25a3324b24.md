onderzoeken en begrijpen dat een verhouding relatief is en dat deze kan worden uitgedrukt in een eindeloze reeks van gelijkwaardige getallenparen; dergelijke gelijkwaardige getallenparen zelf bedenken (bijv. 2 van de 3; 4 van de 6; enz.), ook als reeks van gelijkwaardige breuken (bijv. 2/5 = 4/10 = 8/20)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and understanding that a ratio (*verhouding*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) is relative and that it can be expressed in an endless series of equivalent number pairs; thinking up such equivalent number pairs oneself (e.g. 2 of the 3; 4 of the 6; etc.), also as a series of equivalent fractions (e.g. 2/5 = 4/10 = 8/20)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads that 2 of the 3 children in a group come by bicycle and thinks up further pairs that say the same: 4 of the 6, 6 of the 9, 20 of the 30, 200 of the 300. The child says that the pair 2 of the 3 does not tell how many children there are, and that the series can go on without end. The child writes the same series as fractions, 2/3 = 4/6 = 6/9 = 20/30, and starts another series from 3/4. A child of 10 makes new pairs by doubling and by multiplying both numbers by the same whole number; a child of 12 also divides both numbers, explains why the series has no end and tests whether a given pair belongs to it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has two parts, joined by a semicolon: investigating and understanding that a ratio is relative and can be expressed in an endless series of equivalent number pairs, and thinking up such pairs oneself, also as a series of equivalent fractions. "bijv." (e.g.) introduces two examples inside the wording: the pairs "2 van de 3; 4 van de 6; enz." (2 of the 3; 4 of the 6; etc.) and the series 2/5 = 4/10 = 8/20; they are examples, not limits. The pairs in the example are a part and its total; the wording does not say whether a pair may also set one part against another part. This goal states no number range, and it does not say whether the numbers in a pair are whole numbers. Reasoning about the ratio as a pair of numbers is also in rw/verh/2/02/fase3, and rows of equivalent fractions are in rw/gb/5/05/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child adds the same number to both numbers of the pair and thinks that 3 of the 4 is the same ratio as 2 of the 3. The child thinks that a pair with larger numbers stands for a larger share, so that 20 of the 30 is more than 2 of the 3. The child reads the pair as the real amounts, so that 2 of the 3 means that there are exactly 3 children.
