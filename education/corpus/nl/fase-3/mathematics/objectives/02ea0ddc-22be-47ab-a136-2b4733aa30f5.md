## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

in application situations measuring and reading off temperature with a suitable measuring instrument and writing down the measurement result correctly as 'degrees' or '°C' and interpreting it. Both above 0 and below 0 and 0 itself

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures the temperature of the air outside, of tap water and of water with ice in it, with a thermometer that suits each, waits until the reading no longer changes and reads the scale with its eyes at the height of the mark. The child writes the result as "18 degrees" or "18 °C", with a minus sign below zero as in "-4 °C", and says what it means, for example that -4 °C is below the temperature at which water freezes, so that there can be ice on a pond. For a reading of exactly 0 the child writes "0 °C" and says that this is the temperature at which water freezes, not the absence of a temperature. A child of 10 reads a thermometer, above and below zero, and writes the result down; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal says "a suitable measuring instrument" and names none; the thermometers in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. It names two ways of writing the result: 'degrees' or '°C'. It names three cases: above 0, below 0 and 0 itself. It names no other temperature scale, no conversion and no formula. It states no range of temperatures, and it does not say whether a result is read in whole degrees. The "application situations" are not named. The source prints '0C', with a zero in the place of the degree sign; the gloss writes '°C'.

### Common mistakes

Below zero the child reads the scale in the wrong direction, for example reads the second mark below -10 as -8 where it is -12. The child leaves out the minus sign, or writes the number without 'degrees' or '°C'. The child takes 0 degrees to mean that there is no temperature, or that nothing can be colder.
