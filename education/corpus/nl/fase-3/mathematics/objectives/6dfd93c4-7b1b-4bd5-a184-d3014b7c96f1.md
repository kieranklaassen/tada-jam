## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

solving ratio problems (*verhoudingsproblemen*; *verhouding* covers both ratio and proportion) with compound quantities (*samengestelde grootheden*, such as speed or price per kilogram), both in situations in which a part of a quantity has to be calculated and the reverse: calculating the total quantity on the basis of a given part

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads that cheese costs 12 euro per kilogram and works out in a ratio table that a piece of 250 grams costs 3 euro. In the other direction the child knows that 250 grams costs 3 euro and works out the price of the whole kilogram, 12 euro. A child of 10 solves such a problem step by step in a ratio table, for example from 20 kilometres in 20 minutes to the 60 kilometres of a whole hour; a child of 12 solves it without the table.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names ratio problems with compound quantities in two directions: calculating a part of a quantity, and calculating the total quantity from a given part. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "2/3 of 600 pupils, that is 400 pupils, but also 450 pupils of the 600, that is 3/4 part or 3 in 4" (2/3 van 600 leerlingen dat zijn 400 leerlingen, maar ook 450 leerlingen van de 600, dat is 3/4 deel of 3 op de 4); it is an example, not a limit. The example is about a part of a number of pupils: it calculates a part (2/3 of 600 is 400) and names a given part as a fraction and as a ratio (450 of the 600 is 3/4); it holds no compound quantity and does not calculate a total from a part. Neither the goal nor its example names a compound quantity; speed and price per kilogram in the gloss and in the examples above are the pack's. Compound quantities have their own cluster in this lane, rw/m/8. The goal's own wording states no number range and names no units; the example uses 600, 400 and 450 pupils.

### Common mistakes

The child works in the wrong direction, and divides where the problem asks for the whole from a part, or multiplies where it asks for a part. The child changes one of the two quantities and forgets to change the other in the same way. The child mixes units and combines minutes with kilometres per hour without converting.
