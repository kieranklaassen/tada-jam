## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating the area (*oppervlakte*) of rectangles via the formula: area = length x width. Explaining this solution method in their own words

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures a rectangle of 6 cm by 4 cm, multiplies 6 x 4 = 24 and writes 24 cm². The child explains in its own words why multiplying is right: 6 squares of 1 cm² fit in a row along the length, and 4 such rows fit along the width. The child uses the formula for a floor of 5 m by 3.5 m and finds 17.5 m². A child of 10 calculates an area with the formula and explains the formula with rows of squares it has drawn; a child of 12 calculates and explains without a drawing.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names one kind of figure, rectangles, and one formula: *oppervlakte* = *lengte* x *breedte* (area = length x width). It names no other formula and no other figure; triangles and composite figures are the subject of `rw/m/2/07/fase3`. The formula is the only way of calculating that this goal names; the goals for the perimeter of a rectangle and the *inhoud* of a cuboid (`rw/m/1/08/fase3`, `rw/m/3/08/fase3`) also name informal ways. The goal names no unit and no conversion, states no number range and does not say whether the sides are whole numbers; the area measures of this cluster are listed in `rw/m/2/01/fase3`. The child also explains the method in its own words.

### Common mistakes

The child adds the length and the width, or calculates the perimeter. The child writes the answer in a measure of length, for example 24 cm where it is 24 cm².
