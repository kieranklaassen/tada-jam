## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays 10 rows of 10 squares of 1 dm² in a square of 1 m² and sees that 1 m² = 100 dm². The child then converts in both directions: 3 m² = 300 dm², 2.5 dm² = 250 cm², 450 cm² = 4.5 dm², 1 ha = 100 a = 10,000 m², and 0.5 km² = 50 ha. A child of 10 converts after laying or drawing the squares to find the step of 100; a child of 12 converts without them.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal speaks of area measures and names none; `rw/m/2/01/fase3` lists km², m², dm², cm², mm², the *are* (a) and the *hectare* (ha). The wording has two conditions: also with decimal numbers, and in both directions, from smaller units to larger ones and the reverse. The goal prints no conversion factor: the steps in the paragraph above (1 cm² = 100 mm², 1 dm² = 100 cm², 1 m² = 100 dm², 1 a = 100 m², 1 ha = 100 a, 1 km² = 100 ha) follow from the metric system, not from the wording. The goal names no formula and states no number range and no number of decimal places.

### Common mistakes

The child uses the step of ten of the length measures, for example takes 1 m² for 10 dm². The child uses a relation between lengths for areas, for example takes 1 m² for 100 cm² because 1 m is 100 cm, where 1 m² is 10,000 cm². The child moves the decimal point one place for each step where it has to move two.
