## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a perspective drawing of a street or a railway and lays a ruler along the lines that run into the distance, to see where they come together. The child compares two trees or people that are drawn in different sizes and says which is nearer and whether they could be equally tall in reality. The child says where the maker of the drawing stood and at what height the eyes were. A child of 10 says what is near and what is far and that far things are drawn smaller; a child of 12 finds the point where the lines meet and the line of the horizon, and uses them to say from where the scene is seen.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two activities, investigating and interpreting, and one kind of drawing: perspective drawings. It does not mention making such a drawing. It names no terms such as vanishing point or horizon, no number of vanishing points and no kind of scene.

### Common mistakes

The child takes a thing that is drawn smaller for a thing that is smaller in reality. The child thinks that lines that come together in the drawing also come together in reality, for example the two rails of a track. The child measures a length in the drawing with a ruler and treats it as the real proportion, although lengths that run into the distance are drawn shortened.
