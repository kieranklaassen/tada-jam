## Official wording

opbouwen en memoriseren van een relatienetwerk tussen veelvoorkomende verhoudingen, breuken, decimale getallen en percentages zoals de relatie tussen de standaardverhouding 1 op 100 (1 : 100), de breuk 1/100, het decimale getal 0,01 en 1%

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

building up and memorising a network of relations (*relatienetwerk*) between frequently occurring ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion), fractions, decimal numbers and percentages, such as the relation between the standard ratio 1 in 100 (1 : 100), the fraction 1/100, the decimal number 0.01 and 1%

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays cards in rows so that each row shows one amount in four forms: 1 in 100, 1/100, 0.01 and 1%; 1 in 4, 1/4, 0.25 and 25%; 1 in 2, 1/2, 0.5 and 50%. The child covers one card of a row and names it from the other three, and shades each row on a hundred grid as a check. A child of 10 checks a row by shading it on a hundred grid; a child of 12 works out a row that is not known from one that is, such as 3/4, 0.75 and 75% from 1/4, 0.25 and 25%.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has two verbs: building up and memorising. After "zoals" it gives one example of a relation, in four forms: the standard ratio 1 in 100 (1 : 100), the fraction 1/100, the decimal number 0.01 and 1%; the source prints the decimal number with a comma, 0,01. The goal speaks of frequently occurring ratios, fractions, decimal numbers and percentages and does not list which these are; the other rows in the examples above are the pack's choice. In the example, 1 : 100 stands beside 1/100 and 1%, so the example sets 1 against a total of 100; the goal does not say which ratios are meant, or whether a ratio that sets one part against another part is among them.

### Common mistakes

The child links a percentage to the fraction with the same digit, and takes 1/4 for 4% or 0.4, and 1/5 for 5% or 0.5. The child writes 1% as 0.1. The child knows each form apart and does not see that the four forms are the same amount.
