## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

multiplying and dividing with decimal numbers by estimation (*schattend*, with rounded numbers), in context situations and formal mathematical language, and reasoning whether the precise outcome is larger or smaller than the estimated outcome and on that basis possibly applying a correction

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child estimates 6 × €4.95 as 6 × 5 = 30 euros and reasons that the precise amount is 30 cents lower. The child estimates how many ribbons of 0.48 m come out of 12 m as 12 ÷ 0.5 = 24, and reasons that the precise outcome is a little larger, because each ribbon is slightly shorter than half a metre. A child of 10 rounds the numbers and says whether the precise outcome is larger or smaller; a child of 12 also says why the estimate is off in that direction and corrects it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has three parts: multiplying and dividing by estimation, reasoning whether the precise outcome is larger or smaller than the estimate, and possibly ("eventueel") applying a correction; the correction is not asked for every time. The numbers are decimal numbers, in context situations and in formal mathematical language. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "75 × 4.89 ≈ 75 × 5.00. The precise outcome is a little less than the estimate; 3000 ÷ 0.49 ≈ 3000 ÷ 0.5. The precise outcome is then a little more" (in Dutch notation 75 x 4,89 ≈ 75 x 5,00 and 3000 : 0,49 ≈ 3000 : 0,5); it is an example, not a limit. In the example the sign ≈ (is about equal to) stands between each calculation and its estimate. The goal's own wording states no number range and no number of decimal places; the example uses 75 and 3000 with decimal numbers of one or two decimal places. The open data lists this goal under the cluster for multiplying and dividing with whole numbers (rw/bew/3); the content-line document prints it under multiplying and dividing with decimal numbers (rw/bew/4), and the pack follows the document. The code is kept as the data has it, so it begins with rw/bew/3.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that a division must give less than the number divided, and so rejecting 24 as the estimate for 12 ÷ 0.5. Rounding 0.48 to 0 or to 1. Rounding up in a multiplication and not seeing that the estimate is then too high.
