## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works 0.4 × 0.6 to 0.24 by a rule for counting decimal places, and checks it on a square of 10 by 10 small squares: 4 tenths of 6 tenths covers 24 of the 100 squares. The child hears the claim that 3.6 ÷ 0.9 = 0.4, says that it cannot be right because 0.9 fits into 3.6 several times, and corrects it to 4. A child of 10 checks an outcome by estimating with easy numbers; a child of 12 explains why a rule for placing the decimal point works.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names critical thinking and reasoning about standard procedures and strategies for multiplying and dividing with decimal numbers. Its own wording names no procedure and no strategy; rw/bew/3/15/fase3, rw/bew/3/16/fase3 and rw/bew/3/17/fase3 name examples for their own content. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "How can it be that dividing by a decimal number that is smaller than 1 gives a larger outcome?"; it is an example, not a limit. This goal states no number range, and the example holds no number apart from the 1. The open data lists this goal under the cluster for multiplying and dividing with whole numbers (rw/bew/3); the content-line document prints it under multiplying and dividing with decimal numbers (rw/bew/4), and the pack follows the document. The code is kept as the data has it, so it begins with rw/bew/3.

### Common mistakes

Placing the decimal point by a remembered rule without checking the size of the outcome. Believing that multiplying always gives more and dividing always gives less. Lining up the decimal points in a multiplication as in adding, so that 1.2 × 0.3 becomes 3.6.
