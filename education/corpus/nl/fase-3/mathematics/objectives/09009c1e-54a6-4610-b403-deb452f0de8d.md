## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

understanding thoroughly (*doorzien*) our time system, explaining this and applying it in contexts in which calculating with time is needed, and in which conversions (*herleidingen*) may have to be carried out

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works out times and durations in a setting from daily life and explains each step: a train that leaves at 14:47 and travels for 1 hour and 28 minutes arrives at 16:15, because 47 and 28 minutes make 75 minutes, which is 1 hour and 15 minutes. The child converts where the question needs it, for example adds three parts of a trip of 50 minutes each to 150 minutes and writes that as 2 hours and 30 minutes. The child explains why a new hour starts at 60 minutes and not at 100, and a new day at 24 hours. A child of 10 calculates with a clock face or a timeline to count along; a child of 12 calculates with the written times and explains the method.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three things: understanding our time system thoroughly, explaining it, and applying it in contexts in which calculating with time is needed. Conversions are named with the word *eventueel* (possibly, where needed), so they are not part of every task. The goal names no units, no contexts, no notation and no formula; the times in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. The units of time are listed in `rw/m/6/05/fase3`. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child adds or subtracts times as decimal numbers: it finds 14:47 + 1:28 = 15:75 and leaves it so, or finds 16:00 − 14:47 = 1:53 where the answer is 1:13. The child counts the starting day or the starting hour twice, or leaves it out, when it counts a period. The child gives the answer in a form that the question does not ask for, such as 150 minutes where hours and minutes are asked.
