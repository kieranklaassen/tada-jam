## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about the position of the sun and its effect on the shadow of shapes and figures, among other things by using sight lines (*kijklijnen*, lines from an eye to what is seen)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands a stick upright in the sun, marks the end of its shadow at several times of the day, and says how the shadow turns and becomes shorter or longer as the sun stands higher or lower. The child draws a straight line from the sun past the top of an object to the ground to find where the shadow ends, and in the other direction works out from a shadow where the sun stands. The child predicts on a drawing on which side the shadow of a block falls and how long it is. A child of 10 says that the shadow falls on the side away from the sun and is long when the sun is low; a child of 12 draws the lines and reasons from the shadows in a picture about the position of the sun.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about reasoning: about the position of the sun and its effect on the shadow of shapes and figures. Sight lines are named as one means among others ("onder meer"). The goal names no times of day, no seasons, no compass directions and no measuring of the length of a shadow or of an angle. It names the sun and no other source of light.

### Common mistakes

The child draws the shadow on the same side as the sun. The child thinks that a low sun gives a short shadow and a high sun a long one. The child draws the shadow as a copy of the object lying flat, with the same length whatever the height of the sun.
