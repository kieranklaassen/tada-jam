## English gloss

investigating and coming to know the function of the various common buttons/symbols on the calculator and on the calculator of computers and smartphones

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child presses the keys of a pocket calculator one at a time and writes down what each does: the keys for the four operations, the decimal point, the equals key and the keys that clear. The child opens the calculator of a computer or a phone, finds the same keys there and notes what looks different, such as a star for the times sign. The child tries what happens when the equals key is pressed twice. A child of 10 finds out what each common key does by trying it; a child of 12 knows the keys and compares how two calculators differ.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names "common buttons/symbols" and does not say which buttons count as common. It names two kinds of device: the calculator, and the calculator of computers and smartphones. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

Confusing the key that clears the last entry with the key that clears everything. Looking for a decimal comma, as written in Dutch, where the calculator has a point. Taking the key that changes the sign of a number for the minus key.
