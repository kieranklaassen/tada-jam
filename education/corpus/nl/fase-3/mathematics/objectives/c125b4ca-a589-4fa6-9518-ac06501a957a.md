## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining the insight that fractions represent a ratio (*verhouding*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) of two numbers of things

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a bag with 3 blue and 2 red marbles and says that 3 of the 5 marbles are blue, written 3/5. The child then fills a bag with 6 blue and 4 red marbles, finds that 6 of the 10 are blue and says that this is the same fraction, 3/5. A child of 10 counts the part and the total and writes the fraction; a child of 12 explains that the fraction says how the two numbers stand to each other and not how many marbles there are.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about insight: a fraction shows how two numbers of things stand to each other. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "3/4 part is 3 of the 4" (3/4 deel is 3 van de 4); it is an example, not a limit. The goal's own wording does not say whether the two numbers are a part and its whole or two parts set side by side (3 blue against 2 red); the example sets a part against its whole, 3 of the 4. The goal's own wording states no number range and names no denominators.

### Common mistakes

The child writes the two parts as the fraction and calls a bag with 3 blue and 2 red marbles 3/2 or 2/3 blue. The child thinks that a bag with 6 blue out of 10 holds a larger fraction of blue than a bag with 3 blue out of 5, because the numbers are larger.
