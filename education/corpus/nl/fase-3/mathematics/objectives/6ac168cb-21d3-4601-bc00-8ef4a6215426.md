## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

in application situations measuring (*afmeten*: measuring something, or measuring off a given amount) length with a suitable measuring instrument and writing down and interpreting the measurement result correctly

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child chooses an instrument that suits what is to be measured: a ruler for a drawing, a tape measure for a room, the distance counter of a bicycle for a route. The child measures, writes the result as a number with a measure, for example 3.45 m, and says what the parts mean: 3 metres and 45 centimetres. The child reads a distance counter that shows 42.27 km as 42 kilometres and 270 metres. A child of 10 measures with an instrument that suits what is measured, writes the result with its measure and says what the digits stand for; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording says "a suitable measuring instrument" and names none, and it does not name the "application situations". The Dutch *afmeten* means both measuring something and measuring off a given amount; the wording does not choose. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: after a bicycle ride your *kilometerteller* (the distance counter of a bicycle) shows 42.27 (printed with a point, "42.27"), and the questions are how far you have cycled and what ".27" means; it is an example, not a limit. The distance counter and the number 42.27 in the paragraph above come from that example; the ruler, the tape measure and 3.45 m are the pack's. The goal's own wording names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no precision for the measuring and no number range; the example prints its number without a unit, on a counter of kilometres, with two decimal places. The wording names length only, not perimeter.

### Common mistakes

The child starts measuring at the end of the ruler or at the mark for 1 and not at the zero mark. The child reads the decimals as a whole number of the smaller measure, for example reads 42.27 km as 42 km and 27 m. The child writes the result as a number without a measure.
