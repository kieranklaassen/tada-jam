## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring the different ways of paying that are used such as cash, transferring (*overmaken*, a bank transfer), (contactless) paying by bank card (*(draadloos)pinnen*; *draadloos* is literally wireless) and *tikkies* (payment requests sent through a phone app)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tries out or watches different ways of paying: counting out coins and banknotes, holding a bank card against a pay terminal, filling in an amount and a name for a transfer, and sending a request to pay back a share of a bill. The child says for each way where the money comes from and how the payer can see afterwards what was paid, and compares the ways: which is quick, which lets the payer count the money by hand, which needs a bank account. A child of 10 names the ways and tells them apart; a child of 12 says which way suits which situation and what can go wrong with each, such as a wrong amount in a transfer.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. The ways of paying are given as examples ("zoals"): cash, transferring, paying by bank card (with "wireless" in brackets, which is contactless paying) and *tikkies*. *Tikkie* is the name of a Dutch app for payment requests; the word is also used for the requests themselves. The source prints "(draadloos)pinnen" without a space after the bracket. The goal names no amounts and no calculating, and it states no number range. The goal names no particular way of paying beyond its examples.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that paying by card costs nothing because no coins leave the hand, or that a card holds an endless amount. The child does not connect a card payment or a transfer with the balance of a bank account going down. The child thinks that a payment request is itself the payment.
