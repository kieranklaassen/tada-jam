## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

giving a formal multiplication or division for a multiplication or division situation with whole numbers and the reverse: thinking up fitting multiplication or division situations for a formal multiplication or division

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees 24 crates of 36 bottles being stacked and writes 24 × 36. Given the bare sum 156 ÷ 12, the child makes up two different stories: 156 eggs packed in boxes of 12, where the question is how many boxes, and 156 stickers shared fairly among 12 children, where the question is how many each. A child of 10 thinks up a story for a bare multiplication or division; a child of 12 also checks whether a story that another child thought up fits it, such as a floor of 15 by 8 tiles for 15 × 8.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal goes in two directions: from a situation to a formal multiplication or division, and from a formal multiplication or division to fitting situations. Whole numbers are named for the situation. The wording is about giving the operation and thinking up situations; it does not mention working out the outcome. This goal states no number range; rw/bew/3/02/fase3 and rw/bew/3/03/fase3 state ranges for what they maintain, not for this goal.

### Common mistakes

Knowing division only as sharing, and not recognising "how many groups of 12" as a division. Making up an addition story for a multiplication, such as 15 apples and 8 more apples for 15 × 8. Writing the division the wrong way round, 12 ÷ 156.
