## English gloss

calculating with scale and scale lines (*schaallijnen*, scale bars, as on a map)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures 4 cm between two villages on a map with the scale 1 : 50,000 and works out 200,000 cm, which is 2 kilometres. On a map with a scale line on which 1 cm stands for 500 metres the child measures 3.5 cm and works out 1,750 metres. A child of 10 works out a real distance and writes down each step; a child of 12 works it out without writing down the steps.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two things to calculate with: scale and scale lines. This goal states no number range: it names no scales and no distances, and it does not say in which direction is calculated (from a drawing to reality or the reverse). It does not say where the scale is found; the maps in the examples above are the pack's. Scale notations are named in rw/verh/1/03/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child multiplies by the scale and forgets to convert the centimetres into metres or kilometres, or converts by the wrong power of ten. The child thinks that a map with scale 1 : 50,000 shows more detail than one with 1 : 10,000 because the number is larger. The child measures from the edge of the scale line and not from its zero mark.
