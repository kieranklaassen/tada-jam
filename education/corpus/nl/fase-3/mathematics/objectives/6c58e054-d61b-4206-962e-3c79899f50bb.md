## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading and interpreting data on plans (*plattegronden*, floor plans or street maps) and in doing so making use of a legend, a scale line (*schaallijn*, a scale bar) and/or a grid with coordinates (C5, A4)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks up in the legend what a sign or a colour on the plan stands for and finds the places with that sign. The child finds a place from its grid reference, for example C5: the square where strip C and strip 5 cross, and gives the reference of a place it has found. The child marks the length of the scale line on a strip of paper and steps it along a route to find the real distance: if the scale line stands for 100 metres and fits 4 times along the route, the route is about 400 metres. A child of 10 finds on the plan what a question asks for, with the legend, the scale line or the grid; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two activities, reading and interpreting data on *plattegronden*, and three aids joined by "and/or": a legend, a scale line and a grid with coordinates. The brackets name two coordinates, C5 and A4: each is a letter followed by a number, the form used for the squares of a map grid. The goal names no scale as a ratio (such as 1 : 10,000), no unit of length and no size of grid; the 100 metres in the paragraph above is the pack's example. Compass directions are the subject of `rw/mk/1/04/fase3`. Coordinates in a system of axes are the subject of `rw/verb/1/04/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

The child reads the grid reference in the wrong order, or takes the line for the square, and ends one square away from the right one. The child measures a route that bends as one straight line. The child measures a distance on the plan in centimetres and gives that number as the real distance, without using the scale line.
