## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and comparing and (by estimation) calculating with different currencies in the world

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at coins, banknotes or price tags from other countries, names the currency and compares a price there with a price in its own currency. The child calculates with a rate that is given: at a rate of 1.10 of another currency for 1 euro, 20 euros are 22 of that currency; and it estimates with a round rate, for example when 1 euro is about 150 of a currency, a price of 3000 in that currency is about 20 euros. A child of 10 converts a price with a table of matching amounts; a child of 12 says beforehand whether the answer must be a larger or a smaller number and then calculates.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three activities with different currencies in the world: investigating, comparing and calculating; "estimating" stands in brackets before calculating, so calculating by estimation is included. The source prints "(schattend)rekenen" without a space after the bracket. The goal names no currency, not the euro either, and no exchange rate; the euro and the rates in the paragraph above are the pack's examples, and the rates are made up. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child converts in the wrong direction: it multiplies by the rate where it must divide, and does not notice that the answer is too large or too small. The child thinks that a larger number on a price tag means a higher price, whatever the currency. The child thinks that one unit of every currency is worth the same.
