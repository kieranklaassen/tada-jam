## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking about in which situations it is handier to convert fractions into decimal numbers and the reverse

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child has to add 3/4 kilogram and 1/2 kilogram of flour on kitchen scales that show decimal numbers, decides to write them as 0.75 and 0.5, and gets 1.25 kilograms. The child has to take 0.25 of 36 cards, decides that a quarter of 36 is easier, and takes 9 cards. The child says each time why one form was chosen. A child of 10 tries both forms and says which was handier; a child of 12 chooses beforehand and gives a reason that names the task, such as "the scales show decimals".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names thinking about when a conversion is handier, in both directions: from a fraction to a decimal number and from a decimal number to a fraction. It names no situations, no fractions and no decimal numbers. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

Converting to decimal numbers out of habit, also where the fraction gives a quick answer, such as 1/3 of 12. Writing 1/3 as 0.3 and treating the two as equal. Not being able to say why one form was chosen.
