## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating the *inhoud* (volume or capacity) of a cuboid (*balk*, a box shape with rectangular faces) via informal strategies and via the formula: *inhoud* = length x width x height and *inhoud* = length x depth x height; justifying the method and putting it into words

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child fills a box of 4 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm with centimetre cubes: 4 x 3 = 12 cubes in the bottom layer and 2 layers, so 24 cubes, which is 24 cm³. The child then multiplies the three measurements, 4 x 3 x 2 = 24, and explains that length x width gives the number of cubes in one layer and the height gives the number of layers. For a cupboard the child uses the depth in the place of the width, for example 10 dm x 5 dm x 20 dm = 1000 dm³. A child of 10 uses the formula and checks the result by counting layers of cubes; a child of 12 says why the formula is right.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names one solid, the cuboid (*balk*). It names two ways: informal strategies, which it does not describe, and the formula, which it prints in two forms: *inhoud* = length x width x height and *inhoud* = length x depth x height. The two forms differ only in the name of one measurement (width or depth). The goal names no other formula and no other solid. It names no unit and no conversion, states no number range and does not say whether the lengths are whole numbers; the cubic measures in the paragraph above are those of `rw/m/3/05/fase3`. The child also justifies the method and puts it into words.

### Common mistakes

The child adds the three measurements where it has to multiply them. The child counts only the cubes it can see on the outside of a built or drawn cuboid. The child gives the answer in a measure of length or area, for example cm or cm², or multiplies measurements that are in different measures without converting them first.
