## English gloss

interpreting times in which accuracy to hundredths of seconds is important and in doing so seeing the difference between the base-ten system (for numbers) and the base-60 system that we use for clock times

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a time from a race, for example 1:23.45, as 1 minute, 23 seconds and 45 hundredths of a second, and says that this is 83.45 seconds. The child puts such times in order and finds differences: 59.87 seconds and 1:00.02 differ by 0.15 seconds, because 1:00.02 is 60.02 seconds. The child points out where the steps change: the hundredths run to 99 and then a whole second is added, and the seconds run to 59 and then a whole minute is added. A child of 10 reads and orders such times; a child of 12 calculates differences that pass a whole minute and explains that 1:30 is not 1.30 minutes but 1.5 minutes.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names interpreting times in which accuracy to hundredths of a second matters, and seeing the difference between two systems: the base-ten system (*tientallig stelsel*), with "for numbers" in brackets, and the base-60 system (*60-tallig stelsel*) used for clock times. Hundredths of a second are the smallest part the goal names; it names no thousandths. The goal's own wording names no context. The content-line document prints the goal with an example inside the sentence, after the words "is important" (*belangrijk zijn*): for example in the context of sports performances (*in de context van sportprestaties*); it is an example, not a limit. Neither the wording nor the example names a notation for such times (no colon, point or comma). The race in the paragraph above follows that example; the notation and the times are the pack's, written with a point where Dutch notation has a comma. The source prints the plural verb *zijn* with the singular *nauwkeurigheid*; the gloss reads it as "is". This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child lets the hundredths run to 59 like the seconds, and expects 10.00 seconds straight after 9.59 seconds. The child treats minutes and seconds as a decimal number, and takes 1:30 for 1.30 minutes or 2:05 for 2.5 minutes. The child takes 12.5 seconds for a shorter time than 12.35 seconds, because 5 is less than 35.
