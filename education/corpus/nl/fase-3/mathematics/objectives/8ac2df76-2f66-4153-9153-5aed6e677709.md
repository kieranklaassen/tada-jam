## Official wording

weten en begrijpen dat procenten een standaardverhouding aangeven waarbij het totaal op 100 is gesteld en op basis hiervan deelpercentages berekenen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster rw/verh/3; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 8ac2df76-2f66-4153-9153-5aed6e677709.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing and understanding that percentages indicate a standard ratio (*standaardverhouding*; *verhouding* covers both ratio and proportion) in which the total is set at 100, and on that basis calculating part percentages (*deelpercentages*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child colours 40 of the 100 squares of a hundred grid and says that 40% is coloured and 60% is not. For a class of 25 children of whom 10 cycle to school, the child rewrites 10 of 25 as 40 of 100 in a ratio table and says that 40% cycle. A child of 10 shades the parts on a hundred grid and reads the percentages; a child of 12 works the percentages out in a ratio table and checks that the parts add up to 100%.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has two parts: knowing and understanding that a percentage is a standard ratio with the total set at 100, and calculating *deelpercentages* on that basis. The text does not define *deelpercentages* (part percentages): the word can mean the percentage that a part is of the whole, or a percentage derived from another one, such as 5% from 10%; the wording does not choose. Apart from the 100 at which the total is set, this goal states no number range: it does not say which percentages or which totals. Percentages above 100% are in rw/verh/3/03/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that a percentage is a fixed number of things, so that 40% always means 40 children. The child writes 10 of 25 as 10% and takes the count for the percentage. The parts that the child works out do not add up to 100% and the child does not notice.
