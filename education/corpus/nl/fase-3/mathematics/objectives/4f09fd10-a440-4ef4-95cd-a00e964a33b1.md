## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a ruler level, upright and slanting, and says which position is horizontal and which is vertical. The child draws a horizontal line, a vertical line and a slanting line on squared paper, and draws the diagonals of a rectangle, each from one corner to the opposite corner. The child uses the three words when it tells another person how to draw or build something. A child of 10 uses the words for lines on paper and for things in the room; a child of 12 also finds every diagonal of a figure with more than four corners, and says that a diagonal of a figure can itself lie horizontally, as in a square that stands on one corner.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three concepts and no others: horizontal, vertical and diagonal. *Diagonaal* is used in Dutch both for something that runs slanting across and for the line that joins two corners of a figure that are not next to each other; the goal does not say which is meant. It names no shapes, no instrument (such as a spirit level or a set square) and no measure of an angle. The goal sits in the cluster on constructing (rw/mk/2).

### Common mistakes

The child mixes up the words horizontal and vertical. The child calls every slanting line a diagonal, also one that does not run from corner to corner. The child finds only one diagonal in a rectangle, or takes a side for a diagonal.
