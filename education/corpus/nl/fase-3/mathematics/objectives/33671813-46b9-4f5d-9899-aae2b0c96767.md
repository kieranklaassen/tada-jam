## English gloss

converting into each other larger units of time (century, decade, (leap) year, quarter (*kwartaal*, three months), month, week, 24-hour day (*etmaal*)) and smaller units of time (hour, half hour, quarter of an hour, minute, second)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child changes a duration from one unit into another, in both directions: 3 *etmalen* are 72 hours, 2.5 hours are 150 minutes, 3 quarters of an hour are 45 minutes, 4 minutes are 240 seconds, 2 centuries are 20 decades or 200 years, and 3 quarters of a year are 9 months. The child says which conversions have one answer and which do not: a year has 365 days and a leap year 366, and a month has 28, 29, 30 or 31 days. The child goes by way of a unit in between when a direct step is hard, for example from an *etmaal* to minutes by way of hours: 24 × 60 = 1440 minutes. A child of 10 converts with a table of the relations beside it; a child of 12 converts from memory.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name two groups of units. Larger: century (*eeuw*), decade (*decennium*), year, with "leap" in brackets before it (*(schrikkel)jaar*), quarter of a year (*kwartaal*), month, week and *etmaal* (a full day of 24 hours). Smaller: hour, half hour, quarter of an hour (*kwartier*), minute and second. The brackets name these twelve units, and the wording names no others; hundredths of a second are named in `rw/m/6/06/fase3`. The goal prints no relation between units and no formula; the relations used in the paragraph above are the standard ones, supplied by the pack: 1 century = 100 years, 1 decade = 10 years, 1 year = 4 quarters = 12 months = 365 days, a leap year = 366 days, 1 quarter = 3 months, 1 week = 7 *etmalen*, 1 *etmaal* = 24 hours, 1 hour = 60 minutes, half an hour = 30 minutes, a quarter of an hour = 15 minutes, 1 minute = 60 seconds. A month has no fixed number of days or weeks and a year no whole number of weeks; the goal does not say how such conversions are handled, and it does not say whether conversions stay within one group or also go from one group to the other. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child converts with 100 or 10 where the step is 60, 24, 12 or 7, for example takes 2.5 hours for 250 minutes or for 2 hours and 50 minutes. The child mixes up *kwartier* (a quarter of an hour) and *kwartaal* (a quarter of a year), or takes a quarter of an hour for 25 minutes. The child multiplies where it must divide, and finds more hours than minutes for the same duration.
