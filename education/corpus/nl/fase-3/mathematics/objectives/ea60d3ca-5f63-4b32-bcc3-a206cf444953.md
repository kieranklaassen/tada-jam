## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

working out divisions in which the dividing is carried on (*doordelen*, dividing the remainder further into decimals) and the outcome is a decimal number (which is possibly rounded to an accuracy of two decimal places)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shares €25 among 4 children: 6 euros each, and the 1 euro that remains is changed into 100 cents and shared as well, which gives €6.25 each. The child works 17 ÷ 8 on paper, carries on after the remainder of 1 with tenths, hundredths and thousandths, and gets 2.125. The child works 20 ÷ 3, sees that the sixes keep coming, 6.666..., and rounds to 6.67. A child of 10 divides on by changing what remains into a smaller unit, as euros into cents; a child of 12 divides on in a written division and decides from the question where to stop and round.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names divisions that are carried on and whose outcome is a decimal number. The brackets add that the outcome is possibly ("eventueel") rounded to an accuracy of two decimal places: rounding is not asked for every time, and two decimal places is the only accuracy named. The wording does not say what kind of numbers are divided; the cluster heading in the code scope is multiplying and dividing with decimal numbers (rw/bew/4). The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "450 ÷ 8 = 56.25; 2 ÷ 3 = 0.667. So 0.68" (in Dutch notation 450 : 8 = 56,25; 2 : 3 = 0,667. Dus 0,68); it is an example, not a limit. In the example whole numbers are divided and the outcomes are decimal numbers. The document prints "So 0.68"; 0.667 rounded to two decimal places is 0.67. The goal names no procedure. The goal's own wording states no number range. The open data lists this goal under the cluster for multiplying and dividing with whole numbers (rw/bew/3); the content-line document prints it under multiplying and dividing with decimal numbers (rw/bew/4), and the pack follows the document. The code is kept as the data has it, so it begins with rw/bew/3.

### Common mistakes

Stopping at the remainder and writing it behind the decimal point, so that 17 ÷ 8 becomes 2.1. Cutting the digits off instead of rounding, so that 20 ÷ 3 becomes 6.66. Leaving out a zero in the outcome, so that 61 ÷ 20 becomes 3.5 instead of 3.05.
