## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

in application situations measuring (*afmeten*: measuring something, or measuring off a given amount) *inhouden* (volumes or capacities) with a suitable measuring instrument and writing down and interpreting the measurement result correctly

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures out 25 cl of milk for a batter in a measuring jug, with the jug on a flat table and its eyes at the height of the surface of the milk, and finds how much water is in a bucket by pouring it into the jug in parts and adding the readings. The child writes the result as a number with a measure and says what it means, for example that the mark for 250 ml on the jug is the 25 cl that the recipe asks for. A child of 10 measures with an instrument that suits the amount, writes the result with its measure and says what it means in the situation; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording says "a suitable measuring instrument" and names none, and it does not name the "application situations". The Dutch *afmeten* means both measuring something and measuring off a given amount; the wording does not choose, and the official example shows both. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: two tasks, measuring out the 25 cl of milk that is needed for the pancake batter, and finding how much water is in the bucket; it is an example, not a limit. The milk and the bucket in the paragraph above come from that example; the measuring jug and its mark for 250 ml are the pack's. The goal's own wording names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no precision for the measuring and no number range; the example uses the centilitre.

### Common mistakes

The child reads the jug from above or while holding it at a slant, and so reads the wrong mark. The child takes each small mark for 1 or 10 without checking the scale of the jug. The child writes the number with the wrong measure, for example 250 cl where 250 ml was read, or with no measure.
