## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads 2,050,300 on a card, splits it into groups of three digits and says "two million, fifty thousand, three hundred". The child hears "four hundred and six thousand and twelve" and writes 406,012 with the zeros in the right places. A child of 10 lays a number in a place-value chart before saying it; a child of 12 reads it straight from the digits, such as the number of people who live in a country.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three actions for whole numbers: reading, saying aloud and writing. This goal states no number range. The goal rw/gb/1/03/fase3 names its own range, "tot ten minste 1 miljard" (up to at least 1,000,000,000), for the structure of the counting sequence. The text does not say whether writing means digits, number words or both, and it does not say how the digits are grouped: Dutch writing separates groups of three digits with a point or a space (2.050.300), where this pack's notes use commas.

### Common mistakes

The child leaves out a zero that holds a place and writes four hundred and six thousand and twelve as 40,612 or 4,612. Because Dutch names the ones before the tens (*vierentwintig*, four-and-twenty, for 24), the child reverses two digits inside a long number. The child reads a long number without splitting it into groups of three and names the wrong size, thousands for millions.
