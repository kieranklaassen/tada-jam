converting (*herleiden*) length measures, also with decimal numbers. Both conversions from smaller units of measure to larger units of measure and the reverse

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a length in another measure, in both directions: 2.5 km as 2500 m, 350 cm as 3.5 m, 0.75 m as 75 cm, 45 mm as 4.5 cm, 3 hm as 300 m. The child finds the size of the step from a row of the measures or from the meaning of the prefixes. A child of 10 converts with a row of the measures beside it; a child of 12 converts without the row.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal speaks of length measures and names none; `rw/m/1/01/fase3` lists km, hm, dam, m, dm, cm and mm. The wording has two conditions: also with decimal numbers, and in both directions, from smaller units to larger ones and the reverse. The goal prints no conversion factor and names no formula. It states no number range and no number of decimal places.

### Common mistakes

The child multiplies where it has to divide, or the reverse, so that a length gets a smaller number in a smaller measure. The child moves the decimal point the wrong number of places, for example writes 350 cm as 35 m. With a decimal number the child writes zeros behind the number and leaves the decimal point where it is, for example writes 2.5 km as 2.5000 m.
