## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

multiplying and dividing by a factor of 10 and 100, when calculating with decimal numbers, and explaining this strategy

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays digit cards for 3.45 on a place-value board, multiplies by 10 by sliding every card one place to the left and reads 34.5, and divides 27.5 by 100 by sliding the cards two places to the right, with a zero card in the empty ones place, and reads 0.275. The child starts from 6 × 4 = 24 and says that 6 × 0.4 = 2.4, because 0.4 is a tenth of 4, and that 24 ÷ 0.6 = 40, because 0.6 fits ten times as often as 6. The child explains each time that a digit becomes worth 10 or 100 times as much, or a tenth or a hundredth as much. A child of 10 slides the cards and reads the number; a child of 12 explains it without the board and derives a calculation with decimal numbers from a fact with whole numbers.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The factors named are 10 and 100; a factor of 1000 is not named. The goal joins this to calculating with decimal numbers and adds explaining "this strategy". The content-line document prints three examples with this goal: "5 × 4 = 20 then 0.5 × 4 = 2 and 5 × 0.4 = 2 and 5 × 0.04 = 0.2"; "4 × 25 = 100, then 4 × 2.5 = 10 and 4 × 0.25 = 1"; and "20 ÷ 5 = 4, then 20 ÷ 0.5 = 40; 2 ÷ 0.5 = 4" (in Dutch notation 0,5 x 4 = 2 and 20 : 0,5 = 40); they are examples, not limits. In the first of them the source prints "5 x 0, 04", with a space after the comma; 0,04 (0.04) is plainly meant. The goal's own wording leaves open whether it means multiplying and dividing a decimal number by 10 and 100, or using a factor of 10 or 100 as a step in other calculations with decimal numbers; each of the three examples starts from a fact with whole numbers and gives the outcome when one or both of its numbers are 10 or 100 times as small. The goal's own wording states no number range; the examples use whole numbers up to 100 and decimal numbers with one or two decimal places. The source prints a comma after "100" that has no partner; it is glossed as plainly meant. The open data lists this goal under the cluster for multiplying and dividing with whole numbers (rw/bew/3); the content-line document prints it under multiplying and dividing with decimal numbers (rw/bew/4), and the pack follows the document. The code is kept as the data has it, so it begins with rw/bew/3.

### Common mistakes

"Adding a zero" to multiply by 10, so that 3.45 × 10 becomes 3.450. Moving the digits the wrong way, so that 27.5 ÷ 100 becomes 2,750. Not filling an empty place with a zero, so that 4 ÷ 100 becomes 0.4.
