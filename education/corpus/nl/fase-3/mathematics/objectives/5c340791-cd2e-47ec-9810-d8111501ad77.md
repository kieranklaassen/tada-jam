## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

approximating *inhouden* (volumes or capacities) by making use of measuring off (*afpassen*, fitting a measure in again and again), reference measures (*referentiematen*, familiar things of a known size) and scale

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child estimates how much a bath, a bucket or a box holds in three ways: by counting how many times a known container fits in, for example how many buckets of 10 litres fill the bath; by comparing with a thing whose volume it knows, for example a milk carton of 1 litre; and by working from a drawing or a model that is made to scale. The child gives the answer with "about" and a measure. A child of 10 approximates an *inhoud* in these ways; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three ways of approximating: measuring off, reference measures and scale. Its own wording names no reference measure and does not say how scale is used for *inhoud*. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question how many litres of water, about (*ongeveer*), can go in the bath; it is an example, not a limit. The bath in the paragraph above comes from that example; the bucket of 10 litres, the box and the milk carton are the pack's. The goal does not say how close the approximation has to be. Its own wording names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range; the example uses the litre.

### Common mistakes

The child judges how much a container holds by its height alone. The child loses count while filling, or does not fill the measure to the same height each time. Working from a scale drawing, the child applies the scale once, as for a length, where it applies to each of the three measurements of the thing.
