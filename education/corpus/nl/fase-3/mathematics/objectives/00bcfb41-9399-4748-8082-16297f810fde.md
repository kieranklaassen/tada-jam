## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

maintaining the automating (calculating fluently) of all divisions from the division tables (*deeltafels*) up to and including 10 and the automating (calculating fluently) of multiplications and divisions with multiples of 10

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child answers 56 ÷ 7 = 8, 81 ÷ 9 = 9 and 42 ÷ 6 = 7 quickly, by thinking of the matching multiplication fact. The child works out 6 × 70 = 420, 30 × 40 = 1,200, 420 ÷ 7 = 60 and 2,400 ÷ 80 = 30 without writing, from the table fact and the zeros. A child of 10 works these out fluently; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The stated ranges are: all divisions from the division tables up to and including 10 (*tot en met 10*, so 10 is included); and multiplications and divisions with multiples of 10, for which no upper limit is stated. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "60 × 80; 200 × 40; 50 ÷ 5; 500 ÷ 5; 5000 ÷ 50" (in Dutch notation 60 x 80 and 50 : 5); it is an example, not a limit. The largest number in the example is 5000. The stated way of calculating is automating, which the brackets explain as calculating fluently. The Dutch leaves open whether "maintaining" ("onderhouden") also governs the second part, about multiples of 10. Remainders are not named in this goal; rw/bew/3/07/fase3 is about the remainder.

### Common mistakes

Losing or adding a zero, so that 30 × 40 becomes 120, or 2,400 ÷ 80 becomes 3 or 300. Answering a division with a neighbouring table fact, such as 56 ÷ 7 = 9. Counting up through the table on fingers instead of using the known multiplication fact.
