## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On a chart of the numbers 1 to 50 the child crosses out 1 and every multiple of 2, 3, 5 and 7 except those four numbers themselves, and finds that the fifteen numbers left, from 2 to 47, are the prime numbers. The child lays square tiles into squares of 1, 4, 9, 16 and 25 tiles and notes that the steps between these numbers are 3, 5, 7 and 9. A child of 10 finds such numbers by laying, drawing and crossing out; a child of 12 states a rule for a sequence, tests whether a number such as 91 is prime (it is 7 × 13) and explains the result.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. After the colon the goal names four things: prime numbers, square numbers, sequences and patterns; it does not say whether these four are the whole content or instances of it. This goal states no number range and does not say which sequences or patterns. The verb is investigating; the text does not say that pupils learn lists of prime numbers or square numbers by heart. Patterns are also the subject of the cluster rw/verb/2 of this lane.

### Common mistakes

The child counts 1 as a prime number, or takes every odd number for a prime number, such as 9, 15 or 91. The child mixes the square of a number with its double and says that the square of 6 is 12. The child continues a sequence from the last step only and does not test the rule on the earlier terms.
