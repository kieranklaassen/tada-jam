## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures how far it walks or cycles in a measured time, says the speed as a distance per time, and compares it with speeds it knows: a walker goes about 5 kilometres in an hour, a cyclist about 15 to 20. The child sees that the speed changes on the way (standing still at a crossing, going fast downhill) and that the average speed is the whole distance divided by the whole time: 30 kilometres in 2 hours is an average of 15 kilometres per hour, also when no part was ridden at exactly that speed. A child of 10 compares a measured speed with a reference speed; a child of 12 explains the difference between the speed at one moment and the average over a whole trip.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. The goal names two concepts, in quotation marks, 'speed' and 'average speed', and reference measures for them. A reference measure is here a familiar speed that a child knows, against which another speed can be judged. The goal's own wording names no unit of speed and no reference measure. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the speed of walking or cycling per hour (printed "snelheid lopen/fietsen per uur"); it is an example, not a limit. The example names walking, cycling and the hour, and it names no unit of distance and no number; the kilometre and the speeds in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. This goal states no number range. Calculating with speeds is the subject of `rw/m/8/02/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

The child takes the average speed for the speed at every moment of the trip. The child finds the average of two speeds by adding them and halving, also when the two parts of the trip took different times. The child thinks that the one who went the longest distance was the fastest, without looking at the time.
