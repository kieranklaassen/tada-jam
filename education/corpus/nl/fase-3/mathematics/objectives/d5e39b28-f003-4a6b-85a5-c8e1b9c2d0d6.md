## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with directions (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW; in Dutch N, NO, O, ZO, Z, ZW, W, NW) for describing a place, direction or route, with and without a compass rose (*windroos*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays a compass rose on a map and says in which direction one place lies from another, for example that the school lies north-east of the station. The child describes a route with the directions ("two streets to the east, then to the south-west") and follows a route that another person describes this way. Without a compass rose the child works out the directions from one that is given: if north is at the top of the map, east is on the right, south at the bottom and west on the left, and north-east lies halfway between north and east. A child of 10 describes a route with the directions; a child of 12 also follows a route that another person describes and says where a description went wrong.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name eight directions, and the goal names no others: north (N), north-east (NO), east (O), south-east (ZO), south (Z), south-west (ZW), west (W) and north-west (NW). The Dutch abbreviations differ from the English ones for east (O, *oost*) and south (Z, *zuid*) and for the directions made with them. The directions serve for describing a place, a direction or a route, and the goal says "with and without a compass rose". The content-line document prints an example with this goal: Middelburg lies in the south-west of the Netherlands, and the wind comes from the north-east (*zuidwesten*, *noordoosten*); it is an example, not a limit. The example writes the directions out as words and uses them for where a place lies and for where the wind comes from; the school, the station and the routes in the paragraph above are the pack's. The goal names no compass as an instrument.

### Common mistakes

The child swaps east and west. The child takes north for "up" or "straight ahead" and loses the directions when the map is turned or when it faces another way. The child gives the direction from the wrong end: asked where the school lies as seen from the station, it gives the direction from the school to the station, which is the opposite one.
