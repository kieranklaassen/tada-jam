## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating the area (*oppervlakte*) of triangles (via a rectangle) and of composite figures that consist of several rectangles

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child draws a rectangle of 6 cm by 4 cm around a triangle with a base of 6 cm and a height of 4 cm, sees by cutting or folding that the triangle is half of the rectangle, and finds 24 : 2 = 12 cm². For an L-shaped floor the child draws a line that splits it into two rectangles, for example one of 5 m by 3 m and one of 2 m by 2 m, and adds the areas: 15 + 4 = 19 m². A child of 10 does this on grid paper, with triangles that have a right angle; a child of 12 does it with measured lengths, for triangles of any shape, and works out a side of a part that is not given from the other sides.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two kinds of figure: triangles, and composite figures that consist of several rectangles. For triangles the brackets name the way: via a rectangle. The goal prints no formula, also not one for the area of a triangle. It names no other figure. It names no unit and no conversion and states no number range; the area measures of this cluster are listed in `rw/m/2/01/fase3`. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: an L-shaped living room (*een L-vormige woonkamer*); it is an example, not a limit. The L-shaped floor in the paragraph above follows that example; its measurements and the triangle are the pack's.

### Common mistakes

The child calculates the rectangle around the triangle and does not halve it. For a triangle the child multiplies two sides, one of which is a slanting side and not the height. For a composite figure the child uses a length of the whole figure for one of the parts, so that a piece is counted twice.
