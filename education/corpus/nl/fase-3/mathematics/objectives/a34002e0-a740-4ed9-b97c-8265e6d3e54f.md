## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about the passing of centuries and years in one's own life and in history, ordering times chronologically and using the timeline (*tijdbalk*) for this

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts events from its own life and from history on a timeline and says which came first and how many years lie between them. The child works out in which century a year lies and how much later another year is: the year 1648 lies in the seventeenth century, and the year 1848 comes 200 years, or 2 centuries, after it. The child compares lengths of time: its own life of 10 to 12 years next to the life of a grandparent and next to a period of several centuries. A child of 10 orders events on a timeline with marked years; a child of 12 chooses a fitting division for a timeline that it draws itself.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three things: reasoning about the passing of centuries and years in one's own life and in history, ordering times chronologically, and using the timeline for this. It names two units, centuries and years, and no others. The goal names no events, no periods and no years; the years in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. It does not say whether years before the start of the era are included. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child places a year in the wrong century, for example takes 1648 for the sixteenth century because the year starts with 16. The child thinks of everything "long ago" as one time, and puts events that lie centuries apart close together. The child draws a timeline with sections of unequal length for equal numbers of years.
