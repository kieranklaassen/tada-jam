## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a worked sum in which 2.5 + 1.25 was written with the last digits in line and came to 1.50, says that the outcome cannot be right because 2.5 alone is already more, and finds the error. The child works 7.03 − 2.98 in two ways, in columns and by counting on from 2.98 to 7.03, gets 4.05 both times and says which way was less work. A child of 10 checks an outcome against the size of the numbers; a child of 12 says why a procedure works, for example why the decimal points must stand one under the other.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names critical thinking and reasoning about standard procedures and strategies for adding and subtracting with decimal numbers. Its own wording names no procedure and no strategy; rw/bew/2/02/fase3 names example procedures for its own content. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "In which numbers may zeros be left out and why: 80 – 0.8 – 0.80, 0.708 – 0.080?" (in Dutch notation 80 – 0,8 – 0,80, 0,708 – 0,080); it is an example, not a limit. The example does not make clear whether its dashes separate the numbers of a list or are minus signs; the question itself is about the zeros in the numbers. The goal's own wording states no number range; the example uses 80 and numbers with up to three decimal places.

### Common mistakes

Trusting the outcome of a procedure without asking whether it can be right. Following the steps of a procedure without being able to say why a step is taken. Calling another child's way wrong because it is not the way learned first.
