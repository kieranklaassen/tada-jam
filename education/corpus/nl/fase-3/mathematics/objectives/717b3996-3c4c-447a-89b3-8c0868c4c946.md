## English gloss

rounding decimal numbers to hundredths, tenths and whole numbers.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child rounds 3.146 to hundredths and writes 3.15, rounds it to tenths and writes 3.1, and rounds it to a whole number and writes 3. The child rounds a price of 2.78 euro to tenths as 2.8 and to a whole number as 3, and marks the number and its rounded value on a number line to see which neighbour is nearer. A child of 10 rounds with a number line, on which it marks the number and sees which neighbour is nearer; a child of 12 rounds from the digits.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three places to round to: hundredths, tenths and whole numbers. It does not say how many decimal places the number to be rounded has, and it states no range for the whole-number part. The text names no rule for a number that lies exactly halfway, such as 2.5 rounded to a whole number. The source ends this goal with a full stop, which its neighbours do not have. Rounding whole numbers is in rw/gb/3/03/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child rounds in steps and gets a wrong result: 3.146 becomes 3.15 and then 3.2, where rounding to tenths in one step gives 3.1. The child cuts off the digits without looking at the next one and writes 2.78 as 2.7. The child rounds to the wrong place and gives hundredths where tenths were asked.
