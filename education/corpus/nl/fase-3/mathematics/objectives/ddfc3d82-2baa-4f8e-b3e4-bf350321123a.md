## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

collecting and using some much-used reference measures (*referentiematen*, familiar things of a known size) for length

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child collects lengths of familiar things and checks them by measuring: a large step of about a metre, a door of about 2 metres high, the width of a finger of about a centimetre, its own height. The child uses them to estimate other lengths, for example that a room is a little higher than a door, so about 2.5 metres. A child of 10 uses a reference measure to estimate a length; a child of 12 uses a reference measure to judge whether a stated length can be right.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording asks for "some" much-used reference measures and names none. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: a large step is about a metre, and a door is about 2 metres high; it is an example, not a limit. The step and the door in the paragraph above come from that example; the width of a finger, the child's own height and the room are the pack's. The verbs are collecting and using. The wording names length only, not perimeter. The goal's own wording names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range; the example uses the metre.

### Common mistakes

The child uses a measure of its own body without checking it, for example counts each of its steps as a metre. The child has no reference for the kilometre, which cannot be seen at once, and takes the length of a street for it. The child confuses the reference for the decimetre with the one for the centimetre or the metre, because the decimetre is little used in daily life.
