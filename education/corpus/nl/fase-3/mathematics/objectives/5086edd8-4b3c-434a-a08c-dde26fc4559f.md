## Official wording

kritisch denken en redeneren over (de juistheid van) de informatie en verbanden die in grafische voorstellingen worden gepresenteerd en conclusies die hieruit worden getrokken

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about (the correctness of) the information and relations that are presented in graphic representations and conclusions that are drawn from them

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a chart from a newspaper or an advertisement and asks what it shows and whether it shows it fairly: where the vertical axis starts, whether the steps are equal, what the pictures stand for, where the data come from. The child redraws a bar chart whose vertical axis starts at 90 with an axis that starts at 0, and sees that the bar for 100, which was drawn twice as high as the bar for 95, is only a little higher. The child judges a conclusion that someone draws from a chart and says whether the data support it. A child of 10 finds what is missing or odd in a chart, such as an axis without numbers; a child of 12 explains how the form of a chart changes what a reader thinks, and gives a conclusion that the data do support.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about thinking critically and reasoning about two things: the information and relations presented in graphic representations, with "the correctness of" in brackets, and the conclusions drawn from them. It names no kind of graphic representation, no source of the representations and no way in which one can mislead; the examples in the paragraph above are the pack's. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child trusts a chart because it looks exact and does not ask where the data come from. The child judges by the picture and not by the numbers, for example takes a bar that is twice as high for a value that is twice as large without reading the axis. The child accepts a conclusion that goes further than the data, such as a statement about all children from a count in one class.
