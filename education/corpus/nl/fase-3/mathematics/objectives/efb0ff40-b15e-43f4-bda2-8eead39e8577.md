## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering whole numbers in the counting sequence (*telrij*) and determining whether numbers lie close to each other or further apart; placing numbers roughly and precisely on a number line

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts 49,900, 50,100 and 94,900 in order and says that the first two lie close together, 200 apart, and that 94,900 lies far from both, 45,000 above 49,900. On a number line from 0 to 1,000,000 the child marks 750,000 precisely at three quarters of the line and marks 260,000 roughly, a little past one quarter. A child of 10 places numbers on a number line that has marks; a child of 12 places them on an empty line and adds the marks that are needed.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has two parts, joined by a semicolon: comparing and ordering whole numbers and judging how near to each other or how far apart they lie; and placing numbers on a number line, both roughly ("globaal") and precisely. This goal states no number range and does not say how the number line is marked. The goal rw/gb/1/03/fase3 names its own range, "tot ten minste 1 miljard" (up to at least 1,000,000,000).

### Common mistakes

The child judges nearness by how alike the digits look and calls 49,900 and 94,900 close together. The child places a number on the line by counting marks without checking what one step between marks is worth. The child spaces numbers evenly on an empty line whatever their size, so that 10, 100 and 1,000 stand at equal distances.
