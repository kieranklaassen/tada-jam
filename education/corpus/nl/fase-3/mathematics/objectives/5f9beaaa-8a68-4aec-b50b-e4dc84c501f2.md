## English gloss

positioning decimal numbers in relation to other (decimal) numbers and fractions

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On a number line from 0 to 3 the child marks 2.5 halfway between 2 and 3, marks 0.75 at the same point as 3/4 and marks 1.25 at the same point as 1 1/4. The child places 0.8 just to the right of 3/4, because 3/4 is 0.75, and places 0.3 just to the left of 1/3. A child of 10 places the numbers on a number line that has marks; a child of 12 places them on an empty line and says between which numbers each one lies.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about where a decimal number stands in relation to other numbers, decimal or not ("decimale" is in brackets), and in relation to fractions. The text does not say on what the numbers are positioned; a number line is one reading. This goal states no number range, no number of decimal places and no denominators; rw/gb/4/01/fase3 names four place values (wholes, tenths, hundredths and thousandths).

### Common mistakes

The child places 0.35 to the right of 0.4 because 35 is larger than 4. The child places 0.5 at 1/5 and 0.25 at 1/25, matching the digits with the denominator. The child marks 2.5 close to 2 as "2 and a bit" and does not use the halfway point.
