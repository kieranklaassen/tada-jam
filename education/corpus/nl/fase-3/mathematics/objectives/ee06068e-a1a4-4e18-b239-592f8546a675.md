## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

understanding and explaining the decimal structure of the metric system and the connection between the various area measures (*oppervlaktematen*) and using the meaning of the prefixes milli-, centi-, deci-, deca-, hecto- and kilo- in doing so

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child draws a square of 1 dm by 1 dm on centimetre grid paper and counts 10 rows of 10 squares: 1 dm² = 100 cm². The child explains that the prefix says how the side of the square changes, and that the area changes by that step twice, once for the length and once for the width: 1 m² = 100 dm², 1 dm² = 100 cm², 1 cm² = 100 mm². The child connects the land measures with this: an *are* is a square of 1 dam by 1 dam, so 100 m², and a *hectare* is a square of 1 hm by 1 hm, so 10,000 m², which is 100 *are*. A child of 10 finds the step of 100 by drawing and counting; a child of 12 explains it for any two area measures and explains why 1 km² is 1,000,000 m².

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names six prefixes: milli-, centi-, deci-, deca-, hecto- and kilo-. It names no others. It speaks of "the various area measures" and names none; `rw/m/2/01/fase3` lists km², m², dm², cm², mm², the *are* (a) and the *hectare* (ha). The goal prints no relation as an equation: the steps in the paragraph above follow from the metric system, not from the wording. Converting is the subject of `rw/m/2/02/fase3`. The goal names no formula and states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child applies the meaning of the prefix to the area, for example takes 1 dm² for a tenth of 1 m², or 1 km² for 1000 m². The child takes a *hectare* for 100 m², because hecto- means a hundred, where it is 100 *are*. The child confuses deci- (a tenth) and deca- (ten).
