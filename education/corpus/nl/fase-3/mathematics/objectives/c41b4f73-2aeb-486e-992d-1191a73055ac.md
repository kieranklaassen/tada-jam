## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child divides 130 children over tents for 8, finds 16 with a remainder of 2 and says that 17 tents are needed. The child cuts ribbons of 8 m from a roll of 130 m and says that there are 16 ribbons and that 2 m is left over. The child shares €130 fairly among 8 people, divides the remaining 2 euros as well and gets €16.25 each. A child of 10 says what the remainder stands for in the story and decides from the story what to do with it; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names a division in contexts and two things to do with the remainder: interpreting it or dealing with it (*verwerken*); the Dutch word does not say how or where the remainder is dealt with. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "5940 Ajax supporters go by bus to the match against PSV in Eindhoven. No more than 48 supporters may go in each bus. How many buses must be used?"; it is an example, not a limit. In the example 5940 ÷ 48 gives 123 with 36 left over, so that the remainder makes the answer 124 buses. The wording names no kind of number; the cluster heading in the code scope is multiplying and dividing with whole numbers (rw/bew/3). The goal's own wording states no number range; the example uses 5940 and 48. Dividing on to a decimal number is in rw/bew/3/18/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Writing "16 remainder 2" as the answer whatever the question asks. Always rounding down, and answering 16 tents. Writing the remainder behind a decimal point, 16 remainder 2 as 16.2.
