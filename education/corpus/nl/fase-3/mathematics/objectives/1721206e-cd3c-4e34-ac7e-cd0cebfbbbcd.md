## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

approximating the area (*oppervlakte*) of irregular figures (*grillige figuren*, figures with an uneven outline) by making use of an (underlying) grid

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays a transparent grid over the outline of an island on a map, or draws an outline on grid paper, counts the whole squares inside it, puts parts of squares together to make whole ones, and gives the answer with "about". Where the grid has a scale, the child first works out the area of one square, for example 1 km by 1 km = 1 km², and multiplies: about 160 squares is about 160 km². A child of 10 counts and gives the number of squares; a child of 12 works with the area of one grid square in a standard measure and says between which two values the area must lie.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about irregular figures and names one aid: a grid, with "(underlying)" in brackets. It does not say how large the squares of the grid are, how parts of squares are dealt with or how close the approximation has to be. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question what the area of the island of Texel is, about (*ongeveer*); it is an example, not a limit. The example gives no number and names no grid. The island in the paragraph above follows that example; the grid of 1 km and the count of 160 squares are the pack's and are not a statement of the area of Texel. Neither the wording nor the example names a unit, a conversion or a formula, and the goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child counts every square that the outline touches as a whole square, or leaves out every part of a square, and so finds far too many or too few. The child counts the squares and does not multiply by the area of one square where a square stands for more than one unit, for example counts a square of 2 km by 2 km as 2 km² where it is 4 km². The child counts the grid lines or grid points and not the squares.
