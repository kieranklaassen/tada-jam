## Official wording

weten waarom en waarop informatie op verschillende manieren geordend en weergegeven kan worden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster rw/verb/1; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 7f8b61eb-58e3-4bea-bb6f-f9fc3f7f3089.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing why and *waarop* (literally "on what": by what feature, or on what basis) information can be ordered and displayed in different ways

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes one set of data, for example the ages, the ways of coming to school and the distances to school of a group of children, and orders it in more than one way: by size, by kind, by time. The child shows the same data as a table, as a bar chart and as a line graph or a pie chart, and says what each way makes easy to see and for which question it would choose it. A child of 10 says which of two displays answers a question faster; a child of 12 gives the reason from the kind of data, for example that a line graph suits something that changes over time and a pie chart suits the parts of a whole.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is knowing: knowing why information can be ordered and displayed in different ways, and *waarop*. The source prints *waarom en waarop*; *waarop* means literally "on what", which can be read as by what feature the information is ordered or as on what basis, and the gloss keeps the Dutch word because the source does not make this clear. The goal names no table, chart or graph; the kinds in the paragraph above are the pack's examples, taken from `rw/verb/1/03/fase3`, which names them. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that there is one correct display for a set of data, whatever the question. The child chooses a display by how it looks and not by what it must show. The child thinks that another display of the same data holds other information.
