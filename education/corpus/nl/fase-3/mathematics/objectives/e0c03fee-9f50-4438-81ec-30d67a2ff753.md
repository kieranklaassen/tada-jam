bij een optel- of aftreksituatie met hele getallen een formele bewerking geven en omgekeerd: bij een formele bewerking met hele getallen passende optel- of aftrek situaties bedenken

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

giving a formal operation for an addition or subtraction situation with whole numbers and the reverse: thinking up fitting addition or subtraction situations for a formal operation with whole numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads that a stadium with 12,500 seats has 9,850 visitors and writes 12,500 − 9,850 for the empty seats. Given the bare sum 3,400 + 1,750, the child makes up a story that fits, such as the kilometres driven in two months. A child of 10 writes the operation for a situation and thinks up a story for a bare sum; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal goes in two directions: from a situation to a formal operation, and from a formal operation to fitting situations. It is about adding and subtracting with whole numbers. The wording is about giving the operation and thinking up situations; it does not mention working out the outcome. This goal states no number range; rw/bew/1/03/fase3 and rw/bew/1/04/fase3 state ranges for what they maintain, not for this goal.

### Common mistakes

Choosing the operation from a signal word instead of from the situation, for example writing an addition for "how many more" because of the word "more". Making up only take-away stories for a subtraction. Making up a story that uses the two numbers but asks a question the given sum does not answer.
