## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding and subtracting with whole numbers by estimation (*schattend*, with rounded numbers), in context situations and formal mathematical language, and reasoning whether the actual outcome is (much) larger or smaller than the estimated outcome and on that basis possibly applying a correction

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child estimates the visitors of three days, 4,870, 5,120 and 3,960, as 5,000 + 5,000 + 4,000 = 14,000. The child reasons that two numbers were rounded up by 130 and 40 and one was rounded down by 120, so the actual total is a little lower than 14,000. The child estimates 8,035 − 2,980 as 8,000 − 3,000 = 5,000 and reasons that the actual difference is a little more. A child of 10 rounds to the nearest thousand or hundred and says whether the actual outcome is more or less; a child of 12 chooses how far to round from what the situation needs and corrects the estimate.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has three parts: adding and subtracting by estimation, reasoning whether the actual outcome is (much) larger or smaller than the estimate, and possibly ("eventueel") applying a correction; the correction is not asked for every time. The numbers are whole numbers, in context situations and in formal mathematical language. This goal states no number range, and does not say how to round or how close an estimate must be.

### Common mistakes

Working out the exact answer first and then rounding it. In a subtraction, thinking that rounding up the number that is taken away makes the outcome larger. Rounding by looking only at the first digit, so that 4,870 becomes 4,000.
