## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising relations in (descriptions of) patterns with (geometric) figures, in numerical patterns and in patterns according to simple calculation rules; and designing such patterns oneself

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays a growing row of figures with matches, for example 1 square, then 2 squares side by side, then 3, counts the matches (4, 7, 10) and says the relation: each next figure takes 3 more matches. The child uses the relation to say something about a figure it has not laid: the tenth figure takes 4 + 9 × 3 = 31 matches. The child continues a row of numbers by a rule, such as "times 2, plus 1" starting from 1, which gives 3, 7, 15, 31, and makes up a pattern of figures or numbers for another child to work out. A child of 10 finds the step from one term to the next and continues the row; a child of 12 finds the relation between the place of a term and its value and puts it into words as a rule.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two parts, separated by a semicolon: recognising relations in patterns, and designing such patterns oneself. Three kinds of pattern are named: patterns with figures (with "geometric" in brackets), numerical patterns, and patterns according to simple calculation rules; "descriptions of" stands in brackets, so a pattern may also be given as a description. The calculation rules are "simple"; the goal does not say what counts as simple. It names no formula, no letters for unknown numbers and no number range; the patterns in the paragraph above are the pack's examples.

### Common mistakes

The child looks only at the step from one term to the next, and cannot say anything about a term far along the row without writing out all the terms before it. The child finds a rule that fits the first two terms and does not test it on the others. The child counts shared sides twice in a pattern of figures, for example takes 8 matches for two squares side by side where 7 are needed.
