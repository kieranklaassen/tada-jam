## English gloss

exploring negative numbers and the number sequence (*getallenrij*) with positive and negative numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a thermometer that shows 3 degrees, lets the temperature drop by 5 degrees and finds 2 degrees below zero, written −2. The child counts back along a number line past zero, saying 2, 1, 0, −1, −2, places −4 to the left of −1 and says which of the two is colder. A child of 10 meets negative numbers on a thermometer, in a lift with floors below ground or as a depth below sea level; a child of 12 orders positive and negative numbers on one line and finds 7 steps between −3 and 4.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring ("verkennen"): a first acquaintance with negative numbers and with the number sequence that runs through zero in both directions. This goal states no number range and names no situations. It does not mention adding, subtracting or other operations with negative numbers. Temperature has its own cluster in this lane, rw/m/5.

### Common mistakes

The child calls −4 larger than −1 because 4 is larger than 1. When counting back the child stops at zero or skips it. The child reads the minus sign as an instruction to subtract and not as part of the number.
