## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads that 30% of a class cycles to school and 20% walks, and adds these to 50%, because both are parts of the same class and no child is in both groups. The child then takes a price of 100 euro with 20% off and a further 10% off the reduced price, works out 80 euro and then 72 euro, and sees that the total discount is 28% and not 30%. A child of 10 adds percentages that are parts of one whole, with a strip of 100 squares; a child of 12 explains why percentages of different wholes cannot be added and tests this with amounts.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about understanding when percentages may be added or subtracted and when not; it names no situations and gives no rule. This goal states no number range: it names no percentages and no amounts. Calculating increase and decrease is in rw/verh/3/05/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child adds two percentages that belong to different wholes, such as 20% off and then 10% off the new price. The child thinks that a rise of 10% followed by a fall of 10% gives the starting amount; from 100 it gives 110 and then 99. The child finds the percentage for two groups of unequal size together by adding their percentages and halving the sum.
