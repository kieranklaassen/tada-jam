## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

designing and reasoning about symmetrical patterns and tessellations (*vlakvullingen*, coverings of a flat surface without gaps) with geometric figures

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays or draws a pattern of geometric figures that has a mirror line, and checks it by folding or with a mirror. The child covers a sheet with copies of one figure, for example triangles or hexagons, so that no gap is left and no two figures overlap, and tries which figures can do this and which cannot. The child gives a reason, for example that copies of a circle always leave gaps between them. A child of 10 designs by trying and tells what it sees; a child of 12 predicts whether a figure will fill the surface and reasons from the corners that meet in one point.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two activities, designing and reasoning, and two things they are about: symmetrical patterns, and *vlakvullingen* (tessellations: a flat surface covered with figures without gaps and without overlap). Both are made with geometric figures; the goal names no particular figure, and the figures in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. It does not say which kind of symmetry is meant; line symmetry and point symmetry are named in `rw/mk/3/03/fase3`. It names no measures of angles.

### Common mistakes

The child leaves small gaps or lets figures overlap and still calls the surface filled. The child thinks that only squares and rectangles can fill a surface. The child calls a pattern symmetrical because it repeats, without a mirror line or a turning point that fits.
