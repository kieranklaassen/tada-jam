## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about which area measure (*oppervlaktemaat*) is the best fit in which context

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says in which measure it would give the area of a stamp, a classroom floor, a farmer's field and a town, and gives a reason: cm² for the stamp, m² for the floor, hectares for the field, km² for the town. The child says what goes wrong with a measure that does not fit, for example that the area of a town in dm² is a number too long to read and compare. A child of 10 chooses a measure and says what the number would look like in it; a child of 12 argues why another measure fits less well.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording names no area measure and no context; `rw/m/2/01/fase3` lists the area measures. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question which area measure one uses to give the area of a town or city (*stad*), km², dm² or m²; it is an example, not a limit. The town in the paragraph above comes from that example; the stamp, the floor and the field are the pack's. The verb is reasoning: the child argues for a choice. The goal does not say by what a measure is judged to be the best fit. It names no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child uses m² for everything, also for a stamp or a country. The child gives a measure of length for an area, for example the size of a field in metres. The child chooses a measure that is correct and gives a very large or very small number.
