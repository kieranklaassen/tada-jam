## Official wording

begrijpen dat een verhouding een vergelijking aangeeft van aantallen die naar voren komen in situaties met getallen en meet- en meetkundige aspecten

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

understanding that a ratio (*verhouding*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion) indicates a comparison of numbers of things that come to the fore in situations with numbers and with measuring aspects and geometric aspects

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child mixes lemonade from 1 cup of syrup and 4 cups of water, then fills a larger jug with 2 cups and 8 cups, and says that both taste the same because the amounts stand to each other in the same way. The child measures a drawing in which a tree is 6 cm high and a house 3 cm, and says that the tree is twice as high as the house in the drawing and in reality. A child of 10 finds and names such comparisons with cups, blocks and strips; a child of 12 says that a ratio compares two amounts and does not give the amounts themselves, and points out ratios in recipes, maps and shapes.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about understanding what a ratio is: a comparison of numbers of things. It names where such numbers appear: in situations with numbers, with measuring aspects and with geometric aspects. This goal states no number range and names no ratios. It names no calculation; solving ratio problems is in rw/verh/2/03/fase3 and rw/verh/2/07/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child compares by the difference and not by the ratio, and thinks that 2 cups of syrup with 5 cups of water tastes the same as 1 with 4, because one cup was added to each. The child thinks that a ratio gives the real amounts, so that 1 to 4 means that there are 5 cups. The child swaps the order and reads 1 to 4 as 4 to 1.
