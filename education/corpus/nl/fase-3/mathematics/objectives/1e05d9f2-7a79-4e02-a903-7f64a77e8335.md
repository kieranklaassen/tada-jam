## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child types 2 + 3 × 4 on two different calculators, reads 20 on one and 14 on the other, and works out why: one calculates each step as it is typed, the other applies the precedence rules. The child finds a way to get 14 on the first calculator, by typing 3 × 4 first and then adding 2. A child of 10 notices the difference and writes it down; a child of 12 predicts what a given calculator will give before pressing the equals key.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about investigating and learning how the calculator handles several operations in a row. It does not say which kind of calculator and names no keys. The precedence rules themselves are in rw/bew/5/02/fase3. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

Assuming that every calculator applies the precedence rules. Pressing the equals key after some steps and not after others, and losing track of what the calculator holds. Not noticing that an in-between result was cleared.
