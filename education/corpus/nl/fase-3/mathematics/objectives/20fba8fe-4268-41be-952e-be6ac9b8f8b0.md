## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

multiplying and dividing fluently with simple decimal numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child answers quickly and without writing: 4 × 0.5 = 2, 3 × 1.5 = 4.5, 8 × 0.25 = 2, 6 ÷ 0.5 = 12 and 2.4 ÷ 4 = 0.6. The child ties these to money and measures: four coins of 50 cents make 2 euros, and twelve cartons of half a litre fill 6 litres. A child of 10 answers by thinking of money; a child of 12 answers from number facts alone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names multiplying and dividing, "fluently" (*vlot*) and "simple decimal numbers" (*eenvoudige decimale getallen*), which it does not define. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "4 × 2.25; 12 × 2.5; 10 ÷ 2.5; 4 ÷ 0.2; 1.6 ÷ 4" (in Dutch notation 4 x 2,25; 12 x 2,5; 10 : 2,5; 4 : 0,2; 1,6 : 4); it is an example, not a limit. The goal names no procedure and no context. The goal's own wording states no number range; the example uses whole numbers up to 12 and decimal numbers with one or two decimal places. The open data lists this goal under the cluster for multiplying and dividing with whole numbers (rw/bew/3); the content-line document prints it under multiplying and dividing with decimal numbers (rw/bew/4), and the pack follows the document. The code is kept as the data has it, so it begins with rw/bew/3.

### Common mistakes

Multiplying the whole part and the decimal part as two whole numbers, so that 3 × 1.5 becomes 3.15. Answering 6 ÷ 0.5 = 3, by halving instead of asking how many halves fit. Misplacing the decimal point, so that 0.2 × 0.3 becomes 0.6.
