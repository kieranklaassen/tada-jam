## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating data in a line graph and working data into a line graph (*verwerken in*, putting the data into it)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures or looks up something that changes over time, for example the temperature at each hour of a school day, puts the values in a table and then into a line graph: it draws and labels the two axes, chooses the steps, places a point for each value and joins the points. The child investigates a line graph that is given: it reads off values, says where the line rises, falls or stays level and where the change is fastest, and reads a value between two measured points with care. A child of 10 reads a line graph that is ready and places points on axes that are given; a child of 12 chooses the steps on the axes itself and explains what the shape of the line says about the situation.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two activities with data, investigating and *verwerken* (processing: here working the data into the graph), and one kind of graph: the line graph. The Dutch has the one phrase "in een lijngrafiek" for both verbs; the gloss writes it out twice. This goal names no other kind of graph or chart; tables and pie, picture and bar charts are named in `rw/verb/1/03/fase3`. The goal names no kind of data, no units and no number range, and does not say whether a graph has one line or more than one.

### Common mistakes

The child puts the numbers on an axis at unequal steps, or writes the measured values along the axis in the order found. The child reads the line between two points as measured values, also where nothing was measured or where values in between have no meaning. The child takes the height of the line for its steepness, and says the change is fastest where the line is highest.
