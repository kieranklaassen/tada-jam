## Official wording

onderzoeken en uitleggen dat een verschillende omtrek mogelijk is bij een gelijke oppervlakte

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and explaining that a different perimeter (*omtrek*) is possible with an equal area (*oppervlakte*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays different rectangles with 12 square tiles and finds the perimeter of each, counted in sides of a tile: 1 by 12 has a perimeter of 26, 2 by 6 has 16, 3 by 4 has 14. The child explains that the area is 12 tiles each time, while the perimeter changes because in a long, narrow shape more sides of tiles lie on the outside. A child of 10 finds this by laying and counting; a child of 12 explains it and says beforehand which of two shapes with the same area has the longer perimeter.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states one direction: with an equal area, a different perimeter is possible. The reverse, an equal perimeter with a different area, is not stated in this goal; the relation between perimeter and area in general is the subject of `rw/m/2/10/fase3`. The goal names no figure, no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range; the rectangles of tiles in the paragraph above are the pack's example.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that figures with the same area must have the same perimeter. The child thinks that the figure with the longer perimeter also has the larger area. When finding the perimeter of a figure of tiles, the child counts the tiles along the edge and not the sides of tiles, so that each corner tile is counted once where it has two sides on the outside.
