## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

carrying out standard procedures for comparing, ordering and positioning various (compound) fractions (*samengestelde breuken*: mixed numbers, or fractions within fractions)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

To compare 2/3 and 3/5 the child gives both the denominator 15, writes 10/15 and 9/15 and says that 2/3 is the larger. The child puts 5/6, 2/3 and 3/4 in order by writing them as 10/12, 8/12 and 9/12, and marks 1 3/4 on a number line three quarters of the way from 1 to 2. A child of 10 follows the steps and checks the result with a fraction strip; a child of 12 follows the steps without a strip.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about carrying out standard procedures, that is, fixed steps, for three actions: comparing, ordering and positioning. The text does not say which procedures these are, and it does not say on what the fractions are positioned; a number line is one reading. It speaks of various ("diverse") fractions, with *samengestelde* in brackets. The text does not define *samengestelde breuken*: the term mostly means mixed numbers (such as 1 3/4) and is also used for fractions with a fraction in the numerator or denominator; the wording does not choose, and the examples above use mixed numbers. This goal states no number range and names no denominators.

### Common mistakes

The child compares only the denominators and calls 1/5 larger than 1/3 because 5 is larger than 3. The child compares numerators and denominators as separate whole numbers and calls 3/5 larger than 2/3 because 3 and 5 are larger than 2 and 3. With mixed numbers the child compares the fraction parts and overlooks the whole numbers.
