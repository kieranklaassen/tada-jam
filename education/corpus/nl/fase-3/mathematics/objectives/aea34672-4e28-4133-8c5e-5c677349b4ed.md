## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says a written date aloud and writes down a date that it hears, with the day, the month and the year in the usual Dutch order, the day first. The child does the same with times of day: it says a time that is written in digits, such as 14:35, and writes in digits a time that is said in words. The child says the same time in more than one way, for example "25 minutes to 3 in the afternoon" for 14:35. A child of 10 says a written date or time aloud and writes down one that it hears; a child of 12 also changes between the ways of writing, such as a month as a name or as a number.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two activities, saying aloud and writing down, for two things: dates and times. Its own wording names no notation: not the order of day, month and year, not the month as a name or a number, not a 12-hour or 24-hour clock, and not whether seconds are written. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: 23 maart 2018 (23 March 2018) written as 23-03-2018, and 15:45 *uur* said as "kwart voor 4 's middags" (quarter to 4 in the afternoon); it is an example, not a limit. The example shows a date with the day first, with the month as a name and as a number between hyphens, and a time in 24 hours with a colon that is said in 12 hours with the part of the day; it shows no seconds. The time 14:35 in the paragraph above is the pack's. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child writes the time as it is said in Dutch and gets the hour wrong: for *half drie* (half an hour before three, 2:30) it writes 3:30. The child mixes up the day and the month in a date written in digits. The child writes an afternoon time without adding 12, or adds 12 to a morning time.
