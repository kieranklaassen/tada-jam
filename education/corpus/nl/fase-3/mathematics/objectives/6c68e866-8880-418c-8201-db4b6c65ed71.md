## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

converting (*herleiden*) various weight measures (kg, hg, dag, g, dg, cg, mg) and applying conversions in meaningful situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a weight in another measure, in both directions: 3.2 kg as 3200 g, 450 g as 0.45 kg, 2 g as 2000 mg, 5 hg as 500 g. The child uses this where weights in different measures have to be compared or added, for example adds 750 g of flour and 1.5 kg of flour and finds 2250 g, which is 2.25 kg. A child of 10 converts with a row of the measures beside it; a child of 12 converts without the row.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name seven weight measures: kg, hg, dag, g, dg, cg and mg. The wording names no others; the *ton* is named in `rw/m/4/01/fase3`. The goal prints no conversion factor and names no formula. Unlike the goals for converting length and area (`rw/m/1/10/fase3`, `rw/m/2/02/fase3`), this wording does not have the words "also with decimal numbers" and does not name the two directions of converting; `rw/m/4/04/fase3` is about measures and decimal numbers. The "meaningful situations" are not named. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child multiplies where it has to divide, or the reverse, so that a weight gets a smaller number in a smaller measure. The child moves the decimal point the wrong number of places, for example writes 450 g as 4.5 kg. The child adds or compares weights in different measures without converting them first.
