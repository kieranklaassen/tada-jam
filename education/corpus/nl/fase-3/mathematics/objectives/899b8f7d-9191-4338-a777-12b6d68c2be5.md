## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading off coordinates and placing points in a system of axes (*assenstelsel*, the two axes together) with positive numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads the place of a point in a system of axes: from the point straight down to the horizontal axis for the first number and straight across to the vertical axis for the second, for example (3, 5). In the other direction the child places a point from two numbers: for (3, 5) it goes 3 along the horizontal axis and then 5 up. The child sees that (3, 5) and (5, 3) are two different points, and places a few points that together form a figure. A child of 10 reads and places points by moving a finger along the axes; a child of 12 reads and places them by eye.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two activities, reading off coordinates and placing points, in a system of axes with positive numbers; it names no negative numbers. The goal does not say whether zero is included, whether the numbers are whole numbers only, how far the axes run, or how coordinates are written; the notation (3, 5), with the number along the horizontal axis first, is the usual one and is the pack's example. Coordinates of a letter and a number on a map grid are named in `rw/mk/1/03/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

The child swaps the two numbers, and goes up first and then along. The child counts the squares and not the lines, or starts counting at 1 where the axis starts at 0, and ends one step away from the right place. The child reads a point on an axis wrongly, for example gives (4, 0) as (0, 4) or as 4 alone.
