## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays two rods end to end in both orders to show that 370 + 250 and 250 + 370 give the same length, then tries the same with subtracting and finds that changing the order does not work there. The child explains that 620 − 250 = 370 belongs with 370 + 250 = 620 and uses the one to check the other. The child shows on a number line that adding the same amount to both numbers keeps the difference the same: 83 − 47 and 86 − 50 both give 36. A child of 10 shows a property with material or on a number line; a child of 12 states it in general words and says for which of the two operations it does not hold.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names understanding and explaining the operations adding and subtracting and, in brackets, their properties; its own wording names no property. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "3 + 5 = 5 + 3, but 3 − 5 is not equal to 5 − 3" (3 + 5 = 5 + 3, maar 3 - 5 is niet gelijk aan 5 – 3); it is an example, not a limit. The example shows one property: the two numbers may change places in adding and not in subtracting. rw/bew/1/06/fase3 names examples for its own content. The wording names no kind of number; the cluster heading in the code scope is adding and subtracting with whole numbers (rw/bew/1). The goal's own wording states no number range; the example uses 3 and 5.

### Common mistakes

Changing the order in a subtraction, for example taking 3 − 8 in a column as 8 − 3. Seeing subtracting only as taking away, not as finding a difference or as the reverse of adding. Stating a property as a trick without being able to show why it holds.
