## Official wording

kritisch denken en redeneren over het relatieve karakter van verhoudingen, breuken, decimale getallen en procenten in betekenisvolle probleemsituaties

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about the relative character of ratios (*verhoudingen*, a word that covers both ratio and proportion), fractions, decimal numbers and percentages in meaningful problem situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child compares 10% off a pair of shoes of 50 euro with 5% off a bicycle of 200 euro, works out 5 euro and 10 euro and explains why the smaller percentage gives the larger amount. The child hears that one child ate 1/2 of a small pizza and another 1/3 of a large pizza, and says that the fractions alone do not tell who ate more. A child of 10 shows with strips or drawings that the same fraction of different wholes gives different amounts; a child of 12 asks of every ratio, fraction or percentage in a text what the whole is, and judges a claim from that.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about critical thinking and reasoning on one property that it gives to ratios, fractions, decimal numbers and percentages alike: their relative character, which the pack reads as their meaning depending on the whole or the amount they refer to. The text calls the problem situations meaningful ("betekenisvolle") and does not say what they are. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child compares fractions or percentages of different wholes as if the wholes were equal. The child thinks that the larger percentage always gives the larger amount. The child cannot name the whole to which a fraction or a percentage in a text belongs.
