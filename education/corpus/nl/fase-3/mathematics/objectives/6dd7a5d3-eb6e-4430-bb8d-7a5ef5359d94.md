## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dividing with whole numbers in context situations and in formal mathematical language according to standard procedures such as multiplying up (*opvermenigvuldigen*, building up multiples of the divisor), repeated subtracting, splitting (*splitsen*, splitting a number into parts), a form of column-wise dividing (*kolomsgewijs delen*, multiples of the divisor subtracted in a column) and long division (*cijferend delen*, the written algorithm, digit by digit)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works 432 ÷ 12 by multiplying up: 30 × 12 = 360 and 6 × 12 = 72, so 36. The child works 868 ÷ 7 by splitting 868 into 700, 140 and 28, which gives 100 + 20 + 4 = 124. The child works 1,536 ÷ 24 in a column by taking away 50 × 24 = 1,200, then 10 × 24 = 240, then 4 × 24 = 96, and finds 64. A child of 10 carries out a procedure and writes down every multiple that is taken away; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "zoals" (such as) introduces the procedures as examples: *opvermenigvuldigen*, repeated subtracting, *splitsen*, a form of *kolomsgewijs delen* and *cijferend delen*; the goal does not ask for all of them and does not prescribe one. The numbers are whole numbers, in context situations and in formal mathematical language. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "325 ÷ 13; 2665 ÷ 31; You have to pay €10,500 in 12 months, how many euros is that per month?" (in Dutch notation 325 : 13; 2665 : 31; € 10.500, where the point marks the thousands); it is an example, not a limit. The goal's own wording states no number range; the example divides numbers of three to five digits by numbers of two digits. The remainder is not named in this goal, and 2665 ÷ 31 in the example does not come out whole (85 times, with 30 left over); rw/bew/3/07/fase3 is about the remainder, and rw/bew/3/18/fase3 about divisions whose outcome is a decimal number.

### Common mistakes

Losing track of how many times the divisor was taken away, or adding up those numbers wrongly. Leaving out a zero in the outcome, so that 1,224 ÷ 12 becomes 12 instead of 102. Stopping while what is left is still larger than the divisor.
