## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about relations in (descriptions of) patterns with (geometric) figures, in numerical patterns and in patterns according to simple calculation rules

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child judges a claim about a pattern and gives reasons: in the row 5, 9, 13, 17, where each term is 4 more than the one before, it says whether 41 and 50 can be in the row, and why (41 is 5 plus 9 steps of 4; 50 is not in the row, because 50 − 5 = 45 is not a whole number of steps of 4). The child compares two rules that give the same first terms and shows where they start to differ: "plus 2" and "times 2" both go from 2 to 4, and then give 6 and 8. The child explains why its rule holds for every figure of a pattern, by pointing out what is added in each step. A child of 10 tests a rule on the next terms and says whether it fits; a child of 12 argues why a rule must hold for all terms and finds the error in a rule that another person gives.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about thinking critically and reasoning about relations in the same three kinds of pattern that `rw/verb/2/01/fase3` names: patterns with figures (with "geometric" in brackets), numerical patterns, and patterns according to simple calculation rules; "descriptions of" stands in brackets. The calculation rules are "simple"; the goal does not say what counts as simple. It names no kind of problem, no formula and no number range; the patterns in the paragraph above are the pack's examples.

### Common mistakes

The child takes a rule for proven because it fits the terms that are given, and does not ask whether another rule fits them too. The child gives the answer without a reason, or gives "I can see it" as the reason. The child reasons in proportion where the pattern does not allow it, for example says that the tenth figure of a pattern takes twice as many matches as the fifth.
