## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

complicating (*compliceren*: rewriting with a larger numerator and denominator), simplifying and giving the same denominator to (*gelijknamig maken*) proper fractions and compound fractions (*samengestelde breuken*: mixed numbers, or fractions within fractions)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child rewrites 2/3 as 4/6 and as 8/12 by multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the same number, and rewrites 12/18 as 2/3 by dividing both by 6. To set 3/4 beside 5/6 the child gives both the denominator 12 and writes 9/12 and 10/12; the child does the same with 1 1/2 and 2 1/3 and writes 1 3/6 and 2 2/6. A child of 10 checks each step with a fraction strip or a drawing; a child of 12 chooses the multiplier or the divisor without a drawing and looks for the smallest common denominator.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three actions: *compliceren* (rewriting a fraction with a larger numerator and denominator, the reverse of simplifying), simplifying, and giving fractions the same denominator. It names two kinds of fraction: proper fractions (the numerator is smaller than the denominator) and *samengestelde breuken*. The text does not define *samengestelde breuken*: in Dutch school usage the term mostly means a mixed number (a whole number with a proper fraction, such as 2 1/3), and it is also used for a fraction that has a fraction in its numerator or denominator; the wording does not choose, and the examples above use mixed numbers. This goal states no number range: it names no denominators and no largest whole number.

### Common mistakes

The child multiplies only the numerator or only the denominator. The child adds the same number to the numerator and the denominator and thinks the fraction stays equal, so that 2/3 becomes 3/4. With a mixed number the child also changes the whole number when changing the denominator.
