## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

grasping (*doorzien*) and explaining the decimal structure of the metric system, the connection with our number system and the connection between the various length measures; in doing so knowing the meaning of the prefixes milli-, centi-, deci-, deca-, hecto- and kilo- and using these prefixes

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sets the length measures in a row from km to mm beside the places of a number and explains that each measure is ten times the next smaller one, as each place in a number is worth ten times the place to its right. The child says what each prefix means: kilo- a thousand, hecto- a hundred, deca- ten, deci- a tenth, centi- a hundredth, milli- a thousandth, so that 1 km = 1000 m, 1 hm = 100 m, 1 dam = 10 m, 1 dm = 0.1 m, 1 cm = 0.01 m and 1 mm = 0.001 m. The child reads a length such as 3.456 m as 3 m, 4 dm, 5 cm and 6 mm. A child of 10 reads the meaning of a prefix from the row of measures; a child of 12 explains it and uses it to work out a relation it has not learned, for example that 1 hm is 10,000 cm.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names six prefixes: milli-, centi-, deci-, deca-, hecto- and kilo-. It names no others. It speaks of "the various length measures" and names none; `rw/m/1/01/fase3` lists km, hm, dam, m, dm, cm and mm. The goal prints no relation as an equation: the values in the paragraph above follow from the meaning of the prefixes, not from the wording. Converting is the subject of `rw/m/1/10/fase3`. The goal names no formula and states no number range. The source prints "kilo-weten" without a space; the gloss reads the prefix kilo- followed by the verb *weten* (knowing).

### Common mistakes

The child confuses deci- (a tenth) and deca- (ten). The child takes centi- for a tenth or milli- for a hundredth. The child reads a prefix for a part as a multiple, for example takes 1 dm for 10 m.
