## English gloss

designing scale models (*maquettes*, small three-dimensional models) for objects

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child chooses an object, for example a playhouse or a bridge, sketches it from the front, from the side and from above, and decides how large the model will be. The child cuts and folds cardboard or uses blocks, builds the model, and compares it with the sketches or with the real object. A child of 10 makes a model that has the right parts in the right places; a child of 12 chooses one fixed ratio between the object and the model and keeps every length to it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is five words long in Dutch and names one activity: designing *maquettes* for objects. A *maquette* is a small three-dimensional model of something, for example of a building. The verb is designing. The goal's own wording names no objects, no material and no scale, and it states no number range. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: of your own dream house, of the school (*van je eigen droomhuis, van de school*); it is an example, not a limit. The example names two objects and no material or scale; the playhouse and the bridge in the paragraph above are the pack's.

### Common mistakes

The child makes some parts of the model to another ratio than the rest, for example a door that is half as high as the whole house. The child designs only the front and forgets that a model is seen from every side. The child starts to build without a plan and finds that the parts do not fit together.
