## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

collecting and using some much-used reference measures (*referentiematen*, familiar things of a known size) for area (*oppervlakte*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child collects things whose area it knows and checks them by measuring or by laying them on grid paper: a fingernail of about 1 cm², a hand of about 1 dm², a square of 1 m by 1 m marked on the floor, two football pitches that together are about a hectare. The child uses them to estimate, for example that a table top is about as large as 60 hands, so about 60 dm². A child of 10 uses a reference measure to estimate an area; a child of 12 uses a reference measure to judge whether a stated area can be right.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording asks for "some" much-used reference measures and names none. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the area of a hand is about 1 dm², and a hectare is about as large as two football pitches; it is an example, not a limit. The hand and the two football pitches in the paragraph above come from that example; the fingernail, the square on the floor and the table top are the pack's. The verbs are collecting and using. The goal's own wording names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range; the example uses the square decimetre and the hectare, and the area measures of this cluster are listed in `rw/m/2/01/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

The child uses a length as a reference for an area, for example a step of a metre for a square metre. The child lays its hand along one side of a surface, counts the hands in that one row and gives that number as the area. The child has no reference beyond the square metre, so that its estimates of large areas are far from the real area.
