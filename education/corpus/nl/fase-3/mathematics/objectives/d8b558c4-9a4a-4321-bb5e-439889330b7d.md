## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

choosing the best-fitting weight measure in situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says in which measure it would give the weight of a letter, a bag of potatoes, a person, a lorry load and the active substance in a tablet. The child replaces a measure that does not fit, for example says that 4000 g of potatoes is better given as 4 kg. A child of 10 chooses from measures that are offered; a child of 12 chooses without a list.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording names no weight measure and no situation. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question in which unit of measure (*maateenheid*) one expresses the weight of a person, and that of an insect; it is an example, not a limit. The example names two things to weigh and no measure; the person in the paragraph above also stands in the example, and the other things are the pack's. The weight measures of this cluster are listed in `rw/m/4/05/fase3` and `rw/m/4/06/fase3` (kg, hg, dag, g, dg, cg, mg), and `rw/m/4/01/fase3` names the *ton*. The goal does not say by what a measure is judged to be the best fit. It names no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child uses the kilogram for everything, also for very light things. The child chooses a measure that is correct and gives an awkward number, for example the weight of a person in grams. The child chooses by how large a thing is and not by how heavy it is.
