## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

choosing the best-fitting measure of *inhoud* (volume or capacity) in specific situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says in which measure it would give how much a drinking glass, a bucket and a classroom hold: centilitres for the glass, litres for the bucket, cubic metres for the classroom. The child replaces a measure that does not fit, for example says that the volume of a classroom given in cm³ is better given in m³. A child of 10 chooses from measures that are offered; a child of 12 chooses without a list.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording names no measure and no situation; "specific situations" is all it says. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question in which unit of measure (*maateenheid*) one expresses the *inhoud* of the classroom, m³, cm³ or litre; it is an example, not a limit. The classroom in the paragraph above comes from that example; the glass and the bucket are the pack's. The measures of *inhoud* in this cluster are named in `rw/m/3/04/fase3` and `rw/m/3/05/fase3`. The goal does not say by what a measure is judged to be the best fit. It names no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child uses the litre for everything, also for a room or a load of sand. The child chooses a measure that is correct and gives a very large or very small number. The child follows a fixed rule, litres for liquids and cubic measures for solid things, also where the custom is different, for example the litres of a rucksack or the cubic metres of tap water on a water bill.
