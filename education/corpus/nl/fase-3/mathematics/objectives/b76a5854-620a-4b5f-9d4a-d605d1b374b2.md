## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating the perimeter (*omtrek*) of a rectangle with the help of informal solution methods and with the help of the formula: perimeter = 2 x (l + b) or perimeter = 2 x l + 2 x b (*l* for *lengte*, length; *b* for *breedte*, width); and explaining these formulas

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds the perimeter of a rectangle of 7 cm by 4 cm first in its own way, by adding the four sides: 7 + 4 + 7 + 4 = 22 cm. The child then uses the formula in both forms, 2 x (7 + 4) = 2 x 11 = 22 and 2 x 7 + 2 x 4 = 14 + 8 = 22, and explains that both say the same: a rectangle has two lengths and two widths. A child of 10 uses the formula and checks the result by adding the four sides; a child of 12 explains why the two forms of the formula give the same result.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names one figure, the rectangle. It names two ways: informal solution methods, which it does not describe, and the formula, which it prints in two forms joined by "or": *omtrek* = 2 x (l + b) and *omtrek* = 2 x l + 2 x b. The wording does not explain the letters; l stands for *lengte* (length) and b for *breedte* (width), and the explanation in the gloss is the pack's. The goal names no other formula and no other figure. It names no unit and no conversion and states no number range. The child also explains the formulas.

### Common mistakes

The child adds one length and one width and leaves out the other two sides. The child multiplies length by width and so finds the area. In the form 2 x (l + b) the child ignores the brackets and doubles the length only: 2 x 7 + 4 = 18 where it is 22.
