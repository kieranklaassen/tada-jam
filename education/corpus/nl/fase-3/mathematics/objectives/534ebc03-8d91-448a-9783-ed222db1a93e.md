understanding the time zones on earth and determining time differences between different places on earth

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child turns a globe in front of a lamp and sees that it is day on one side and night on the other, so that it cannot be the same time of day everywhere. The child reads on a map of time zones how many hours two places differ and works out the time in the other place: if a place is 6 hours ahead, 09:00 here is 15:00 there, and if a place is 8 hours behind, 03:00 here is 19:00 of the day before there. The child uses this for a question from daily life, such as at what time it can call someone in another country. A child of 10 finds the difference on a map of time zones and works out the time in the other place; a child of 12 also says why places to the east are ahead.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two things: understanding the time zones on earth, and determining time differences between different places on earth. It names no places, no number of time zones and no size of difference; the differences in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. It does not say whether summer time, a change of date or differences of half an hour are included. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child adds the difference where it must subtract, and makes it later in a place to the west. The child keeps the same date when the calculation passes midnight. The child adds the time difference to a travel time as if it were hours that pass.
