## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children type the same bill of five items of about €6 each and get 31.4 and 314; they reason from the prices which of the two can be right and look for the typing error. The child types 1 ÷ 3 × 3, reads 0.9999999 on one calculator and 1 on another, and reasons about what the first calculator did with the digits it could not show. A child of 10 says whether an outcome can be right; a child of 12 also reasons about why a calculator gives an unexpected outcome.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names critical thinking and reasoning when working with the calculator, and nothing more. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "How do you work out 2345 × 5432 on a calculator of which the '2' button does not work?" (in Dutch notation 2345 x 5432); it is an example, not a limit. rw/bew/7/05/fase3 and rw/bew/7/06/fase3 name interpreting and checking outcomes as their own content. The goal's own wording states no number range; the example uses two numbers of four digits.

### Common mistakes

Treating the calculator as always right. Blaming the calculator instead of looking for the entry or the plan that was wrong. Giving the outcome without a reason why it fits the problem.
