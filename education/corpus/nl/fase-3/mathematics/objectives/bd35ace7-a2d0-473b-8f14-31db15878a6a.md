## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says in which unit it would give a duration and why: the time of a sprint in seconds, a lesson in minutes, a night's sleep in hours, a holiday in days or weeks, its own age in years, the age of an old building in centuries. The child changes an awkward statement into a fitting one, for example says 2 hours for 7200 seconds and 2 weeks for 336 hours. The child gives the reason for its choice: who the statement is for and how exact it must be. A child of 10 picks a fitting unit for everyday durations; a child of 12 compares two possible units and combines units where that fits, such as hours and minutes.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names one activity: choosing the best-fitting unit of time, in application situations (situations in which time is used for something). The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question what you pay attention to when choosing a measure for the passing of time; it is an example, not a limit. Neither the wording nor the example names a unit or a situation; the units and durations in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. The units of time are listed in `rw/m/6/05/fase3`. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child keeps to the unit it knows best, most often minutes or days, also where that gives very large or very small numbers. The child changes the unit and keeps the number, for example says 90 hours where it means 90 minutes. The child names a unit that does not fit the exactness that is needed, such as hours for a race that is decided by seconds.
