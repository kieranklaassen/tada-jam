## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

in the search for a finer measure (*maatverfijning*, refining the unit of measurement), investigating the role of decimal numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures a table with a metre stick and finds that it is longer than 1 metre and shorter than 2, then reads the decimetres and writes 1.3 m, then the centimetres and writes 1.34 m, then the millimetres and writes 1.342 m. The child says what each new digit after the decimal mark stands for: a unit ten times as small as the one before. A child of 10 measures and writes down each finer result; a child of 12 explains that a further decimal place can always be added when the measure is made finer.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about investigating what decimal numbers do when a measurement is made more precise. It names no quantity (such as length, weight or volume), no unit and no measuring tool. This goal states no number range and no number of decimal places; rw/gb/4/01/fase3 names four place values (wholes, tenths, hundredths and thousandths).

### Common mistakes

The child reads 1.34 m as 1 metre and 34 of some smaller unit without knowing which unit, and writes 1 m 5 cm as 1.5 m. The child thinks that a number with more digits after the decimal mark is a larger measure, so that 1.342 m is longer than 1.4 m. The child thinks that no number lies between 1.3 and 1.4.
