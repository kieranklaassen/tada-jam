## Official wording

begrijpen dat een deling geschreven kan worden als een breuk: 2:3=2/3

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

understanding that a division can be written as a fraction: 2 ÷ 3 = 2/3

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shares 2 pancakes fairly among 3 children by cutting each pancake into three equal pieces and giving every child one piece of each pancake, and sees that every child has two thirds of a pancake. The child writes the sharing as 2 ÷ 3 and the result as 2/3, and says that both stand for the same amount. The child does the same with 3 pizzas for 4 children and writes 3 ÷ 4 = 3/4. A child of 10 cuts and deals out the pieces to see it; a child of 12 writes a division of two whole numbers as a fraction straight away, such as 5 ÷ 8 = 5/8.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about understanding, and the one case it prints is 2 ÷ 3 = 2/3. The Dutch prints the division with a colon (2:3=2/3), the division sign used in Dutch schools. This goal states no number range. It does not mention writing the outcome as a decimal number; rw/bew/3/18/fase3 is about divisions whose outcome is a decimal number.

### Common mistakes

Turning the numbers round and writing 2 ÷ 3 as 3/2. Thinking that a smaller number cannot be divided by a larger one. Reading a fraction only as a part of a whole and not as the outcome of a division.
