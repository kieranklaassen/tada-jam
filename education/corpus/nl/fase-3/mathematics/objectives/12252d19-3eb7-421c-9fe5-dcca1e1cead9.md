## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child divides 250 children over buses with 48 seats, reads 5.2083333 and says that 5 buses are full, that 10 children are left, and that a sixth bus is needed for them. The child divides €10 among 3 children, reads 3.3333333 and says that each gets €3.33 and that 1 cent is left over. The child reads 4.5 after a calculation in euros and says "4 euros 50", not "4 euros 5". A child of 10 says what the number on the calculator stands for in the story; a child of 12 also works out from the digits after the point how much is left over, and decides from the situation whether to round up or down.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names interpreting the outcomes on the calculator correctly in relation to the context problem. "bijvoorbeeld" (for example) introduces one case of this inside the wording, how to interpret the 'remainder'; it is an example, not a limit. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "You calculate with the calculator that 4.23 buses are needed to transport the children… What does this mean?" (in Dutch notation 4,23); it is an example, not a limit. The example gives no number of children and no number of seats, and it leaves the question open. The goal does not say how an outcome is to be rounded. This goal states no number range. The remainder of a division in contexts is also the subject of rw/bew/3/07/fase3.

### Common mistakes

Copying all the digits of the calculator as the answer. Rounding down where the situation needs rounding up, and answering 5 buses. Reading the digits after the point as the remainder, so that 5.2083333 is taken for 5 buses with 2 children left over.
