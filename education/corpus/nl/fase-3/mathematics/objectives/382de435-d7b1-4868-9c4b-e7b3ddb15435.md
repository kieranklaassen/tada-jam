## English gloss

understanding the difference between indications of time (*tijdaanduidingen*, statements of when something is) and duration

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts statements into two kinds: those that say when something is ("at 14:30", "on 3 May") and those that say how long something lasts ("2 hours and 30 minutes", "three days"). The child shows the difference on a clock or a timeline: a point for a moment, and the stretch between two points for a duration. The child gives both for one event, for example that a film that starts at 19:15 and ends at 21:00 lasts 1 hour and 45 minutes. A child of 10 tells the two apart in everyday sentences; a child of 12 explains that "2:30" can be a moment or a duration, and what shows which one is meant.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names one thing to understand: the difference between a *tijdaanduiding*, which says when something is (a point in time), and a duration, which says how long something lasts. The two explanations are the pack's; the goal gives none. It names no units, no notation and no clock; the times in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. The verb is understanding; calculating with time is named in `rw/m/6/07/fase3`. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child reads a duration as a time of day, for example takes "2:30" on a stopwatch for half past two. The child gives the end time when the duration is asked, or the duration when the end time is asked. The child subtracts two times as if they were decimal numbers and finds 21.00 − 19.15 = 1.85.
