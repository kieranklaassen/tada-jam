## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

calculating increase, decrease, profit or loss, also with percentages above 100%

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures a bean plant of 20 cm that has grown to 50 cm some weeks later, finds an increase of 30 cm and works out that this is 150% of the first height. For a bicycle bought for 40 euro and sold for 50 euro the child finds a profit of 10 euro, which is 25% of the buying price, and for a club that goes from 250 to 200 members a decrease of 50 members, which is 20%. A child of 10 works out the difference and sets it against the starting amount in a ratio table; a child of 12 works it out without the table.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names four things to calculate: increase, decrease, profit or loss. The words "ook met percentages boven 100%" (also with percentages above 100%) include those percentages and set no upper limit. This goal states no range for the amounts. The text does not say whether the outcome is an amount, a percentage or both, and it does not say which amount counts as 100%. What percentages of more than 100% mean is in rw/verh/3/03/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child takes the new amount as 100% and not the starting amount, and calls the growth from 20 cm to 50 cm 60%. The child mixes the change with the result: a plant that grows by 150% is then 250% of its first height. The child thinks that an increase of 25% followed by a decrease of 25% gives the starting amount again.
