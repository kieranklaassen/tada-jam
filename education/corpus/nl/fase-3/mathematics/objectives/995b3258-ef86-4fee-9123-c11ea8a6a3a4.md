## Official wording

doorzien en uitleggen van het decimale systeem van inhoudsmaten (litermaten en kubieke maten) in het metrieke stelsel en de relaties hiertussen; herleiden op basis van die relaties: 1 dl = 10 cl; 1 dl = 1/10 l; 1 l = 10 dl; 1 l = 100 cl; 1 m3 = 1000 dm3 en 1 dm3 = 1000 cm3.; 1 l = 1 dm3, enzovoort

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

grasping (*doorzien*) and explaining the decimal system of *inhoudsmaten* (measures of volume or capacity) (litre measures and cubic measures) in the metric system and the relations between them; converting (*herleiden*) on the basis of those relations: 1 dl = 10 cl; 1 dl = 1/10 l; 1 l = 10 dl; 1 l = 100 cl; 1 m³ = 1000 dm³ and 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³; 1 l = 1 dm³, and so on

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child fills a hollow cube of 1 dm in each direction from a litre jug and sees that it holds exactly 1 litre; it builds the same cube from ten layers of ten rows of ten centimetre cubes and counts 1000 cm³. The child pours 1 dl ten times into a litre jug and explains that each step between l, dl and cl is a step of ten, while each step between m³, dm³ and cm³ is a step of a thousand. The child then converts, for example 3 l = 30 dl = 300 cl, 5 dl = 50 cl = 0.5 l, and 2 m³ = 2000 dm³ = 2000 l. A child of 10 finds a relation by pouring or building and converts with it; a child of 12 explains why the step is ten in the litre measures and a thousand in the cubic measures.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal prints seven relations: 1 dl = 10 cl; 1 dl = 1/10 l; 1 l = 10 dl; 1 l = 100 cl; 1 m³ = 1000 dm³; 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³; and 1 l = 1 dm³. The list ends with *enzovoort* ("and so on"), so it is not closed. The measures in the printed relations are the litre, the decilitre and the centilitre, and the cubic metre, the cubic decimetre and the cubic centimetre. The wording names no particular measure beyond those in the printed relations, and *enzovoort* leaves open which others are meant. Six relations are in whole numbers and one has a fraction (1/10); the wording does not mention decimal numbers and states no number range. The goal names no formula. The source prints the exponents as plain digits ("m3", "dm3", "cm3") and prints a full stop before the semicolon after "1000 cm3"; the gloss writes m³, dm³ and cm³ and leaves the full stop out.

### Common mistakes

The child uses the step of ten of the litre measures for the cubic measures, for example takes 1 m³ for 10 dm³ or 100 dm³. The child joins the litre to the wrong cubic measure and takes 1 l for 1 cm³ or 1 m³. The child multiplies where it has to divide, so that an amount gets a smaller number in a smaller measure.
