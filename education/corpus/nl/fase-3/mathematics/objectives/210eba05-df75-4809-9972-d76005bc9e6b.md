## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing the concepts 'length', 'width' and 'perimeter' (*omtrek*, also circumference) and using them in the right situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child points along a table top and says which side it calls the length and which the width, and moves a finger along the whole edge to show the perimeter. The child uses the three words when it measures and describes things: the length and width of a room, the length of a route, the perimeter of a field, the distance around a tree trunk. A child of 10 uses the words for rectangles; a child of 12 also uses them for other figures and things and says when a word does not fit, for example that a round pond has an *omtrek* and no length and width.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three concepts: length, width and perimeter (*omtrek*). It does not name height or depth, which the formula in `rw/m/3/08/fase3` uses. The "right situations" are not named. The goal names no unit, no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child confuses perimeter and area. The child thinks that the length is always the side that lies from left to right, and is unsure which side is the length when the figure is turned. Asked for the perimeter, the child adds one length and one width and leaves out the other two sides.
