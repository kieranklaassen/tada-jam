## Official wording

optellen en aftrekken volgens standaardprocedures met hele getallen in contextsituaties en in formele wiskundetaal. Procedures kunnen zijn: splitsen, rijgen, vormen van kolomsgewijs rekenen en cijferen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding and subtracting according to standard procedures with whole numbers in context situations and in formal mathematical language. Procedures can be: splitting (*splitsen*, working per place value), stringing (*rijgen*, the second number added or subtracted in steps), forms of column-wise calculating (*kolomsgewijs rekenen*, whole place values, written in columns) and the written algorithm (*cijferen*, digit by digit in columns)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child adds 4,687 + 2,536 in columns digit by digit, carrying to the next column, and gets 7,223. The child subtracts 5,203 − 1,847 in columns and gets 3,356. The child works 735 − 280 on an empty number line by going back 200 to 535 and then back 80 to 455. A child of 10 carries out a procedure and writes every in-between result down; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. "Procedures can be" introduces a list of possible procedures: *splitsen*, *rijgen*, forms of *kolomsgewijs rekenen* and *cijferen*; the goal does not ask for all four and does not prescribe one. The Dutch leaves open whether "forms of" also covers *cijferen*. The numbers are whole numbers, in context situations and in formal mathematical language. This goal states no number range; rw/bew/1/04/fase3 states "to at least 100" for fluent calculating, its own content.

### Common mistakes

Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger one in every column, whichever number is on top. Forgetting to add a carried ten, or borrowing wrongly across the zero in 5,203. Lining up numbers of different lengths from the left instead of by place value.
