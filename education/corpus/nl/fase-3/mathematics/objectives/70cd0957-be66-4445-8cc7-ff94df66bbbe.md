## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining the insight that fractions arise from sharing-out situations (*verdeelsituaties*) and measuring situations (*meetsituaties*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shares 3 pancakes fairly among 4 children by cutting, and finds that each child gets three quarters, written 3/4. The child measures a table with a paper strip, finds that the strip fits 2 times with a piece left over, folds the strip into 4 equal parts and sees that the piece left over is as long as 1 of those parts, so the table is 2 1/4 strips long. A child of 10 cuts or folds and names the parts; a child of 12 says beforehand what fraction each share will be and explains why sharing 3 among 4 gives 3/4.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about insight into where fractions come from, and it names two kinds of situation: sharing out and measuring. This goal states no number range: it names no denominators and no size for the numbers that are shared or measured. It does not mention calculating with fractions; operations with fractions have their own cluster in this lane, rw/bew/6.

### Common mistakes

The child cuts unequal parts and still names them quarters. The child reads 3/4 as two separate whole numbers, a 3 and a 4, and not as one amount. When measuring, the child does not name the left-over piece as a part of the measuring strip and says "2 strips and a bit".
