## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says what a speed means, for example that at 60 kilometres per hour a car covers 60 kilometres in one hour, 30 kilometres in half an hour and 1 kilometre in one minute. The child compares speeds that are given in different ways by bringing them to the same form: a runner who covers 100 metres in 10 seconds goes 10 metres per second, which is 36,000 metres in an hour or 36 kilometres per hour, faster than a cyclist at 25 kilometres per hour. The child works out a distance from a speed and a time, and a time from a speed and a distance: at 80 kilometres per hour, 200 kilometres take 2.5 hours, which is 2 hours and 30 minutes. A child of 10 calculates in a table of matching distances and times; a child of 12 calculates without the table and says beforehand whether the outcome must be larger or smaller.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three activities with speeds: interpreting, comparing and calculating, with *herleiden* (converting from one unit or form into another) in brackets after calculating. It names no unit of speed, no unit of distance or time and no formula; kilometres per hour and metres per second in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. This goal states no number range. The concepts 'speed' and 'average speed' are the subject of `rw/m/8/01/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

The child reads 2.5 hours as 2 hours and 50 minutes. The child compares the numbers of two speeds without looking at the units, for example takes 30 metres per second for slower than 50 kilometres per hour. The child multiplies where it must divide, and finds a longer time for a higher speed over the same distance.
