## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about *inhoud* (volume or capacity) in problem situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works on a question about volume and gives reasons: what "twice as large" means for a box when it is said of its length, of its area and of its volume; whether a stated amount can be right, for example a bath of 15 litres; how many crates of a given size fit in a van. The child estimates with amounts it knows, calculates, and checks the result against the situation. A child of 10 reasons by filling and counting; a child of 12 reasons with measurements and conversions and says which data are missing.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal's own wording says "problem situations" and names none. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question what "2 times as large" (*2 keer zo groot*) means when one looks at the length of something, when one looks at the area, and when one looks at the *inhoud*; it is an example, not a limit. The first question in the paragraph above comes from that example; the bath and the crates are the pack's. Neither the wording nor the example names a unit, a measuring instrument, a conversion or a formula, and the goal states no number range. The goals of this kind for length and perimeter, area and weight are `rw/m/1/12/fase3`, `rw/m/2/10/fase3` and `rw/m/4/07/fase3`.

### Common mistakes

The child takes "twice as large" to mean the same for a length, an area and a volume. The child divides one volume by the other to find how many boxes fit in a space and does not check whether the boxes fit by their measurements. The child accepts a result that cannot be right and does not check it against an amount it knows.
