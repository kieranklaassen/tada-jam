## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about money in various (problem) situations: offers (*aanbiedingen*, special offers), saving money and *geld lenen* (borrowing money, or lending it)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child compares two offers, for example "3 for the price of 2" at 1.50 euros each and "1.10 euros each", and works out the price of three: 3.00 euros against 3.30 euros. The child works out how long saving for a purchase takes, for example 12 weeks at 5 euros a week for 60 euros. The child reasons about borrowing: that borrowed money must be paid back, and what it means when more must be paid back than was borrowed. A child of 10 works out which of the offers it compares costs less; a child of 12 also asks whether it needs the extra items of an offer.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. After the colon the goal names three situations: offers, saving money and *geld lenen*. The Dutch verb *lenen* means both borrowing and lending; the goal's own wording does not say which. The word "problem" stands in brackets, so the situations need not be problems. The goal's own wording names no kind of offer, no amounts, no interest and no percentages. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: discounts on subscriptions for mobile phones, interest (*rente*), profit (*winst*), paying in instalments (*betalen in termijnen*), the saying "geld lenen kost geld" (to *lenen* money costs money), and the question when you would rather have 10% discount and when rather 10 euros discount, with the request to explain; it is an example, not a limit. So the example names one kind of offer, interest, a percentage (10%) and an amount (10 euros), and its saying is about what *lenen* costs, which fits borrowing. The offers and the amounts in the paragraph above are the pack's examples. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child takes every offer for a saving and does not compare the price per item or ask whether it needs the extra items. The child thinks that borrowed money is extra money and does not count paying it back. The child compares the prices of two packs without looking at how much each holds.
