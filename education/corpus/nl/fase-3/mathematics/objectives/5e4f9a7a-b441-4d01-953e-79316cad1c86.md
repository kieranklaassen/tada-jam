optellen en aftrekken met decimale getallen, met en zonder gelijk aantal decimalen in contextsituaties en formele wiskundetaal waarbij een doelmatige oplossingsmanier wordt gekozen op basis van eigenschappen van bewerkingen en de structuur van getallen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

adding and subtracting with decimal numbers, with and without an equal number of decimal places, in context situations and formal mathematical language, in which an effective way of solving is chosen on the basis of properties of operations and the structure of numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees 4.98 + 3.25 and works it as 5 + 3.25 − 0.02 = 8.23. The child sees 10 − 9.85 and counts on from 9.85 to 10 instead of subtracting in columns, and gets 0.15. The child adds 2.75 + 1.8 + 0.25 by first joining 2.75 and 0.25 to 3, and gets 4.8. A child of 10 uses a shortcut when it sees that a number is close to a whole number; a child of 12 looks at the numbers first, chooses between a shortcut and a standard procedure, and says why.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about choosing an effective way of solving on the basis of properties of operations and the structure of numbers; it names no strategies. rw/bew/1/06/fase3 lists example strategies for whole numbers, for its own content. The numbers are decimal numbers with and without an equal number of decimal places, in context situations and in formal mathematical language. This goal states no number range and does not say how many decimal places. It does not say whether the working is done mentally or with notes on paper.

### Common mistakes

Using the written algorithm for every sum, also for 10 − 9.85. Compensating in the wrong direction, working 4.98 + 3.25 as 5 + 3.25 + 0.02. Rounding 4.98 to 5 and forgetting the correction.
