schattend vermenigvuldigen en delen met hele getallen in contextsituaties en formele wiskundetaal en beredeneren of de werkelijke uitkomst (veel) groter of kleiner is dan de geschatte uitkomst en op basis daarvan eventueel nog een correctie toepassen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

multiplying and dividing with whole numbers by estimation (*schattend*, with rounded numbers), in context situations and formal mathematical language, and reasoning whether the actual outcome is (much) larger or smaller than the estimated outcome and on that basis possibly applying a correction

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child estimates the cost of 48 chairs of €19 each as 50 × 20 = 1,000 euros and reasons that the actual amount is lower, because both numbers were rounded up. The child estimates how many boxes of 24 are needed for 1,150 eggs as 1,200 ÷ 24 = 50 and reasons that slightly fewer than 50 boxes are needed. A child of 10 rounds to tens and hundreds that fit a table fact; a child of 12 also judges how far off the estimate is and corrects it, for example from 1,000 down to a little over 900.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has three parts: multiplying and dividing by estimation, reasoning whether the actual outcome is (much) larger or smaller than the estimate, and possibly ("eventueel") applying a correction; the correction is not asked for every time. The numbers are whole numbers, in context situations and in formal mathematical language. This goal states no number range, and does not say how to round or how close an estimate must be.

### Common mistakes

Rounding both numbers of a multiplication up and not seeing that the estimate is then clearly too high. In a division, rounding each number to the nearest ten or hundred instead of to numbers that divide easily. Losing or adding a zero in 50 × 20.
