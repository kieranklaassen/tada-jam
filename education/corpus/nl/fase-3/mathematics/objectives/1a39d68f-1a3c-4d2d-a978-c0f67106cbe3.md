## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

saying aloud and writing down measures for length and perimeter (*omtrek*, also circumference): km, hm, dam, m, dm, cm, mm

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads lengths aloud from signs, maps and drawings, for example "four point two kilometres" for 4.2 km and "thirty-five millimetres" for 35 mm, and writes a length it hears with the right abbreviation. The child says the full name for each of the seven abbreviations, also for the little-used hm (hectometre), dam (decametre) and dm (decimetre). A child of 10 says and writes the measures; a child of 12 does the same, so the two differ little here.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. After the colon the goal names seven measures: km, hm, dam, m, dm, cm and mm. It names no others, and the same seven serve for length and for perimeter. The goal is about saying and writing: it names no conversion (converting is the subject of `rw/m/1/10/fase3`) and no formula, and it states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child confuses dam (decametre) and dm (decimetre). The child reads hm or dam letter by letter because it does not know the name. The child writes a length without a measure, or writes cm where mm is meant.
