## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking critically and reasoning about ratio problems (*verhoudingsproblemen*; *verhouding* covers both ratio and proportion), also ones in which the ratio is not directly visible

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads that 4 painters paint a fence in 6 hours, is asked how long 8 painters take, and reasons that twice as many painters need half the time, 3 hours, so that here doubling the one number halves the other. The child reads that a child of 5 years is 110 cm tall and says that the height at 10 years cannot be found by doubling, because age and height do not stand in a fixed ratio. A child of 10 says in simple cases whether twice as much of the one gives twice as much of the other; a child of 12 finds the ratio that a text, a table or a drawing does not show directly, and says when there is none.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about critical thinking and reasoning on ratio problems, and it includes ("ook") problems in which the ratio is not directly visible. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: "Jos drives 75 km in his new car and uses 5 litres of petrol for it. The manufacturer claims that the fuel consumption of Jos's car is therefore 1 in 20. Is that claim right?" (1 op 20, the Dutch way of saying 1 litre for 20 km); it is an example, not a limit. The goal's own wording does not say what makes a ratio not directly visible or what the problems are about, and states no number range; in the example 75 km on 5 litres is 15 km on 1 litre, so the ratio has to be worked out before the claim of 1 in 20 can be judged.

### Common mistakes

The child applies "twice as much gives twice as much" to every problem with numbers, also where no fixed ratio exists or where more of the one gives less of the other. The child takes the numbers from the text and calculates without asking what stands in ratio to what. The child gives an outcome that cannot be right in the situation and does not check it.
