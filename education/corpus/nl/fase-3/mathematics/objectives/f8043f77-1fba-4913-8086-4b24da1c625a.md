begrijpen dat je met een kleinere maat nauwkeuriger kunt meten en dat het van de situatie afhangt of je een grovere of een fijnere maat gebruikt, nadenken over maatverfijning en meetfouten

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

understanding that you can measure more precisely with a smaller measure and that it depends on the situation whether you use a coarser or a finer measure, thinking about refining a measure (*maatverfijning*, going to a smaller unit) and measurement errors

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures the same table with a rule that is marked in decimetres only and with a tape that is marked in millimetres, and compares the results: about 12 dm with the first, 1 m 23 cm 4 mm with the second. The child says which measure a situation asks for: millimetres for a plank that has to fit into a frame, metres for the length of a playground, kilometres for a bicycle ride. Several children measure the same length, find results that differ by a few millimetres, and talk about the causes: where the tape began, whether it lay straight, how the mark was read. A child of 10 sees that a finer measure gives a more precise result; a child of 12 also says how precise a result has to be for a situation and that no measurement is exact.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal has three parts: a smaller measure gives a more precise measurement; the situation decides between a coarser and a finer measure; and thinking about refining a measure and about measurement errors. It names no unit and no measuring instrument, and the units in the paragraph above are those of `rw/m/1/01/fase3`. It names no way of stating or reducing a measurement error and no rule for rounding. It names no conversion and no formula, and it states no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks that every careful measurement is exact, and that two results that differ mean that someone measured wrongly. The child always uses the finest measure, also where it serves no purpose, for example gives the distance between two towns in metres. The child thinks that a result written in a smaller measure is always the more precise one, also where it was only converted, for example 1200 mm written for a measurement of 12 dm.
