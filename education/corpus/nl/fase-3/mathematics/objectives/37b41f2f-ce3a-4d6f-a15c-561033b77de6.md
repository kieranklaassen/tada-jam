## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

giving a formal operation for an addition or subtraction situation with decimal numbers and the reverse: thinking up fitting addition or subtraction situations for a formal operation with decimal numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears that a piece of 0.85 m is sawn off a plank of 2.4 m and writes 2.4 − 0.85. Given the bare sum 1.75 + 0.5, the child makes up a story that fits, such as a bottle of 1.75 litres and a carton of 0.5 litre poured into one jug. A child of 10 makes up a story for a bare sum; a child of 12 also checks whether a story that another child made up fits the sum, such as how much further 4.2 m is than 3.65 m for 4.2 − 3.65.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal goes in two directions: from a situation to a formal operation, and from a formal operation to fitting situations. It is about adding and subtracting with decimal numbers. The wording is about giving the operation and thinking up situations; it does not mention working out the outcome. This goal states no number range and no number of decimal places; rw/bew/2/02/fase3 names decimal numbers with and without an equal number of decimal places for its own content.

### Common mistakes

Choosing the operation from a signal word ("more" means adding) instead of from the situation. Making up only take-away stories for a subtraction, never a difference or a comparison. Making up a story in which the decimal numbers cannot occur, such as 2.5 children.
