## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

carrying out operations with fractions with insight, in contexts and in formal mathematical language, and explaining the way of solving and comparing it with that of others. (this concerns: adding and subtracting fractions with the same denominator (*gelijknamig*) and with different denominators (*ongelijknamig*), and knowing for this the standard procedure of making the denominators the same (*gelijknamig maken*); completing fractions to 1, and comparing and finding the difference between fractions with the same and with different denominators; multiplying a whole number by a fraction and the reverse: multiplying a fraction by a whole number; multiplying a fraction by a fraction or taking a part of a part, and knowing that this is the same, particularly in context situations; and dividing a whole number by a fraction and dividing a fraction by a whole number, particularly in context situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child adds 1/2 and 1/3 of a paper strip by first folding both into sixths, 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6, and says that 1/6 is missing to make 1. The child pours 3/4 litre three times and writes 3 × 3/4 = 9/4, which is 2 1/4 litres; takes half of half a chocolate bar and writes 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4; and finds that 8 glasses of 1/4 litre come out of 2 litres, 2 ÷ 1/4 = 8. The child tells another child how the answer was found and the two compare their ways. A child of 10 works with strips, pieces and measuring jugs; a child of 12 also writes the calculations in formal notation and makes denominators the same without the material.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The bracket lists what the goal concerns: adding and subtracting fractions with the same and with different denominators, with the standard procedure of making the denominators the same; completing a fraction to 1; comparing fractions and finding their difference; a whole number times a fraction and a fraction times a whole number; a fraction times a fraction, or a part of a part; a whole number divided by a fraction and a fraction divided by a whole number. Dividing a fraction by a fraction is not in the list. "Particularly in context situations" is attached to multiplying a fraction by a fraction and to the two kinds of division. The content-line document prints two examples inside this goal: with knowing that a fraction times a fraction and a part of a part are the same, "1/4 part of 1/2 litre and 1/4x1/2" (1/4 deel van 1/2 liter en 1/4x1/2); and at the end, after the two kinds of division, "dividing 10 litres over containers of 2½ litres can be written as 10:2½" (10 liter verdelen over bakjes van 2½ liter kun je noteren als 10:2½, with the colon as division sign); they are examples, not limits. The goal's own wording states no number range and names no denominators; the examples use 1/4, 1/2 and 2½. The source opens a bracket after a full stop and does not close it, and prints "een heel getallen" where "een heel getal" (a whole number) is plainly meant.

### Common mistakes

Adding the numerators and the denominators, so that 1/2 + 1/3 becomes 2/5. Expecting that multiplying always gives more, and so doubting that 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4. Reading 2 ÷ 1/4 as 2 divided into 4 parts and answering 1/2 instead of 8.
