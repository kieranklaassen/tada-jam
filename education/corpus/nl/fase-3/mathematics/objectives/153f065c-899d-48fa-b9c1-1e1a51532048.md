## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading, writing and saying aloud decimal numbers, also in wholes, tenths, hundredths and thousandths

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads 3.405 aloud, digit by digit after the decimal mark, and then names it as 3 wholes, 4 tenths, 0 hundredths and 5 thousandths. The child hears "seven hundredths" and writes 0.07, and hears "2 wholes and 15 thousandths" and writes 2.015. A child of 10 lays a decimal number in a place-value chart to name its places; a child of 12 names the places straight from the written number, such as 0.125 kilogram.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names four place values: wholes, tenths, hundredths and thousandths; thousandths are the smallest place named. The word "ook" (also) adds naming a number in these places to reading, writing and saying it aloud; the text does not say that numbers with more decimal places are left out. The goal states no range for the whole-number part. The text does not say how the number is written down: Dutch writing puts a comma before the decimals (3,405), where this pack's notes put a point.

### Common mistakes

The child reads the digits after the decimal mark as a whole number and says "three point four hundred and five", which hides the place values. The child writes seven hundredths as 0.7 or as 0.007. The child leaves out a zero that holds a place and writes 2.15 for 2 wholes and 15 thousandths.
