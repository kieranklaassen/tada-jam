## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child starts from 1/3 and writes in a row 2/6, 3/9, 4/12 and 5/15, and says what happens to the numerator and the denominator at each step. The child starts a second row from 3/4 and writes 6/8, 9/12 and 12/16, then checks two of them by folding a paper strip. A child of 10 builds the row step by step with strips or a drawing; a child of 12 writes down a fraction far along the row, such as 100/300 for 1/3, and explains why it belongs there.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal asks for rows of equivalent fractions that are thought up ("bedenken"), not only read or completed. This goal states no number range: it names no denominators, no starting fractions and no length for a row. The same idea for ratios, as a series of equivalent number pairs, is in rw/verh/2/05/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child continues the row by adding the same number to the numerator and the denominator, and writes 1/3, 2/4, 3/5. The child multiplies the numerator and the denominator by different numbers. The child thinks the row ends at some point, or that the fractions further along the row are larger because their numbers are larger.
