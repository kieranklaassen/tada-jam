## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing and understanding that a percentage represents a part-whole ratio (*deel-geheelverhouding*; *verhouding* covers both ratio and proportion) and is a relative figure (*relatief gegeven*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child works out 50% of a class of 20 children and 50% of a school of 300 children, finds 10 and 150, and says that the same percentage stands for different numbers because the wholes differ. The child shades a quarter of a short strip and a quarter of a long strip and says that each is 25% of its own strip. A child of 10 shows this with strips; a child of 12 asks "percent of what?" on reading a percentage and names the whole it belongs to.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 3 (*groep* 7 and 8, about ages 10 to 12), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about knowing and understanding two things: a percentage shows how a part stands to its whole, and a percentage is relative, so it gives no amount until the whole is known. This goal states no number range: it does not say which percentages or which wholes. It names no calculation; calculating is named in other goals of this cluster, such as rw/verh/3/01/fase3 and rw/verh/3/04/fase3.

### Common mistakes

The child compares two percentages as if they were amounts and says that 50% is always more than 25%, also when the wholes differ, as with 50% of 20 and 25% of 300. The child does not ask what the whole is. The child thinks that 10% of a class means 10 children.
