## Official wording

ervaren van verschillende gespreksdoelen (bijv. verhaal vertellen om te amuseren, overleg hoe een conflict op te lossen of informatie uitwisselen over gebeurtenissen in een prentenboek)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing different purposes of conversation (e.g. telling a story to amuse, talking over how to resolve a conflict, or exchanging information about events in a picture book)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells a funny story and sees the others laugh. After a quarrel over the bicycle, the child and another child each say what they want and agree who rides first. Looking at a picture book with a classmate, the child says what happens on the page and hears what the other saw. A child of 4 takes part in these talks as they come; a child of 6 takes part in the same talks and sees what comes of each: laughter after the story, a turn on the bicycle after the talk about the quarrel.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experiencing: the child takes part in conversations that have different purposes; the wording does not speak of naming a purpose. The three purposes in brackets (amusing with a story, talking over how to resolve a conflict, exchanging information about events in a picture book) are examples.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the listener's side of the purpose: they tell a story for their own pleasure and do not check whether the others follow or enjoy it. In a talk about a conflict many repeat what the other child did wrong and do not reach the question of what to do next.
