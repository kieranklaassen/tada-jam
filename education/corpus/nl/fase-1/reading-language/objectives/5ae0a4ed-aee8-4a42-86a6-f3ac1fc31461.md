## Official wording

vertellen wat de gebruikte tekens in het eigen schrift betekenen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

telling what the signs used in one's own writing (*schrift*: handwriting, or exercise book) mean

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child has filled a line with loops, shapes that look like letters and a few real letters, and "reads" it to the teacher with a finger under it: "this says: mama, I am at school". The teacher writes the sentence under it. A child of 4 gives a meaning to scribbles and may give a different one a little later; a child of 7 reads back their own words and can say which letter stands for which sound.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The signs are the child's own; the goal does not say that they are letters or that others can read them. *Schrift* can mean handwriting or script, or an exercise book; in both readings the signs are those the child wrote. The goal names no signs and does not mention spelling.

### Common mistakes

Giving the writing a new meaning each time it is "read". Believing that others can read the signs as the child meant them. Telling a long story with a few signs, with no link between how much is said and how much is written.
