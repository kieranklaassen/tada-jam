## Official wording

vloeiend en verstaanbaar praten zoals klinkers en medeklinkers verstaanbaar uitspreken en gebruiken van eenvoudige intonatie (bijv. vraagzin)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

talking fluently and intelligibly, such as pronouncing vowels and consonants intelligibly and using simple intonation (e.g. question sentence)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells something in the circle and the other children understand the words without the teacher repeating them. The child says a question so that it sounds like a question, with the voice going up at the end, and gives the puppets different voices in the puppet corner. A child of 4 says a word again, more slowly, when the others did not understand it; a child of 6 makes "are you coming?" sound like a question and "come here!" like a call.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. What follows "such as" are examples of fluent and intelligible talking: pronouncing vowels and consonants intelligibly, and using simple intonation, with the question sentence as an example of that. The wording sets no speed of speech and no length of utterance. The statement names no sounds that should be mastered; *toel* for *stoel* under Common mistakes is the pack's example.

### Common mistakes

Dropping or replacing a sound where two consonants stand together, for example saying *toel* for *stoel* (chair). Repeating words or parts of words at the start of a sentence while planning what to say, which is common at this age. Saying a question with flat intonation, so that listeners hear a statement.
