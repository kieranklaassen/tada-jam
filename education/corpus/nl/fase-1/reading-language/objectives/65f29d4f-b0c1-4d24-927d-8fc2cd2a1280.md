## Official wording

naspelen of navertellen van een voorgelezen verhaal (met of zonder illustraties)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

acting out or retelling a story that was read aloud (with or without illustrations)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After the reading, the child plays the story with hand puppets, dolls or classmates: one is the wolf, one is the goat, and they say what the figures said. Or the child tells the story again in their own words, turning the pages of the book and using the pictures, or without the book. A child of 4 acts out one or two scenes with the pictures in front of them; a child of 7 retells the beginning, middle and end in order without the book.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The story is one that was read aloud to the child, not one the child read. The goal gives two ways, acting out or retelling, joined by "or", and the brackets allow both with and without illustrations. It sets no length for the story and does not say how complete the retelling must be.

### Common mistakes

Telling the events in the wrong order, or starting with the most exciting part. Leaving out the beginning or the end. Describing each picture separately ("here is a wolf, here is a house") without linking them into a story.
