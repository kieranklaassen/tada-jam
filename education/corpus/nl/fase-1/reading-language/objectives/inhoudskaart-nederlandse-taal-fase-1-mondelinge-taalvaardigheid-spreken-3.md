## Official wording

klanken vormen met het Nederlandse klanksysteem (fonologie)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

forming sounds with the Dutch sound system (phonology)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says words with the sounds of Dutch, including sounds such as *ui*, *eu* and *sch*. The child repeats a rhyme or a new word after the teacher and makes sounds with the mouth in play: hissing like a snake, buzzing like a bee. A child of 4 says a new word with an easier sound in place of a hard one and tries again after hearing it once more; a child of 6 repeats a long new word after the teacher with every sound in its place.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names the Dutch sound system and puts the term phonology in brackets. It is about forming sounds in speech; letters are not mentioned. The statement lists no sounds and sets no order in which they are formed; the sounds and words named above and under Common mistakes are the pack's examples.

### Common mistakes

Replacing a sound that is formed late with an easier one, for example *l* or *j* for *r*. Leaving out an unstressed syllable in a longer word (*naan* for *banaan*, banana). Children who learn Dutch as a second language often do not yet make the difference between a long and a short vowel (*maan*, moon, and *man*, man).
