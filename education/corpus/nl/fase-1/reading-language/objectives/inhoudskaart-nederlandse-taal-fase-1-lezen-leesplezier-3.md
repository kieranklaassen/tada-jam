## Official wording

plezier in zelf (kunnen) lezen en zelf (kunnen) voorlezen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

enjoyment in (being able to) read oneself and (being able to) read aloud oneself

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a book they know and "reads" it to a classmate or a soft toy, telling the story from the pictures and from memory. The child reads their own name and other known words aloud and shows an adult that they can. A child of 4 tells the story of a known book from its pictures in a reading voice; a child of 6 reads some words or a short sentence and reads a short book aloud to a younger child.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names enjoyment, in two things: reading oneself and reading aloud oneself. The Dutch puts "(kunnen)" in brackets both times, so it covers the enjoyment of doing it and of being able to do it. It does not say what the child reads and names no letters or words.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often confuse: they take telling a known book from memory to be reading the words.
