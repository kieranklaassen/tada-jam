## Official wording

waarnemen van signalen van (bekende) luisteraars

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

perceiving signals from (familiar) listeners

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While telling in the circle, the child looks at the classmates and sees that one is laughing, one is frowning and one has a hand up. The child stops for a moment when someone calls "what?" or when the teacher tilts her head. A child of 4 looks mostly at the teacher or at the object in hand and notices only loud reactions; a child of 6 looks round the group and notices a puzzled face or restlessness.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is about perceiving the signals; responding to them is a separate statement of the card (Spreken / 9). "Familiar" stands in brackets before "listeners". The statement does not say which signals and does not separate spoken from non-verbal ones.

### Common mistakes

Looking at the floor, at the object or only at the teacher while telling, so that the listeners' faces are not seen. Noticing a signal but not connecting it with their own telling (the others are restless "because they are naughty"). Missing quiet signals such as a frown.
