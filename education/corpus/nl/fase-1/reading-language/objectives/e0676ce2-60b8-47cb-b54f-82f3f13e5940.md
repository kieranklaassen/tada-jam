listening to and understanding: - picture books; - stories; - poems; - (theatre) performances; - experiences of an adult or child; - information about concrete subjects; - opinion of others

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sits with the class while a picture book is read aloud, looks at the pictures and afterwards tells what the fox wanted. The child listens to a short poem and joins in with the rhyming word, watches a puppet show, and listens to a classmate who tells about a broken arm. When a classmate says the book was boring, the child hears that this is what the classmate thinks. A child of 4 follows a short story with pictures or puppets and recalls one event; a child of 7 follows a longer story without pictures, retells it in order and can say what another child thinks of it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement lists seven things to listen to and understand: picture books, stories, poems, performances (with "theatre" in brackets), experiences told by an adult or a child, information about concrete subjects, and the opinion of others. The list is not introduced as examples. Information is limited to concrete subjects. The source prints the last item without a linking word (*mening anderen*); the gloss reads it as the opinion of others. The goal itself sets no length or difficulty of text. The content-line document prints text features for listening at fase 1 (kinds of text, a length for informative and for narrative texts, structure), which the lane's frame quotes; the two lengths are "informatieve teksten: enkele minuten" (informative texts: a few minutes) and "verhalende teksten: tot 10 minuten" (narrative texts: up to 10 minutes). The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/02/02, titled *Luisteren als lid van een live publiek* (listening as a member of a live audience).

### Common mistakes

Following the pictures and not the words, so that what is told but not drawn is missed. Remembering separate striking moments and not how one led to the next. Hearing another child's opinion as a fact or as an error ("That is not true, it is a nice book").
