## Official wording

schrijven vooral via tekenen, krabbelen, letterachtige vormen, letter-/tekenreeksen, spontane spelling en ook naschrijven, nastempelen, en typen op de computer

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing mainly by way of drawing, scribbling, letter-like shapes, strings of letters/signs, spontaneous spelling (*spontane spelling*, the child's own invented spelling) and also copying writing, copying with stamps, and typing on the computer

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child "writes" a message by drawing it, fills lines with scribbles, or makes rows of shapes that look like letters and rows of real letters, and says what it says. The child writes a word as they hear it, "fits" for "fiets". The child copies a word from a word card, stamps it with letter stamps, or types it on a keyboard. A child of 4 mostly draws and scribbles and writes a few letters of their name; a child of 6 writes words with a letter for most sounds and copies or types a word letter by letter.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the document itself names the years, *groep* 1 and 2 (about ages 4 to 6). The content-line document prints the goal under the label "groep 1/2:"; the open data lacks this sub-cluster of writing ("inhoud/vorm", content/form), so the record is read from that document. The wording lists ways of writing in two groups: after "vooral" (mainly) come drawing, scribbling, letter-like shapes, strings of letters or signs, and spontaneous spelling; after "en ook" (and also) come copying writing, copying with stamps, and typing on the computer. The list is not introduced as examples. *Spontane spelling* is the term for a child's own way of writing a word as the child hears it; the wording uses it without explaining it. The goal does not ask for correct spelling and names no way of forming letters.

### Common mistakes

Mirroring letters or changing their order when copying or stamping. Writing a word with only its first sound or its consonants. Taking a row of known letters for a word, without a link between the letters and the sounds of the word.
