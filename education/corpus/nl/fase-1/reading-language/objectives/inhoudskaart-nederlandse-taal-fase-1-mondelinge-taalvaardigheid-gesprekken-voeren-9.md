## Official wording

(met hulp) kiezen en productief gebruiken van (nieuwe) woorden tijdens gesprekken

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) choosing and productively using (new) words during conversations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

During a theme about the bakery, the child says "dough" and "knead" in the talk at the play table after hearing them in the story. When the child points and says "that thing", an adult offers the word and the child repeats it in a sentence of their own. A child of 4 uses a new word in the same sentence in which they heard it; a child of 6 uses it in a new sentence and chooses between two words, such as "big" and "huge".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording puts "with help" in brackets at the front, and "new" in brackets, so both known and new words are meant. It is about words used during conversations; productive use means that the child says the word, not only understands it. The statement sets no number of words and names no word list.

### Common mistakes

Understanding a new word but continuing to use a general word ("thing", "do") when speaking. Using a new word for too many things, for example calling every tool a "hammer". Using the new word only in the phrase in which it was heard.
