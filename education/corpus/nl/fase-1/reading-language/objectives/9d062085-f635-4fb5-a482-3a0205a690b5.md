## English gloss

getting to know and using terms about language (*taalbegrippen*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While talking about a book or about their own writing, the child uses terms about language: "that is a long word", "those two rhyme", and in *groep* 3 "my name starts with that letter". The child points to a word, and in *groep* 3 to a letter or a sentence, when asked for one. A child of 4 uses "woord" (word) and "rijmen" (to rhyme), not always for the right thing; a child of 7 also uses "letter", "zin" (sentence) and "klank" (sound) and adds ones such as capital letter and full stop.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the goal itself names no year, and the content-line document prints lists of terms under it per pair of *groepen*, each term offered from the pair it stands under. The lists are not in the open data and are no records; the lane's frame quotes the lists for *groep* 1-2 and for *groep* 3-4, and *groep* 3-4 is the last year of fase 1 and the first of fase 2. The goal itself names no terms and sets no number; those above are the pack's examples, taken from those lists: "woord" and "rijm(en)" stand in the list for *groep* 1-2, and "letter", "zin", "klank", "hoofdletter" (capital letter) and "punt" (full stop) in the list for *groep* 3-4. Two verbs are used: getting to know and using.

### Common mistakes

Saying "letter" for a word, or "word" for a sentence. Saying "letter" for a numeral. Using "sound" and "letter" as if they were the same.
