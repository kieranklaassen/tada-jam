## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

developing knowledge about how a text is built up (with the help of reading strategies) (*groep* 3, the third year of primary school)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before reading a short information text, a child in *groep* 3 looks at the title and the pictures and says what the text will be about. While reading, the child points to where the text starts, where a new part begins, and which sentence belongs with which picture. Afterwards the child says what came first, what came next and how it ended.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the wording itself names the year, *groep* 3 (about ages 6 to 7). The open data ends the wording with "(groep3)", printed without a space; the content-line document prints no such tag and carries the year in the heading of the cluster, "Zakelijke teksten: aanpak (vanaf groep 3)" (from *groep* 3). The cluster of this goal is about non-fiction texts (*zakelijke teksten*). The goal does not name the reading strategies; predicting, rereading and underlining are named in the goal that carries the code "PO Kerndoel 06: Informatie ordenen". It names no parts of a text, such as title, heading or paragraph.

### Common mistakes

Reading word by word without first looking at the title or the pictures. Seeing a text as a row of separate sentences, so the link between a heading or picture and the lines near it is missed. Naming the last sentence read when asked what the text is about.
