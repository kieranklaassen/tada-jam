## Official wording

kennismaken met en gebruiken van elementaire gespreksregels (bijv. op de beurt wachten en naar elkaar luisteren)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know and using elementary conversation rules (e.g. waiting for one's turn and listening to each other)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child raises a hand in the circle before speaking and keeps quiet while another child talks. The child looks at the speaker and afterwards says something about what was said. A child of 4 calls out when an idea comes and needs a reminder; a child of 6 waits with a hand up while others speak and tells another child "it is her turn now".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording has two verbs: getting to know the rules and using them. It speaks of elementary rules; waiting for one's turn and listening to each other are given as examples, so the set of rules is not fixed. The statement does not say who sets the rules.

### Common mistakes

Calling out the answer while another child has the turn. Waiting for the turn but not listening in the meantime, so that the child repeats what was just said or starts on something unrelated. Keeping to the rule only while the teacher is watching.
