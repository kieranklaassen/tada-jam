## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and understanding signal words that indicate chronological order or question-and-answer structure

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears or reads a recipe and puts the picture cards of the steps in order by attending to words such as "eerst" (first), "dan" (then), "daarna" (after that) and "tot slot" (finally). In an information text the child finds the question, for example a line that begins with "waarom" (why), and points to where the answer starts. A child of 4 follows "first" and "then" in spoken instructions; a child of 7 points out such words in a short written text and says what they tell about the order.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two functions of signal words are named: indicating chronological order and indicating a question-and-answer structure. The goal names no words; the Dutch words above are the pack's examples. Signal words for other relations, such as cause, contrast or a list, are not named. The goal does not say whether the text is heard or read; its cluster is about non-fiction texts.

### Common mistakes

Taking the order in which things are told for the order in which they happen, so a sentence with "voordat" (before) or "nadat" (after) is understood the wrong way round. Reading on without noticing the signal word and ordering the steps by what seems likely. Not linking an answer to its question when other sentences stand between them.
