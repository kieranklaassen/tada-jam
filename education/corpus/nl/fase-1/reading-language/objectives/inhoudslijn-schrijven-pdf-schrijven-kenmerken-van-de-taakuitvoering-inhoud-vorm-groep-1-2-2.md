schrijven: >(losse) woorden (bijv. labels of lijstjes) >zinnen (bijv. bij een tekening)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing: > (separate) words (e.g. labels or lists) > sentences (e.g. with a drawing)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes "auto" on a strip of paper and sticks it on the box with toy cars, and makes a shopping list for the play shop: "melk", "ei", "brood". Under a drawing of a dog the child writes a sentence, "dit is mijn hond". A child of 4 writes a word with a few letters they know or with shapes that look like letters; a child of 6 writes several words with a letter for most sounds and a short sentence with a drawing.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the document itself names the years, *groep* 1 and 2 (about ages 4 to 6). The content-line document prints the goal under the label "groep 1/2:"; the open data lacks this sub-cluster of writing ("inhoud/vorm", content/form), so the record is read from that document. Two things are named: words, with "separate" in brackets, and sentences. Labels or lists follow "bijv." and are examples for the words; with a drawing follows "bijv." and is an example for the sentences. The goal sets no number of words or sentences and does not mention correct spelling. In what ways children of these years write is the subject of the third goal under the same label; under the label "groep 3:" the same row names short texts and short sentences.

### Common mistakes

Writing a word with only its first sound or its consonants: "mlk" for "melk". Writing the words of a sentence without spaces between them. Writing the items of a list in one row, so that they cannot be told apart.
