## Official wording

dicteren teksten met hulp ter ondersteuning bij het formuleren (bijv. door vragen te stellen, of woorden te noemen/introduceren)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dictating texts with help to support the formulating (e.g. by asking questions, or naming/introducing words)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says what should be on a card for grandma, and a helper writes it down as the child says it. When the child stops, the helper asks "And what happened then?" or offers a word, and the child says the sentence again with that word in it. The child says the text slowly enough to be written down and listens when it is read back. A child of 4 dictates one sentence to go with a drawing; a child of 6 dictates a few sentences in order and changes a word after hearing the text read back.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the document itself names the years, *groep* 1 and 2 (about ages 4 to 6). The content-line document prints the goal under the label "groep 1/2:"; the open data lacks this sub-cluster of writing ("inhoud/vorm", content/form), so the record is read from that document. The child dictates; the goal does not say who writes the text down or who helps. The help is there to support the formulating, that is, putting the text into words. Asking questions and naming or introducing words follow "bijv." and are examples of that help. The source prints the verb in front of its object (*dicteren teksten*), the word order of a sentence whose subject is left out; the gloss reads it in the form of the lane's other goals. The goal sets no kind or length of text.

### Common mistakes

Speaking as fast as when telling a story, too fast for the text to be written down. Telling the helper about the event instead of saying the words that are to stand in the text. Answering a helping question with one word and not making it into a sentence for the text.
