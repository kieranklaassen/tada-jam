## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells a classmate which block is wanted, and the classmate understands without asking again. The child says words with several consonants in a row, such as *straat* and *schrift*, sometimes slowly or at a second attempt. When not understood, the child says the word again more slowly or begins the sentence again. A child of 4 still replaces or drops some sounds, often the *r* or one consonant of a group, and is understood by people who know the child; a child of 7 pronounces nearly all sounds so that an unfamiliar listener understands.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The measure is intelligibility: the listener can understand what is said. The brackets say that pauses, hesitations and rephrasings may occur, so speech without them is not asked for here. The statement names no particular vowels or consonants and does not mention sentence building or intonation.

### Common mistakes

Replacing a sound with an easier one (*t* for *k*, *l* or *j* for *r*) or dropping one consonant from a group ("taat" for *straat*). Leaving out an unstressed syllable or the end of a word ("naan" for *banaan*). Speaking too fast or too softly to be understood when excited or shy.
