## Official wording

blijven bij het gekozen onderwerp (bijv. met behulp van ondersteunend materiaal)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

staying with the chosen topic (e.g. with the help of supporting material)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells about the autumn walk with the collected leaves and chestnuts on the table and picks them up one by one while telling. The child tells about a holiday with a photo in hand and returns to the photo after starting on something else. A child of 4 says something about each object and stops; a child of 6 gives a connected account of the chosen subject and uses the objects as reminders.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Supporting material is given as an example of what helps the child to stay with the topic; the wording does not say what material. The topic is one that has been chosen; the statement does not say by whom. It sets no length of telling.

### Common mistakes

Following an association to another subject and not coming back. Describing the object in hand (its colour, where it was bought) instead of telling about the chosen subject. Stopping after one sentence because the child does not know what else belongs to the subject.
