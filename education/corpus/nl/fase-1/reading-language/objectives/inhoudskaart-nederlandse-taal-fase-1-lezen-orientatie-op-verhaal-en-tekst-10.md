## Official wording

afleiden van de betekenis van (onbekende) woorden met behulp van de kaft en afbeeldingen bij een tekst

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

deriving the meaning of (unknown) words with the help of the cover and pictures with a text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

When an unknown word comes up in a book that is read aloud, the child looks at the picture on that page, points to the thing it could be, and says what they think the word means. Before the reading the child looks at the cover and says what the book will be about. A child of 4 points to the thing in the picture; a child of 6 says the meaning in their own words and checks whether it fits the sentence.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about the meaning of words, with "unknown" in brackets. It names two aids: the cover, and the pictures with a text. The sentence around the word is not named as an aid. The statement does not say whether the text is read aloud or read by the child.

### Common mistakes

Linking the word to the most striking thing in the picture. Guessing from another word that sounds like it. Not noticing that a word is unknown, and so not asking or looking.
