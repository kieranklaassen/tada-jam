## Official wording

ervaren dat geschreven taal communicatieve doelen heeft

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing that written language has communicative purposes

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 (about 6 or 7) writes a note to a classmate and gets a note back. The child writes a wish on a card for someone who is ill, and puts a sign with *niet aankomen* (do not touch) by a block building and sees that others do not touch it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card prints this statement under a heading that says from *groep* 3 on (about ages 6 to 7). The wording says experiencing. It names communicative purposes and lists none; two writing purposes are named in `Aanvankelijk schrijven (vanaf GROEP 3) / 3`. It sets no length or kind of text and does not mention spelling; *niet aankomen* above is the pack's example.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the reader: they write for the teacher or as an exercise, and leave out who the note is for and who it is from.
