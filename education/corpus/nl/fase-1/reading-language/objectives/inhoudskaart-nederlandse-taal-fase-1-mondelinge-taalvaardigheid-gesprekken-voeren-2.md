## Official wording

reageren op elkaar, vragen stellen en beantwoorden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

responding to each other, asking and answering questions

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to a classmate's story about a lost tooth and says something that fits it, or asks where the tooth is now. Asked a question by another child, the child gives an answer to that question. A child of 4 asks "why did it fall out?" and, asked whether they have a loose tooth too, nods; a child of 6 asks who found the tooth and, asked the same question, tells which of their own teeth is loose.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names three acts: responding to each other, asking questions and answering them. It names no kind of question, no topic and no group size. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Telling something about themselves when invited to put a question ("I have a dog too" in place of a question). Answering a different question from the one asked, often the question they expected. Asking a question whose answer was just given.
