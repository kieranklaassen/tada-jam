## Official wording

ervaren van verschillende emoties in een verhaal (bijv. verdriet, boosheid, blijdschap)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing different emotions in a story (e.g. sadness, anger, joy)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While a story is read aloud, the child looks at a character's face in the picture, makes the same face, and says "he is sad". The child laughs, becomes silent or holds a neighbour's arm at the tense part. A child of 4 shows the feeling with face and body and names happy, angry and sad; a child of 7 says why the character feels that way and that a character can feel two things in one story.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Sadness, anger and joy are given as examples; the goal sets no list of emotions. The verb is experiencing: the goal does not ask the child to name or explain the emotions. The wording leaves open whether the emotions are those of the characters or the child's own while listening or reading.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often notice only the feeling at the end of the story and miss that it changed during the story. Young children often call every unpleasant feeling "angry" or "sad" and have no word for afraid, jealous or disappointed.
