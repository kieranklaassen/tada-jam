## Official wording

onderscheiden van de hoofdcomponenten van een verhaal (wie, wat, waar, wanneer) (bijv. met behulp van picto's)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing the main components of a story (who, what, where, when) (e.g. with the help of pictograms)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story has been read aloud, the child says who it was about, what happened, where it was and when. The child lays a pictogram card for each of the four next to a fitting picture from the book. A child of 4 says who the story is about and where it takes place; a child of 6 answers all four questions with the help of the cards.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The first brackets name four components: who, what, where and when. Pictograms are given as an example of an aid. Why and how are not named in this statement, and it does not say whether the story is read aloud or read by the child.

### Common mistakes

Giving a place when asked "when"; the time of a story is the component children find hardest to give. Naming every character when asked who the story is about. Retelling the whole story when asked "what".
