## Official wording

spelen met klanken (fonemen) en symbolen, woorden verklanken

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster NE/LE/08; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau c9387834-bcc3-4268-b38c-6d057b060550.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

playing with sounds (phonemes) and symbols, turning words into sound (*verklanken*: sounding a word out, or saying it aloud)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says a word slowly in its separate sounds, "v-i-s", and puts down one block for each sound, or hears "m-aa-n" and says "maan". The child listens for the first sound of their own name and looks for things that start with the same sound. The child lays letter cards or magnetic letters next to each other and says the sounds they stand for. A child of 4 plays with the first sound of a word and with a few letters, often those of their own name; a child of 7 splits a short word into all its sounds and blends the sounds of a written word into the word.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sounds meant are phonemes, the separate speech sounds of a word, as the brackets say. The goal names no particular sounds, letters or words, and sets no number of sounds in a word; the words above are the pack's examples. "Symbols" is not explained in the wording: it may mean letters or other written signs. *Verklanken* can mean sounding out a written word or saying a word aloud; the wording does not choose. The verb is playing: the goal does not say that the child reads.

### Common mistakes

Splitting a word into syllables, or into its first sound and the rest ("v-is"), when asked for the separate sounds. Saying the name of a letter ("em") where its sound ("mmm") is asked, or adding a vowel after a consonant ("buh"), which makes blending hard. Saying the separate sounds correctly and then naming a different word that starts with the same sound.
