## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The adult explains, with the materials on the table, how to fold and glue a paper lantern, and the child then begins with the right first step. The child tells a classmate who came in late what the task is. When part of the explanation was not clear, the child looks at what the adult shows or asks. A child of 4 follows an explanation of one or two steps that is shown as well as said; a child of 7 follows a spoken explanation of several steps and can say the steps back in order.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about explanation of activities and tasks; NE/MT/02/01/02/fase1 is the separate goal about instructions. The goal itself sets no number of steps and no length, and does not say whether the explanation is shown as well as spoken. The content-line document prints text features for listening at fase 1 (kinds of text, a length for informative and for narrative texts, structure), which the lane's frame quotes; the two lengths are "informatieve teksten: enkele minuten" (informative texts: a few minutes) and "verhalende teksten: tot 10 minuten" (narrative texts: up to 10 minutes). Among the kinds of text it names "korte instructieve teksten (bijv. aanwijzingen en instructies)" (short instructive texts, e.g. directions and instructions), and under structure it names visual and auditory support.

### Common mistakes

Remembering only the last step, or only the first, of an explanation with several steps. Copying what a neighbour does instead of listening, and so copying the neighbour's error. Starting before the explanation is finished.
