## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gets a card from a classmate who is ill and hears it read, dictates a shopping list to the teacher and takes it to the shop corner, and puts a note saying "niet aankomen" (do not touch) next to a building made of blocks. The child sees that the others read the note and leave the building alone. A child of 4 sees that an adult reads a message and acts on it; a child of 7 writes a short note alone and gets an answer.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experiencing: the goal does not ask the child to name purposes of written language. It names no purposes; two writing purposes, giving pleasure and informing, are named in NE/SCH/02/01/04/fase1. It names no kinds of text and does not say who does the writing.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often think that the reader knows what the writer meant, so they expect a note with a few letters or a drawing to say everything. A young child may think that writing is something done only at school for the teacher.
