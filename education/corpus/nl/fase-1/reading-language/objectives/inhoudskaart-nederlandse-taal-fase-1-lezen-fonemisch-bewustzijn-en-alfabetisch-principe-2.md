## Official wording

ontdekken van het alfabetisch principe (in een speelse context)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering the alphabetic principle (in a playful context)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees that the letter at the start of their written name also starts a classmate's name, and hears that both names begin with the same sound. In the play shop the child writes a list and puts down a letter for the first sound of each thing. A child of 4 knows the first letter of their own name and looks for it on labels and book covers; a child of 6 says a short word slowly and puts down a letter for each sound they hear.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says discovering, and the brackets say in a playful context. The card does not explain the alphabetic principle; the term means that the sounds of spoken words are written with letters. The statement names no letters, no number of letters and no words.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that a big thing needs a long word and a small thing a short one. Thinking that one letter stands for a whole word or a whole syllable. Knowing letters as shapes with a name and not linking them to a sound in a spoken word.
