## Official wording

ontdekken welke onderwerpen men leuk of niet leuk/saai vindt om over te lezen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/07/03; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 632ece4c-4eeb-473b-b28d-8bce8cd28bb3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering which subjects one finds fun or not fun/boring to read about

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child leafs through books about animals, vehicles, princesses, football and space in the book corner and puts aside the ones they want to look at or hear. The child tells a classmate "I like books about dinosaurs, not about babies". A child of 4 picks by the cover and the pictures; a child of 7 has tried reading about several subjects and names a few they choose again and a few they stop reading.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about subjects to read about, not about kinds of text or about authors. The verb is discovering: the goal sets no number of subjects and does not ask for reasons. It names no subjects; those above are the pack's examples.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often keep choosing the one subject they already know and so do not find out about others. Some children call a subject boring when the book was too hard to read, so the dislike is about the difficulty and not about the subject.
