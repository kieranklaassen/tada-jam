## Official wording

plezier hebben in zelf (kunnen) lezen en zelf (kunnen) voorlezen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster NE/LE/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 4ab0b875-9cfc-4f50-991f-dd899ef35f16.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking pleasure in reading (being able to read) oneself and in reading aloud (being able to read aloud) oneself

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sits with a book and "reads" it by telling the story from the pictures or from memory, or reads the words they can read. The child reads a page aloud to a classmate, a doll or a parent and shows that they are proud of it. A child of 4 pretends to read and turns the pages in order; a child of 7 reads a short book alone and chooses to read it aloud to someone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about pleasure in two things: reading oneself and reading aloud oneself. The bracketed word "kunnen" (being able to) adds the pleasure of being able to do it. The goal sets no reading level, no kind of text and no speed.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often choose a book that is too hard, cannot read many of its words and put it back. A child who has to read aloud to others before being ready may become afraid of making mistakes and stop wanting to.
