## Official wording

benoemen van het verschil tussen gesproken en geschreven taal

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming the difference between spoken and written language

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells a sentence to an adult, watches it being written down, and hears the same words read back later. The child says that what was said is gone and that what is written stays and can be read again by someone else. A child of 4 notices that an adult reads the same words from a book each time; a child of 6 says in their own words that speech is heard and writing is seen and stays.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says naming, and "the difference" in the singular; it does not say which difference is meant. It does not ask the child to read or write.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that the reader makes up the story anew from the pictures at each reading. Speaking at normal speed when an adult writes down their words, not seeing that writing takes longer than speaking. Calling a drawing writing.
