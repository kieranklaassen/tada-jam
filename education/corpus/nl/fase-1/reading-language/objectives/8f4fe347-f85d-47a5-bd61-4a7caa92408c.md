## Official wording

vragen stellen over woordbetekenis wanneer dat het luisteren/begrip belemmert

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

asking questions about word meaning when that hinders listening/understanding

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

During reading aloud the child hears "the fox was sly" and asks "What is sly?". At an unknown word in an instruction the child raises a hand and asks ("What is a ruler?") before starting the task. After the adult's answer the child listens on and uses the answer to follow the story. A child of 4 asks "What is that?" about a thing in a picture and seldom about a word that was only heard; a child of 7 notices an unknown word in a spoken text and asks about it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The condition is part of the statement: the question is asked when not knowing what a word means hinders listening or understanding, so the child is not asked to ask about every unknown word. The wording's "that" points back loosely to the word meaning, and the source prints "listening/understanding" with a slash. The statement does not mention help and names no other way of finding a meaning.

### Common mistakes

Not noticing that a word is unknown, and so not asking; the child believes the story was understood. Noticing it and staying silent, from shyness or so as not to interrupt. Guessing a meaning from a word that sounds similar and listening on with the wrong meaning.
