## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

becoming aware of everyday language (*omgangstaal*, the informal language of daily contact)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears that people greet a friend with *hoi* and the head teacher with *goedemorgen*, and that a friend is addressed with *je* and an unfamiliar adult with *u*. The child tries both in the home corner, speaking once as a friend and once as a shop customer. With the adult, the child says the same request in two ways, once to a friend and once to a visitor. A child of 4 uses everyday language without noticing it and copies a polite form when the adult models it; a child of 7 can say that one speaks differently to a friend than to an unfamiliar adult and gives an example.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "becoming aware": the statement asks for awareness, not for correct use of formal language. It gives no examples and does not define *omgangstaal*. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/01/04, titled *Afstemmen op gesprekspartner(s)* (adjusting to the conversation partner or partners).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often assume there is one way of speaking for everyone: they say *je* and *hoi* to every adult, or use playground words in a talk with a visitor. After being corrected, some treat informal words as wrong words, where the point is that they fit some situations and not others.
