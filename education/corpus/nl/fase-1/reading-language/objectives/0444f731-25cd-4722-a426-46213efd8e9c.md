## Official wording

experimenteren met het afstemmen van tekst op publiek (groep 1/2)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/SCH/02/04; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 0444f731-25cd-4722-a426-46213efd8e9c.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experimenting with fitting a text to an audience (*groep* 1/2, the first two years of primary school)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child makes a card for a baby brother with a big drawing and one word, and a card for grandma with more "writing" and her name on it. The child dictates an invitation for the parents and says what they need to know: which day and where. A child of 4 chooses a drawing or colours that the receiver will like; a child of 6 changes what is written depending on who will read it, and asks whether the reader can read it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the wording itself names the years, *groep* 1 and 2 (about ages 4 to 6). The open data ends the wording with "(groep 1/2)"; the content-line document prints the label "groep 1/2:" on a line above the statement instead. The verb is experimenting: trying out, with no result stated. The goal does not name an audience. It does not say whether the text is dictated, drawn or written by the child. For *groep* 3, adapting a text after reactions of classmates is worded in NE/SCH/02/04/02/fase1.

### Common mistakes

Assuming that the reader knows everything the writer knows. Making every text the same, whoever it is for. Thinking of what they like themselves and not of what the reader likes or needs.
