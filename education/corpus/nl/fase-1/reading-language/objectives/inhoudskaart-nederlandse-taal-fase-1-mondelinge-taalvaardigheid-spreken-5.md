## Official wording

gebruiken van gebaren en mimiek om boodschappen te ondersteunen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using gestures and facial expression to support messages

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shows with both hands how big the dog was, points to where it happened, and makes an angry face when telling what the giant said. The child nods or shakes the head with a yes or a no. A child of 4 gestures freely and sometimes in place of the word; a child of 6 uses a gesture together with the word and on purpose, to make a story clearer or more exciting.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names gestures and facial expression and gives their purpose: supporting messages. It does not mention posture, eye contact or voice. It names no particular gestures.

### Common mistakes

Using the gesture in place of the word ("it was like this"), so that a listener who is not looking misses the content. Telling with a face that does not match the content, for example smiling out of shyness while telling about something sad. Pointing towards something the listener cannot see.
