## Official wording

ervaren van verschillende emoties in een verhaal (bijv. verdriet, boosheid, blijdschap)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing different emotions in a story (e.g. sadness, anger, joy)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to a story and looks sad when a character loses something and glad at the end. The child points to a face in a picture and says "he is angry", and makes the face themselves. A child of 4 shows and names the feeling of the moment; a child of 6 says at which point in the story the feeling changed.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says experiencing. Sadness, anger and joy are given as examples of emotions; other emotions are not excluded and none is required. The statement does not ask the child to name the emotions.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often confuse: sad and angry, in faces and in words. Many take the feeling from the face in the picture alone and not from what happens in the story.
