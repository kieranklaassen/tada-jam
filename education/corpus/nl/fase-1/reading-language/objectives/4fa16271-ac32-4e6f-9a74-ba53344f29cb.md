## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The wording places this in *groep* 3, so the child is 6 or 7. The child tells about a school trip in the order it happened: the bus ride first, then the animals, then lunch, then the ride home. The child tells about fishing as a list, "I need three things: a rod, a float and bait", and counts them on the fingers. Before telling, the child lays picture cards in order or draws in three boxes to settle the order.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the wording itself names the year, *groep* 3 (about ages 6 to 7). The open data ends the wording with "(groep 3)"; the content-line document prints the label "groep 3:" on a line above the statement instead. The brackets name two structures: chronology and enumeration. The structure is called simple. "Text" here is a spoken text, since the open data lists the goal under speaking (NE/MT/03). The statement sets no length.

### Common mistakes

Telling the most exciting part first and then adding earlier parts out of order. Announcing an enumeration ("three things") and giving two or four, or leaving the list for a story about one item. Saying order words ("first", "then") while the events are not in that order.
