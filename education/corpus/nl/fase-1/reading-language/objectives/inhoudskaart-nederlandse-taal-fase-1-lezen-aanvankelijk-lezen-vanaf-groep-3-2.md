## Official wording

decoderen van klankzuivere woorden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

decoding sound-pure words (*klankzuivere woorden*, words spelled exactly as they sound)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 (about 6 or 7) reads a short word such as *vis* (fish) or *maan* (moon) by saying the sound of each letter or letter pair in order and joining the sounds. The child lays a word with letter cards, swaps one card for another, and reads the new word. The child reads rows of such words aloud to a classmate.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card prints this statement under a heading that says from *groep* 3 on (about ages 6 to 7). The statement is limited to sound-pure words: words in which each sound is written with its usual letter or letter pair. It names no letters, no word length, no word structure and no reading speed; *vis* and *maan* above, and *stop* under Common mistakes, are the pack's examples, not the card's. Words that are not sound-pure are named in `Aanvankelijk lezen (vanaf GROEP 3) / 3`.

### Common mistakes

Saying the separate sounds and then saying a different word. Mixing up letters that look alike, such as b and d. Leaving out a sound where two consonants stand next to each other, for example reading *stop* as *sop*.
