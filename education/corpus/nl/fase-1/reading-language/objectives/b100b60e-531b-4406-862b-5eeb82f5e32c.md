## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 puts a finger under the first letter of a short word, says the sound of each letter from left to right, "r-oo-s", and then says the whole word, "roos". The child does this many times with word cards, with letters laid in a row, and in the first reading books. With practice the child looks at a short word and says it at once, and sounds out only a new or long word.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the content-line document names the year in the heading of the cluster, "Technisch lezen (vanaf groep 3)" (technical reading, from *groep* 3), which within fase 1 is *groep* 3, about ages 6 to 7. The open data titles the cluster "Technisch lezen" and carries no year. The wording does not list the elementary reading acts. In Dutch reading teaching the term usually means looking at the letters of a word in order, saying the sound of each, and blending the sounds into the word; that reading is the pack's, not the goal's. The verb is practising. The goal names no letters or words and sets no speed. Which words are read is said in NE/LE/09/02/fase1 and NE/LE/09/03/fase1.

### Common mistakes

Saying each sound correctly and then not blending them, or saying a different word. Starting at the wrong end or changing the order of the sounds: "pen" read as "nep". Guessing the word from its first letter or from the picture next to it.
