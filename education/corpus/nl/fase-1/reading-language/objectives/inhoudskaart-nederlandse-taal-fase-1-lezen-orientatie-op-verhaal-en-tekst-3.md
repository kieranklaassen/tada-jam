## Official wording

kennismaken met poëzie, simpele rijmpjes, versjes en liedjes die vooral vormvast zijn (bijv. wiegeliedjes, slaapliedjes, dansliedjes, kringliedjes, aftelversjes, springliedjes, sinterklaasversjes, raadselrijmen, spotversjes)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know poetry, simple rhymes, verses and songs that mainly have a fixed form (e.g. cradle songs, lullabies, dance songs, circle songs, counting-out rhymes, skipping songs, Sinterklaas verses, riddle rhymes, teasing verses)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sings and says verses together with the group, makes the movements that go with them, and uses a counting-out rhyme to choose who starts. The child jumps or dances in time with a song. A child of 4 joins in with the movements and the last word of each line; a child of 6 says a whole verse from memory and notices when a word in it is changed.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says getting to know. It names poetry, simple rhymes, verses and songs, and says these mainly have a fixed form. The nine kinds in brackets are examples; Sinterklaas verses belong to the Dutch feast of Sinterklaas. The statement sets no number of verses and does not ask the child to make verses.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the meaning: they sing a line as a row of sounds and replace an unknown word with one that sounds like it. Many can say a verse with the group and cannot say it alone.
