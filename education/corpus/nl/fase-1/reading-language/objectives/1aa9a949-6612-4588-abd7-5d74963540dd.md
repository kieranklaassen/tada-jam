## Official wording

luisteren en begrijpen van gesproken tekst op radio, tv en internet (bijv. (educatieve) series of programma's, digitale prentenboeken en luisterboeken)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

listening to and understanding spoken text on radio, TV and the internet (e.g. (educational) series or programmes, digital picture books and audiobooks)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child watches an episode of an educational children's programme with the class and afterwards tells what the presenter made. The child listens through headphones to an audiobook or a digital picture book and turns to the matching page of the paper book. The child listens to a short radio item for children and answers a question about it. A child of 4 follows a short item with moving pictures and a clear voice and recalls one thing; a child of 7 follows a longer item, also one without pictures, and retells what it was about.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The media named are radio, TV and the internet. Series or programmes (with "educational" in brackets), digital picture books and audiobooks follow "e.g." and are examples. The source prints "listening and understanding of" without the word for "to" (*luisteren en begrijpen van*); the gloss reads it as listening to and understanding. The goal itself sets no length of text, no speaking rate and no amount of time with media. The content-line document prints text features for listening at fase 1 (kinds of text, a length for informative and for narrative texts, structure), which the lane's frame quotes; the two lengths are "informatieve teksten: enkele minuten" (informative texts: a few minutes) and "verhalende teksten: tot 10 minuten" (narrative texts: up to 10 minutes).

### Common mistakes

Watching the pictures without taking in the spoken words, so that the child can tell what was seen and not what was said. No longer following when the speaker is not in view or speaks faster than the teacher, since a recorded speaker cannot be asked to repeat. Remembering the tune or one funny moment and not what the item was about.
