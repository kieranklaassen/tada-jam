## Official wording

kennismaken met verschillende gesprekssoorten met bijbehorende doelen (bijv. verhaal vertellen om te amuseren, overleg hoe een conflict op te lossen of informatie uitwisselen over gebeurtenissen in een prentenboek)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know different kinds of conversation with the purposes that belong to them (e.g. telling a story to amuse, consultation (*overleg*, talking over what to do) on how to resolve a conflict, or exchanging information about events in a picture book)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to a funny story that a classmate tells and laughs, and at another time sits with the adult and one other child to talk about who may ride the bicycle first. After a picture book has been read aloud, the child tells what happened to the bear and hears what others remember. When the adult asks, the child says what the talk was for: to laugh, to settle a quarrel, or to find something out. A child of 4 takes part in each kind without naming it; a child of 7 can often say that a talk was meant to solve a problem or to tell something enjoyable.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "getting to know": the child meets different kinds of conversation, and the statement does not ask the child to name or sort them. The three kinds in brackets, each with its purpose, follow "e.g." and are examples. The statement gives no list of kinds of conversation and no number of kinds.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What children most often leave out is the purpose: they use a talk about settling a quarrel to tell their own side again, or they answer a question for information with an unrelated story of their own.
