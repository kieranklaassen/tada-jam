## Official wording

reflecteren in tekst besprekingen over wat de leerling heeft opgeschreven (over de vraag wat de leerling heeft bedoeld en de vraag waarom de lezer bepaalde dingen wel/niet kan begrijpen) (groep 3)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reflecting, in discussions of texts, on what the pupil has written down (on the question of what the pupil meant and the question of why the reader can or cannot understand certain things) (*groep* 3, the third year of primary school)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 reads their text to the teacher and a few classmates. They say what they understood, and the child says what was meant: "I meant that the dog ran away, not me". Together they find the place where the reader could not follow, for example a missing word or a "he" without a name.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the wording itself names the year, *groep* 3 (about ages 6 to 7). The open data ends the wording with "(groep 3)"; the content-line document prints the label "groep 3:" once, on a line above the statement before this one (NE/SCH/02/05/03/fase1), and this statement follows under the same label. The setting is named: discussions of texts. The brackets name two questions: what the pupil meant, and why the reader can or cannot understand certain things. The goal does not mention spelling, handwriting or rewriting; adapting a text after reactions is NE/SCH/02/04/02/fase1. The open data prints "tekst besprekingen" as two words; the content-line document prints it as one word, "tekstbesprekingen".

### Common mistakes

Explaining aloud what was meant and believing that the text therefore says it. Thinking that the reader did not understand because the reader did not pay attention. Talking about the event itself and not about the text.
