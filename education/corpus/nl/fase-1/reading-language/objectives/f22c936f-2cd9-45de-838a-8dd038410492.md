kennismaken met: - informatieve teksten (bijv. informatieve prentenboeken, nieuwsberichtjes op het prikbord, weerbericht op website); - instructieve teksten (bijv. pictogrammen voor dagelijkse handelingen, recept, bouwtekening); - betogende teksten (bijv. een oproep, reclame)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know: - informative texts (e.g. informative picture books, short news items on the noticeboard, weather report on a website); - instructive texts (e.g. pictograms for daily actions, recipe, building plan); - persuasive texts (e.g. an appeal, advertising)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks through an information picture book about the farm, follows a recipe with pictures step by step while baking, and builds from a drawing that shows which block goes where. The child looks at the pictograms that show the steps of washing hands, and at a poster that asks everyone to come to the school fair. A child of 4 uses the pictures and pictograms and hears the text read aloud; a child of 7 reads short pieces of such texts alone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three kinds of non-fiction text are named: informative, instructive and persuasive (*betogende*, texts that argue for something). Everything in brackets is an example, introduced by "bijv.". The verb is getting to know only: the goal does not say that the child reads these texts alone, and some of the examples (pictograms, a building plan) are images, not written words. It sets no length or difficulty.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often do the steps of an instruction in the order they prefer and not in the order shown. Young children take an advertisement for plain information and do not see that it wants them to buy or do something.
