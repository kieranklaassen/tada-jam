## Official wording

luisteren en begrijpen van gesproken tekst op radio, tv en internet (bijv. (educatieve) series of programma's, digitale prentenboeken en luisterboeken)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

listening to and understanding spoken text on radio, TV and the internet (e.g. (educational) series or programmes, digital picture books and audiobooks)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child watches an episode of a school television series for young children and afterwards tells what the episode was about. The child listens to an audiobook with headphones in the reading corner, or follows a digital picture book in which a voice reads the text. A child of 4 follows a short programme with strong support from pictures; a child of 6 follows a story from sound alone and tells what happened.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names three media: radio, TV and the internet. Series or programmes (with "educational" in brackets), digital picture books and audiobooks are examples. The source prints "luisteren en begrijpen van" here, without the word *naar* (to) that the neighbouring statements have; the gloss gives what is plainly meant. The statement sets no length of listening or viewing and does not say how often.

### Common mistakes

Following the moving pictures and taking in little of what is said. Not being able to ask a recorded voice a question, and so no longer following after one unknown word. Recalling a striking detail (a sound, a joke) and not what the programme was about.
