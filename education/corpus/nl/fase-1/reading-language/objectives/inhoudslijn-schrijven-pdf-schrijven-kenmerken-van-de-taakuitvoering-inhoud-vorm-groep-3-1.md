## Official wording

schrijven korte teksten met meestal één inhoudselement, vaak met een chronologische opbouw

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudslijn po - Nederlands: Schrijven, page 2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing short texts with mostly one content element, often with a chronological structure

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 writes three or four sentences about one thing that happened, such as a visit to the swimming pool. The sentences follow the order of the event: getting changed, jumping in, going home. The child reads the text aloud to hear whether the order is right.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the document itself names the year, *groep* 3 (about ages 6 to 7). The content-line document prints the goal under the label "groep 3:"; the open data lacks this sub-cluster of writing ("inhoud/vorm", content/form), so the record is read from that document. The texts are short, with "meestal" (mostly) one content element and "vaak" (often) a chronological structure: neither is set as a rule. "Short" is not given a number of sentences or words; the three or four sentences above are the pack's illustration. The wording does not say what a content element (*inhoudselement*) is; NE/MT/02/07/01/fase1, a listening goal of this lane, gives an event as an example of one. The source prints the verb in front of its object (*schrijven korte teksten*), the word order of a sentence whose subject is left out; the gloss reads it in the form of the lane's other goals. Sentences are the subject of the second goal under the same label.

### Common mistakes

Writing the events in the order in which they come to mind, not the order in which they happened. Starting on a second subject halfway through the text. Stopping after the first event, so that the text has a beginning and no end.
