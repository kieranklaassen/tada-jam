## Official wording

onderscheiden van de hoofdcomponenten van een verhaal (wie, wat, waar, wanneer)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing the main components of a story (who, what, where, when)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story has been read aloud, the child points in the pictures to who the story is about and where it happens, and says what happened. The child lays picture cards of the characters, the place and the event next to the book and says which is which. A child of 4 names who is in the story and one thing that happened; a child of 7 also says where and when it happened, for a story read aloud or one read alone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name four components: who, what, where and when. The goal does not mention why or how, and it does not say whether the story is read aloud or read by the child. It names no kind of story and no length.

### Common mistakes

Answering "what" with a detail remembered from one picture and not with the main event. Mixing up where and when, or giving no answer for when because stories for young children often leave the time unstated. Naming a favourite character and not the one the story is about.
