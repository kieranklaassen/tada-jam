## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing fantasy stories from other, realistic stories

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After hearing two stories, the child says which one could happen in real life and which could not, and points to what shows it: an animal that talks, a child that flies, a witch. The child sorts story books into "could happen" and "could not happen". A child of 4 often still believes that giants or talking animals may exist; a child of 7 names the things in the story that cannot happen.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is about two kinds of story: fantasy stories and realistic stories. The source prints "andere realistische verhalen" ("other realistic stories") without a comma, which read literally would count fantasy stories among the realistic ones; the gloss reads it as "other stories, the realistic ones". The goal names no signs of a fantasy story and does not ask for the terms. Fairy tales have their own goal, NE/LE/07/01/04/fase1.

### Common mistakes

Believing that fantasy figures are real, so the two kinds are not told apart at all. Calling a realistic story "real" in the sense that it truly happened. Judging by the pictures alone: a story with drawn animals is called fantasy even when the animals behave as animals do.
