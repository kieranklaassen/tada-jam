staying with the topic of the conversation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The class talks about a visit to a farm, and the child says something about the cows, the smell or the tractor. When another child tells about feeding a lamb, the child asks or adds something about the lamb. When a thought about something else occurs to the child, the child keeps it until the talk about the farm is over. A child of 4 stays with the topic for one or two turns, helped by an object or picture in the middle of the circle; a child of 7 stays with it through a longer talk and notices when someone else changes the subject.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is one short phrase and states no length of conversation, no number of turns and no kind of topic. It does not mention help or supporting material; NE/MT/03/02/01/fase1, the goal about staying with a chosen topic when speaking, does name supporting material as an example.

### Common mistakes

Hearing one word in what was said and starting a story of one's own that is linked only to that word (the talk is about the farm, a classmate mentions a dog, and the child tells about a grandmother's dog). Saying something that was prepared beforehand although the talk has moved to another point. Changing the subject when the topic is unfamiliar and the child lacks the words for it.
