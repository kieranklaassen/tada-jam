## Official wording

onder woorden brengen van gedachten, gevoelens en meningen (mondeling of schriftelijk)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

putting thoughts, feelings and opinions into words (orally or in writing)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells about a drawing they made: what it shows, how they felt, what they think of it; the teacher writes the sentence under it, or the child writes it. The child writes or dictates a card: "ik vind je lief" (I think you are sweet). A child of 4 tells it aloud and has it written down; a child of 7 writes one or two sentences alone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three things are put into words: thoughts, feelings and opinions. The brackets allow either way, orally or in writing, so the wording covers a child who says it without writing, although the goal stands in the writing line. It sets no length and names no kind of text.

### Common mistakes

Naming what can be seen ("this is a house") and leaving out the thought or feeling. In writing, putting down a few words and assuming the reader knows the rest. Writing much less than they can tell, because forming the letters takes all their attention.
