## Official wording

herkennen van lettergrepen in geschreven woorden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising syllables in written words

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 (about 6 or 7) looks at a longer written word, draws a small curve under each syllable and reads the parts one after another, then the whole word. The child cuts a word card into its syllables and puts the pieces back in order. The child claps once for each part while reading it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card prints this statement under a heading that says from *groep* 3 on (about ages 6 to 7). The wording says recognising, and it is about written words; hearing syllables in spoken words is not named in this statement. It sets no number of syllables and gives no rule for where a word is divided; *oe* and *ka-tten* under Common mistakes are the pack's examples.

### Common mistakes

Dividing the word after a fixed number of letters, so that a letter pair such as *oe* is cut in two. Reading each syllable correctly with a pause after each, and then not recognising the whole word. Putting both of two middle consonants in the same part, for example *ka-tten*.
