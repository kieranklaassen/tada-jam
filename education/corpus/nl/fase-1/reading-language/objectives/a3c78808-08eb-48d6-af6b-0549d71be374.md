## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a picture book about a bear who cannot sleep has been read aloud, the adult asks "What was this book about?", and the child says "about a bear who cannot sleep". After a short spoken item about hedgehogs the child says "it was about hedgehogs". The child picks, from three objects or pictures, the one that fits what the text was about, and says its name. A child of 4 names the main character or the animal; a child of 7 says the topic in a short sentence and, for a story, what the problem was.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement asks for naming the theme or topic; the source prints the two words with a slash and does not say how they differ. "Text" here is a text that is listened to, since the open data lists the goal under listening (NE/MT/02). The goal itself sets no kind or length of text and does not mention help. The content-line document prints text features for listening at fase 1 (kinds of text, a length for informative and for narrative texts, structure), which the lane's frame quotes; the two lengths are "informatieve teksten: enkele minuten" (informative texts: a few minutes) and "verhalende teksten: tot 10 minuten" (narrative texts: up to 10 minutes).

### Common mistakes

Naming one striking detail or the last event instead of what the whole text was about ("about a cake", when the story is about a birthday on which everything goes wrong). Retelling the whole story when asked what it was about. Naming something from the pictures that the text did not deal with.
