## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stops at something in language and thinks about it aloud: "why is it called a butterfly?", "that is not a real word", "at home we say it another way". The child notices that a sentence sounds wrong and tries to say it right, and that one word can mean two things. A child of 4 laughs at or corrects a word that sounds wrong; a child of 6 says what is different between two words or two sentences.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is three words. It names no part of language to reflect on (sounds, words, sentences or use), no terms and no method, and it does not mention help. This statement stands directly under the heading Taalbeschouwing (reflection on language), which on the card has three statements and no sub-headings.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often attend to the meaning and not to the form: they call a sentence "wrong" because what it says is untrue, not because it is badly formed. Many take the name of a thing as part of the thing and think it could not be called anything else.
