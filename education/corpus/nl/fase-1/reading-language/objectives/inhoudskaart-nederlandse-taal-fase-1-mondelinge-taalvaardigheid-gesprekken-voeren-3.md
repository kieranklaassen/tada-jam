## Official wording

kennismaken met verschillende gesprekssoorten (dialoog, discussie, kringgesprek)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know different kinds of conversation (dialogue, discussion, circle conversation (*kringgesprek*, the class talking together seated in a circle))

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sits in the circle with the whole class and waits while others speak, talks with one partner across a table, and joins a short talk in which children give different views on where to build the hut. A child of 4 takes part in each as it comes; a child of 6 notices while taking part that the wait for a turn is longer in the circle than with one partner.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is getting to know: the wording is about meeting these kinds of conversation, not about naming them or telling them apart. The brackets name dialogue, discussion and the circle conversation. The statement does not say how often or how long.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often behave in a discussion as in a circle round: each tells something of their own without responding to the view given before theirs. In a dialogue many leave the partner no turn.
