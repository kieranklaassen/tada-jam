## Official wording

(met hulp) reflecteren op eigen bijdrage aan het gesprek

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/01/08; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau ac8fadcf-607f-48bc-9197-73dba1fdf4e2.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) reflecting on one's own contribution to the conversation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After the circle talk the adult asks "Did you tell something? Did you listen to someone?", and the child answers and names one thing. The child points to a picture card of a mouth or of an ear to show what went well: talking or listening. A child of 4 answers yes or no and recalls one thing after a prompt; a child of 7 says what they added to the talk, whether they waited for their turn, and one thing to do differently next time.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it after the verb. The statement does not say who helps or how. The reflection is on the child's own contribution, not on that of other children or on the conversation as a whole. The statement names no points to reflect on and no form (talking, pointing, drawing).

### Common mistakes

Answering "good" to every question about how it went, without an example. Telling the content of the talk again instead of saying something about one's own part in it. Talking about what another child did wrong.
