## Official wording

(met hulp) bewust worden van aspecten van het eigen leesleerproces (groep 3)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) becoming aware of aspects of one's own process of learning to read (*groep* 3, the third year of primary school)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With the teacher, a child in *groep* 3 looks at a book read at the start of the year and one read now, and says what has changed: "I know all the letters now", "long words are still hard". The child says what they do when they cannot read a word, and what they want to practise next.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the wording itself names the year, *groep* 3 (about ages 6 to 7). The open data ends the wording with "(groep 3)"; the content-line document prints no such tag and carries the year in the heading of the cluster, "Zakelijke teksten: aanpak (vanaf groep 3)" (from *groep* 3). The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it at the end of the sentence. The wording does not say which aspects are meant. Its cluster is about the approach to non-fiction texts. It asks for no record, mark or level.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often judge their reading by speed alone or by comparing themselves with a classmate. Many say only "good" or "bad" and cannot name what goes well or what is hard unless a question points to it.
