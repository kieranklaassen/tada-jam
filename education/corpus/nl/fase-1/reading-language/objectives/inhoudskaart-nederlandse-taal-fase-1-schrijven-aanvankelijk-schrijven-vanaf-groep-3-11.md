## Official wording

reflecteren in tekstbesprekingen over wat de leerling heeft opgeschreven (Wat heeft de leerling bedoeld? Waarom kan de lezer bepaalde dingen wel/niet begrijpen?)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reflecting in text discussions on what the pupil has written down (What did the pupil mean? Why can the reader understand, or not understand, certain things?)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a small group a child in *groep* 3 (about 6 or 7) reads their text aloud. The others say what they understood and ask about what they did not understand, and the writer says what they meant. Together they point to the place in the text where it does not yet say that.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card prints this statement under a heading that says from *groep* 3 on (about ages 6 to 7). The statement names reflecting in text discussions on what the pupil has written. The brackets hold two questions: what the pupil meant, and why the reader can or cannot understand certain things. Spelling and handwriting are not named. This statement does not say "with help".

### Common mistakes

Explaining aloud what was meant and believing that the text says it. Saying only "nice" about a classmate's text. Talking about the handwriting or the drawing where the content is the question.
