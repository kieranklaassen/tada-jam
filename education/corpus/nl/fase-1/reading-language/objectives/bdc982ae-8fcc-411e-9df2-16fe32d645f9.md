## Official wording

(met hulp) kiezen en productief gebruiken van (nieuwe) woorden tijdens gesprekken

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) choosing and productively using (new) words during conversations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a walk the class talks about what was seen, and the child says "puddle", "branch" and the newly learned "moss" while holding a piece of moss. When the child says "that thing", the adult offers "the watering can", and the child uses it in the next sentence. A child of 4 uses a new word when holding the object and just after the adult has said it; a child of 7 picks the more exact word unprompted ("crawl" instead of "go") and uses a word learned days earlier.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement begins with "with help" in brackets and does not say who helps or how. "Productively" means the child says the words, as distinct from only understanding them. "New" stands in brackets, so the words may be new ones or ones the child already knows. The statement is about conversations; NE/MT/03/05/01/fase1 and NE/MT/03/05/02/fase1 are the goals about words in speaking. It names no words, no themes and no number of words.

### Common mistakes

Using a general word or pointing ("that", "thing", "do") where a specific word is known. Using a new word for too many things (calling every tool a "hammer") or for too few (only the one watering can in the classroom). Repeating a new word right after the adult and not using it again later.
