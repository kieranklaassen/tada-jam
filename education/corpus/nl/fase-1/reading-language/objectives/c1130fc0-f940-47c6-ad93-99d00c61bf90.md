## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

staying with the chosen topic (e.g. with the help of supporting material)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells the class about a pet rabbit and holds a photo of it; after each look at the photo, the next sentence is again about the rabbit. The child lays three objects or pictures in a row and tells something about each in turn. When a thought about something else occurs to the child, the child looks at the material again and continues with the topic. A child of 4 says a few sentences about the thing they are holding; a child of 7 tells a longer piece about one topic with two or three pictures as support and returns to the topic after a listener's question.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The topic is a chosen one; the statement does not say who chooses it. Supporting material follows "e.g." and is an example of what helps; the statement does not say what the material is. It sets no length of telling. NE/MT/01/03/02/fase1 is the matching goal for conversations.

### Common mistakes

Moving from the topic to a linked memory and not returning (from the rabbit to the pet shop to an ice cream eaten that day). Describing the supporting material itself (the photo and its colours) instead of telling about the topic. Stopping after one sentence because the child can think of nothing more about the topic.
