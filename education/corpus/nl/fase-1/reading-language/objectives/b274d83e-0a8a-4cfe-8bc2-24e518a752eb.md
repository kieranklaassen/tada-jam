## Official wording

(met hulp) signaleren wanneer regels voor beurtwisseling overtreden worden

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) *signaleren* (noticing, or pointing out) when rules for turn-taking are broken

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the circle one child starts to talk while another is still speaking, and the adult asks "What happened just now?"; the child says that two were talking at the same time. The child raises a hand to show that someone has been skipped or that one child has had many turns. A child of 4 notices it mostly when the lost turn is their own and says so after the adult's prompt; a child of 7 notices it for others too and says it calmly, for example "Sam had not finished yet".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it after the verb. The statement does not say who helps or how. *Signaleren* can mean noticing or pointing out; the wording does not choose. The statement is about the rules for turn-taking only, not other conversation rules, and it does not ask the child to correct the other child.

### Common mistakes

Noticing a broken rule only when the child's own turn is lost, and not when the child interrupts someone else. Pointing it out by interrupting, which breaks the same rule. Reporting every small overlap, including a short friendly reaction such as "yes" or a laugh, as a broken rule.
