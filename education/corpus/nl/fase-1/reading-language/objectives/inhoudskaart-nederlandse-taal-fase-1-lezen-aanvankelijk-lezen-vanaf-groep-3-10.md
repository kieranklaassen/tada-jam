## Official wording

(met hulp) bewust worden van aspecten van het eigen leesleerproces

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) becoming aware of aspects of one's own process of learning to read

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With the teacher, a child in *groep* 3 (about 6 or 7) looks back on their reading: which words went easily, which were hard, and what they did when they could not read a word. The child says what they can read now that they could not read some weeks ago. The child picks a book and tries a page to see whether they can read it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card prints this statement under a heading that says from *groep* 3 on (about ages 6 to 7). The brackets at the start say that the child does this with help. It says becoming aware, and it does not say which aspects of learning to read are meant. It does not ask the child to plan or steer their own learning.

### Common mistakes

Judging their own reading only by speed or by the number of pages. Saying that everything went well, or that everything went badly, without naming a word or a moment. Comparing themselves with a classmate where their own earlier reading is the question.
