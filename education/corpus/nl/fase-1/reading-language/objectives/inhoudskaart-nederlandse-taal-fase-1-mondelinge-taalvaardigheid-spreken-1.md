## Official wording

vertellen voor vertrouwd publiek: - uitleg geven (bijv. bij een tekening of ander materiaal) - vertellen of navertellen van een verhaal - vertellen over een gebeurtenis of eigen ervaring (bijv. na het spelen van een spel)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

telling in front of a familiar audience: - giving an explanation (e.g. with a drawing or other material) - telling or retelling a story - telling about an event or one's own experience (e.g. after playing a game)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds up a drawing in the circle and explains what is on it and how it was made. The child retells a picture-book story to a few classmates with the book on their lap, or tells the class what happened in the game they just played outside. A child of 4 tells about the object in their hand and goes on when someone asks a question; a child of 6 tells a short story with a beginning and an end without questions in between.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The audience is a familiar one; an unfamiliar audience is not mentioned. The wording names three kinds of telling: giving an explanation, telling or retelling a story, and telling about an event or an own experience. A drawing or other material, and the moment after playing a game, are examples. The statement sets no length and no size of audience.

### Common mistakes

Leaving out who, where and when, because the child assumes that the listeners were there. Speaking to the teacher only, too softly for the group to hear. Listing loose events joined by "and then", with no ending.
