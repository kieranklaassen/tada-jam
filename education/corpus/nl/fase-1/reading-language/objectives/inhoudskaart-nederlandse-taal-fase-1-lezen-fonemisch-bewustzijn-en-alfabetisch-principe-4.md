## Official wording

ontdekken dat letters met klanken corresponderen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering that letters correspond to sounds

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child points to a letter in their own name and says its sound, finds the same letter on a label or a book cover, and hears that it stands for the same sound there. With magnetic letters the child lays the first letter of a word they say aloud. A child of 4 links a letter from their own name to its sound; a child of 6 says the sound of a letter on a classmate's label and uses a letter to write the first sound of a word.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says discovering that letters correspond to sounds. It sets no number of letters and does not say which letters, whether capitals or small letters, or in which order. It does not mention letter combinations, letter names or the reading of words; the letters under Common mistakes are the pack's examples.

### Common mistakes

Giving the name of the letter ("em") in place of its sound ("mmm"). Mixing up letters that look alike, such as b, d and p. Thinking that a letter belongs to one person or one word only, such as "my letter".
