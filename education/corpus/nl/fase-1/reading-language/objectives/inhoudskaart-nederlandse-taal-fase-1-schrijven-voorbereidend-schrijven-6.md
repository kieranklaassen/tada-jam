## Official wording

vertellen wat gebruikte tekens in het eigen schrift betekenen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

telling what the signs used in one's own *schrift* (writing, or exercise book) mean

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shows an adult a sheet with drawings, scribbles and letters and says what it says, pointing to the signs while telling. A child of 4 tells a message with their scribbles and may tell another message later; a child of 6 points to separate letters or words and gives the same message when asked again.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names telling what the signs that were used mean. *Schrift* can be read as the child's own writing or as the child's own exercise book, and the wording does not choose. It does not say that the signs are letters, and it does not ask that another person can read them.

### Common mistakes

Telling a different message at each asking. Describing the drawing and not what the signs say. Asking the adult to read the scribbles, in the belief that an adult can read all writing.
