distinguishing fiction from non-fiction

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts a pile of books into two piles: story books that are made up, and books that tell how real things are, such as a picture book about a bear who goes to school and a book with photos of bears. The child says why a book goes on a pile: "bears do not really wear clothes", "this tells what bears eat". A child of 4 sorts by clear signs such as photos or talking animals; a child of 7 can also say that a made-up story may hold real things, and that an information book is meant to teach something.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names only the two categories, fiction and non-fiction, and no text types within them. It does not say by which signs the child tells them apart, and it does not ask for the words "fiction" and "non-fiction". It does not say whether the texts are read aloud or read by the child. Telling fantasy stories from realistic stories is a separate goal, NE/LE/07/01/03/fase1.

### Common mistakes

Treating every book with drawings as made up and every book with photos as real. Calling a realistic story non-fiction because "it could happen". Believing that everything printed in a book is true.
