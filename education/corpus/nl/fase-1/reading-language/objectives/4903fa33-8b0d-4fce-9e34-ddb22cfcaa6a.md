## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

regularly using gestures and facial expression to support contributions to a conversation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While telling how big the dog was, the child spreads both arms; while saying "no", the child shakes the head. The child points to the place in the room that the sentence is about and shows with the face that something was frightening or funny. A child of 4 often lets the gesture replace the word, pointing and saying "that one"; a child of 7 says the words and adds the gesture, such as showing a size or a direction with the hands.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Gestures and facial expression are named, and their role is to support what the child contributes to a conversation, not to replace it. "Regularly" is not given a number. The source prints the verb in front of "regularly" (*gebruiken regelmatig*), the word order of a sentence whose subject is left out, unlike the neighbouring goals; the gloss reads it in the same form as theirs. NE/MT/03/07/01/fase1 is the matching goal for speaking.

### Common mistakes

Pointing or acting something out instead of saying the word, so that a listener who is not looking misses it. Keeping the face and body still when unsure or shy, so that the listener gets no extra cue. Making large gestures that have nothing to do with what is said.
