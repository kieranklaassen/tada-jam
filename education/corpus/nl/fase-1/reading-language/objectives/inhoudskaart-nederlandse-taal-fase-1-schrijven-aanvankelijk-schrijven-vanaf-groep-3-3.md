## Official wording

ervaring opdoen met de schrijfdoelen 'een ander informeren' en 'een ander plezier verschaffen'

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with the writing purposes 'informing someone else' and 'giving someone else pleasure'

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 (about 6 or 7) writes a note that tells a parent when the class outing is and what to bring. The child writes a short funny story or a card to make a classmate or a grandparent glad, and gives it to that person.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card prints this statement under a heading that says from *groep* 3 on (about ages 6 to 7). The wording says gaining experience. It names two writing purposes, in quotation marks: informing someone else and giving someone else pleasure. Other purposes, such as persuading, are not named in this statement. It sets no length or kind of text.

### Common mistakes

Leaving out what the reader needs to know, such as who, when or where, because the writer knows it. Writing the same kind of text whatever the purpose. Not thinking about who will read the text.
