## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 writes short words as they sound, with one letter or fixed letter pair for each sound, and meets the first groups of words that share a spelling, for example words with "oe" or words that end in "ng". The child sorts word cards by such a group and writes a new word of the same group. With practice the child writes words that are spelled as they sound and a few groups of words with a fixed letter combination.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the goal itself names no year, and the content-line document prints the spelling categories and rules under it per pair of *groepen*, each offered from the pair it stands under: the column *groep* 1-2 is empty and the lists start at *groep* 3-4, which within fase 1 is *groep* 3, about ages 6 to 7. The lists are not in the open data and are no records; the lane's frame quotes the lists for *groep* 3-4. The goal itself names no spelling categories and no rules, and sets no number; the groups above are the pack's examples. A spelling category is a group of words that share a spelling feature; the wording uses the term without explaining it. Two verbs are used: getting to know and using. The goal itself does not mention punctuation or capital letters; the document's lists for *groep* 3-4 have a row for them.

### Common mistakes

Writing a word as it sounds where the spelling differs: "hont" for "hond". Leaving out a sound where two consonants stand together: "sap" for "stap". Writing one letter for a sound that takes two: "bok" for "boek".
