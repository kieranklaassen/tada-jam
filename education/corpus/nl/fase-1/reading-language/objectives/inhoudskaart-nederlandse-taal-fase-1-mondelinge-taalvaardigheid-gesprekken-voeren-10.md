## Official wording

(met hulp) reflecteren op eigen bijdrage aan het gesprek

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) reflecting on one's own contribution to the conversation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After the circle talk the child answers a question from the teacher such as "did you wait for your turn?" or "did you tell about the subject?". The child shows with a thumb up or down, or says in a few words, how the talking went for them, and names one thing to do next time. A child of 4 answers a concrete question with a thumb up or down; a child of 6 tells what went well and what did not, with an example.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording puts "with help" in brackets: the reflecting is done with support. It is about the child's own contribution, not about the conversation as a whole or about what other children contributed. The statement names no criteria for a good contribution.

### Common mistakes

Answering what the child thinks the adult wants to hear ("I listened well"), whatever happened. Reporting on another child's behaviour instead of their own. Judging the talk by whether it was fun, not by what they contributed.
