## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before reading aloud, the adult says on one day "Listen so that you can tell me afterwards where the mouse lives" and on another day "Listen and enjoy this story". The child hears that the first asks for remembering something and the second does not. Before the explanation of a craft task the child says, together with the adult, "Now I listen to know what to do". A child of 4 experiences the different kinds of listening without naming them; a child of 7 can say why they are listening: to enjoy, to find something out, or to know what to do.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is "becoming aware": the statement does not ask the child to choose a purpose or to listen in a different way; NE/MT/02/04/02/fase1 is the goal about matching the way of listening to the purpose. It names no listening purposes and gives no examples.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often think that listening is one thing, namely sitting still and being quiet, and answer "to be quiet" when asked why they listen. Many do not yet see a difference between listening to a story for enjoyment and listening to remember something.
