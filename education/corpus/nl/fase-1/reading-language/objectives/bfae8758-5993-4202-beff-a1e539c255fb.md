## Official wording

ervaring opdoen met de schrijfdoelen plezier verschaffen en informeren

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/SCH/02/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau bfae8758-5993-4202-beff-a1e539c255fb.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

gaining experience with the writing purposes of giving pleasure and informing

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

To give pleasure, the child makes a card, a small story or a rhyme for someone and sees them smile when it is read. To inform, the child writes a label for the nature table, a note that says who is the helper today, or a message for home about the school trip. A child of 4 dictates the text or writes a few letters with a drawing; a child of 7 writes a sentence or two alone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two writing purposes are named: giving pleasure and informing. Other purposes, such as instructing or persuading, are not named in this goal. The wording is "gaining experience": it does not ask the child to name the purpose. It names no kinds of text; kinds are given as examples in NE/SCH/02/02/01/fase1.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. In a text meant to inform, children most often leave out what the reader needs to know, such as who, when or where. A child may write the same kind of text, for example a card with "ik hou van jou" (I love you), for every purpose.
