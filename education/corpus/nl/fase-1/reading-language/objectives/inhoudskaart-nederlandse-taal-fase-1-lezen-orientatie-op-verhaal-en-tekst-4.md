## Official wording

kennismaken met informatieve teksten (bijv. informatieve prentenboeken, nieuwsberichtjes op het prikbord, weerbericht op website)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know informative texts (e.g. informative picture books, short news items on the notice board, a weather report on a website)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks through an informative picture book about animals with an adult and asks what the text beside a photo says. The child looks at a news item on the notice board about the class outing, and looks at the weather report with an adult before going outside. A child of 4 looks at the photos and names what they see; a child of 6 asks a question and looks with an adult for the page that answers it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says getting to know: a first meeting, not reading the texts alone. The three kinds in brackets are examples; one of them is on a website. The statement does not ask that the child can read the text.

### Common mistakes

Expecting a story in an information book and asking what happens next. Looking only at the pictures and not asking what the text says. Not telling apart a book that gives facts and a book that tells a made-up story.
