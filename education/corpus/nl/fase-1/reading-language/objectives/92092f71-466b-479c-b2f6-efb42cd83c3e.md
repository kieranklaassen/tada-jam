## Official wording

deelnemen aan geplande en ongeplande gesprekken: - om informatie uit te wisselen (bijv. over gebeurtenissen, n.a.v. prentenboeken, of om elkaar te helpen); - voor discussie en overleg (bijv. over spel, ervaringen, activiteiten)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking part in planned and unplanned conversations: - to exchange information (e.g. about events, prompted by picture books, or to help each other); - for discussion and consultation (*overleg*, talking over what to do) (e.g. about play, experiences, activities)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sits in the circle with the class, tells what happened at the weekend and listens to what another child tells. With a picture book open, the child points at a page, says what the animal is doing and answers a question about it. In the building corner the child talks with two others about who builds which part of the tower. A child of 4 says one or two short sentences and talks mostly with the adult; a child of 7 continues a conversation with other children for several turns and gives a reason for a view.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two purposes are named: exchanging information, and discussion and *overleg*. What follows "e.g." in each pair of brackets is an example: events, picture books and helping each other for the first purpose; play, experiences and activities for the second. The statement covers planned and unplanned conversations alike, and sets no group size, no length and no number of turns.

### Common mistakes

Telling a story of one's own without responding to what the other child just said. Answering a question with one word or a nod and then stopping. When talking over what to do, repeating one's own wish more loudly instead of giving a reason or proposing something else.
