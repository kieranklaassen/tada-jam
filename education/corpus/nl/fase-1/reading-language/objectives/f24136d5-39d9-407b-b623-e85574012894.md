## Official wording

(met hulp) vormen van eigen mening over (een inhoudselement van) een tekst

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/02/08; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau f24136d5-39d9-407b-b623-e85574012894.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) forming an opinion of one's own about (an element of the content of) a text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story the adult asks "What did you think of the part where the boy hid the key?", and the child says "I thought it was mean, because his sister was looking for it". The child puts a card with a smiling, neutral or frowning face next to the book and says why. The child hears that a classmate thinks differently and keeps or changes their own view. A child of 4 says "nice" or "not nice", often about one picture or moment; a child of 7 gives an opinion with a reason taken from the text.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement begins with "with help" in brackets and does not say who helps or how. The opinion is the child's own, and the brackets allow it to be about one element of the content or about the text as a whole. The text is one that is listened to, since the open data lists the goal under listening (NE/MT/02). The statement does not ask for a reason or for comparing opinions.

### Common mistakes

Repeating the opinion of the adult or of the child who spoke first. Retelling the part instead of saying what one thinks of it. Giving a reason that has nothing to do with the text ("nice, because I like books").
