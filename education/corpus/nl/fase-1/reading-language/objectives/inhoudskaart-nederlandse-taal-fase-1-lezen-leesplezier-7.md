## Official wording

zich inleven in alledaagse onderwerpen (aansluiten bij de leefwereld en bij onderwerpen die verder afstaan van het kind)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

imagining oneself into everyday topics (connecting with the child's own world (*leefwereld*) and with topics that are further from the child)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to a book about a visit to the dentist or a new baby and tells about their own visit or their own family. With a book about a child in another country or about a job they do not know, the child asks questions and plays the situation in the home corner. A child of 4 links the book to something that happened to them; a child of 6 says what is the same and what is different between their own life and the life in the book.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names everyday topics, and the brackets name two things to connect with: the child's own world and topics that are further from the child. It names no topics; the ones above are the pack's examples. It names no kind of text; the card prints it under reading enjoyment (*Leesplezier*).

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the book: they start telling about themselves and say nothing more about the topic of the book. With a topic far from their own life, many assume that everything is as it is at their home.
