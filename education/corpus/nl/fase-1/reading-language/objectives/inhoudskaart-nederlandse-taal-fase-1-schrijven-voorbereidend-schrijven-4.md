## Official wording

schrijven met eigen grafische middelen (tekeningen, picto's, krabbels, symbolen) (bijv. tekenen, krabbelen, letterachtige vormen, letter-/tekenreeksen, spontane spelling , ook naschrijven, nastempelen, typen op de computer)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing with one's own graphic means (drawings, pictograms, scribbles, symbols) (e.g. drawing, scribbling, letter-like shapes, strings of letters/signs, spontaneous spelling (*spontane spelling*, the child's own invented spelling), also copying writing, copying with stamps, typing on the computer)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts a message on paper in their own way: a drawing with scribbles under it, a row of letter-like shapes, a string of letters they know, or a word written by its sounds, such as *fis* for *vis* (fish). The child also copies a word from a card, stamps it with letter stamps or types it. A child of 4 draws and scribbles and says what it says; a child of 6 writes strings of letters and words spelled by their sounds.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The first brackets name the means: drawings, pictograms, scribbles and symbols. The second brackets give examples: drawing, scribbling, letter-like shapes, strings of letters or signs and spontaneous spelling, and after "also" copying writing, copying with stamps and typing on the computer; the source prints a space before the comma in front of "also". Correct spelling is not named. The statement names no letters and no words; *fis* above is the pack's example.

### Common mistakes

Writing letters in any order to fill the line, with no link to the sounds of the word. Writing only the sounds that are heard most clearly, often the first sound and the consonants. Writing from right to left, or with mirrored letters.
