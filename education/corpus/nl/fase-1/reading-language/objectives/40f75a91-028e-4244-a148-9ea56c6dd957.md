## Official wording

vloeiend en verstaanbaar spreken voor bekenden en eenvoudige intonatie gebruiken (bijv. vraagzin) waarbij pauzes, aarzelingen en herformuleringen mogen voorkomen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

speaking fluently and intelligibly for familiar people and using simple intonation (e.g. a question sentence), in which pauses, hesitations and rephrasings may occur

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells the class about a new baby brother in sentences that follow one another, loudly enough for the far side of the circle. The child's voice rises at the end of "Do you want to see the photo?" and falls at the end of a telling sentence. Halfway through a sentence the child stops, says "uh" and begins it again in another way. A child of 4 speaks in short sentences with many new starts and is understood by people who know the child; a child of 7 says several sentences in a row at an even pace and lets the voice show a question or surprise.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The listeners are familiar people. The intonation is called simple, and the question sentence follows "e.g." and is an example. The statement itself says that pauses, hesitations and rephrasings may occur, so "fluently" does not mean without them. NE/MT/01/06/01/fase1 is the goal about pronouncing sounds intelligibly in conversations. The statement does not mention grammar, although the title of its sub-cluster in the open data names grammatical control (*grammaticale beheersing*).

### Common mistakes

Speaking too softly, or toward the object being held, so that the group cannot hear. Speaking in one flat tone, or letting the voice rise at the end of every sentence, so that a listener cannot hear which sentence is a question. Speaking very fast when excited, so that words are not kept apart.
