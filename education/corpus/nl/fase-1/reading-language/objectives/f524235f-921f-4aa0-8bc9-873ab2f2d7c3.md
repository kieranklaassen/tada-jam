(with help) productively using offered words during conversations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The adult has introduced "sink" and "float" at the water table; later the adult asks "What did the cork do?", and the child answers "It floated". The child talks with a classmate about the test and uses both words, and the adult says a word once more when the child hesitates. At the theme table the child picks up an object and says a sentence with its new name. A child of 4 uses an offered word on the same day, with the object within reach and after a prompt; a child of 7 uses it days later in a talk about something else.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it at the end of the sentence. The statement does not say who helps or how. The words are offered words, that is, words that have been presented to the child; "productively" means the child says them, as distinct from only understanding them. The wording says "during conversations", although the open data lists the goal under speaking (NE/MT/03). The statement names no words and no number of words.

### Common mistakes

Understanding the offered word and pointing correctly, and still saying the old general word. Saying the word only as an echo right after the adult. Using the offered word in a wrong form (in Dutch, "drijfde" for *dreef*, the past of *drijven*, to float).
