## Official wording

herkennen van persoon, plaats, gebeurtenis in gesproken tekst

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising person, place, event in spoken text

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After hearing a story the child says who it was about, where it took place and what happened: "the girl", "in the forest", "she lost her boot". The child points to the right figure and the right background picture, or places them on the table while the story is told again. A child of 4 names the main person; a child of 6 also names the place and what happened.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names three elements: person, place and event, in spoken text. It does not mention time, the order of events or the reasons for events. It sets no kind or length of text.

### Common mistakes

Naming the person and what happened but leaving out where it took place. Mixing up who did what when a story has two similar characters. Adding a person or an event from another story they know.
