discovering that letters correspond with sounds

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child points to the first letter on their name card and says its sound, then finds the same letter on a classmate's card and on the wall. The child picks from a few letter cards the one that goes with a sound the teacher says, and says the sound for a letter that is shown. A child of 4 knows the sound of a few letters, most often from their own name; a child of 7 says the sound for every letter and for letter pairs that stand for one sound, such as "oe" and "ui".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discovering: the goal does not say that the child knows all the letters, it names no letters and no sounds, and it sets no number. It does not say whether lower-case letters, capitals or both are meant. It does not mention letter names, or letter pairs that stand for one sound; the pairs above are the pack's examples. Decoding words is a separate goal, NE/LE/09/02/fase1.

### Common mistakes

Saying the letter's name where its sound is asked. Mixing up letters that look alike when turned or mirrored: b, d and p; u and n. Reading a letter pair that stands for one sound as two sounds: "o-e" for "oe".
