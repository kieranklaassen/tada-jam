## Official wording

samenvatten van wat wordt verteld (bijv. wat was de belangrijkste gebeurtenis in het verhaal?)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

summarising what is told (e.g. what was the most important event in the story?)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story the child answers "what was the most important thing that happened?" in one or two sentences. The child chooses, from three pictures of the story, the one that shows the main event and says why. A child of 4 names the event at the end of the story; a child of 6 tells the beginning, the main event and the ending.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The question about the most important event in the story is an example. The wording sets no length for the summary and no kind of text; "what is told" is wider than stories. The statement does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Retelling everything in order with "and then" instead of selecting. Choosing the most exciting or the funniest detail as the most important event. Giving only the ending.
