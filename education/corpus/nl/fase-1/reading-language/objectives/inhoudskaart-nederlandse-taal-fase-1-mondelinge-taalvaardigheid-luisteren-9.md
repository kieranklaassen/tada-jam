## Official wording

precies luisteren, ook door (met hulp) stellen van begripsvragen (inzetten van luisterstrategieën) (bijv. over woordbetekenis wanneer dat het luisteren/begrip belemmert)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

listening precisely, also by (with help) asking comprehension questions (putting listening strategies to use) (e.g. about the meaning of a word when that hinders the listening/understanding)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While a story is read aloud, the child raises a hand at an unknown word and asks "what is a lantern?". The teacher first shows how to ask, and the child later asks during a classmate's story: "what do you mean?", "who is 'he'?". A child of 4 shows by a puzzled look or by turning away that something was not understood, and asks when invited; a child of 6 asks about a word or a sentence without being prompted.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Asking comprehension questions is named as one way of listening precisely ("also by"), and "with help" in brackets belongs to the asking. The wording calls this putting listening strategies to use and names no other strategy. A question about the meaning of a word, when that word hinders listening or understanding, is the example. The statement sets no kind of text.

### Common mistakes

Not noticing that they did not understand, so that no question comes. Noticing it but staying silent, and nodding when asked whether it is clear. Asking about something other than the part they missed, or telling an experience in place of a question.
