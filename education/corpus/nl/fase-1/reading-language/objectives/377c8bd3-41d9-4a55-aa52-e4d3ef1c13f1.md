## English gloss

perceiving signals from familiar listeners

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While telling in the circle, the child looks up from the object they are holding and sees that classmates are looking, laughing, frowning or turning away. The child hears a classmate say "What?" or sees a raised hand. After the adult's question "Look at your listeners; can everyone hear you?", the child looks round the circle. A child of 4 notices a loud signal such as laughter or a called-out question; a child of 7 also notices quiet ones, such as a puzzled face or restless moving.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about perceiving the signals; responding to them is the subject of NE/MT/03/04/02/fase1. The listeners are familiar ones, people the child knows. The statement does not say which signals, and does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Looking at the object, the floor or the adult the whole time and so not seeing the listeners. Seeing a signal and not linking it to the telling (a frown is taken to mean that the listener is cross, not that something was not understood). Noticing only the adult's reactions and not those of the other children.
