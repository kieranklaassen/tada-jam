## Official wording

spelen met klanken (fonemen) en symbolen, woorden verklanken

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

playing with sounds (phonemes) and symbols, sounding out words (*woorden verklanken*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says a word slowly, stretches its first sound, and swaps a sound to make a nonsense word. The child handles letter stamps, magnetic letters and other signs, and with an adult says the sounds of a short word one after another. A child of 4 finds words that begin with the same sound as their own name; a child of 6 says the separate sounds of a short word and joins sounds that an adult says into a word.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says playing. It names sounds, explained in brackets as phonemes, and symbols, and does not say which sounds or which symbols. *Woorden verklanken* is glossed as sounding out words; the wording does not say whether the words are spoken or written, and it names no word length or word kind. This statement does not use the word letters; letters are named in `Fonemisch bewustzijn en alfabetisch principe / 4`.

### Common mistakes

Hearing the first sound of a word and not the middle or the last sound. Breaking a word into syllables when asked for its sounds. Saying the name of a letter where its sound is meant.
