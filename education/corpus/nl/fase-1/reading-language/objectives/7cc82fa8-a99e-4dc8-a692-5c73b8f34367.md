## Official wording

(met hulp) ook inzetten op de schrijfstrategieën: - selecteren en ordenen van informatie; - reflecteren (vooral op inhoud, minder op vorm); - verzorgen van de lay-out van de tekst (groep 3)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) also working on the writing strategies: - selecting and ordering information; - reflecting (mainly on content, less on form); - taking care of the layout of the text (*groep* 3, the third year of primary school)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 looks at the words collected about the guinea pig, crosses out what will not go in the text, and numbers the rest in the order in which to write them. After writing, the child reads the text with the teacher and checks whether everything is in it and whether a reader can follow it. The child then writes it out with a title at the top and a drawing in a place left open for it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the wording itself names the year, *groep* 3 (about ages 6 to 7). The open data puts "(groep 3)" after the last item; the content-line document prints the label "groep 3:" on a line above the whole statement, so the year covers all three strategies. The brackets at the start say that the child does this with help. The content-line document prints "onder begeleiding" (under guidance) where the open data has "(met hulp)" (with help). Three strategies are named: selecting and ordering information, reflecting, and taking care of the layout. The word "also" points back to the two strategies of NE/SCH/02/05/02/fase1. Reflecting is bounded in brackets: mainly on content, less on form. The goal does not mention spelling.

### Common mistakes

Keeping everything that was collected. Writing the points in the order in which they were collected, not in an order a reader can follow. When reading back, looking only at neatness or at single words and not at whether the content is complete.
