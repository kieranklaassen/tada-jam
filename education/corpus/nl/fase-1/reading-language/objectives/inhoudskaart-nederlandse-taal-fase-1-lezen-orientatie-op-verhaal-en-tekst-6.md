## Official wording

kennismaken met betogende teksten (bijv. een oproep, reclame)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know argumentative texts (*betogende teksten*, texts that aim to persuade) (e.g. a call to do something, advertising)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks with an adult at a poster that asks everyone to bring old toys to a collection, or at a toy advertisement in a leaflet, and talks about what the maker wants the reader to do. In play the child makes a poster for the class shop. A child of 4 says what is shown on the advertisement; a child of 6 says that the advertisement wants people to buy the thing.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says getting to know: a first meeting. The two kinds in brackets are examples: a call (*oproep*) and advertising. The statement does not ask the child to judge an argument or to write such a text.

### Common mistakes

Taking everything an advertisement says as plain fact. Not seeing that someone made the text because they want something from the reader. Not telling apart an advertisement and a text that gives information.
