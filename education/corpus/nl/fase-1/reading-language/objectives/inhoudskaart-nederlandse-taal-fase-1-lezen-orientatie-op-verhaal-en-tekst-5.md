## Official wording

kennismaken met instructieve teksten (bijv. pictogrammen voor dagelijkse handelingen, recept, bouwtekening)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know instructional texts (e.g. pictograms for daily actions, a recipe, a building plan)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child follows a row of pictograms for washing hands, one step after another. The child bakes with a picture recipe, pointing to each step, and builds a block structure from a building plan. A child of 4 does what a pictogram shows, with an adult beside them; a child of 6 follows the steps of a picture recipe in order and notices when a step was skipped.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says getting to know: a first meeting. The three kinds in brackets are examples: pictograms for daily actions, a recipe and a building plan. Pictograms and a building plan can be followed without reading words. The statement sets no number of steps.

### Common mistakes

Skipping a step, or doing the steps in an order of their own. Looking only at the last picture, which shows the result, and trying to make that without the steps. Following a row of pictograms from the wrong end.
