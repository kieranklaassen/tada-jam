## Official wording

herkennen van lettercombinaties en spellingpatronen in niet-klankzuivere woorden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising letter combinations and spelling patterns in words that are not sound-pure (*niet-klankzuivere woorden*, words not spelled exactly as they sound)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 (about 6 or 7) looks at a written word and points out a group of letters that belongs together, such as *sch*, *ng* or *ooi*, before reading the word. The child sorts word cards into piles by the letter group they share and reads each pile aloud. In a book the child stops at a word with such a group and reads the group as one piece.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card prints this statement under a heading that says from *groep* 3 on (about ages 6 to 7). The wording says recognising, in words that are not sound-pure; it does not say spelling these words. It names no letter combination and no spelling pattern; *sch*, *ng* and *ooi* above, and *ring* and *bomen* under Common mistakes, are the pack's examples, not the card's. Sound-pure words are named in `Aanvankelijk lezen (vanaf GROEP 3) / 2`.

### Common mistakes

Reading each letter of a combination as a separate sound, for example n and g in *ring*. Reading a single vowel letter at the end of a syllable as a short sound, for example in *bomen* (trees). Recognising a pattern in a practised word and not seeing the same pattern in a new word.
