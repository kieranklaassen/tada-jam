## Official wording

kennismaken met verhalende teksten, vooral expressief (bijv. sprookjes, dierenverhalen, realistische verhalen, fantasieverhalen, voorleesverhalen, prentenboeken)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

getting to know narrative texts, mainly expressive (e.g. fairy tales, animal stories, realistic stories, fantasy stories, read-aloud stories, picture books)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to fairy tales, animal stories and picture books, looks at the pictures and says the repeated lines together with the adult. The child asks for a favourite story again and looks through the book alone afterwards. A child of 4 follows a short picture book with a little text on each page; a child of 6 follows a longer story that is read in parts.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says getting to know: a first meeting, not reading the texts alone. It names narrative texts, "mainly expressive", and the card does not explain that term. The six kinds in brackets are examples.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the link between the events: they follow the pictures and can no longer follow the story on a page without a picture. Many take everything in a story to have really happened.
