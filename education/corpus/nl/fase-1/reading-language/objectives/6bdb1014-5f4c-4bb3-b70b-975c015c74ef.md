## Official wording

(met hulp) begrijpen van informatieve prentenboeken (bijv. juiste plaatje aanwijzen tijdens of na het lezen)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/03/02; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 6bdb1014-5f4c-4bb3-b70b-975c015c74ef.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) understanding informative picture books (e.g. pointing to the right picture during or after the reading)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While an information picture book about the seasons is read aloud, the child points to the picture that goes with the sentence just read: the tree without leaves, the bird on the nest. Afterwards the child answers a question by finding the page and pointing. A child of 4 points to single things that are named; a child of 7 points to the picture that shows what the text explains, such as which animal sleeps through the winter, and says it in a sentence.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets at the start say that the child does this with help. The kind of text is informative picture books only. Pointing to the right picture, during or after the reading, is given as an example of showing understanding, not as the only way. The wording says "the reading" without saying who reads; it does not say that the child reads the book alone.

### Common mistakes

Pointing to a picture of a thing whose name was heard, without attention to what was said about it. Pointing to the picture they like most. Answering from what they already believed about the subject and not from the book.
