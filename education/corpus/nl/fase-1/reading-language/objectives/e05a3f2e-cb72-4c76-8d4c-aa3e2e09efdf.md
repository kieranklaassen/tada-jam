## Official wording

vertellen voor vertrouwd publiek: - uitleg geven (bijv. bij een tekening); - een verhaal vertellen of navertellen; - vertellen over een gebeurtenis of ervaring

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

telling to a familiar audience: - giving an explanation (e.g. with a drawing); - telling or retelling a story; - telling about an event or experience

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds up a drawing in the circle and explains what is on it and why the house has no door. The child retells a picture book to a small group while turning the pages, or tells a made-up story with two puppets. On Monday the child tells the class about falling off a bicycle at the weekend. A child of 4 tells a few separate sentences while the adult asks "and then?"; a child of 7 tells a longer piece alone, in an order the listeners can follow.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The audience is a familiar one: people the child knows. Three kinds of telling are listed: giving an explanation, with a drawing as the example after "e.g."; telling or retelling a story; and telling about an event or experience. The statement sets no length and no size of audience, and does not mention help. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/03/01, titled *Een monoloog houden* (holding a monologue).

### Common mistakes

Assuming the listeners know what the child knows: starting in the middle and saying "he" and "there" without saying who or where. Joining events with "and then" in the order they are remembered and leaving out the beginning or the end. In retelling, describing the pictures one by one without saying what links them.
