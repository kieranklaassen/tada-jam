## Official wording

(met hulp) vormen van eigen mening over (een inhoudselement van) een gesproken tekst (bijv. waardering geven: wat vind je ervan?) (evalueren)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) forming one's own opinion about (an element of the content of) a spoken text (e.g. giving an appreciation: what do you think of it?) (evaluating)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story the child says whether they liked it and, when asked "why?", points to a part: "I liked it when the mouse hid in the shoe". The child puts a card with a happy or an unhappy face next to the book and tells the teacher the reason. A child of 4 says "nice" or "not nice"; a child of 6 gives an opinion about one part or one character, with a reason taken from the text.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording puts "with help" in brackets at the front. The opinion may be about a whole spoken text or, as the brackets say, about one element of its content. Giving an appreciation (what do you think of it?) is an example, and the wording labels the activity as evaluating. The statement does not mention giving reasons.

### Common mistakes

Repeating the opinion of the child who spoke first or of the teacher. Saying "nice" about everything without being able to point to a part. Judging a character instead of the text ("the wolf is bad, so the story is bad").
