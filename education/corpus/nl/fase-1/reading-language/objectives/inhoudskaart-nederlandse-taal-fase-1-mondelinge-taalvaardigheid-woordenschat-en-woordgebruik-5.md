forming (new) words (morphology)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While talking, the child makes a plural (*boek*, *boeken*; book, books), a diminutive (*huis*, *huisje*; house, little house) and a past form (*loop*, *liep*; walk, walked), and puts two words together into a compound: *poppen* and *huis* make *poppenhuis* (doll's house). The child invents a word for something that has no name yet, such as a "sock box". A child of 4 applies the regular rule everywhere; a child of 6 uses many irregular forms correctly and takes a compound apart into its two words.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is three words and a term in brackets, morphology; "new" stands in brackets, so both forming words and forming new words are meant. It names no word forms: plurals, diminutives, verb forms and compounds are not listed. It stands under Mondelinge taalvaardigheid (oral language skills); spelling is not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Applying the regular rule to an irregular word: *loopte* for *liep* (walked), *schippen* for *schepen* (ships). Choosing the wrong diminutive ending (*bloemtje* for *bloemetje*, little flower). Reversing the two parts of a compound, so that the meaning changes.
