## Official wording

oefenen van de elementaire leeshandelingen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

practising the elementary reading acts (*elementaire leeshandelingen*, the basic steps of reading a word)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 (about 6 or 7) looks at a short written word, points to each letter from left to right and says its sound, and then says the sounds together as one word. The child does this with letter cards laid in a row and with the words of a first reading book. After reading the word the child checks it against the picture or the sentence it stands in.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card prints this statement under a heading that says from *groep* 3 on (about ages 6 to 7). The wording says practising. It names the elementary reading acts and does not list them; in Dutch reading teaching the term is commonly used for going from the letters of a word to their sounds and joining those sounds into the word. The statement names no letters, no word length and no reading speed. Which kinds of words are read is the subject of `Aanvankelijk lezen (vanaf GROEP 3) / 2` and `Aanvankelijk lezen (vanaf GROEP 3) / 3`.

### Common mistakes

Saying each sound correctly and then not being able to join the sounds into the word. Saying the name of a letter where its sound is needed. Guessing the word from its first letter or from the picture.
