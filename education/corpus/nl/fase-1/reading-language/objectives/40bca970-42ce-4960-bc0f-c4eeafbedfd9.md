## Official wording

zich inleven in alledaagse onderwerpen (aansluiten bij de leefwereld en bij onderwerpen die verder afstaan van het kind)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster NE/LE/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 40bca970-42ce-4960-bc0f-c4eeafbedfd9.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

imagining oneself into everyday subjects (linking up with the child's own world and with subjects that are further away from the child)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With a book about going to the dentist, a new baby or moving house, the child tells what that was like for them, or asks what it would be like. With a book about a child who lives on a boat or in a far country, the child says what would be the same as their own day and what would be different. A child of 4 links a book to things from home and school; a child of 7 can also picture a day in a life they do not know.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names everyday subjects. The brackets name two things to link up with: the child's own world, and subjects that are further away from the child; the wording does not say how far. It names no subjects and no kinds of text, and it does not say whether the text is read aloud or read by the child.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often assume that everyone's day is like their own, so they miss what is different in a book about another way of living. With a subject they know, a child may tell only their own experience and stop listening to the book.
