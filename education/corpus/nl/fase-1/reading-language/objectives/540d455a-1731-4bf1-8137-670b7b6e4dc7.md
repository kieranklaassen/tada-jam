## Official wording

bekijken en lezen van (beeld-)tekst om informatie op te doen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/LE/03/01; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 540d455a-1731-4bf1-8137-670b7b6e4dc7.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

looking at and reading (picture) text to gain information

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a page of an information book about frogs, studies the pictures in order and says what they learned: "first it is an egg, then it has a tail". The child looks at the pictograms on the day chart to find out what comes after lunch, and reads the words under them when they can. A child of 4 gains the information from the pictures and from the text read aloud; a child of 7 reads the captions and short texts alone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The bracket form "(beeld-)tekst" covers both text and *beeldtekst*, a text made of or with images. The purpose is named: to gain information. Two verbs are used, looking at and reading; the goal does not say that the child reads the words alone. It names no kinds of text; kinds are given as examples in NE/LE/02/01/01/fase1.

### Common mistakes

Looking at the most striking picture and missing the one that holds the answer. Telling what they already knew about the subject and not what the page shows. Looking at pictures in any order where the order carries the meaning, as in a row of steps.
