## Official wording

verschillende en nieuwe woorden kiezen en gebruiken (betekenisuitbreiding, semantiek)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

choosing and using different and new words (extension of meaning, semantics)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child chooses between words that are close in meaning when telling: "walked", "ran" or "sneaked"; "big" or "enormous". The child hears a known word in a second meaning (*bank* as a sofa and as a bank for money) and uses it. A child of 4 uses one general word for a group of things or actions; a child of 6 picks the more exact word and can say that two words mean nearly the same or the opposite.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording has two verbs, choosing and using, and two objects, different words and new words. The brackets name extension of meaning and the term semantics. The statement sets no number of words and names no relations between words, such as opposites or categories.

### Common mistakes

Using one general word ("nice", "do", "go") where the child knows more exact ones. Holding that a word can have only one meaning and rejecting the second (a *bank* is for sitting on, so it cannot be where money is kept). Using a new word where it does not fit, for example "enormous" for something that is very good.
