## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child notices that their own name and a classmate's name start with the same letter and with the same sound. The child changes one magnetic letter in "kat" to make "mat" and hears that the word changes at that place. The child writes a word for the shop corner with the letters they know, one letter for each sound they hear. A child of 4 links one or two letters to the sound at the start of a known name; a child of 7 knows that each sound in a spoken word is written with a letter or a fixed pair of letters, in the same order from left to right.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The alphabetic principle is the idea that the letters of a written word stand for the sounds of the spoken word; the wording uses the term without explaining it. The brackets set a condition: a playful context. The verb is discovering: the goal does not say how many letters the child knows, and it names no letters, sounds or words. That letters correspond with sounds is worded again as a separate goal, NE/LE/08/04/fase1.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that a written word is a kind of picture of the thing, so a big thing needs a long word and a small thing a short one. Thinking that a letter stands for a whole word or name: "that is the letter of mama". Writing only the sounds that are heard most clearly, such as the first and the last: "bm" for "boom".
