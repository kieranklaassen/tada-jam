## Official wording

gebruiken van eenvoudige signaalwoorden (bijv. en toen, omdat) voor samenhang in een verhaal

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using simple signal words (*signaalwoorden*, words that link sentences) (e.g. *en toen* "and then", *omdat* "because") for coherence in a story

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells what happened at the swimming pool: "First we got changed, and then we went into the water, and then I went down the slide". Asked why the tower fell, the child says "because the big block was on top". The child lays three picture cards in order and tells the story with "first", "then" and "at the end". A child of 4 links nearly everything with "and then"; a child of 7 also uses "because", "but", "first" and "after that", so that the listener can follow what came first and why.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The signal words are called simple, and the two in brackets, *en toen* ("and then") and *omdat* ("because"), follow "e.g." and are examples. Their use is for coherence in a story. The statement gives no list of signal words and no number.

### Common mistakes

Starting every sentence with "and then", also where the events did not follow each other. Using "because" with cause and result exchanged ("It rained because the street was wet"). In Dutch, keeping main-clause word order after *omdat* ("omdat ik was moe" for "omdat ik moe was").
