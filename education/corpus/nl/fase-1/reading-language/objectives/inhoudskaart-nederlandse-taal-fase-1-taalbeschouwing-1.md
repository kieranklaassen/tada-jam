## Official wording

gesprekken voeren over taal en taalgebruik

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having conversations about language and language use

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child talks with the teacher and classmates about words: that one word is long and another short, that two words rhyme, that grandmother says a word differently, that a classmate speaks another language at home and how "thank you" sounds in it. The child says why you ask the teacher "may I?" and do not say "give me". A child of 4 notices and remarks on a funny or strange word; a child of 6 compares two words or two ways of saying something and gives a reason.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names two subjects of conversation: language and language use. It names no topics within them and no terms a child should know. This statement stands directly under the heading Taalbeschouwing (reflection on language), which on the card has three statements and no sub-headings.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often talk about the thing a word stands for and not about the word: asked which word is longer, "train" or "caterpillar", they choose "train" because a train is long. Many do not yet separate what was said from how it was said.
