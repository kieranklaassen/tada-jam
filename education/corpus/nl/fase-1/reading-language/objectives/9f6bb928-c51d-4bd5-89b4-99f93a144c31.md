## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before a story is read aloud, the adult shows the cover and the child says what the story could be about. Halfway, the adult stops and the child says what might happen next, and then listens to find out whether that was right. When a part was not understood, the child asks to hear it again. A child of 4 predicts from the cover picture after the adult's question; a child of 7 uses the title and what is already known about the subject, and asks a question of their own during a pause.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it at the end of the sentence. The statement does not say who helps or how. It names no listening strategies and no number of them. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/02/04, titled *Luisterdoelen en -strategieën* (listening purposes and strategies); a goal with the same wording, NE/MT/02/09/02/fase1, is listed under *Aanpak* (approach).

### Common mistakes

Making a prediction and keeping to it after the story continues differently. Guessing what the story is about from one detail of the cover without using what the adult has said. Not noticing that a part was not understood, and so not asking.
