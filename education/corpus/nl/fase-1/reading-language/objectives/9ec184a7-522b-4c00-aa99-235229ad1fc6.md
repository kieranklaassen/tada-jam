## Official wording

schrijven over concrete onderwerpen uit eigen leefwereld. Ze gaan over het hier en nu of over de nabije omgeving, de nabije toekomst/verleden

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

writing about concrete subjects from one's own world. They are about the here and now or about the near surroundings, the near future/past

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes or dictates about the rabbit at home, the new shoes, what happened in the playground this morning, or the birthday on Saturday. The child draws it first and then writes a word or sentence with the drawing. A child of 4 names what is in front of them or what has just happened; a child of 7 writes a few sentences about yesterday or about a plan for the weekend.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The subjects are concrete and from the child's own world. The second sentence bounds them further: the here and now, or the near surroundings, the near future or the near past; the wording does not say how near. Abstract subjects and subjects far away in place or time are outside this goal. It sets no length and names no kind of text.

### Common mistakes

Writing as if the reader was there: "he took it", without saying who or what. Leaving out when or where something happened. Joining events with "en toen" (and then) again and again, without an ending.
