## Official wording

opzoeken en kennismaken met de opzet van verschillende informatiebronnen (groep 3)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

looking up and getting to know the set-up of different sources of information (*groep* 3, the third year of primary school)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 takes an information book about animals, finds the page about the fox with the help of the table of contents or the page numbers, and sees that each animal has its own page with a title. The child looks in a picture dictionary for children and finds that the words stand in the order of the alphabet. With the teacher the child looks at how a website for children shows its subjects.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the wording itself names the year, *groep* 3 (about ages 6 to 7). The open data ends the wording with "(groep 3)"; the content-line document prints no such tag and carries the year in the heading of the cluster, "Zakelijke teksten: studievaardigheden (vanaf groep 3)" (from *groep* 3). The wording leaves open whether the child looks for sources or looks things up in them. No sources are named; the book, dictionary and website above are the pack's examples. The second verb is getting to know: the goal does not say that the child uses a table of contents, an index or alphabetical order alone.

### Common mistakes

Leafing through the whole book page by page without using the table of contents. Not knowing the order of the alphabet well enough to find a word. Expecting the answer on the first page opened and stopping when it is not there.
