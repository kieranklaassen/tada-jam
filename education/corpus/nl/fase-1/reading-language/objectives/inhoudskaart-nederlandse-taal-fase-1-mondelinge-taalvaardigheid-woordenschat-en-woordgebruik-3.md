## Official wording

passende woorden gebruiken voor concrete zaken/schoolse omgang en onbekende woorden omschrijven (met woorden labelen van personen, dingen en plaatsen)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using fitting words for concrete matters/dealings at school (*schoolse omgang*, how people deal with each other at school) and describing unknown words (labelling persons, things and places with words)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child asks for "the scissors" and "the glue" by name, says "may I join in?" and "it is my turn" in the routines of the class, and names people, things and places in the school: the caretaker, the gym, the coat rack. When a word is missing, the child describes the thing: "the thing you use to make holes in paper". The child also tells a classmate what a word means when that classmate does not know it. A child of 4 names familiar things and points to the rest; a child of 6 describes by use, look or place a thing whose name they do not know.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording has two parts: using fitting words for concrete matters and for dealings at school, and describing unknown words. The brackets name labelling persons, things and places with words. *Onbekende woorden omschrijven* can be read as describing in other words a thing whose word the child does not know, or as describing what an unknown word means; the wording does not choose. The statement names no word list.

### Common mistakes

Using a general word ("thing", "there", "do") where the child knows a specific word. Pointing or saying "that one" instead of describing when the word is missing. Calling a thing by the name of a related thing (every writing tool a "pen").
