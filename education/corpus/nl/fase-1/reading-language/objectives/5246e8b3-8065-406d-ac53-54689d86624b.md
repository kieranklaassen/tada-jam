## Official wording

weglaten van onbelangrijke details bij het weergeven van een inhoudselement (bijv. gebeurtenis)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_subcluster NE/MT/02/07; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 5246e8b3-8065-406d-ac53-54689d86624b.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

leaving out unimportant details when relating an element of the content (e.g. an event)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story has been read aloud, the adult asks "What happened when the wolf came to the door?", and the child tells it in one or two sentences: the wolf knocked, and the goats did not open. The child tells a classmate who was absent what happened in one part of a story. From four picture cards of one event the child chooses the two that are needed to tell it. A child of 4 tells one event in a sentence after the adult's questions and adds whatever else is remembered; a child of 7 tells an event briefly and leaves out the colour of the door and what the goats were eating.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about relating one element of the content, with an event as the example after "e.g."; it does not speak of summarising a whole text. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/02/07, titled *Samenvatten* (summarising). The statement does not say who decides which details are unimportant, and does not mention help.

### Common mistakes

Telling everything that is remembered, in the order it is remembered, with each detail given the same weight. Keeping the funny or frightening detail and leaving out what happened. Making the account so short that the event itself is missing ("The wolf came").
