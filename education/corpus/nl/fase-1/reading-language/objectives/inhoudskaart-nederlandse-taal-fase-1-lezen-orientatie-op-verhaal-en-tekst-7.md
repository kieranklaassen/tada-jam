## Official wording

herkennen en onderscheiden van fantasieverhalen en realistische verhalen (fictie en non-fictie)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and distinguishing fantasy stories and realistic stories (fiction and non-fiction)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story the child says whether this could really happen, and why: an animal that talks, a child that flies, or a child that goes to the shop with a parent. The child sorts a few known books into two piles. A child of 4 says "that is not real" about one striking thing, such as a talking animal; a child of 6 gives a reason and sorts several books.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names two things: recognising, and distinguishing, fantasy stories and realistic stories. The brackets name fiction and non-fiction; the card does not say how that pair relates to the two kinds of story, and a realistic story can be made up too. The statement does not ask the child to use the terms.

### Common mistakes

Calling a made-up story with ordinary people "really happened". Deciding by the pictures alone: a drawing is not real and a photo is real. Calling a whole story not real because of one thing they find strange.
