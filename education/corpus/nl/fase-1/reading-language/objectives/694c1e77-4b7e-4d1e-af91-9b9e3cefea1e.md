## Official wording

labelen met woorden personen, dingen en plaatsen uit teksten na het luisteren

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

labelling persons, things and places from texts with words after listening

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story has been read aloud, the child names who was in it ("the baker", "the girl"), which things were in it ("the oven", "the basket") and where it took place ("the bakery", "the market"). The child points at a picture from the book and says the word the text used for it. From a basket of objects that belong to the story the child picks up each one and says its name. A child of 4 names a few familiar things while holding the object or seeing the picture; a child of 7 names persons, things and places from memory and uses the words of the text, including new ones.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names three kinds of things to label: persons, things and places from texts, and it places the labelling after the listening. The open data lists it under sub-cluster NE/MT/02/06, titled *Woordenschat en -strategieën* (vocabulary and vocabulary strategies). It names no words and no number of words, and does not mention events or actions.

### Common mistakes

Using a general word for a specific one from the text ("man" for "baker", "bird" for "owl"). Pointing or saying "that one" instead of saying the word. Naming things from the pictures that the text did not mention and leaving out things the text did mention.
