## Official wording

uitbreiden van de receptieve woordenschat

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

expanding the receptive vocabulary

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears new words in a picture book, in the theme corner and in the teacher's talk, and shows understanding by pointing to the right picture, fetching the named object or doing what the word says ("stir", "pour"). The child hears the same word again over several days and in different places. A child of 4 understands mainly words for things and actions that are present; a child of 6 also understands words for things that are not in the room, and words for categories, feelings and positions.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Receptive vocabulary is the words a child understands on hearing them; the words a child uses are a separate statement of the card (Woordenschat en woordgebruik / 2). The wording sets no number of words and names no word list or themes.

### Common mistakes

Linking a new word to the whole situation and not to its exact meaning, for example taking "stir" to mean cooking in general. Understanding a word only in the sentence or the book in which it was first heard. Seeming to understand by following what other children do.
