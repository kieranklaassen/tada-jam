## Official wording

vasthouden en blijven bij het gespreksonderwerp

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

holding on to and staying with the topic of conversation

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a circle talk about pets, the child tells about their rabbit and, when the next child speaks about a cat, adds something about animals and not about a birthday. The child comes back to the subject after a reminder or after looking at the object in the middle of the circle. A child of 4 soon follows an association of their own and tells about a birthday; a child of 6 keeps to pets while others speak and notices when someone else leaves the subject.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording sets no length of conversation and no number of turns. It does not mention help or supporting material; the card's statement Spreken / 6, about staying with a chosen topic, gives supporting material as an example.

### Common mistakes

Moving to a new subject by association: one word in another child's turn reminds the child of an unrelated experience, and the child tells that. Repeating their own earlier contribution instead of adding to the subject. Returning to the subject only when an adult names it again.
