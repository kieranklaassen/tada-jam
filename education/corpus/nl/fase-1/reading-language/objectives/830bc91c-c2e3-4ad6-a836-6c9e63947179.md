## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 draws small arcs under the parts of a written word, "ta-fel", "ap-pel-boom", claps the parts while reading and then reads the word as a whole. The child cuts a word card into its syllables and puts it together again. With practice the child sees the parts in a written word of two or three syllables and reads it part by part.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the content-line document names the year in the heading of the cluster, "Technisch lezen (vanaf groep 3)" (technical reading, from *groep* 3), which within fase 1 is *groep* 3, about ages 6 to 7. The open data titles the cluster "Technisch lezen" and carries no year. The goal is about syllables in written words; it does not name syllables in spoken words. It sets no number of syllables and names no words; the words above are the pack's examples. It stands under technical reading: it does not mention the rules for breaking a word at the end of a line, or the spelling of open and closed syllables.

### Common mistakes

Splitting at the wrong place, "bom-en" for "bo-men", which gives the wrong vowel sound. Reading each syllable with full stress and then not recognising the word. Counting sounds or letters when asked for syllables.
