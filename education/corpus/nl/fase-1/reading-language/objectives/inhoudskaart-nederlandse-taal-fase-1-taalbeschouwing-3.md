improving language use

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears the teacher give back their sentence in the correct form ("yes, you walked to school") and says it again in that form. The child starts a sentence again when a listener does not understand, picks a more exact word, or says "please" and "may I?" after a reminder. A child of 4 sometimes repeats the corrected form; a child of 6 corrects themselves in the middle of a sentence and tries a better word unasked.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording does not say what is improved (pronunciation, word choice, sentence building or politeness), by what standard, or with whose help. It does not say whether spoken or written language use is meant. This statement stands directly under the heading Taalbeschouwing (reflection on language), which on the card has three statements and no sub-headings.

### Common mistakes

Repeating the original form after hearing the correct one, because the child attends to the content of the reply and not to its form. Applying a correction too widely (after learning *liep*, walked, saying *liepte*). Becoming quiet after frequent correction and saying less.
