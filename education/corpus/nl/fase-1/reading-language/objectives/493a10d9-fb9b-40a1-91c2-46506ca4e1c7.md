## Official wording

in aanraking komen met verschillende spreekdoelen en (met hulp) kiezen van een spreekdoel uit gegeven doelen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

coming into contact with different speaking purposes and (with help) choosing a speaking purpose from given purposes

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On different days the child tells a story to make the class laugh, explains to a classmate how the marble run works, and tells what happened at the dentist. Before the child speaks about a shell from the beach, the adult shows two picture cards, for example one for "telling what happened" and one for "explaining how it works", and the child picks one. A child of 4 speaks for different purposes without naming them and picks a card with the adult's guidance; a child of 7 chooses between two or three given purposes and says why that one fits.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts: coming into contact with different speaking purposes, and choosing one. The choosing is with help (in brackets) and is from purposes that are given, so the child is not asked to think of a purpose. The statement names no speaking purposes and no number of them.

### Common mistakes

Picking a purpose and then doing something else, such as choosing "explaining how it works" and telling a story about the day the thing was bought. Choosing the same purpose every time. Taking "purpose" to mean the topic ("my purpose is my rabbit").
