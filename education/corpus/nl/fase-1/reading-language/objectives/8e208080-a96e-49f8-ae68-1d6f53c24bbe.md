## English gloss

decoding phonetically regular words (*klankzuivere woorden*, words written exactly as they sound)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 reads short words in which each letter or fixed letter pair has its usual sound, such as "vis", "maan" and "boek": first sound by sound, then as a whole word. The child builds such words with letter cards and reads what a classmate built. With practice the child reads these words at once, also ones with two consonants next to each other, such as "krant".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do; the content-line document names the year in the heading of the cluster, "Technisch lezen (vanaf groep 3)" (technical reading, from *groep* 3), which within fase 1 is *groep* 3, about ages 6 to 7. The open data titles the cluster "Technisch lezen" and carries no year. The goal is limited to *klankzuivere* words: words in which every letter, or fixed letter combination, is said with its usual sound. Words that are not written as they sound are in NE/LE/09/03/fase1. The goal names no words, no word length and no word structure (such as consonant, vowel, consonant), and it sets no speed; the words above are the pack's examples. It does not mention sentences or texts.

### Common mistakes

Sounding out the letters and then not blending them into the word. Reading a two-letter vowel as two sounds: "b-o-e-k". Dropping or moving a consonant where two stand together: "krant" read as "kant".
