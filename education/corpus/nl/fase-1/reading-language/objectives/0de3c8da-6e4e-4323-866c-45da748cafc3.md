## Official wording

(met hulp) oriënteren voorafgaand aan het gesprek op onderwerp en elementaire gespreksregels (bijv. naar elkaar luisteren)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

(with help) getting an orientation, before the conversation, on the topic and on elementary conversation rules (e.g. listening to each other)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before the circle talk starts, the adult holds up an autumn leaf and asks what the talk will be about; the child says "about autumn" and thinks of one thing to tell. Together with the adult, the child repeats the rule for this talk, for example by pointing to a picture card of an ear for listening. A child of 4 names the topic from the object shown and needs to hear the rule again; a child of 7 says in a sentence what the talk is about and what the group agreed, and thinks beforehand of a question to ask.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording carries "(met hulp)" (with help) at the start; the content-line document prints it in the middle of the sentence, after "voorafgaand aan het gesprek" (before the conversation). The statement does not say who helps or how. The orientation takes place before the conversation and covers two things: the topic and elementary conversation rules; listening to each other follows "e.g." and is an example. NE/MT/01/03/01/fase1 is the goal about the rules themselves, and NE/MT/01/08/02/fase1 the goal about reflecting on one's own contribution to the conversation.

### Common mistakes

Starting to tell the story at once when asked what the talk will be about. Saying the rule correctly beforehand and not applying it a minute later. Attending to the object shown and not to the topic (talking about the one leaf when the topic is autumn).
