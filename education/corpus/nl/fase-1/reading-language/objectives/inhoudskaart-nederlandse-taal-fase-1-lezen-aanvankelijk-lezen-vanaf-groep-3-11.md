## Official wording

herkennen en begrijpen van signaalwoorden die chronologische volgorde of vraag-antwoord structuur aangeven

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Nederlandse taal: ontluikende en beginnende geletterdheid - jonge kind (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and understanding signal words that indicate chronological order or a question-and-answer structure

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in *groep* 3 (about 6 or 7) reads a short text and points to the words that say in what order things happen, such as *eerst* (first), *daarna* (after that) and *toen* (then). The child lays picture cards or sentence strips in the order those words give. In a text with a question, the child finds the question and the sentence that answers it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card prints this statement under a heading that says from *groep* 3 on (about ages 6 to 7). The statement names two kinds of signal words: those that indicate chronological order and those that indicate a question-and-answer structure. It lists no words; the Dutch words above and under Common mistakes are the pack's examples, not the card's. Signal words for other relations, such as cause or contrast, are not named in this statement.

### Common mistakes

Skipping the small words and attending only to the nouns and verbs. Taking the order of the sentences as the order of the events, also where a word such as *voordat* (before) reverses the order. Recognising a signal word and not being able to say what it tells about the order.
