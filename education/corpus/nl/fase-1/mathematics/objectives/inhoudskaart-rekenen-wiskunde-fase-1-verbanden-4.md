## Official wording

lezen van een betekenisvolle passende tabel zoals een dag- en weekplanning van de eigen groep

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading a meaningful, suitable table such as a day and week schedule of one's own class (*groep*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at the row of picture cards for today and says what comes after fruit time. On the week chart the child finds the row with their own name or picture and the column for today, and reads which task or play corner is theirs. A child of 4 follows the day's cards in order from the first to the last; a child of 6 finds a cell by looking along a row and down a column and says what is planned on another day.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The day and week schedule of the class is an example, introduced by "such as" (*zoals*). The statement asks that the table is meaningful and suitable and says nothing about its size or content. It says reading; making a table is not mentioned. It sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child reads only along the row or only down the column and gives the content of the wrong cell. The child reads the day schedule as separate pictures, not as an order in time. The child moves to a neighbouring row by mistake when following a wide table with a finger.
