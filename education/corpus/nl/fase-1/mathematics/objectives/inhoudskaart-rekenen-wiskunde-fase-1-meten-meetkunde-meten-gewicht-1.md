## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering and experiencing what 'weighing' involves

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lifts a full bag and an empty one and feels the difference in the arms. The child holds a stone in one hand and a sponge in the other and says which one pushes the hand down more, then puts both on a balance and sees one side go down. At a kitchen scale the child puts things on and watches the pointer or the numeral change. A child of 4 lifts, carries and says "heavy"; a child of 6 says that weighing means finding out how heavy something is and that the heavier side of a balance goes down.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verbs are discovering and experiencing: the statement is about finding out what weighing is, and asks for no result. It names no instruments and no units; informal weighing instruments are named in the card's statement Gewicht / 5.

### Common mistakes

The child expects the bigger thing to be the heavier one. The child thinks the pan of the balance that goes up holds the heavier thing, because up means more. The child presses on the scale or keeps holding the object while weighing and does not see that this changes the result.
