## Official wording

splitsen en samenvoegen van kleine hoeveelheden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

splitting (*splitsen*, breaking an amount into parts) and joining small quantities

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shakes five two-coloured counters and says how many show red and how many yellow, then shakes again and finds another pair. The child hides some of six blocks under a cup and a partner says how many are hidden. The child pushes two small groups together and says how many there are in all. A child of 4 splits three or four objects into two groups and names both; a child of 6 finds several ways to split one small amount and names the hidden part without seeing it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement itself says "small" (*kleine*) and gives no number for it. It does not mention written number pairs or signs.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks the total changes when the objects are divided differently and counts everything again. The child finds one way to split an amount and stops. The child guesses the hidden part and does not use the part that is visible.
