## Official wording

tellen van hoeveelheden (resultatief tellen) en de regels van het tellen leren

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

counting quantities (resultative counting, *resultatief tellen*: counting to find how many) and learning the rules of counting

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child touches or moves each object in a group once, says one number word for each, and gives the last word as the answer to "how many?". The child counts the same group from another starting point and finds the same number, and counts things of different kinds together. A child of 4 counts a handful of objects in a row correctly; a child of 6 counts a larger group that lies scattered, and pushes counted objects aside so that none is counted twice.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The brackets name *resultatief tellen*. The statement does not list the rules of counting. Reciting the counting sequence without objects is another statement of the card, Telrij / 1.

### Common mistakes

The child counts an object twice or skips one, most often when the objects lie scattered. The child says the number words faster or slower than the finger moves. Asked "how many?" after counting, the child counts again instead of giving the last number.
