## Official wording

koppelen van telwoorden, nummers, hoeveelheden en getallen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

linking number words (*telwoorden*), numbers used as labels (*nummers*), quantities and numbers (*getallen*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears "six", takes six beads, and lays the card with 6 beside them. The child finds coat hook number 6 in a numbered row, and matches cards with dots, cards with digits and spoken words. A child of 4 links the word, a group of objects and the digit for the numbers up to about five; a child of 6 does this for numbers to about twenty and starts from any of the four.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. Dutch has two words where English has "number": *nummer*, a number that labels something or gives its place in a series, and *getal*, a number as a value; the statement names both and does not explain them. It does not say in which direction the links are made.

### Common mistakes

The child names a digit correctly but cannot put that many objects with it. The child recites up to a number without linking the last word to a quantity. The child thinks a higher label, such as a higher house number, means more of something.
