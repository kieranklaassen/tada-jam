dealing with concepts around money such as expensive/more expensive/most expensive/equally expensive, cheap/cheaper/cheapest, euro, coins, banknotes, paper money

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the play shop the child compares two price tags and says which toy is more expensive and which is cheaper, and finds two things that are equally expensive. The child pays with coins and notes and names them. A child of 4 says "that costs a lot" and hands over any coins; a child of 7 uses "more expensive", "cheapest" and "equally expensive" after comparing prices in whole euros.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The words after "zoals" are examples: the degrees of *duur* (expensive) with *even duur*, the degrees of *goedkoop* (cheap), and *euro*, *munten*, *bankbiljetten*, *papiergeld*. No amounts are stated. The word cent is not among the examples.

### Common mistakes

The child calls a thing expensive because it is big or because they want it. The child uses "cheap" to mean "not nice". The child mixes "more expensive" with "more" and thinks the dearer thing is the one of which you get more.
