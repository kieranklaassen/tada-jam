## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out two biscuits for each child and finds how many are needed for three children. The child makes lemonade with one scoop of syrup for each cup of water, and threads a string with two red beads after every blue one. A child of 4 gives each doll one cup and one spoon; a child of 7 keeps "two for each" going for several children and says how many are needed in all.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. This goal states no number range. The verb is exploring. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: with a baking recipe: one cake needs 2 eggs, 2 cakes need … eggs ("bij een bakrecept: voor één cake zijn 2 eieren nodig, voor 2 cakes zijn … eieren nodig"); it is an example, not a limit. Fractions, percentages and a notation for ratios are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child gives two to the first children and one to the rest when the pile gets small. The child changes one amount and not the other, such as more water with the same syrup, and expects the same taste. The child counts the total and loses the rule "for each".
