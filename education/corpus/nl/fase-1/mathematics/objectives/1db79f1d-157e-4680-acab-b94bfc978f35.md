## English gloss

dealing with concepts around numbers such as larger (*groter*), smaller (*kleiner*), largest (*grootste*), smallest (*kleinste*), equal (*gelijk*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children each turn over a number card and say which number is larger and which is smaller, or that the two are equal. From five cards in the hand the child picks the largest and the smallest number. A child of 4 uses "larger" and "smaller" for two numbers under 10 with dots or objects beside them; a child of 7 uses all five words for numbers up to 20 and further without objects.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The five words after "zoals" are examples: *groter*, *kleiner*, *grootste*, *kleinste*, *gelijk*. This goal states no number range. The signs <, > and = are not mentioned. Words for comparing quantities, such as more and fewer, are in rw/gb/2/04/fase1.

### Common mistakes

The child calls the number that is printed in a larger size the larger number. The child mixes "larger" for numbers with the size of the objects that are counted. The child knows "largest" and not yet "smallest", or takes "smaller" to mean "small".
