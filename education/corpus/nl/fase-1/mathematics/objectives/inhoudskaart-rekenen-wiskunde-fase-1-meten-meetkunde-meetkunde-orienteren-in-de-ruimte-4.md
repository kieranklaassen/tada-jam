onderzoeken wat wel en niet zichtbaar is vanuit bepaalde standpunten

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating what is and is not visible from certain viewpoints

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child crouches behind the cupboard and checks whether the others can still see them, then changes places to look from the other side. The child looks at a block building from the front, from the back and from above and says which blocks can be seen from each place. The child looks through a tube or a window and says what can be seen through it and what cannot. A child of 4 finds out by walking round and looking; a child of 6 predicts what a doll or another child on the other side can see before going to check.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about investigating what can and cannot be seen from certain viewpoints; it names no viewpoints and no objects. It does not mention drawing a view or matching photos to viewpoints.

### Common mistakes

The child assumes that someone else sees what the child sees, and thinks they are hidden when they themselves cannot see the other person. The child says what they know is there instead of what can be seen from that place. The child forgets that a tall thing in front hides a low thing behind it.
