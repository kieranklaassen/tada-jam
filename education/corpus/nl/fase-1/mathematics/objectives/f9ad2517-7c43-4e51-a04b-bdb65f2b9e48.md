## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about time in suitable problem situations and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: an outcome against expectation, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children disagree about who ran longer; one ran fast and one ran far, and they do it again with a sand timer. A child asks why it is still light at bedtime in summer, and the group talks about it. A child of 4 says that the one who came first or went furthest took the longest; a child of 7 keeps who was fastest apart from who took the most time and uses a count or a sand timer to show it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names no clock. The text does not explain *conflictsituaties* (conflict situations): the word can mean a situation in which the outcome goes against what a child expects, or a disagreement between children, and the wording does not choose. It does not say which situations are suitable. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the questions "When do you find that a minute lasts long? And when short?" and "If you both have your birthday on the same day, are you then also the same age?" ("Wanneer vind je een minuut lang duren? En wanneer kort? Als je allebei op dezelfde dag jarig bent, ben je dan ook even oud?"); it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks the one who went furthest or fastest took the most time. The child thinks a taller person is always the older one. The child holds that an activity took longer because it was dull.
