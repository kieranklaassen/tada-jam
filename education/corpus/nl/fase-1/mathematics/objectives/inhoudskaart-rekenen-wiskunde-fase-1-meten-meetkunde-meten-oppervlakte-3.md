## English gloss

dealing with opposites between concepts around area

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays a big sheet and a small sheet side by side and names the pair, then finds a cloth that is bigger than the table and one that is smaller. The child says the opposite when the teacher says one word of a pair. The child cuts a piece off a sheet and says that it is now smaller. A child of 4 names big and small for two flat things that differ a lot; a child of 6 uses bigger and smaller as a pair and says that if the blue mat is bigger than the red one, the red one is smaller.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about opposites between area concepts; it names no pairs. The card gives no example of a pair; big and small is the pack's example. No units are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child treats big and small as fixed properties and is confused that the same sheet is big next to one sheet and small next to another. The child cannot yet turn a comparison round, from bigger than to smaller than. The child means long and short when saying big and small.
