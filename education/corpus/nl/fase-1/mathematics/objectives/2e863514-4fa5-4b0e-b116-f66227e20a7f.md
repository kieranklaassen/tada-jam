## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

organising quantities up to at least 20 in a handy way (*handig*) by structuring, grouping, laying in a row

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child has a heap of 14 bottle caps and lays them in a row, or in groups of two or five, before counting. The child puts eggs in a box of ten or sticks in bundles, and moves counted objects to one side. A child of 4 lays the objects in a row and counts each one; a child of 7 makes groups of five or ten and uses them to find the total faster.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states its range as "tot ten minste 20" (up to at least 20): the offer reaches 20 at the least, no upper limit is set, and the wording does not say whether 20 itself is included. The goal names three ways of organising: structuring, grouping and laying in a row. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the structure of five and the structure of doubles ("vijfstructuur, dubbelstructuur"); it is an example, not a limit. The statement itself states no size for the groups.

### Common mistakes

The child counts a scattered heap without moving anything and counts some objects twice. The child makes groups of unequal size and then counts the groups as if they were equal. The child makes neat groups and then still counts every object one by one from one.
