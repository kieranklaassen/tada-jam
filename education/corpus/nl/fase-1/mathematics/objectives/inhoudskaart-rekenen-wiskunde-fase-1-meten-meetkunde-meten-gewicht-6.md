## Official wording

redeneren over wegen en gewichten in passende probleem- en conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about weighing and weights in suitable problem and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: a clash of ideas, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child predicts which is heavier, a large bag of cotton wool or a small bag of marbles, checks on the balance and says why the guess was right or wrong. The child presses a ball of clay flat, says whether it now weighs more, less or the same, and tests it. Two children disagree about which pan of the balance will go down, and each gives a reason before trying. A child of 4 guesses and tries; a child of 6 explains that a small thing can be heavier than a big thing and that changing the shape of the clay does not change its weight.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card does not explain *conflictsituaties*: the word can mean a situation in which what a child expects and what the child finds do not agree, or a disagreement between children. No situation is named. The statement mentions no units.

### Common mistakes

The child reasons that bigger means heavier. The child thinks a flattened or broken-up piece of clay weighs more or less than before. The child keeps the first prediction when the balance shows the opposite.
