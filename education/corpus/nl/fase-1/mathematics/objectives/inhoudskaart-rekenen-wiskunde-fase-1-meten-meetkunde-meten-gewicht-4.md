comparing and ordering by weight

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes two bags, one in each hand, and says which is heavier, then checks on a balance. The child puts three or four closed pots of the same size with different fillings in a row from lightest to heaviest by lifting them two at a time. The child finds two things that weigh the same. A child of 4 compares two things that differ clearly; a child of 6 orders three or more things and uses the balance when the hands cannot tell.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two actions are named, comparing and ordering. The statement sets no number of objects and names no instruments and no units. Informal weighing instruments are the card's statement Gewicht / 5.

### Common mistakes

The child orders by size instead of weight. When ordering three things the child compares two of them and puts the third at the end without comparing it. The child forgets which hand held which thing and names the wrong one.
