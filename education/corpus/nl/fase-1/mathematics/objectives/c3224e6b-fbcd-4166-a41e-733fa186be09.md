## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with concepts around indicating time (*tijdsaanduiding*) such as early/earlier (*vroeger*, also: in the past), late/later, on time, too late, back then (*toen*), now, in a while (*straks*), yesterday/today/tomorrow, it takes long-short, fast-slow, for a moment (*even*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the circle the child tells what they did yesterday, what they are doing today and what will happen tomorrow. The child says who came in early and who was late, waits "*even*" for a turn, and says whether tidying up took long or short. A child of 4 uses "now" and "*straks*", and uses "yesterday" for any day in the past; a child of 7 uses yesterday, today and tomorrow correctly and compares earlier with later and fast with slow.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The words after "zoals" are examples. *Vroeger* means earlier and also in the past; the text does not choose. Clock times and dates are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child uses "yesterday" for everything in the past and "tomorrow" for everything to come. The child mixes "fast" with "early" and "slow" with "late". The child mixes "long" for time with "long" for length.
