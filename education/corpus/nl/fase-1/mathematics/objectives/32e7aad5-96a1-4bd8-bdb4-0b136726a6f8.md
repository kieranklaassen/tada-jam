## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sits still with eyes closed for one turn of a sand timer and then plays for one turn, and says which felt longer. The child waits for a turn, takes one link a day off a paper chain until a party, and tells whether the holiday felt long or short. A child of 4 notices that waiting takes long; a child of 7 can say that the same time feels long when waiting and short when playing.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal is two words and is about experience. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: experiencing how long a minute lasts ("ervaren hoe lang een minuut duurt"); it is an example, not a limit. The statement itself names no means and no skill.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out that the time itself was the same, and say that the waiting "was longer" than the playing. Many give no thought to how long something took unless an adult asks.
