## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about length and perimeter (*omtrek*, the distance around something) in suitable problem situations and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: an outcome against expectation, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children measure the same carpet in steps and get 8 and 11; they find out how that can be. A rope lies curled beside a straight stick, and the children say which is longer and then pull the rope straight. A child of 4 says that the thing that reaches further is the longer one; a child of 7 explains that the steps differed in size and that a rope keeps its length when it is bent.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The text does not explain *conflictsituaties* (conflict situations): the word can mean a situation in which the outcome goes against what a child expects, or a disagreement between children, and the wording does not choose. It does not say which situations are suitable. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: questions such as "Do your clothes get too small, or do you get too big for your clothes?" and "What happens if we measure the same thing, but I with a short strip and you with a long strip?" ("Worden je kleren te klein of jij te groot voor je kleren? Wat gebeurt er als we hetzelfde meten, maar ik met een korte strook en jij met een lange strook?"); it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks one of two counts must be wrong, and not that the units differed. The child believes an object becomes longer when it is pushed forward past the other one. The child judges the distance around a thing by its height.
