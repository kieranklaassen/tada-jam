## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

shortened counting (*verkort tellen*, not counting every object one by one) up to at least 20

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees a group of five in a pile and counts on from there: six, seven, eight. The child counts pairs of socks by twos, or takes two full hands as ten and goes on with the loose fingers. A child of 4 counts every object from one; a child of 7 starts from a group they know at sight and counts on, or counts in twos or fives.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states its range as "tot ten minste 20" (up to at least 20): the offer reaches 20 at the least, no upper limit is set, and the wording does not say whether 20 itself is included. The statement itself names no shortened way of counting; jumps of 2, 5 and 10 are named in rw/gb/1/06/fase1 and number images in rw/gb/2/06/fase1. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: counting shoes in pairs ("schoenen in paren tellen"); it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child says the number of the known group again for the first loose object, which gives one too few. The child goes on in twos when single objects are left, so that a single object is counted as two. The child counts everything from one again as soon as the pile looks unfamiliar.
