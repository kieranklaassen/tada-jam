## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays one sheet on top of another to see which sticks out, and says which is bigger. The child puts three or four leaves, mats or pieces of card in a row from smallest to biggest. For two shapes that do not fit on each other the child covers each with the same tiles and counts them, or cuts one up and lays the pieces on the other. A child of 4 compares two flat things by laying one on the other; a child of 6 orders several, and compares surfaces of different shapes by covering them with equal tiles.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two actions are named, comparing and ordering. The statement sets no number of objects and names no shapes, instruments or units.

### Common mistakes

The child compares one side only and calls the longer or the taller shape the bigger one. The child thinks a shape gets bigger or smaller when it is turned, or when it is cut up and laid out differently. When counting tiles the child compares counts made with tiles of different sizes.
