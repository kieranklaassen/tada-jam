## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lifts a bag of sand and a bag of feathers and feels the difference in the arms. The child puts things on a balance and sees one pan go down, sits on a seesaw with a lighter child, and hangs two bags on the ends of a coat hanger to see which end dips. A child of 4 lifts, carries and says "heavy"; a child of 7 knows that weighing is finding out how heavy something is and that a balance shows it more surely than the hands.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names no instruments and no units; informal weighing instruments are named in rw/m/4/05/fase1. Grams and kilograms are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks big things are always heavy and small things always light. The child thinks the pan of the balance that goes up holds the heavier thing. The child thinks a thing gets heavier or lighter when it is held in another way.
