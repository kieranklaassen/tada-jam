## Official wording

vergelijken en ordenen van hoeveelheden maar ook van grotere hoeveelheden zonder precies tellen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering quantities but also larger quantities without counting exactly

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child counts two groups of buttons and says which has more, or lays them in two rows, one button opposite one button. The child puts three or four bowls of beads in order from fewest to most. With two large heaps of bricks the child looks and says which heap has more without counting them. A child of 4 compares two small groups by eye or by rows; a child of 6 compares by counting, orders several groups, and judges large heaps by eye.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement adds "larger quantities" and gives no number for them. The Dutch leaves open whether "without counting exactly" belongs to the larger quantities alone or to all comparing and ordering.

### Common mistakes

The child calls the group that takes up more space the larger one, also when its objects are only spread wider. The child calls the group with the bigger objects "more". When ordering three or more groups the child compares each group with its neighbour only and does not check the whole order.
