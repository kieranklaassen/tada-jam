## Official wording

meten met informele instrumenten en maten waarmee je inhoud kunt uitdrukken (blokken, bekers, flessen)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

measuring with informal instruments and measures with which you can express volume (*inhoud*, also capacity: how much fits inside) (blocks, cups, bottles)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child fills a small cup with water again and again, pours each one into a jug and makes a mark for each cup. The child fills a box with equal blocks, counts them and says "the box holds eight blocks". The child uses a bottle as a measure and finds out how many bottles fill the bucket. A child of 4 fills, pours and counts a few cups with help; a child of 6 fills the cup to the same height each time, counts all the cups and gives the result as a number of cups.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The instruments and measures are informal. The brackets name blocks, cups and bottles. The statement sets no number range for the count and mentions no litres or other standard units.

### Common mistakes

The child fills the measuring cup to a different height each time. The child miscounts by one because a cup is counted twice or not at all. The child compares two results that were measured with different cups, such as 4 large cups and 6 small cups, and says the second container holds more.
