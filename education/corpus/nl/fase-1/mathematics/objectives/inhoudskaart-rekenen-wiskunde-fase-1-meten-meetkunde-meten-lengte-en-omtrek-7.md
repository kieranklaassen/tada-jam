## Official wording

redeneren over lengte en omtrek in passende probleem- en conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about length and perimeter (*omtrek*, the distance around something) in suitable problem and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: a clash of ideas, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees two equal strips, one of them pushed forward, and says whether one is now longer; the child lays them level to check. Two children measure the same mat in steps and get different numbers, and they work out why. The child predicts whether a rope that goes round the small table will also go round the large one and explains the guess. A child of 4 judges by which end sticks out and checks by laying things side by side; a child of 6 explains that a rope stays as long when it is bent, and that bigger steps give a smaller number.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card does not explain *conflictsituaties*: the word can mean a situation in which what a child expects and what the child finds do not agree, or a disagreement between children. No situation is named. The statement mentions no units.

### Common mistakes

The child says a strip is longer because its end sticks out further. The child thinks a bent rope is shorter than the same rope laid straight. The child thinks a higher count means a longer object without asking which unit was used.
