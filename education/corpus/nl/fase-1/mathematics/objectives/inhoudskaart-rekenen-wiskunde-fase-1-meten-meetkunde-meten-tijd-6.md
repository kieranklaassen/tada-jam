## Official wording

aflezen van tijd (hele uren) zowel op een analoge als een digitale klok

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading off time (whole hours) both on an analogue and on a digital clock

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at the classroom clock when the long hand points straight up and reads the hour from the short hand: "it is three o'clock". The child sets a play clock to a whole hour that someone says and matches it to a card that shows 3:00. The child connects whole hours with the day: at twelve o'clock we eat. A child of 4 knows that the hands show the time and recognises one or two familiar hours with help; a child of 6 reads a whole hour on a clock with hands and on a digital clock.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets limit reading the time to whole hours; half hours, quarters and minutes are not part of the wording. Both an analogue and a digital clock are named. The statement does not say whether digital times after 12:00, such as 15:00, are included.

### Common mistakes

The child reads the hour from the long hand instead of the short hand. On a digital clock the child reads 3:00 as "three zero zero" or as three hundred. The child reads a time a little before or after the hour as a whole hour, without looking at the long hand.
