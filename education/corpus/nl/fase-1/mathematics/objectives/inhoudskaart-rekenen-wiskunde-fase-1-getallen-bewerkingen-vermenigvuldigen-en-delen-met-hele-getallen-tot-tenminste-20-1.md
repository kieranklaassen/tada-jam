## Official wording

handelend uitdelen en verdelen van hoeveelheden maar ook met grote hoeveelheden waarbij het om de handeling gaat

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

hands-on (*handelend*, through the child's own actions) handing out (*uitdelen*) and sharing out (*verdelen*) of quantities, but also with large quantities where the action is what matters

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gives every child at the table one cracker and goes round again while crackers remain. The child deals a pile of cards over three players in turns, or scoops a large tub of beads into four bowls spoon by spoon without counting them. A child of 4 hands out one thing to each person; a child of 6 deals out a counted amount in rounds and checks that every share is the same.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. This statement stands under the card's sub-heading for multiplying and dividing; the statement itself names only handing out and sharing out, and does not mention multiplying. It adds large quantities, for which it says the action is what matters, and gives no number for "large".

### Common mistakes

The child gives everyone a share but not the same number, and stops when the pile is gone. The child gives several objects at once to one person and one object to the next. When objects remain that cannot be shared equally, the child gives them all to one person or does not know what to do with them.
