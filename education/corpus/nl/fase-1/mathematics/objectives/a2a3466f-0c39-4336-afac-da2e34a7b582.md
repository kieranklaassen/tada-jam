## Official wording

handelend optellen, aftrekken en splitsen van hoeveelheden onder ten minste 20

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster rw/bew/1; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau a2a3466f-0c39-4336-afac-da2e34a7b582.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

hands-on (*handelend*, by acting with real things) adding, subtracting and splitting (*splitsen*, parting a quantity into parts) of quantities under at least 20

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts 3 more chestnuts with a pile of 8 and counts how many there are now, takes 4 away from a pile of 12 and counts what is left, and parts a row of 10 beads into two groups in different ways. The child does each of these with the objects in hand, not on paper. A child of 4 adds one or two objects to a small pile, or takes them away, and counts everything again; a child of 7 counts on from the first pile and names the two parts of a quantity under 20 without recounting both.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states its range as "onder ten minste 20" (under at least 20): the offer reaches 20 at the least, no upper limit is set, and the wording does not make clear whether 20 itself is included. The goal names three operations, adding, subtracting and splitting, done on quantities by acting. It does not mention written sums or the signs +, − and =.

### Common mistakes

The child counts everything again from one after adding, or counts the starting number as the first step when counting on and so ends one too low. After splitting a pile, the child believes that there are now more or fewer objects in all. The child does not keep the objects that were taken away apart from the rest and loses track of what is left.
