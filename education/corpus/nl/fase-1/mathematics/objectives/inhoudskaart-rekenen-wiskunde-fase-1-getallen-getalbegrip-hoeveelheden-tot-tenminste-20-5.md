## Official wording

handig organiseren van hoeveelheden door structureren, groeperen, op een rijtje leggen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

organising quantities in a handy way by structuring, grouping, laying in a row

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before counting a scattered pile of shells the child lays them in a row, or pushes them into pairs or groups of five. The child puts counters into an egg box, so that the number can be seen from the filled and the empty places. A child of 4 lays objects in a row so that none is counted twice; a child of 6 makes groups of two or five without being asked and uses the groups to say how many there are.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement names three ways: structuring, grouping and laying in a row. It does not say which structures or group sizes, and it names no material.

### Common mistakes

The child counts a scattered pile as it lies and no longer knows which objects were counted. The child makes groups but then counts every object one by one again. The child makes groups of unequal size and counts the groups as if they were equal.
