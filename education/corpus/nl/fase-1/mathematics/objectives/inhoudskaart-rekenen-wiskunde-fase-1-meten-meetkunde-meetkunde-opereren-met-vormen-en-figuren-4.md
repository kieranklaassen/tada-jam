## Official wording

experimenteren met vormen en figuren in spiegels en spiegeleffecten

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experimenting with shapes and figures in mirrors and mirror effects

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands a small mirror on half a drawn butterfly or next to a row of blocks and sees the whole figure or a double row. The child moves and turns the mirror and watches the picture change, and raises a hand in front of a large mirror to see which hand the reflection raises. With two mirrors held at an angle the child makes one block look like several. A child of 4 moves the mirror about and tells what they see; a child of 6 predicts where to put the mirror to make a whole figure or to make two blocks look like four.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experimenting: the statement asks for no result and no rule. Mirrors and mirror effects are named; the word symmetry is not used. No shapes or figures are named.

### Common mistakes

The child expects the mirror picture to be the same way round as the object and is confused that left and right are swapped. The child looks behind the mirror for the object. The child cannot yet predict what a mirror placed on a line will show and finds it only by trying.
