## Official wording

omgaan met begrippen rond getallen zoals groter, kleiner, grootste, kleinste, gelijk

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with concepts around numbers such as larger (*groter*), smaller (*kleiner*), largest (*grootste*), smallest (*kleinste*), equal (*gelijk*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds two number cards and says which number is larger, then lays three cards on the table and points at the largest and the smallest. The child rolls two dice with a partner and says "equal" when both show the same number. A child of 4 uses "larger" and "smaller" for two small numbers with objects beside them; a child of 6 uses all five words for written numbers without objects.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The five words after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples. The words are applied to numbers here; the card has a separate statement on words for quantities, Hoeveelheden / 4. The statement does not mention the signs <, > and =.

### Common mistakes

The child calls the number that is printed in larger type the larger number. The child uses "larger" correctly but answers "which is smaller?" by pointing at the larger number. The child judges a two-digit number by its separate digits and calls 9 larger than 12.
