## Official wording

construeren van ruimtelijke figuren met vrij en meetkundig constructiemateriaal

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

constructing three-dimensional figures (*ruimtelijke figuren*, literally spatial figures) with free and geometric construction material

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child models an animal from clay or builds a hut from boxes and cloth. With blocks, cubes or bricks that click together the child builds a tower, a bridge, a staircase or a house with a roof. A child of 4 stacks pieces and puts them in a row and names the result afterwards; a child of 6 plans a building, makes it stable and uses the shape of a block on purpose, for example a triangular block as a roof.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two kinds of material are named, free and geometric; the card explains neither and gives no examples. In Dutch early-years teaching free material usually means material without a fixed shape, such as clay, sand and boxes, and geometric material means blocks and other pieces with fixed shapes; the card itself does not say this. The statement names no figures to be made, no number of pieces and no size.

### Common mistakes

The child builds a tower on a small or uneven base, so it falls over. The child lays the blocks flat on the table as a picture when a standing figure is meant. The child puts the two supports of a bridge further apart than the block that has to lie across them is long.
