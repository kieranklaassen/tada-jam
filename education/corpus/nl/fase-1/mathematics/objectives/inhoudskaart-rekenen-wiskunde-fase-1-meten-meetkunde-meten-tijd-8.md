## Official wording

meten van tijd met (informele) tijdmeters

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

measuring time with (informal) timers (*tijdmeters*, instruments that measure time)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child turns over a sand timer and brushes teeth or tidies up until the sand has run through. The child counts aloud or claps while a classmate hops across the playground, and compares the counts of two turns. The child watches the coloured disc of a timer get smaller, or listens for the kitchen timer to ring. A child of 4 waits until the sand has gone and says that the time is over; a child of 6 finds out which of two activities lasts longer by counting or by the number of times the sand timer was turned.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The word informal is in brackets, so both informal timers and other timers fit the wording; none is named. The statement mentions no seconds, minutes or hours and no stopwatch.

### Common mistakes

The child counts faster one time and slower the next, so the two counts cannot be compared. The child starts the timer after the activity has begun, or turns the sand timer before it is empty. The child thinks an activity lasted longer because it was dull, or shorter because it was fun, against what the timer showed.
