## Official wording

redeneren over hoeveelheden in passende probleem- en conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about quantities in suitable problem and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: a clash of ideas, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees a row of six counters spread out and a row of six pushed together, and says whether one has more and how they know. The child works out whether there are enough chairs for the children in the circle and explains the answer. A child of 4 answers by what is seen and checks by counting when asked; a child of 6 argues that nothing was added or taken away, so the number is the same.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement does not say what a suitable problem situation or a conflict situation is; the Dutch *conflictsituatie* can be read as a situation in which two ideas or outcomes clash, or as a disagreement between children.

### Common mistakes

The child says that the longer row has more although both rows have the same number. The child counts again after the objects are moved and expects another number. The child gives the answer without a reason.
