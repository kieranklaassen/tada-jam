## English gloss

exploring and measuring measurements of one's own body

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands against the wall while another child marks their height, lies on a roll of paper while someone draws round them, and cuts a ribbon as long as their arm or as long as the distance around their head. The child compares their foot with a classmate's cut-out foot and looks for things in the room that are as long as their own hand span. A child of 4 compares hand on hand and back to back; a child of 7 measures height, foot and span with strips or cubes and gives the result as a number.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: shoe size and height, the length of the body ("schoenmaat, lengte"); it is an example, not a limit. The goal names no particular measurements of the body, no instruments and no units.

### Common mistakes

The child stands on tiptoe or bends while being measured. The child lays the ribbon loosely or at a slant around the head or the waist. The child thinks the oldest child must be the tallest.
