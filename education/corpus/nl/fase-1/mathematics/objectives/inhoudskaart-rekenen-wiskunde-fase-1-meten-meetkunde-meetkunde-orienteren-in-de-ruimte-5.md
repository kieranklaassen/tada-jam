onderzoeken en ontwerpen van eenvoudige 'routes'

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and designing simple 'routes'

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child walks the way from the classroom to the gym and says what they pass: through the door, past the coats, along the corridor. The child lays a route on the floor with rope or arrows for another child to follow, or drives a toy car along a path on a play mat from the house to the shop. The child compares two ways to the same place and says which is longer. A child of 4 follows and tells a short familiar route by its landmarks; a child of 6 designs a route with several turns, describes it in order and draws it as a line with a few landmarks.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The routes are to be simple; the card puts the word routes in quotation marks and does not explain it. Two actions are named, investigating and designing. The statement mentions no map, no grid, no compass directions and no left and right.

### Common mistakes

The child names the landmarks of a route in the wrong order or leaves out a turn. The child gives the start and the end and nothing in between. On the way back the child expects the turns to go the same way as on the way there.
