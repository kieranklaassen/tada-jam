## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with concepts around *inhoud* (capacity, volume or contents) such as full/fuller/equally full, empty/emptier/equally empty, much, more/most/the same amount (*evenveel*)/too much, little, less/least/too little, enough

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

When drinks are poured the child says whose cup is fuller and makes two cups equally full. The child says when the jug is empty, when there is too much sand for the bucket, when there is too little water to fill the bottle, and when it is enough. A child of 4 uses "full", "empty" and "more"; a child of 7 uses "fuller", "equally full", "less", "too much", "too little" and "enough" when comparing two containers.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The Dutch *inhoud* can mean capacity (how much something can hold), volume (how much space something takes up) or contents (how much is in something); the wording does not choose. The words after "zoals" are examples. The source prints "vol/voller, /even vol", with a comma before the slash; the gloss gives it as full/fuller/equally full. No unit is among the examples.

### Common mistakes

The child judges "more" by the height of the liquid only. The child knows "more" and not yet "less". The child mixes "equally full" (the same level) with "the same amount" when the containers differ in width.
