## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about weighing and weights in suitable problem situations and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: an outcome against expectation, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A big ball of wool and a small stone lie ready; the children say which will be heavier and then weigh them. A lump of clay is weighed, pressed flat and weighed again, and the child says whether it has become heavier. A child of 4 is surprised and repeats the weighing; a child of 7 explains that size does not decide the weight and that the same clay weighs the same in another shape.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names no instruments and no units. The text does not explain *conflictsituaties* (conflict situations): the word can mean a situation in which the outcome goes against what a child expects, or a disagreement between children, and the wording does not choose. It does not say which situations are suitable. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question "Is something that is bigger also always heavier?" ("Is iets dat groter is ook altijd zwaarder?"); it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child holds that the bigger thing must be the heavier one. The child believes the weight changes when the shape changes or when a thing is broken into pieces. The child distrusts the balance when it shows something other than what they expected.
