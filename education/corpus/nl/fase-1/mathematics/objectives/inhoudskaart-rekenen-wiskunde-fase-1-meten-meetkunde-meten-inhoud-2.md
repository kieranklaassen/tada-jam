## English gloss

dealing with concepts around volume (*inhoud*, also capacity: how much fits inside)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While pouring, the child uses the words full, empty, half full, more, less and the same amount, and says "it fits" or "it does not fit in". The child says that the bucket holds more than the cup and that the bottle is nearly empty. At snack time the child says whose cup has the most. A child of 4 uses full and empty; a child of 6 uses half full, nearly full, more, less and the same amount, and says how many cups fit in.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names no concepts and gives no examples. Opposites between these concepts are the card's statement Inhoud / 3, and comparing and ordering by volume is Inhoud / 4. No units are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child calls a glass full as soon as there is any drink in it. The child says big and small for how much a container holds, and means its height. The child says "more" for the higher water level without looking at how wide the container is.
