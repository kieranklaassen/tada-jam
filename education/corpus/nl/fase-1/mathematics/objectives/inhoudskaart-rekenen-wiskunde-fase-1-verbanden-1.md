## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

using bar charts to order and compare quantities and information

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The children each put one block on the stack for their favourite fruit, and the child looks at the stacks and says which fruit was chosen most and which least. The child colours one square for each rainy day and each sunny day and compares the two bars at the end of the week. A child of 4 points at the tallest stack and says "the most"; a child of 6 puts the bars in order, says how many each stands for and how many more one has than another.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names bar charts and two uses, ordering and comparing. It sets no number range and no number of bars. It does not mention a numbered axis or a scale in which one square stands for more than one.

### Common mistakes

The child compares bars that do not start at the same line or whose squares differ in size. The child reads the tallest bar as the best or the winner, not as the largest number. The child says which bar is taller but cannot say how many it stands for.
