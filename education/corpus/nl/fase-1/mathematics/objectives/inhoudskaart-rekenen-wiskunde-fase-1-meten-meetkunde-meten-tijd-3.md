## Official wording

omgaan met dagritme, weekritme en jaarritme en cyclische tijdsaanduidingen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with the rhythm of the day, the rhythm of the week and the rhythm of the year and cyclical indications of time

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at the day chart with pictures, says what comes after lunch and turns over the card of what is finished. The child knows that gym is on a fixed day, that the weekend comes after Friday and that the week starts again after Sunday. The child talks about what comes back every year: their birthday, the seasons, the festivals in winter. A child of 4 follows the rhythm of the day and knows what comes next; a child of 6 says that the days of the week and the seasons come back in the same order again and again.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three rhythms are named: of the day, of the week and of the year. Cyclical indications of time are named with them and are not explained. The statement does not mention clock times or dates. The names of days, months and seasons are the examples of the card's statement Tijd / 1.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks the week or the year ends and does not see that it starts again. The child expects a yearly event, such as a birthday, to come back as soon as a weekly one. The child knows the order of an ordinary school day from memory and cannot yet find a changed order on the day chart.
