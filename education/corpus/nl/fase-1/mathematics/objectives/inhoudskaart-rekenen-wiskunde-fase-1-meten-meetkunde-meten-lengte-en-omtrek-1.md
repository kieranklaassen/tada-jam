## Official wording

ontdekken en ervaren van het meten van lengte en omtrek

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering and experiencing the measuring of length and perimeter (*omtrek*, the distance around something)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lies on the floor next to a rope to see which is longer, and walks along the edge of the sandpit to feel how far it is all the way round. The child lays blocks end to end along a table or steps heel to toe across the room. The child puts a ribbon round a tree trunk or round their own head and then stretches it out. A child of 4 holds two things side by side and walks round things; a child of 6 lays a row of equal blocks along an object and counts them, and uses a string to find how far it is round something.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verbs are discovering and experiencing: the statement asks for no result. It names no instruments or units; informal instruments and measures for length are named in the card's statement Lengte en omtrek / 5. Length and perimeter are named together and are not defined.

### Common mistakes

The child leaves gaps between the blocks or lets them overlap. The child does not start at the end of the object. The child thinks "round" is only for round things and does not see that a table also has a distance round it.
