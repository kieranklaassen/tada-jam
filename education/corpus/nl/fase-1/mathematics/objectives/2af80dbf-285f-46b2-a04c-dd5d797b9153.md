## English gloss

dealing with 'area' and concepts around area such as big/bigger/biggest/equally big, small/smaller/smallest/equally small

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays two cloths on each other and says which is bigger, and finds two sheets that are equally big. The child chooses the biggest leaf from a pile, and the smallest piece of paper that still covers a card. A child of 4 uses "big" and "small" for flat things; a child of 7 uses "bigger", "smallest" and "equally big" for how much surface a thing covers, and begins to use the word area.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The word 'area' is itself part of the goal. The words after "zoals" are examples: the degrees of big and of small, each with its "equally" form. No units are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child calls the longer or taller of two shapes the bigger one, although it covers less. The child does not know which size "big" refers to: the length, the height or the surface. The child calls two shapes of different form unequal in size without laying them on each other.
