Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing and investigating geometric shapes

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child picks all the triangles out of a box of mixed shapes and says how they can tell: three corners, three straight sides. The child traces the edge of a shape with a finger, counts its corners, turns it round and checks whether it is still the same shape. The child finds circles, squares and rectangles in the classroom, such as a clock or a window. A child of 4 matches shapes that look alike and names the circle; a child of 6 tells a square from a rectangle and from a triangle by counting sides and corners, also when the shape is turned.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names no shapes and does not say whether flat shapes, solids or both are meant. Two actions are named, distinguishing and investigating; naming the shapes is not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child accepts a triangle only when it has a side at the bottom and sides of about equal length, and rejects a long thin triangle or a turned one. The child calls a square that stands on a corner a different shape. The child calls any shape with a point a triangle, or any rounded shape a circle.
