## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

constructing three-dimensional figures (*ruimtelijke figuren*) with free construction material (e.g. with clay) and geometric construction material (e.g. building with blocks)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child rolls clay into a ball and a long roll and presses a lump into the shape of a box. With wooden blocks the child builds a tower, a wall or a bridge, looks at the work from all sides and changes it until it stands. A child of 4 presses, stacks and names the result afterwards; a child of 7 plans what to make, forms a ball or a roll on purpose and chooses the blocks for each part of a building.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names two kinds of material: free construction material (*vrij constructiemateriaal*) and geometric construction material (*meetkundig constructiemateriaal*). Each has one example in brackets after "bijv.": clay for the first, building with blocks for the second. These are examples; the goal does not explain the two terms further, and it names no figures. Building a copy of an example is in rw/mk/2/04/fase1 and constructing with paper in rw/mk/2/05/fase1.

### Common mistakes

The child makes something that looks right from the front only and is flat or open at the back. The child builds high on a small base, so that the work falls over. The child joins two parts of clay at one small spot, so that they come apart.
