## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reading off time (whole hours) both on an analogue and on a digital clock

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at the clock with hands, sees the long hand at the 12 and the short hand at the 3, and says "three o'clock". The child reads 3:00 on a digital clock and sets the hands of a practice clock to the same time. The child links a time to the day: at twelve o'clock we eat. A child of 4 knows a few fixed times by the place of the hands, as a picture; a child of 7 reads every whole hour on both kinds of clock.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets say whole hours: half hours, quarters and minutes are not named. Both kinds of clock are named, analogue and digital. The goal does not say whether the digital clock shows 12 or 24 hours.

### Common mistakes

The child mixes up the long hand and the short hand and reads three o'clock as twelve o'clock or as a quarter past twelve. The child reads 3:00 as "three hundred" or "three, zero, zero". The child does not link 3:00 on the digital clock with the hands at three as the same time.
