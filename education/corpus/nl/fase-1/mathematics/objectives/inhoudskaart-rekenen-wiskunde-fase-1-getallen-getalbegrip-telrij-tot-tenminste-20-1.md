## Official wording

de telrij opzeggen (akoestisch tellen), de structuur van de telrij verkennen en zo ver mogelijk tellen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reciting the counting sequence (acoustic counting, *akoestisch tellen*), exploring the structure of the counting sequence and counting as far as possible

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says the number words in order, in a counting rhyme, while climbing stairs or while waiting for a turn. The child notices that the words after twenty use the words for one to nine again (*eenentwintig*, *tweeëntwintig*). The child counts on as far as they can and asks what comes next. A child of 4 recites to about ten and sometimes leaves out or swaps a word; a child of 6 recites past twenty and uses the repeating pattern to go on.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement itself says "as far as possible" (*zo ver mogelijk*) and names no number. The brackets name *akoestisch tellen*, saying the number words in order without counting objects. The statement does not say what the structure of the counting sequence is.

### Common mistakes

The child skips or swaps number words, most often between ten and twenty, where the Dutch words *elf*, *twaalf*, *dertien* and *veertien* are irregular. The child stops at a change of tens, such as after 19 or 29, and does not know which word comes next. The child recites the words as one string and cannot start anywhere but at one.
