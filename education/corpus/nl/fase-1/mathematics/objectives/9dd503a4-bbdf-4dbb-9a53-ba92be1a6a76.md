## Official wording

de telrij opzeggen (akoestisch tellen) tot ten minste 20, de structuur van de telrij verkennen en zo ver mogelijk tellen

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reciting the counting sequence (acoustic counting, *akoestisch tellen*) up to at least 20, exploring the structure of the counting sequence and counting as far as possible

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says the number words aloud in order, in a counting rhyme, while climbing stairs or while waiting for a turn, with no objects to count. The child hears that after twenty the words for one to nine return (in Dutch *eenentwintig*, *tweeëntwintig*) and tries how far they can go. A child of 4 says the words to about ten and may skip or swap some after that; a child of 7 counts past 20 through the tens and notices the part that repeats.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states its range as "tot ten minste 20" (up to at least 20): the offer reaches 20 at the least, no upper limit is set, and the wording does not say whether 20 itself is included. Beyond that the goal says "as far as possible" and sets no end. This goal is about saying the sequence; counting objects is in rw/gb/2/01/fase1. The goal does not say which structure of the sequence is explored.

### Common mistakes

The child skips or swaps words between ten and twenty, where the Dutch words are irregular (*elf*, *twaalf*, *dertien*, *veertien*). The child stops at a number that ends in nine (29, 39) and does not know which ten comes next. The child says the sequence as one run of sounds in which the separate number words are not yet told apart.
