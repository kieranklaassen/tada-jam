## Official wording

(her)kennen van getalbeelden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing and recognising (*(her)kennen*) number images (*getalbeelden*, fixed arrangements such as dots on dice)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child rolls a die and says the number without counting the dots. The child sees four fingers held up, a domino half, or five counters laid as on a die, and names the number at once. The child lays out counters in the pattern of a given number. A child of 4 knows the die patterns for one, two and three; a child of 6 knows the die patterns to six and finger patterns to ten, and sees a larger pattern as two smaller ones.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement does not say which number images: it names no dice, fingers or other arrangements, and no highest number. The source prints "(her)kennen", which joins *kennen* (knowing) and *herkennen* (recognising).

### Common mistakes

The child counts the dots one by one every time. The child knows a number in one arrangement only, and does not recognise five when the dots lie in a row. The child confuses patterns that look alike, such as the die patterns for four and five.
