## Official wording

redeneren over getallen in passende probleem- en conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about numbers in suitable problem and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: a clash of ideas, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says why 8 is more than 6, and shows it with fingers, blocks or the number line when another child doubts it. The child reacts when a puppet or an adult puts number cards down as 5, 7, 6, and explains what is wrong. A child of 4 says that something is wrong and shows the right order by counting; a child of 6 gives a reason in words, such as "eight comes after six, so it is more".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement does not say what a suitable problem situation or a conflict situation is; the Dutch *conflictsituatie* can be read as a situation in which two ideas or outcomes clash, or as a disagreement between children.

### Common mistakes

The child gives a correct answer but no reason, or repeats the answer as the reason. The child accepts a wrong statement because an adult made it. The child reasons from how a number looks, such as the size of the print, not from its value.
