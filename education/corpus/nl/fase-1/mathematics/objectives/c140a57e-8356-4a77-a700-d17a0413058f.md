## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

measuring with informal instruments and measures with which you can express *inhoud* (capacity, volume or contents) (blocks, cups, bottles)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child fills a bucket with cups of water, makes a mark for each cup and then says "the bucket holds nine cups". The child fills a box with equal blocks and counts them, and finds how many small bottles fill a large one. A child of 4 scoops and pours and counts some of the scoops; a child of 7 fills the measure the same each time, counts every one, and compares two containers by their numbers of cups.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The Dutch *inhoud* can mean capacity (how much something can hold), volume (how much space something takes up) or contents (how much is in something); the wording does not choose. The brackets name blocks, cups and bottles. The instruments and measures are called informal: litres, millilitres and a measuring jug with a scale are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child uses cups that are filled to different levels. The child changes the measure halfway, from a cup to a bigger cup, and adds the counts together. The child compares "nine cups" with "four bottles" as numbers, without regard to the size of the measure.
