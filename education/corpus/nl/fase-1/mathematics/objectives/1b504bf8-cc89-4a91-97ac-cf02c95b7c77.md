## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about *inhouden* (capacities, volumes or contents) in suitable problem situations and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: an outcome against expectation, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The same amount of lemonade is poured into a wide glass and a narrow glass, and two children disagree about who has more. The child pours both back into two equal cups to settle it. A child of 4 points to the glass with the higher level; a child of 7 explains that the level in the wide glass is lower and that the amount is the same.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The Dutch *inhouden* is the plural of *inhoud*, which can mean capacity (how much something can hold), volume (how much space something takes up) or contents (how much is in something); the wording does not choose. The statement itself names no units. The text does not explain *conflictsituaties* (conflict situations): the word can mean a situation in which the outcome goes against what a child expects, or a disagreement between children, and the wording does not choose. It does not say which situations are suitable. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question "Does more water always go into a tall vase than into a low vase?" ("Gaat er in een hoge vaas altijd meer water dan in een lage vaas?"); it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child keeps to the higher level as "more" after seeing the pouring. The child thinks that pouring changes the amount. The child looks at one dimension only, the height or the width.
