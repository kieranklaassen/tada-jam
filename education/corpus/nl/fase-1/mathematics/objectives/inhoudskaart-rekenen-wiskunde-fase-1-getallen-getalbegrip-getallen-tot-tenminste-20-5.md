## Official wording

onderzoeken van getalrelaties zoals buurgetallen, groot of klein verschil

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating number relations such as neighbouring numbers (*buurgetallen*, the numbers just before and after), large or small difference

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child points at a number on a number line and names its two neighbours, or holds up the cards that belong on each side of a given card. The child builds block towers of 8 and 9 and of 3 and 9, and says for which pair the difference is small and for which it is large. A child of 4 names the number that comes after a small number; a child of 6 names both neighbours of any number in the range and says whether two numbers differ by a little or by a lot.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The two relations after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples: neighbouring numbers, and a large or small difference. The statement says large or small difference; it does not ask for the difference as a number.

### Common mistakes

The child names the number after a given number but not the number before it. The child takes "difference" to mean any way in which two towers or cards are unlike, such as colour, not how many more one has. The child judges the difference by the size of the numbers, and calls the difference between 18 and 19 large because both numbers are large.
