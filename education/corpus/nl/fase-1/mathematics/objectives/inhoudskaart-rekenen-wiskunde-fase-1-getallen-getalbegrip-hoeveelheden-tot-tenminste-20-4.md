## Official wording

omgaan met begrippen rond hoeveelheden zoals meer/minder, meeste/minste, evenveel, weinig/veel, alles/niets, ongeveer, hoeveel

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with concepts around quantities such as more/fewer (*meer/minder*), most/fewest (*meeste/minste*), the same number (*evenveel*), few/many (*weinig/veel*), all/nothing (*alles/niets*), about (*ongeveer*), how many (*hoeveel*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child uses these words while handling things: "you have more than I have", "this bowl has the fewest", "we have the same number", "there are about ten". The child asks and answers "how many?" at the fruit table or when tidying up, and points at the group with the most. A child of 4 uses "more", "many", "all" and "nothing"; a child of 6 also uses "fewer", "the fewest", "the same number" and "about" when comparing two or three groups.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The words after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples: *meer/minder*, *meeste/minste*, *evenveel*, *weinig/veel*, *alles/niets*, *ongeveer*, *hoeveel*. In Dutch *meer*, *minder*, *veel*, *weinig* and *hoeveel* are used both for things one counts and for amounts such as sand or water. The statement is about words in use and does not mention written signs.

### Common mistakes

The child understands "more" well before "fewer", and answers "which has fewer?" by pointing at the group with more. The child takes "the same number" to mean the same kind of things. The child answers "how many?" by counting aloud again without ending on one number.
