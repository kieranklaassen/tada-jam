## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays one sheet on another to see which sticks out, and puts four cloths in order from smallest to biggest. For two shapes that do not fit on each other, the child covers each with the same tiles and counts. A child of 4 lays two things on each other when one fits within the other; a child of 7 compares shapes of different form by covering them, or by cutting one and laying the pieces on the other, and orders several.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: by eye, laying one on the other ("via 'op het oog', op elkaar leggen"); it is an example, not a limit. Covering is named in rw/m/2/01/fase1. The goal names no shapes and no units, and it states no number of objects.

### Common mistakes

The child compares one side only and calls the longer shape the bigger one. The child believes a shape gets bigger or smaller when it is cut and its pieces are laid in another way. The child lays shapes on each other without lining up an edge, so that both stick out and nothing can be decided.
