## Official wording

handelend omgaan met begrippen rond optellen, aftrekken en splitsen zoals erbij tellen, weghalen, erbij, nog eentje, eraf, weg, samen, over

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing hands-on (*handelend*, by acting with real things) with concepts around adding, subtracting and splitting such as counting on to it (*erbij tellen*), taking away (*weghalen*), added (*erbij*), one more (*nog eentje*), taken off (*eraf*), gone (*weg*), together (*samen*), left over (*over*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts two more cars in the garage when someone says "*erbij*", takes one out at "*eraf*", and says "*nog eentje*" when adding one block to a tower. The child pushes two piles into one and says how many there are "*samen*", and after handing out biscuits says how many are "*over*". A child of 4 acts on the words when an adult says them; a child of 7 uses the words to tell what was done with a quantity.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The eight expressions after "zoals" are examples: *erbij tellen*, *weghalen*, *erbij*, *nog eentje*, *eraf*, *weg*, *samen*, *over*. The words are met while acting with objects. This goal states no number range. The words plus and minus and the written signs for them are not among its examples.

### Common mistakes

The child takes "*erbij*" as a signal to count everything again and not as a change in the quantity. The child uses "*weg*" and "*over*" for the same pile, so that it is unclear whether the part taken or the part that remains is meant. The child understands "*samen*" only as doing something with another child and not as two quantities joined.
