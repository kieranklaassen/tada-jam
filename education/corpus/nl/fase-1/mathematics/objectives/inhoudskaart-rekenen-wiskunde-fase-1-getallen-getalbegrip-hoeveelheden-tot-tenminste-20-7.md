## Official wording

verkort tellen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

shortened counting (*verkort tellen*, not counting every object one by one)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees a group of five at once and counts on from there: "five, six, seven". The child counts a row of shoes in twos, or counts on from the number on the first die when two dice are rolled. A child of 4 counts every object from one; a child of 6 starts from a group that is recognised or already counted and counts only the rest.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement is two words and names no way of shortening the count. Counting in jumps of 2, 5 and 10 is the card's statement Telrij / 6, and number images are the subject of Hoeveelheden / 6.

### Common mistakes

The child starts again at one although part of the group is already known. When counting on, the child says the starting number again for the first new object and ends one too low. The child says the row of twos but points at single objects.
