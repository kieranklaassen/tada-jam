## Official wording

vergelijken en ordenen van hoeveelheden tot ten minste 20 maar ook van grotere hoeveelheden zonder precies tellen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster rw/gb/2; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau a9357f6e-1801-45cd-b570-42993c24a14a.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering quantities up to at least 20 but also larger quantities without counting precisely

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays two rows of buttons side by side, one against one, and sees which row has buttons left. The child puts three dishes of beads in order from fewest to most. With two big heaps of leaves or two full jars of marbles, the child says by looking which has more. A child of 4 compares two piles that differ clearly; a child of 7 compares piles that differ by one or two by counting or pairing, and orders several piles.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states its range as "tot ten minste 20" (up to at least 20): the offer reaches 20 at the least, no upper limit is set, and the wording does not say whether 20 itself is included. The words "without counting precisely" stand after the larger quantities; the wording does not make clear whether they also apply to quantities up to 20. No size is given for the larger quantities.

### Common mistakes

The child says that the row that is longer or spread wider has more. The child judges by the size of the objects and calls three large blocks more than five small ones. The child counts both piles correctly and still does not know which number is more.
