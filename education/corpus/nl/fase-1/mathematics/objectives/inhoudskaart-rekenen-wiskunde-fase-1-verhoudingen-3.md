## Official wording

redeneren over (kwalitatieve) verhoudingen in passende probleem- en conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about (qualitative) proportions (*verhoudingen*, also: ratios) in suitable problem and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: a clash of ideas, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees a small bear on a large chair and a large bear on a small chair and says what is wrong and how to put it right. The child mixes syrup and water in two cups and says which will taste sweeter and why, or says why a giant needs a longer bed than a gnome. A child of 4 says "too big" or "too small" and swaps the things; a child of 6 gives a reason that names both sides, such as "more water with the same syrup makes it less sweet".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets around "qualitative" (*kwalitatieve*) are in the wording: proportions without numbers are named, and the statement does not say whether proportions with numbers are also meant. The statement does not say what a suitable problem situation or a conflict situation is; the Dutch *conflictsituatie* can be read as a situation in which two ideas or outcomes clash, or as a disagreement between children. The statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

The child looks at one of the two things only, for example the amount of syrup and not the amount of water. The child says that something is wrong but cannot say what would make it fit. The child thinks that twice as much of both syrup and water gives a sweeter drink.
