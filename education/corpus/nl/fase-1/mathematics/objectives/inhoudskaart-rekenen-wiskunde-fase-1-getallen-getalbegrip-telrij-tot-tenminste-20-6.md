## Official wording

tellen met sprongen (2, 5, 10)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

counting in jumps (2, 5, 10)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child counts pairs of shoes in twos, the fingers of raised hands in fives and bundles of ten sticks in tens. The child jumps along number tiles onto every second tile and says each number reached. A child of 4 joins in saying "two, four, six" with the group without linking it to objects; a child of 6 counts real pairs or groups of five in jumps and gets the same total as by counting one by one.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The brackets name 2, 5 and 10. The statement does not say from which number the jumps start, how far they go, or whether they also go backwards. It does not mention multiplication tables.

### Common mistakes

The child says the jump numbers as a rhyme but points at single objects, so the last number said is not the number of objects. The child leaves the pattern after a few jumps and continues counting by one. The child can say the row of twos only from two, and cannot go on from another number in the row such as eight.
