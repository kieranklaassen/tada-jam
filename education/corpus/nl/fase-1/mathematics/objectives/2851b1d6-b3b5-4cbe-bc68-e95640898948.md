## Official wording

vergelijken en ordenen op inhoud zoals 'op het oog', afpassen, naast elkaar houden, overgieten (zand), vullen (blokken) en uitgieten

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering by *inhoud* (capacity, volume or contents) such as 'by eye', measuring off (*afpassen*), holding side by side, pouring from one into the other (sand), filling (blocks) and pouring out

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at two pots and says which holds more, then fills one with sand and pours it into the other to see whether it overflows or leaves room. The child fills two boxes with blocks and counts which takes more, and pours two bottles out into equal glasses to compare. A child of 4 pours from one into the other and sees it overflow; a child of 7 puts three or four containers in order from the one that holds least to the one that holds most and checks by pouring.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The Dutch *inhoud* can mean capacity (how much something can hold), volume (how much space something takes up) or contents (how much is in something); the wording does not choose. The six ways after "zoals" are examples: by eye, measuring off, holding side by side, pouring over, filling, and pouring out; the brackets name sand with pouring over and blocks with filling. No units and no number of containers are stated.

### Common mistakes

The child chooses the tallest container as the one that holds most and ignores the width. The child believes there is more water after it has been poured into a tall, narrow glass. The child spills, or does not fill to the top, and reaches a wrong result.
