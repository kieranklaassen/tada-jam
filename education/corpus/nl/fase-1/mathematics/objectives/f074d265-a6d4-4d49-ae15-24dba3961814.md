## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing and recognising (*(her)kennen*) number images (*getalbeelden*, fixed patterns that show a quantity) up to at least 20

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child throws a die and says the number without counting the dots. The child shows seven with fingers as five and two, sees at once how many eggs are in a box of ten, and reads a quantity on a bead frame with two rows of ten. A child of 4 knows the dot patterns of a die up to about four; a child of 7 knows patterns up to 20 that are built on fives and tens.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states its range as "tot ten minste 20" (up to at least 20): the offer reaches 20 at the least, no upper limit is set, and the wording does not say whether 20 itself is included. The statement itself does not say which images are meant (dots as on a die, tally marks or others). The content-line document prints an example with this goal: seeing without counting that three fingers are held up ("zonder te tellen zien dat er drie vingers zijn opgestoken"); it is an example, not a limit. The Dutch "(her)kennen" covers both knowing and recognising.

### Common mistakes

The child counts the dots one by one although the pattern is known. The child knows a pattern in one layout only and does not recognise five when the dots lie in a row. The child names a pattern by its outline and says "four" for a square of four dots with a fifth in the middle.
