## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with 'length' and 'perimeter' (*omtrek*, the distance around something) and concepts around length and perimeter such as big/bigger/biggest, small/smaller/smallest, equally big, equally small, long/longer/longest, short/shorter/shortest, equally long, high/higher/highest, low/lower/lowest, equally high, thin/thinner/thinnest, thick/thicker/thickest, equally thick, around (it) (*(er) omheen*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays two ribbons side by side and says which is longer, builds a higher and a lower tower, and picks the thickest and the thinnest pencil. The child walks around the table and lays a rope around a hoop, saying "*eromheen*". A child of 4 uses "big", "small", "long" and "high"; a child of 7 uses the comparing forms and "equally long", picks the word that fits the kind of size, and uses the words length and perimeter.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The words 'length' and 'perimeter' are themselves part of the goal. The words after "zoals" are examples: the degrees of big, small, long, short, high, low, thin and thick, the forms with "equally", and *(er) omheen*. Wide and narrow are not among the examples. No units are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child uses "big" for long, high and thick alike. The child mixes "long" and "high" for the same object when it is laid down or stood up. The child calls the longer of two things also the thicker one, because it is "bigger".
