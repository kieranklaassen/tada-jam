representing quantities

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shows how many there are in another form: holds up as many fingers, lays as many counters, draws as many dots or tally marks, or picks the matching number card. The child draws on paper how many cars are parked in the play corner so that another child can read it later. A child of 4 shows a small number with fingers or by laying one counter for each object; a child of 6 uses tally marks, a dot pattern or a written number and reads it back.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement does not say with what a quantity is represented; fingers, counters, drawings, tally marks and digits are not named.

### Common mistakes

The child draws the objects themselves in detail and forgets the number while drawing. The child makes marks without matching one mark to one object, so the number of marks differs from the number of objects. The child writes a digit but cannot show the same number with objects.
