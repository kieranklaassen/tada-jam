## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

distinguishing and investigating geometric shapes such as circle, rectangle, square, triangle, rhombus (*ruit*, a diamond shape), sphere, cube

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child feels shapes in a cloth bag and takes out the one that is asked for, counts the corners and sides of a triangle and a square with a finger, and lets a sphere and a cube go down a slope. The child looks for the shapes in the room: a round clock, a rectangular door. A child of 4 picks out circle, square and triangle among clear examples; a child of 7 tells a square from a rectangle and from a rhombus, a sphere from a circle, and says what makes a shape that shape.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The seven shapes after "zoals" are examples: five flat ones (circle, rectangle, square, triangle, rhombus) and two solid ones (sphere, cube). The verbs are distinguishing and investigating; the wording does not use the verb naming. No properties of the shapes are listed.

### Common mistakes

The child recognises a triangle only when it has equal sides and lies on a flat base, and not when it is long, thin or turned. The child no longer calls a square a square when it stands on a corner. The child calls every shape with four corners a square.
