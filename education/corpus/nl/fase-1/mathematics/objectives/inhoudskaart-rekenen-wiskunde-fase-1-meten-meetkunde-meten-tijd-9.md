## Official wording

redeneren over tijd in passende probleem- en conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about time in suitable problem and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: a clash of ideas, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says whether there is enough time to finish a puzzle before going home and gives a reason, such as "the sand timer is nearly empty". Two children disagree about who took longer to walk round the playground; they do it again while counting and compare. The child works out from the week chart whether gym is before or after the day of the outing. A child of 4 says that something took long because they got tired of it; a child of 6 uses the timer, the chart or the order of events as a reason.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card does not explain *conflictsituaties*: the word can mean a situation in which what a child expects and what the child finds do not agree, or a disagreement between children. No situation is named. The statement does not say which part of time the reasoning is about: order, how long something lasts, the clock or the calendar.

### Common mistakes

The child judges how long something took by how it felt and not by a timer or a count. The child thinks that going further always takes longer, also when two children started and stopped at the same moment. The child goes in the wrong direction from yesterday or tomorrow when working out which day something is.
