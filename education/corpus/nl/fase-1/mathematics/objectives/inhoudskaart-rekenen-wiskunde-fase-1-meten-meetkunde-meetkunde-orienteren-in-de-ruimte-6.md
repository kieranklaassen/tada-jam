## Official wording

redeneren over 'oriënteren in de ruimte' in passende probleem- en conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about 'orienting in space' in suitable problem and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: a clash of ideas, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children sit opposite each other and disagree about which side of the building is the front; they change seats and talk about what each one saw. The child says why the ball cannot be seen from the door and where one must stand to see it. The child predicts which of two routes is shorter, walks both, counts the steps and says what they found. A child of 4 says what they see from their own place and checks by moving; a child of 6 explains that another person sees something else because that person stands somewhere else.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The quoted words are the name of the card's sub-heading, so the reasoning is about what the statements Oriënteren in de ruimte / 1 to / 5 cover: geometric concepts, the place of objects, drawings and plans, viewpoints and routes. The card does not explain *conflictsituaties*: the word can mean a situation in which what a child expects and what the child finds do not agree, or a disagreement between children. No situation is named.

### Common mistakes

The child reasons only from their own viewpoint and treats another child's different answer as wrong. The child states what they see and gives no reason. The child judges a route with many turns as longer than a straight one without checking.
