## Official wording

handelend optellen, aftrekken en splitsen van hoeveelheden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

hands-on (*handelend*, through the child's own actions) adding, subtracting and splitting (*splitsen*, breaking an amount into parts) of quantities

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts three blocks with four blocks and counts how many there are now, or takes two apples out of a bowl of six and counts what stays. The child divides a handful of seven beads over two saucers and says how many lie on each. A child of 4 does this with a few objects and counts everything again from one; a child of 6 does it with larger amounts and sometimes counts on from the first group.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement names three actions done with quantities of things; it does not mention written sums or the signs +, − and =.

### Common mistakes

After adding, the child counts only the group that was added and gives that as the total. After taking away, the child counts the objects taken away, not those that stay. When splitting, the child finds one way to divide the amount and does not see that the same amount can be divided in other ways.
