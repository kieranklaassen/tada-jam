## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing hands-on (*handelend*, by acting with real things) with concepts around *delen* (sharing, or dividing) such as fair (*eerlijk*), unfair (*oneerlijk*), sharing (*delen*), sharing out (*verdelen*), left over (*over*), the same number (*evenveel*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shares 8 crackers over 2 plates and says whether it is "*eerlijk*". When one plate has more, the child says "*oneerlijk*" and moves crackers until both plates have "*evenveel*". With 9 crackers the child puts the one that is "*over*" back in the tin. A child of 4 says that it is unfair when the other pile is clearly bigger; a child of 7 counts both shares, uses "*evenveel*" and "*over*", and says what to do with what is left.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The six words after "zoals" are examples: *eerlijk*, *oneerlijk*, *delen*, *verdelen*, *over*, *evenveel*. The Dutch word *delen* means both sharing and dividing; the text does not choose. The words are met while acting with objects. This goal states no number range. Words for a half and a quarter and a sign for dividing are not among its examples.

### Common mistakes

The child calls a sharing fair when everyone has something, although the numbers differ. The child calls one share bigger because its objects lie spread wider. The child gives what is left over to one of the children and still calls the shares equal.
