## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at the classroom clock, a wristwatch, an alarm clock, a kitchen timer and the clock on a tablet, and says what is the same and what differs. The child turns the hands of a practice clock, watches how far the long hand has moved during play time, and says what people use a clock for. A child of 4 knows that a clock tells when it is time for something; a child of 7 points out the hands and the numbers and knows that both kinds of clock show the same time.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. The goal names analogue and digital clocks, and the function of a clock. Reading off the time is in rw/m/6/06/fase1.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks the long hand tells the hour because it is the bigger one. The child reads a digital display as one number, or as two numbers that have nothing to do with each other. The child thinks the hands stand still, because their movement is too slow to see.
