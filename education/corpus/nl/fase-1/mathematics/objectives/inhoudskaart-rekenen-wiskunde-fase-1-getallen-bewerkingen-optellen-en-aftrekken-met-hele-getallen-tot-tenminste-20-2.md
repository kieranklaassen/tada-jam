## Official wording

handelend omgaan met begrippen rond optellen, aftrekken en splitsen zoals erbij tellen, weghalen, erbij, nog eentje, eraf, weg, samen, over

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing hands-on (*handelend*, through the child's own actions) with concepts around adding, subtracting and splitting (*splitsen*, breaking an amount into parts) such as adding on (*erbij tellen*), taking away (*weghalen*), added (*erbij*), one more (*nog eentje*), taken off (*eraf*), gone (*weg*), together (*samen*), left over (*over*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While handling things the child hears and says these words: "one more" when a car joins the row, "take two away" when clearing plates, "together we have five", "one is left over". The child carries out a spoken request such as "put two more with them" or "take one off". A child of 4 uses "more", "gone" and "together"; a child of 6 also uses "add on", "take away" and "left over" in a full sentence about what was done.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The eight words and phrases after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples: *erbij tellen*, *weghalen*, *erbij*, *nog eentje*, *eraf*, *weg*, *samen*, *over*. The statement is about words used while acting; the words plus and minus are not among its examples, and it does not mention written signs.

### Common mistakes

The child uses "more" for any change, also when something is taken away. The child understands "together" as standing near each other, not as one joined amount. The child takes "left over" to mean the things that were taken away.
