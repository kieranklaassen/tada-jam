## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering by length and perimeter (*omtrek*, the distance around something)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays pencils side by side with one end against the edge of the table and puts them in order from shortest to longest. The children of the class stand in a row from shortest to tallest. The child lays a ribbon around two tree trunks in turn and compares the two lengths of ribbon. A child of 4 compares two things side by side; a child of 7 orders five or more things, and compares two things that cannot be moved by laying the same rope or strip along each.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names length and perimeter. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: by eye, measuring off (*afpassen*), holding side by side ("via 'op het oog', afpassen, naast elkaar houden"); it is an example, not a limit. The goal names no objects and no units, and it states no number of objects.

### Common mistakes

The child does not line up one end and judges by the other end. The child calls a bent or curled rope shorter than a straight one of the same length. The child judges the distance around a thing by its height or its width only.
