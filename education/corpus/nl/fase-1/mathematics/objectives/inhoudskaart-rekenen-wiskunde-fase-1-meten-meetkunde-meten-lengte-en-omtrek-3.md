## Official wording

omgaan met tegenstellingen tussen begrippen rond lengte en omtrek

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with opposites between concepts around length and perimeter (*omtrek*, the distance around something)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds a long and a short pencil, a high and a low tower, a wide and a narrow strip, a thick and a thin book, and names each pair. The child says the opposite when the teacher says one word, and makes a short roll of clay longer and a thick one thinner. The child stands near the door and then far from it. A child of 4 names long and short, high and low for things that differ a lot; a child of 6 uses longer and shorter as a pair and says that if the red ribbon is longer than the blue one, the blue one is shorter.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about opposites between concepts of length and perimeter; it names no pairs. The card gives no example of a pair; long and short, and high and low, are the pack's examples. No units are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child uses small as the opposite of every word: long, high, wide and thick. The child treats long and short as fixed properties and is confused that a pencil is long next to a crayon and short next to a broom. The child mixes up the pairs and says low for short or thin for narrow.
