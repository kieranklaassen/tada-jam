## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds a heavy thing and a light thing in the room and puts them in two hoops. When one side of the balance is heavier, the child says that the other side is lighter, and when one bag is the heaviest the child looks for the lightest. A child of 4 gives "light" as the opposite of "heavy"; a child of 7 turns a sentence round: "the stone is heavier than the cork, so the cork is lighter than the stone".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal lists no opposites; the words around weight are in rw/m/4/02/fase1. No units are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child gives "not heavy" or "small" as the opposite of heavy. The child does not see that "the stone is heavier than the cork" and "the cork is lighter than the stone" say the same. The child calls a thing heavy in every comparison, also when it is the lighter of two.
