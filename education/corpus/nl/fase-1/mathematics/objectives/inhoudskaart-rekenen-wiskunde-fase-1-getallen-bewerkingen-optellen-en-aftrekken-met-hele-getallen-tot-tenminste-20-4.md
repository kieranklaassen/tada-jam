## Official wording

probleemoplossen, kritisch denken en redeneren over optellen, bij elkaar tellen, aftrekken, splitsen met hoeveelheden in betekenisvolle situaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

problem solving, critical thinking and reasoning about adding, counting together (*bij elkaar tellen*), subtracting, splitting (*splitsen*, breaking an amount into parts) with quantities in meaningful situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At the table the child works out whether there are enough cups when two more children sit down, and says how they know. The child tells another child why six pencils shared between two pots can be three and three, or four and two. A child of 4 solves such a question by acting it out and counting; a child of 6 predicts the outcome first, gives a reason, and checks by counting.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement names quantities and "meaningful situations" (*betekenisvolle situaties*) and does not say what those situations are. The Dutch leaves open whether "with quantities" belongs to splitting alone or to all four actions. The statement does not mention written sums.

### Common mistakes

The child gives an answer without being able to say how it was found. The child guesses a number instead of acting the situation out. The child keeps the first answer when the count shows a different number.
