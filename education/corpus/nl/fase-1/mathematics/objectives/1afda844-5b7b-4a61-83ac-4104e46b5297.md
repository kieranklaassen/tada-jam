## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising, reading and writing digits and numbers up to at least 20 and exploring larger numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds the 7 among number cards, reads the number on a door or a bus, and writes the number of their age. The child traces digits in sand, writes 14 on a ticket for the play shop, and looks at large numbers on house fronts and price tags and says what they see. A child of 4 recognises a few digits, often those of their age; a child of 7 reads and writes the numbers up to 20 and reads many numbers of two digits.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states its range as "tot ten minste 20" (up to at least 20): the offer reaches 20 at the least, no upper limit is set, and the wording does not say whether 20 itself is included. Larger numbers are explored only: reading and writing them is not asked, and no upper end is set. Number words written out in letters are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child writes digits mirrored (most often 3, 7 and 9) and confuses 6 and 9. The child reverses the digits of the numbers from 13 to 19: the Dutch word *veertien* names the four first, so the child writes 41. The child reads 12 as "one, two" and does not see it as one number.
