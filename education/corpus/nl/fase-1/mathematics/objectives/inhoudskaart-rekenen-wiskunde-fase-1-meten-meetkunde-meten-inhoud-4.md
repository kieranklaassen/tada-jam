## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering by volume (*inhoud*, also capacity: how much fits inside)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child fills one container with water or sand and pours it into another to see which holds more: it overflows, or room is left. The child puts three bottles in a row from the one that holds least to the one that holds most, after pouring from one into the next. The child compares two boxes by filling each with the same blocks. A child of 4 compares two containers that differ clearly by looking and pouring; a child of 6 compares containers of different shapes by pouring and orders three or more.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two actions are named, comparing and ordering. The statement sets no number of containers and names no materials, instruments or units. Informal instruments and measures are the card's statement Inhoud / 5.

### Common mistakes

The child judges by height alone and says the tall narrow glass holds more than the low wide one. The child thinks the amount of water changes when it is poured into a container of another shape. When ordering three containers the child compares two and places the third without comparing it.
