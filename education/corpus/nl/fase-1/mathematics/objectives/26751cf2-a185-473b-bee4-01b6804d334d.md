## English gloss

reasoning about 'operating with shapes and figures' in suitable problem situations and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: an outcome against expectation, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children disagree whether a square turned onto its corner is still a square, and they turn the card back to check. A child says why a ball cannot go at the bottom of a tower and a cube can. A child of 4 shows it by doing; a child of 7 gives a reason from the properties of the shape, such as "it still has four equal sides".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The words in quotation marks are the name of this cluster (rw/mk/3), whose other goals are about sorting, flat and solid shapes, mirrors, and light and shadow. The text does not explain *conflictsituaties* (conflict situations): the word can mean a situation in which the outcome goes against what a child expects, or a disagreement between children, and the wording does not choose. It does not say which situations are suitable. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question "How can you make a square or a rectangle with small triangles?" ("Hoe kun je met driehoekjes een vierkant of een rechthoek maken?"); it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child reasons from how a shape looks at that moment and not from its properties. The child keeps to a name that was learned for one position of the shape. The child gives a reason that only repeats the name ("because it is a square").
