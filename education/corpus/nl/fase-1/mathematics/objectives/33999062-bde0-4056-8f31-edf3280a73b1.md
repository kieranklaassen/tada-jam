## English gloss

dealing with opposites between concepts around *inhoud* (capacity, volume or contents)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands a full and an empty bottle side by side, and fills one cup with much water and one with little. When one glass is fuller the child says that the other is emptier. A child of 4 gives "empty" as the opposite of "full"; a child of 7 pairs "more" with "less" and "too much" with "too little", and turns a comparison round.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The Dutch *inhoud* can mean capacity (how much something can hold), volume (how much space something takes up) or contents (how much is in something); the wording does not choose. The goal lists no opposites; the words around *inhoud* are in rw/m/3/02/fase1. The goal names no units.

### Common mistakes

The child gives "not full" as the opposite of full and calls a glass that is half full empty. The child does not see that "fuller" for one glass means "emptier" for the other. The child treats "little" and "empty" as the same.
