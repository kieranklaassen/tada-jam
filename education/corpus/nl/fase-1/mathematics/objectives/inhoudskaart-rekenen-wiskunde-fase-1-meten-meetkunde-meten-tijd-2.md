## Official wording

omgaan met begrippen rond tijdsaanduiding

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with concepts around the indication of time (*tijdsaanduiding*, ways of saying when)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child uses words that say when: now, soon, later, first, then, before, after, yesterday, today, tomorrow, early, late. The child says "first we eat fruit, then we play outside" and "I was at grandma's yesterday". The child also uses words for how long something lasts: a short while, a long time. A child of 4 uses now, later, first and then; a child of 6 uses yesterday, today and tomorrow correctly and says that something lasts a long or a short time.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names no concepts and gives no examples. *Tijdsaanduiding* means a way of saying when something is; the card does not say which words are meant, or whether clock times and words for how long are included. The names of days, parts of the day, seasons and months are the examples of the card's statement Tijd / 1, and clocks are the subject of Tijd / 5 and Tijd / 6.

### Common mistakes

The child uses yesterday for any time in the past and tomorrow for any time in the future. The child mixes up before and after, most often when a sentence names the events in another order than they happened. The child uses soon and later without a sense of how long that is, and asks again at once.
