## Official wording

redeneren over 'opereren met vormen en figuren' in passende probleem- en conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about 'operating with shapes and figures' in suitable problem and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: a clash of ideas, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says which block made a given print and checks by pressing the block into the clay. Two children disagree whether a turned square is still a square; they lay one on top of the other and say what they find. The child predicts what a mirror or a torch will show before trying it and afterwards says why the guess was right or wrong. A child of 4 says what they see and tries it out; a child of 6 gives a reason, such as "it is still a triangle because it has three corners".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The quoted words are the name of the card's sub-heading, so the reasoning is about what the statements Opereren met vormen en figuren / 1 to / 5 cover: sorting, flat and solid figures, shapes, mirrors and shadows. The card does not explain *conflictsituaties*: the word can mean a situation in which what a child expects and what the child finds do not agree, or a disagreement between children. No situation is named.

### Common mistakes

The child judges by how a shape looks at that moment and says that a turned shape has become another shape. The child gives an answer and no reason, or repeats the answer as the reason. The child keeps a first prediction when the trial shows something else.
