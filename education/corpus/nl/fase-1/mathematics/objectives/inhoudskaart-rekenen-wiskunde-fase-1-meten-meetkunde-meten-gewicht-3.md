## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with opposites between concepts around weight

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a heavy thing in one hand and a light thing in the other and names the pair: this one is heavy, that one is light. The child finds something lighter than the book and something heavier than the book, and says the opposite when the teacher says one word of a pair. On a balance the child makes the low pan come up by taking something off it or by adding to the other pan. A child of 4 names heavy and light for two things that differ a lot; a child of 6 uses heavier and lighter as a pair and says that if the stone is heavier than the shoe, the shoe is lighter than the stone.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is about opposites between weight concepts; it names no pairs. The card gives no example of a pair; heavy and light is the pack's example. No units are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child knows one word of a pair, most often heavy, and says "not heavy" for the other. The child treats heavy and light as fixed properties and is confused that the same thing is heavier than one thing and lighter than another. The child knows that one thing is heavier than a second and cannot yet say that the second is lighter than the first.
