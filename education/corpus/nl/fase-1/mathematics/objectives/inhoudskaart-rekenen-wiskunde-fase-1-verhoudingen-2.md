## Official wording

verkennen van getalsmatige verhoudingen (bijv. bij een bakrecept: voor één cake zijn 2 eieren nodig, voor 2 cakes zijn ... eieren nodig)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring numerical proportions (*verhoudingen*, also: ratios) (e.g. with a baking recipe: for one cake 2 eggs are needed, for 2 cakes ... eggs are needed)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays two eggs from the play shop beside one cake tin, adds a second tin and works out how many eggs are needed now. The child gives every doll two shoes or every toy car four wheels and counts how many are needed for two or three of them. A child of 4 gives each cake its two eggs, one cake at a time; a child of 6 says the number for two or three cakes beforehand and checks by laying the eggs out.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The recipe is an example, introduced by "e.g." (*bijv.*); its numbers, 2 eggs for one cake and 2 cakes, belong to the example and are not a range. The three dots are in the wording: the number of eggs for 2 cakes is left open. The statement says exploring and sets no number range. It does not mention multiplication or a ratio table.

### Common mistakes

The child adds one egg for the second cake instead of the same two again, and says three. The child says two again, because "a cake needs two eggs". The child counts the cakes and the eggs together as one group.
