## Official wording

omgaan met begrippen rond de telrij zoals verder, door, terug, naast, tussenin

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with concepts around the counting sequence such as further (*verder*), on (*door*), back (*terug*), next to (*naast*), in between (*tussenin*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child follows and gives spoken directions on a row of number tiles or a number line: "count further", "go one back", "which number is next to seven?", "which number lies in between four and six?". The child moves a pawn or steps along the tiles as the word says. A child of 4 follows "further" and "back" by one step; a child of 6 uses all five words and names the number in between two others without counting from one.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The five words after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples: *verder*, *door*, *terug*, *naast*, *tussenin*. The statement is about words in use on the counting sequence; the words for before and after are not among its examples.

### Common mistakes

The child confuses "further" and "back" and moves in the wrong direction. For "next to" the child names only the number that follows, not the one before. For "in between" the child names one of the two given numbers.
