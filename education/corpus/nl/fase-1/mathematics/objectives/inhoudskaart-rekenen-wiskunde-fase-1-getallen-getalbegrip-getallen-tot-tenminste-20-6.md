## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring the different meanings and functions of numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child meets numbers used in different ways: five apples in a bowl, the third place in the line, bus 5, a shoe size, the 5 on a clock or a ruler, their own age. The child collects numbers seen in the classroom or on a walk and tells what each number says there. A child of 4 knows that a number can say how many and how old; a child of 6 tells apart a number that counts, a number that gives a place in an order, a number that measures and a number that only labels.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement does not list the meanings and functions. It says exploring; it does not ask for the names of kinds of numbers.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks every number says how many, and expects bus 12 to be more than bus 5. The child thinks the person with the highest house number or shirt number has the most. The child treats a number on a ruler or thermometer as a count of things.
