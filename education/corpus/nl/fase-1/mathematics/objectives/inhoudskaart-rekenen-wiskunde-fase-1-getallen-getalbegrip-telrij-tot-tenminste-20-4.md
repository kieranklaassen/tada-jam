## Official wording

omgaan met rangtelwoorden zoals eerste, tweede... tiende, zover als je kunt; en verkennen wanneer je die gebruikt

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

dealing with ordinal number words (*rangtelwoorden*) such as first, second... tenth, as far as you can; and exploring when you use them

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands in a line and says who is first, second and third, or puts the fourth bead of a string in another colour. The child tells apart "take three cars" and "take the third car". The child notices where ordinal words are used: a place in a race, the floors of a building, the date, whose turn it is. A child of 4 uses "first" and "last" and sometimes "second"; a child of 6 uses the ordinal words to tenth and counts from the correct end of the row.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The ordinal words after "such as" (*zoals*) are examples that run from first to tenth (*eerste, tweede... tiende*), and the statement adds "as far as you can" (*zover als je kunt*): it sets no end point. The second part says exploring when ordinal words are used; it does not list those uses.

### Common mistakes

The child takes three objects when asked for the third. The child counts from the wrong end of the row. The child forms ordinal words by rule and gets the irregular Dutch ones wrong, saying for example "*driede*" for *derde* (third).
