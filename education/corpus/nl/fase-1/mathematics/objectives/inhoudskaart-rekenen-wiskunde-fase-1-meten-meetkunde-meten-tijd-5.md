exploring various analogue and digital clocks and the function of a clock

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at clocks at school and at home: a wall clock with hands, a wristwatch, an alarm clock with numerals, the clock on an oven or a phone. The child turns the hands of a play clock, sees that the long hand goes round faster, and compares the numerals on the dial with the numerals on a digital clock. The child says what a clock is for: to know when it is time to go home or to eat. A child of 4 points out clocks and says that they tell the time; a child of 6 tells the long hand from the short hand and says that a digital clock shows the time in numerals.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is exploring. Two kinds of clock are named, analogue and digital, and the function of a clock. Reading the time is not part of this statement; it is the card's statement Tijd / 6, which keeps to whole hours.

### Common mistakes

The child mixes up the long hand and the short hand. The child thinks that moving the hands changes what time it is, for example that it makes it time to go home. The child does not see that a clock with hands and a digital clock show the same thing.
