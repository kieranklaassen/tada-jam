## Official wording

splitsen en samenvoegen van kleine hoeveelheden tot ten minste 20

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster rw/gb/2; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau 894db17e-1112-4414-a958-3f7eb2e944b3.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

splitting (*splitsen*, parting a quantity into parts) and joining small quantities up to at least 20

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child parts 6 chestnuts over two hands in different ways and says each pair: 1 and 5, 2 and 4, 3 and 3. The child pushes two small piles into one and says how many there are together, and hides part of a pile under a cup for another child to work out. A child of 4 parts and joins up to about 5 objects and counts each part; a child of 7 names the pairs for the numbers to 10 without counting and parts quantities up to 20 with objects.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says both "small quantities" and "tot ten minste 20" (up to at least 20): the offer reaches 20 at the least, no upper limit is set, and the wording does not say whether 20 itself is included or how "small" fits with the range. The goal does not say into how many parts a quantity is split, and it mentions no written notation.

### Common mistakes

The child believes the total changes when a pile is parted and counts everything again. The child finds one way to part a quantity and does not look for others. The child does not see that 2 and 4 are the same parts as 4 and 2.
