## Official wording

voortzetten en zelf ontwerpen van ketting- en mozaïekpatronen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

continuing and designing by oneself chain patterns (*kettingpatronen*, patterns in a row, as on a necklace) and mosaic patterns

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child threads beads in a repeating order, such as red, blue, red, blue, and goes on from where a started string stops. On a peg board or with mosaic tiles the child lays a repeating row or a figure that repeats around a centre. The child thinks up a pattern of their own and says its rule aloud. A child of 4 continues a pattern of two alternating colours; a child of 6 continues a pattern with three elements or with a doubled element, such as red, red, blue, and designs one for another child to continue.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Two kinds of pattern are named: chain patterns and mosaic patterns. Two actions are named: continuing a pattern and designing one. The statement sets no number of elements in the repeating part and names no materials, colours or shapes.

### Common mistakes

The child looks only at the last bead and adds the other colour, which fails as soon as the repeating part has three elements or a doubled one. The child starts the repetition correctly and changes to a free choice of colours after a few beads. The child makes a decoration with no repeating part and calls it a pattern.
