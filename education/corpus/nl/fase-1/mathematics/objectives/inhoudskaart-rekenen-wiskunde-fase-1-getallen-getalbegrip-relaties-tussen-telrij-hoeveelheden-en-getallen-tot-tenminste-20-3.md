## Official wording

nadenken over 'nul' als getal en als hoeveelheid

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking about 'zero' as a number and as a quantity

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes the last block out of a box and says how many are left, then looks for a way to show that on a card or with fingers. The child counts back along a number line and talks about what comes before one, or says how many elephants are in the classroom. A child of 4 says "nothing" or "all gone" for an empty plate; a child of 6 uses the word zero, recognises the digit 0 and gives it a place before 1.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The inverted commas around 'zero' ('nul') are in the wording. The statement says thinking about zero; it does not mention calculating with zero or the 0 in 10 and 20.

### Common mistakes

The child says that zero is not a number because there is nothing to count. The child starts the number sequence at one and has no place for zero. The child reads the 0 in 10 as "nothing" and takes 10 to be the same as 1.
