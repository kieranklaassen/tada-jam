## Official wording

verhoudingsgewijs vergelijken en ordenen (bijv. als je groter wordt, moeten je kleren ook groter zijn)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing and ordering in proportion (*verhoudingsgewijs*) (e.g. when you get bigger, your clothes must be bigger too)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out three dolls or bears by size and gives each the chair, bowl or coat that fits its size. The child holds a baby sock, their own sock and an adult's sock next to the feet they belong to. A child of 4 matches big with big and small with small; a child of 6 orders two sets of three or more things by size, pairs them one to one, and says why the middle bear gets the middle chair.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The clothes that must be bigger when you get bigger are an example, introduced by "e.g." (*bijv.*). The statement uses no numbers and sets no number range; it does not say how many things are compared or ordered.

### Common mistakes

The child matches the largest and the smallest correctly but places the middle ones at random. The child orders each set by itself and does not pair the two sets. The child matches by a favourite colour or picture, not by size.
