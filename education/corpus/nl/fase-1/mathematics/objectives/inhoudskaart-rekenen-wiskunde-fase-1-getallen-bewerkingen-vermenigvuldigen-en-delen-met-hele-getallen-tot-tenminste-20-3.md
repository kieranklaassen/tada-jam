## Official wording

redeneren over uitdelen en verdelen van hoeveelheden in passende probleem- en conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about handing out (*uitdelen*) and sharing out (*verdelen*) of quantities in suitable problem and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: a clash of ideas, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children each want the same number of grapes from a bunch of nine; the child says why one grape is left and proposes what to do with it. The child explains why ten biscuits for two children gives more each than ten biscuits for five children. A child of 4 says "not fair" and points at the larger share; a child of 6 gives a reason with numbers and checks it by dealing the objects out.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement does not say what a suitable problem situation or a conflict situation is; the Dutch *conflictsituatie* can be read as a situation in which two ideas or outcomes clash, or as a disagreement between children. The statement does not mention multiplying, although it stands under the card's sub-heading for multiplying and dividing.

### Common mistakes

The child states that a division is fair or unfair but cannot say why. The child thinks that more people to share with means more for each. The child argues from what the child wants, not from the numbers.
