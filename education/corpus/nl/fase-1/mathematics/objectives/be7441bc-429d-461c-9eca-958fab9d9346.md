## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a stone in one hand and a cork in the other and says which is heavier and which is lighter. With three bags the child finds the heaviest and the lightest, and looks for two things that are equally heavy on the balance. A child of 4 uses "heavy" and "light" for single things; a child of 7 uses the comparing forms and "equally heavy" for two or more things.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The words after "zoals" are examples: the degrees of *zwaar* (heavy), *even zwaar*, and the degrees of *licht* (light). The source wording ends with a comma, so the list may be cut off; the gloss keeps the comma. No units are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child uses "big" and "small" for heavy and light. The child knows "heavier" and says "not heavy" where "lighter" is meant. The child confuses *licht* (light in weight) with *licht* (not dark), which is the same word in Dutch.
