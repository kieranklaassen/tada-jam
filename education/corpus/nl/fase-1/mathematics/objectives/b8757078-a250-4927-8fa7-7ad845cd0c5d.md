## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about money in suitable problem situations and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: an outcome against expectation, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A toy costs 5 euros and the child has three coins; the child works out whether that can be enough and says what it depends on. Two children disagree about who has more money, one with many small coins and one with a single note. A child of 4 says the one with more coins has more; a child of 7 adds up the values and explains that the number of coins does not decide it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states no amounts. The text does not explain *conflictsituaties* (conflict situations): the word can mean a situation in which the outcome goes against what a child expects, or a disagreement between children, and the wording does not choose. It does not say which situations are suitable. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question "Are two coins always worth more than one coin?" ("Zijn twee munten altijd meer waard dan één munt?"); it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child judges an amount by the number or the size of the coins. The child thinks that getting change means getting extra money. The child chooses what to buy without checking whether the money is enough.
