## Official wording

ontdekken dat er munten en geldbiljetten zijn met verschillende waarden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering that there are coins and banknotes with different values

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts a tray of play euro coins and notes by kind and sees that they differ in size, colour and the numeral on them. The child hears and says that one coin is worth more than another and that a note is worth more than the coins. In the play shop the child finds out that one 2-euro coin pays for the same as two 1-euro coins. A child of 4 sorts coins and notes by how they look and calls all of them "money"; a child of 6 reads the numeral on a 1-euro and a 2-euro coin and on a 5-euro note and says which is worth more.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discovering: the child finds out that coins and banknotes exist and that their values differ. The statement names no coins, notes or amounts and does not say that the child knows the value of each. Making up amounts is the card's statement Geld / 4, which keeps to whole euros.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks the biggest coin is worth the most. The child counts coins as objects, so that three coins are "three" whatever their value, and thinks that more coins is always more money. The child does not yet see that a note and a set of coins can be worth the same.
