## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At the water table the child fills a bottle with cups of water and counts the cups, and pours a full jug into a bowl to see whether it fits. In the sandpit the child fills a bucket with scoops of sand. A child of 4 fills, pours and empties and says "full" or "empty"; a child of 7 counts how many cups go in and knows that this number tells how much the bottle holds.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The Dutch *inhoud* can mean capacity (how much something can hold), volume (how much space something takes up) or contents (how much is in something); the wording does not choose. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: filling and emptying bottles, cups and buckets with sand and water; filling boxes with blocks ("vullen/legen van flessen, bekers en emmers met zand en water; vullen van dozen met blokken"); it is an example, not a limit. The filling with water and sand under "In a child's hands" follows this example. The statement itself names no units; informal instruments and measures are named in rw/m/3/05/fase1.

### Common mistakes

The child does not fill the cup the same each time, half full one time and heaped the next. The child loses count while pouring. The child thinks the taller container always holds more.
