## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

working with simple drawings, *bouwplaten* (sheets to cut out and fold, or pictures to build from) and *plattegronden* (floor plans or maps, drawn as seen from above)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds a block house from a simple drawing and puts the furniture of the doll's house where the floor plan shows it. The child finds a hidden object in the classroom with a plan that has a cross on it, and cuts out and folds a printed sheet into a small house. A child of 4 matches a drawing with the real building or with a corner of the room; a child of 7 turns the plan so that it lies the same way as the room, walks to a marked spot and draws a simple plan of the table or the room.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names three things: drawings, *bouwplaten* and *plattegronden*. The word "simple" stands before the first; the wording does not make clear whether it applies to all three. *Bouwplaat* can mean a printed sheet to cut out and fold into a model, or a picture that shows how to build something; the text does not choose. The verb is "working with": the goal does not say whether the child reads them, makes them or both. Scale, a legend and compass directions are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child holds the plan in another direction than the room and looks in the turned or mirrored place. The child does not understand that a plan shows things from above and looks on it for the front of the cupboard. The child counts only the blocks that a drawing shows and misses the hidden ones.
