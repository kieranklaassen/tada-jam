## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about the counting sequence up to at least 20 in suitable problem situations and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: an outcome against expectation, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Number cards hang on a line and one is missing or two are swapped; the child finds out which and says how they know. Two children count the same stairs and reach different last numbers, and they look for the place where the counts differ. A child of 4 notices that something is wrong and counts again from one; a child of 7 gives a reason, such as "after 12 comes 13, so the 14 is in the wrong place".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal states its range as "tot ten minste 20" (up to at least 20): the offer reaches 20 at the least, no upper limit is set, and the wording does not say whether 20 itself is included. The text does not explain *conflictsituaties* (conflict situations): the word can mean a situation in which the outcome goes against what a child expects, or a disagreement between children, and the wording does not choose. It does not say which situations are suitable. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: the question "Why must you always use counting words in the same order?" ("Waarom moet je telwoorden steeds in dezelfde volgorde gebruiken?"); it is an example, not a limit.

### Common mistakes

The child counts again from one without looking for the place of the error. The child accepts what an adult or a sure classmate says without checking. The child gives a reason that only repeats the answer ("because it is so").
