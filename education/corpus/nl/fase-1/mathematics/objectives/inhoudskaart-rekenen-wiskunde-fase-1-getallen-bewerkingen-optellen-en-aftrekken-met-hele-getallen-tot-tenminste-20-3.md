## Official wording

handelend optellen en aftrekken in de context van de telrij

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

hands-on (*handelend*, through the child's own actions) adding and subtracting in the context of the counting sequence (*telrij*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands on a number tile on the floor, takes two steps forward and says the number reached, or moves a pawn three places back along a numbered track. The child puts a finger on 6 on a number line of cards and counts two further aloud: "seven, eight". A child of 4 does this with one step forward on a short row of numbers; a child of 6 takes several steps forward or back and says beforehand where the steps will end.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement places adding and subtracting in the counting sequence; it does not say how large the steps are, and it does not mention written sums.

### Common mistakes

The child counts the starting number as the first step, and so ends one too low when adding and one too high when subtracting. When counting back the child skips a number or changes to counting forwards. The child takes the steps correctly but cannot say afterwards how many steps were taken.
