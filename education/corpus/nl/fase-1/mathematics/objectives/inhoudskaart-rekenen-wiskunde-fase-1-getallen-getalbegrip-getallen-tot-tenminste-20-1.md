## Official wording

herkennen, lezen en schrijven van cijfers en getallen en verkennen van grotere getallen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising, reading and writing digits (*cijfers*) and numbers (*getallen*) and exploring larger numbers

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child picks the card with 7 out of a row of number cards, reads the number on a classmate's birthday crown, and writes the digits of their own age or house number. The child traces a digit in sand with a finger before writing it with a pencil. The child looks at larger numbers on a calendar, a tape measure or a page and tries to read them. A child of 4 recognises and names a few digits; a child of 6 reads and writes most numbers to 20 and asks what a number such as 100 is called.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. For "larger numbers" the statement says exploring, not reading and writing, and it does not say how large. Dutch separates *cijfer* (a digit) from *getal* (a number); the statement names both.

### Common mistakes

The child writes digits mirrored, most often 2, 3, 5, 7 and 9. The child confuses 6 and 9. The child writes the two digits of a teen number in the wrong order, for example 31 for 13, because the unit is heard first in the Dutch word (*dertien*).
