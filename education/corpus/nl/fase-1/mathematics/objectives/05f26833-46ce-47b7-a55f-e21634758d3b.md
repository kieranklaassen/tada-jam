## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts a tray of play euros into kinds, looks at the number and the picture on each, and lays the same coins together. In the play shop the child finds that one coin of 2 euros buys what two coins of 1 euro buy, and that a note is worth more than a coin. A child of 4 sorts coins by colour and size and knows they are for paying; a child of 7 reads the value on euro coins and notes and says which of two is worth more.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal names coins and banknotes and no values. It does not name a currency; the euro is named in rw/m/7/02/fase1 and rw/m/7/04/fase1. The verb is discovering: counting amounts is not asked here.

### Common mistakes

The child thinks the largest coin is worth the most, although the coin of 50 cents is larger than the coin of 1 euro. The child thinks more coins is always more money, so that five coins of 1 euro are more than one note of 10. The child reads the number without the unit and takes 50 cents as more than 2 euros.
