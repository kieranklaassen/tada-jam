## Official wording

werken met eenvoudige tekeningen, bouwplaten en plattegronden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

working with simple drawings, building sheets (*bouwplaten*: cut-out model sheets, or pictured building instructions) and floor plans (*plattegronden*, plans or maps seen from above)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a simple drawing of the block corner and finds the real cupboard and table that it shows. The child cuts out and folds a printed sheet into a small house, or builds what a pictured building sheet shows. On a floor plan of the classroom the child puts a finger on the place of their own chair and walks to a spot that someone marked on the plan. A child of 4 recognises objects in a drawing and finds them in the room; a child of 6 uses a plan of the classroom or the playground, seen from above, to place or find things.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Three kinds of material are named: drawings, *bouwplaten* and *plattegronden*. The word simple stands before the first; whether it also covers the other two is not said. *Bouwplaat* can mean a printed sheet to cut out and fold into a model, or a sheet that pictures how to build something; the card does not say which. The statement does not say whether the child reads these materials, makes them, or both, and it mentions no scale and no legend.

### Common mistakes

The child reads a plan as a picture seen from the side and looks on it for the legs of the table. The child holds the plan turned the wrong way relative to the room and walks to the wrong side. The child finds a thing on the plan and cannot say what is next to it in the room.
