## English gloss

dealing with geometric concepts such as in front of (*voor*), behind, next to, on, under, in, above, between, close by, far away, opposite, left, right, before (*voor*), after (*na*), straight on, further

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts the bear on, under, behind and next to the chair when asked, and says where it is after another child has placed it. In the gym the child walks straight on, turns left at the bench and stops between two cones. A child of 4 acts on "on", "under", "in" and "behind"; a child of 7 uses "left", "right", "opposite" and "between" in their own instructions to another child.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The seventeen words after "zoals" are examples. The source prints *voor* twice, first beside *achter* (behind) and then beside *na* (after); the gloss gives the first as "in front of" and the second as "before". The goal does not say from whose point of view left and right are taken.

### Common mistakes

The child mixes left and right, most of all for a person who faces them. The child mixes "in front of" and "behind" for things that have no front, such as a ball or a tree. The child uses "next to" for every position that is near.
