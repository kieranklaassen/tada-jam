## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures the table in hand spans, the classroom in steps and a book in paper clips laid end to end, and gives the result as a number of them. The child measures the same table with a strip of paper as long as their foot and marks each time where the strip ends. A child of 4 lays blocks along a thing and counts some of them; a child of 7 lays equal units without gaps from one end to the other, counts them, and notices that larger steps give a smaller number.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The instruments and measures are called informal. The content-line document prints an example with this goal: with strips, steps, pieces of string, blocks ("met stroken, stappen, touwtjes, blokken"); it is an example, not a limit. Metres, centimetres and a ruler are not mentioned. The goal speaks of length only; perimeter is not named in it.

### Common mistakes

The child leaves gaps or overlaps between the units. The child uses units of different length in one measurement, such as large and small steps. The child does not understand why two children get different numbers for the same table when their hands differ in size.
