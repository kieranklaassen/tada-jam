## Official wording

redeneren over de telrij in passende probleem- en conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

reasoning about the counting sequence (*telrij*) in suitable problem and conflict situations (*conflictsituaties*: a clash of ideas, or a disagreement)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to a puppet that counts "seven, eight, ten" and says which word is missing and where it belongs. Two children get different numbers when counting the same stairs; the child says how that can happen and how to find out who is right. A child of 4 notices that a count sounds wrong and repeats it correctly; a child of 6 says what went wrong, such as a skipped word or a word said twice, and says what must come after a number the child has not counted to before.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The sub-heading above this statement on the card gives the range "up to at least 20" (*tot tenminste 20*): no upper bound is set, and the Dutch *tot* leaves open whether 20 itself is included. The statement does not say what a suitable problem situation or a conflict situation is; the Dutch *conflictsituatie* can be read as a situation in which two ideas or outcomes clash, or as a disagreement between children.

### Common mistakes

The child notices an error near the start of the sequence but not higher up. The child corrects the count but cannot say what was wrong. The child thinks the counting sequence ends at the highest number the child knows.
