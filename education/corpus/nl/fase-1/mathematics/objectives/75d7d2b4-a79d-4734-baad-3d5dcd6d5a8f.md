## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts leaves or cloths into a pile of big ones and a pile of small ones, and lays the biggest beside the smallest. When one mat is bigger the child says that the other is smaller. A child of 4 gives "small" as the opposite of "big"; a child of 7 turns the comparison round and sees that the same sheet is the big one beside a card and the small one beside the table top.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The goal lists no opposites; the words around area are in rw/m/2/02/fase1. No units are mentioned.

### Common mistakes

The child treats "big" and "small" as fixed properties of a thing and not as a comparison. The child gives "not big" where "small" is meant. The child gives an opposite for length (short) where the surface is meant.
