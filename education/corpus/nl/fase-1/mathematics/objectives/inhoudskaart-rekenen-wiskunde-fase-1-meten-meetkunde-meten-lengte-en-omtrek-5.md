## Official wording

meten met informele instrumenten en maten waarmee je lengte kunt uitdrukken

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Rekenen-wiskunde - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

measuring with informal instruments and measures with which you can express length

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures the table with hand spans, the room with steps or with feet placed heel to toe, and a drawing with a row of blocks or paper clips. The child counts the units and says the result: "the table is nine hands long". The child uses a strip of paper or a piece of string as a measure, lays it down again and again and marks where it ended each time. A child of 4 lays things in a row along an object and counts some of them; a child of 6 lays equal units without gaps or overlap from one end to the other and gives the result as a number of units.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The instruments and measures are informal; none is named. The wording names length only, not perimeter. The statement sets no number range for the count and mentions no centimetres, metres or ruler.

### Common mistakes

The child leaves gaps or overlaps between the units, or uses units of different sizes in one measurement. The child says a number word that does not go with a step or a span, and so counts too many or too few. The child is surprised that a classmate with bigger feet gets a smaller number and thinks one of them is wrong.
