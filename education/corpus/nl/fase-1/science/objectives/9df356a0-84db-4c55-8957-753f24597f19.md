## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

encountering, wondering at and realising the great diversity of plants, animals and fungi (mushrooms)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child goes into the schoolyard, a park or a wood and looks for as many different living things as can be found: beetles under a stone, a worm, birds, leaves of different shapes, moss, a mushroom on a tree stump. The child looks through a magnifying glass, calls others to come and look, and lays out collected leaves or draws what was found. Back in class the child tells what was surprising. A child of 4 stops, looks and shows a find to someone; a child of 7 notices how many different kinds were found in one small place.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording joins three verbs: encountering, wondering and realising; the first two describe an experience and an attitude, not a skill. Its subject is the great diversity of three groups: plants, animals and fungi, with mushrooms in brackets. The statement does not ask for the names of species, for counting kinds, or for sorting into groups.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out what is small or does not move: they look at large animals and overlook insects, mosses and mushrooms. Many count a mushroom as a plant, and count as animals only those with four legs and fur.
