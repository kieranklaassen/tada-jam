## Official wording

ervaren en onderzoeken van weersverschijnselen en de relatie met de seizoenen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing and investigating weather phenomena and the relation with the seasons

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the morning circle the child looks out of the window and says what the weather is, then goes outside and feels it: wet rain, cold wind, warm sun. The child stamps in puddles, lets a ribbon blow in the wind and looks for the child's own shadow when the sun shines. The child chooses the picture that fits the day's weather and hangs it up. A child of 4 names the weather of this moment; a child of 6 can say that snow and ice belong to winter and warm days to summer.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card names no weather phenomenon: it speaks of weather phenomena in general and of their relation with the seasons. It asks for experiencing and investigating, not for measuring in units or for instruments. The sub-heading speaks of weather, climate and heavenly bodies, but this statement does not mention climate, and it does not name the seasons.

### Common mistakes

Naming the weather of this moment as the season ("it is winter because it rains"). Thinking a kind of weather belongs to one season only, for example that the sun does not shine in winter or that it never rains in summer. Thinking the wind is made by the moving trees.
