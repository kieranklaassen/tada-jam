discovering that some animals lay eggs and that birth is preceded by development in an egg

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a hen's egg, sees one broken open in a bowl and points to shell, white and yolk. The child watches eggs in an incubator in class or follows frogspawn in a tank, and sees chicks or tadpoles come out after days of waiting. The child sorts pictures of animals into those that lay eggs and those that do not, and looks at a bird's nest or snail eggs outside without touching them. A child of 4 knows that a chick comes out of an egg; a child of 7 names several animals that lay eggs, such as birds, fish, frogs and insects, and can say that the young grows inside the egg before it comes out.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says "some animals", so it does not cover all animals, and it names none. It holds two parts: that these animals lay eggs, and that the young develops in an egg before it is born. The statement sets no level of detail on what happens inside the egg, and it does not mention fertilisation or the difference between an egg that is eaten and an egg that hatches.

### Common mistakes

Thinking only birds lay eggs, and leaving out fish, frogs, snakes and insects. Thinking every egg from the shop holds a chick. Thinking the chick is complete in the egg from the start and only waits there, instead of growing.
