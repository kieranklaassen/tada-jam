## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that people usually have to process and prepare food

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child washes and peels a carrot, cuts soft fruit with a blunt knife, kneads dough and sees it come out of the oven as bread. The child shells peas or cracks an egg and compares raw with cooked: hard potato and soft potato, grain and flour. The child tells what happened to a food before it was on the plate at home. A child of 4 takes part in washing, peeling and stirring; a child of 7 can put the steps from wheat to bread or from cow to cheese in order and can name foods that are eaten as they are.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says "usually" (*meestal*), so it leaves room for food that is eaten as it is. It names two things people do to food, processing and preparing, and gives no example of either. The statement names no food and no place where food is processed or prepared, and it does not mention shops or where food comes from.

### Common mistakes

Thinking food comes from the shop or the refrigerator, with no step before that. Not linking a prepared food to what it was made from, such as chips to potatoes or cheese to milk. Thinking that nothing has been done to food that comes ready in a packet.
