## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering and wondering about: light (light and dark), sound (volume, pitch), temperature (heat), force (of magnets, of water) and electricity (lamp and buzzer)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child makes shadows with a torch and goes into a dark tent to find out what can still be seen. The child strikes, plucks and blows to make loud and soft, high and low sounds, and feels warm and cold water and a wall in the sun and in the shade. The child feels a magnet pull and push, feels running water push against a hand or turn a water wheel, and with an adult connects a battery to a small lamp or a buzzer. A child of 4 tries, repeats and shows surprise; a child of 7 changes one thing on purpose, such as a shorter string or a greater distance to the magnet, and says what happened.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names five subjects, each with brackets: light (the brackets name light and dark), sound (volume and pitch), temperature (heat), force (of magnets and of water) and electricity (a lamp and a buzzer). The verbs are discovering and wondering; the wording does not ask for explaining, measuring or stating a rule. It states no safety condition for heat or electricity. Apart from the magnets, the lamp and the buzzer in the brackets, it names no apparatus or instrument.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up loud with high and soft with low when describing a sound. Thinking a magnet pulls all metal, or all shiny things. Thinking a shadow is a thing of its own that belongs to the body, not a place where the light is blocked.
