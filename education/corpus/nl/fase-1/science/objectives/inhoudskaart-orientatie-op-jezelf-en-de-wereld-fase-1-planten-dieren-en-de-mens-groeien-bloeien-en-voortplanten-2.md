## Official wording

realiseren dat mensen voedsel meestal moeten bewerken en bereiden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that people usually have to process and prepare food

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child washes an apple, peels a mandarin, spreads butter on bread and stirs batter for pancakes. The child smells and tastes a raw piece of carrot and a cooked one and says which is hard and which is soft. In the home corner the child plays cooking: cutting, putting a pan on the toy stove, serving. A child of 4 helps with one step such as washing or stirring; a child of 6 can tell in order what was done to make soup or bread.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says "usually" (*meestal*), so it leaves room for food that is eaten as it is. It names two things people do to food, processing and preparing, and gives no example of either. The statement names no food and no place where food is processed or prepared, and it does not mention shops or where food comes from.

### Common mistakes

Thinking food comes from the shop or the refrigerator, with no step before that. Not linking a prepared food to what it was made from, such as chips to potatoes or cheese to milk. Thinking that nothing has been done to food that comes ready in a packet.
