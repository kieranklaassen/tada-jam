## Official wording

verkennen van de globale werking van lichaamsdelen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring the general working of body parts

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child moves along with a movement song and finds out what arms, legs, fingers and toes can do: waving, stamping, picking up a bead, wiggling. The child feels with a hand that the belly moves when breathing and that the heart beats fast after running. The child says what a part is for when asked, such as "with my teeth I bite". A child of 4 shows the use of a part by doing it; a child of 6 can also say it in a short sentence.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The word *globale* limits this to how body parts work in broad outline: what a part does, not how it does it. The wording asks for exploring, not for explaining. It uses the general term body parts (*lichaamsdelen*) and names no particular parts; it does not say whether parts inside the body are meant, so the choice of parts is left open. The senses have their own statement, De mens / 3 on the card.

### Common mistakes

Naming a part without being able to say what it is for. Thinking an arm or a leg can bend anywhere along its length and not only at the joints. Giving a part one use only, such as hands for holding, and leaving out its other uses.
