## Official wording

realiseren dat planten en dieren voedsel zijn voor andere planten en dieren en voor de mens

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that plants and animals are food for other plants and animals and for humans

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child feeds lettuce to a snail or a rabbit and sees it eat, watches birds at a feeding table, and says what a cat, a cow or a bird eats. At lunch the child tells that the apple comes from a tree and the egg from a hen. The child puts two or three pictures in a row, such as grass, cow, milk. A child of 4 says what one animal eats; a child of 6 can link three, such as leaf, caterpillar and bird, and says that people eat plants and animals too.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording states that plants and animals are food for other plants and animals and for humans. It asks for a realisation; it does not ask for drawing a chain or a cycle, and it names no plants or animals. The wording counts plants among those for whom plants and animals are food, and does not say how. The card does not print the words food chain, food cycle, predator or prey.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that an animal that eats another animal is bad or mean. Leaving people out: thinking that human food comes from the shop and not from plants and animals. Seeing only single pairs, such as cat eats mouse, without linking them, so that the plant at the start is left out.
