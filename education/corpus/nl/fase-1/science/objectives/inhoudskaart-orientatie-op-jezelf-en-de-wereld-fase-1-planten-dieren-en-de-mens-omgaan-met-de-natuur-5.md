## Official wording

met zorg omgaan met planten en dieren

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

treating plants and animals with care

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child strokes a rabbit softly with a flat hand, speaks quietly near it and lets it go when it wants to leave. The child waters a plant with a small can, looks at a ladybird on a hand without squeezing it, and puts a worm back on the soil. A child of 4 is shown how and tries with an adult close by; a child of 6 does a small care task when it is the child's turn and reminds other children to be gentle.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording describes a way of acting, with care, towards plants and animals; it is an attitude shown in behaviour, not knowledge. It does not say which plants or animals, or which care tasks, and it names no setting and no length of time, so these choices are left open. It states no safety or hygiene condition for handling animals.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out that an animal or a plant has needs of its own: they squeeze or chase an animal out of affection, pick up what should be left alone, or give far too much water or food. Many are careful with a furry animal and not with an insect, a spider or a plant.
