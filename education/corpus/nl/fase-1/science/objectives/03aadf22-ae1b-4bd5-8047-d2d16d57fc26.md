## Official wording

waarnemen en verkennen van elementaire fasen in groei, ontwikkeling (bloei) en voortplanting van planten, van dieren en van de mens

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

observing and exploring elementary stages in growth, development (*bloei*, flowering or flourishing) and reproduction of plants, of animals and of humans

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sows cress or a bean, looks each day and draws what has changed: seed, root, stalk, leaves, flower, new seeds. The child watches tadpoles or caterpillars in class change over the weeks, and puts photos of a baby, a toddler, a child and an adult, or of an egg, a chick and a hen, in order. The child brings a baby photo and compares it with how the child looks now. A child of 4 sees that the plant or animal has grown and says what is new; a child of 7 puts four or five stages in order and can say that the grown plant or animal makes seeds or young again.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The word *elementaire* limits this to basic stages, and the bracket puts *bloei* next to development; the Dutch word means both flowering and flourishing, and the wording does not separate them. The wording covers growth, development and reproduction, for plants, for animals and for humans, and asks for observing and exploring, not explaining. On reproduction the text sets no level of detail. It does not say which plants or animals, or how many stages.

### Common mistakes

Thinking a seed is not alive, or that a small complete plant is already inside it. Thinking growing means only getting bigger, and not seeing that the shape changes, as from caterpillar to butterfly or from flower to fruit. Putting the stages in a row that stops at the adult, without seeing that the adult makes seeds, eggs or young again.
