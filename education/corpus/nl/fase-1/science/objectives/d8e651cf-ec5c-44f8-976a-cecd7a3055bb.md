## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and naming parts of one's own body (human)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child touches and names parts of the child's own body when asked or in a song: head, shoulders, knee, elbow, heel. The child lies on a large sheet of paper while another child draws the outline, then points to the parts in the outline and says their names. The child draws a person and adds the parts the child knows. A child of 4 names the large parts such as head, belly, arms and legs; a child of 7 also names smaller parts such as wrist, ankle, eyebrow and chin.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is limited to the child's own body, and the bracket adds the single word human (*mens*). It asks for recognising and naming parts; it does not say which parts or how many, and does not say whether parts inside the body are meant. How body parts work is in another statement, `ojw/pdm/1/02/fase1`.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up parts that lie near each other or have a matching part elsewhere on the body, such as elbow and knee, wrist and ankle, or chin and cheek. Using one word for a whole region, such as "leg" for the foot or "arm" for the hand. Leaving out the neck, shoulders or trunk when drawing or naming a person, so that the arms and legs are attached to the head.
