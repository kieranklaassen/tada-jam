## Official wording

maken van een object naar eigen ontwerp (idee en inzicht) met materialen en voorwerpen (bijv. bouwen met constructiemateriaal)

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making an object to one's own design (idea and insight) with materials and objects (e.g. building with construction material)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child decides what to make, such as a bridge, a boat, a garage or a marble run, and says or draws the plan first. The child chooses blocks, parts of a construction set, boxes, tubes, tape and string, builds, tries whether the thing stands or works, and changes what does not. The child shows the result and tells what the idea was and what was changed. A child of 4 starts building and names the thing during or after the work; a child of 7 makes a simple drawing first, picks materials for a reason and improves the build after a test.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording asks for making an object to the child's own design, with materials and objects; the first brackets name idea and insight next to the word design. Building with construction material is given as an example (*bijv.*), not as the only way. The statement does not ask for a drawing beforehand, for moving parts, or for a product that meets a set requirement, and it names no tools.

### Common mistakes

Starting without an idea and naming the result afterwards, so that there is no design to compare it with. Choosing materials by colour or liking and not by whether they are strong, stiff or light enough. Copying another child's build or an example instead of using an own idea, or stopping at the first failure instead of changing the build.
