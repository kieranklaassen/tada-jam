## Official wording

onderzoeken en ervaren van bewegings- en overbrengingsprincipes zoals de werking van een touw met opwindas (hijskraan) en een scharnier

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and experiencing principles of movement and transmission such as the working of a rope with a winding axle (crane) and a hinge

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child turns the handle of a toy crane in the building corner or the sand pit and sees the bucket go up as the rope winds on, and down as it unwinds. The child swings the door of a play house or the lid of a box open and shut and looks at the hinge. The child winds a string round a stick to pull a small bucket of sand up. A child of 4 turns and pulls and sees the result; a child of 6 points to the part that turns and says "when I turn here, the rope gets shorter and the bucket comes up".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is about principles of movement and of transmission, that is, passing a movement on from one part to another. "Such as" (*zoals*) introduces two examples: how a rope with a winding axle works, with a crane (*hijskraan*) in brackets, and a hinge. The verbs are investigating and experiencing, not explaining or building. The wording names no other principle of movement or transmission; the list after *zoals* is not given as complete, so the choice of further principles is left open.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the load goes up because the handle is turned hard, without seeing that the rope winds round the axle. Not seeing the hinge as the fixed line of turning, and expecting a door or lid to open on either side. Seeing each device as a separate thing and not noticing the same part, an axle or a hinge, in another device.
