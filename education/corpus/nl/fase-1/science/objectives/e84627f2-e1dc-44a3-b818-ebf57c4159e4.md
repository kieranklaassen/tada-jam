## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child picks up everyday objects such as a spoon, a cup, a ball, scissors, a sponge and a key, and says what each is made of: metal, wood, plastic, glass, rubber, cloth. The child looks at the shape and tries the object out: a cup is hollow and holds water, a wheel is round and rolls, a spoon has a bowl to scoop with. The child tries a task with an object that does not fit it, such as scooping soup with a fork, and says why it does not work. A child of 4 names the material of familiar things and shows what the thing is for; a child of 7 can say why the shape fits the use.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording holds two parts: saying what objects are made of, and investigating how the form of an object relates to its function. It names no objects and no materials. It does not ask why a material was chosen, and it does not mention how objects are made. Properties of materials are in another statement, `ojw/nattech/1/01/fase1`.

### Common mistakes

Naming the object or its colour when asked for the material. Calling all metal "iron" and all see-through material "glass". Explaining the shape by looks ("because it is nice") and not by what the object has to do.
