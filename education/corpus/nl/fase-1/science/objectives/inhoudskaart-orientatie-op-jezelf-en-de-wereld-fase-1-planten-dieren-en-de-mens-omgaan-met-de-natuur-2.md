## Official wording

ervaren van schoonheid en esthetische waarde van natuur (met planten en dieren)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing the beauty and aesthetic value of nature (with plants and animals)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a flower through a magnifying glass, smells it, strokes a soft leaf or a feather, and watches a ladybird walk over a hand. The child chooses the leaf, shell or chestnut the child likes best and lays it on the nature table, or makes a picture on the ground with leaves and petals. A child of 4 shows a find to an adult and looks at it for a long time; a child of 6 says what is beautiful about it, such as the colour or the dots.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experiencing: the wording describes an experience of beauty, not knowledge or a skill, and it holds nothing that a child can get right or wrong. The brackets name plants and animals as part of the nature that is meant. The statement does not mention making art. Nature (*natuur*) is a general term, and apart from the plants and animals in the brackets the wording names no particular part of it. Care for plants and animals is in another statement of the card, Omgaan met de natuur / 5.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out what is small, common or not brightly coloured, such as moss, bark or a sparrow, and call beautiful only flowers and large or furry animals. Many pick or catch what they find beautiful, without seeing that this harms it.
