## Official wording

kennisnemen van enkele veel voorkomende planten en dieren in de eigen omgeving met naam, enkele kenmerken en de leefomstandigheden

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking note of some common plants and animals in one's own surroundings with name, some characteristics and the living conditions

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds a daisy and a dandelion in the grass, a snail on the wall, a blackbird or a pigeon in the yard, and hears from an adult what each is called. The child says the name, looks at a feature such as the yellow flower or the shell, and sees where it was: in the grass, under a stone, in a tree. The child finds the same plant or animal again on another day. A child of 4 knows two or three by name; a child of 6 names several from the child's own street or yard and tells one thing about how each looks and where it lives.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb *kennisnemen van* means taking note of or being told about; it asks less than recognising or naming from memory. The wording is limited to "some" plants and animals that are common in the child's own surroundings, and for these it names three things: the name, some characteristics and the living conditions. It gives no list of species and no number, so which plants and animals are meant depends on where the child lives.

### Common mistakes

Using one name for a whole group, such as "bird" for every bird, "flower" for every plant in bloom, or "bug" for every small animal. Knowing farm and zoo animals from books better than the animals of the child's own street, and naming those instead. Knowing a name without knowing anything about where the plant or animal lives or what it needs.
