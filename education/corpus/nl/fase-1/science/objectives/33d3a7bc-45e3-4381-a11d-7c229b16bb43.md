## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring and naming things and phenomena in physics with characteristic properties as a result of: I see, I hear, I feel, I smell, I taste

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a thing or a phenomenon, such as an ice cube, a magnet, a torch, a ringing bell or a shadow, and uses the senses on it one after the other. The child says in a full sentence what each sense gives: "I see that it shines", "I hear that it rings", "I feel that it is cold and wet". The child names the thing or phenomenon and the property that goes with it, such as ice that is cold and melts. A child of 4 says one thing that is seen or felt; a child of 7 uses several senses in turn and picks the property that is typical of the thing.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ties the exploring and naming to five kinds of observation, written from the child's side: I see, I hear, I feel, I smell, I taste. What is named are things and phenomena in physics with their characteristic properties; the wording gives no example of a thing, a phenomenon or a property. It does not mention measuring or instruments, and it states no condition for smelling or tasting.

### Common mistakes

Saying what a thing is or what it is for instead of what is seen, heard or felt ("it is a bell" instead of "I hear a high sound"). Mixing what is observed with what is thought, or naming the wrong sense ("I see that it is cold"). Using the eyes only and leaving out the other senses.
