## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing that trees (and other plants) change with the seasons

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child visits the same tree near school in each season and looks, feels and collects: buds and blossom in spring, full green leaves in summer, coloured and falling leaves and fruits in autumn, bare branches in winter. The child makes a bark rubbing, gathers fallen leaves and chestnuts, and draws or photographs the tree each time, then lays the pictures next to each other. A child of 4 sees and says what the tree looks like now; a child of 7 puts the four pictures in order, says which season each shows and tells what will come next.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experiencing, so the wording describes seeing and feeling the change, not explaining it. Trees are named first and other plants are added in brackets. The statement does not name the seasons or the changes, names no particular kinds of tree or plant and makes no exception for trees that stay green, and does not ask why leaves fall.

### Common mistakes

Thinking a bare tree in winter is dead. Thinking all trees lose their leaves, and not noticing those that stay green. Thinking the leaves fall only because the wind blows them off, or seeing the change as something that happens once and not every year.
