## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing the beauty and aesthetic value of nature (with plants and animals)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stops outside to look at a spider's web with dew, a butterfly, the colours of autumn leaves or a singing bird, and says what the child finds beautiful. The child picks out a favourite leaf, shell or stone, lays leaves and flowers in a pattern, or draws or paints what was seen. The child listens with closed eyes to birds or to wind in the trees. A child of 4 points and says "look, pretty"; a child of 7 can say what makes it beautiful to the child, such as the colours, the pattern or the sound.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experiencing: the wording describes an experience of beauty, not knowledge or a skill, and it holds nothing that a child can get right or wrong. The brackets name plants and animals as part of the nature that is meant. The statement does not mention making art. Nature (*natuur*) is a general term, and apart from the plants and animals in the brackets the wording names no particular part of it. Care for plants and animals is in another statement, `ojw/pdm/4/05/fase1`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out what is small, common or not brightly coloured, such as moss, bark or a sparrow, and call beautiful only flowers and large or furry animals. Many pick or catch what they find beautiful, without seeing that this harms it.
