observing the elementary parts of the plant and of the animal and their function

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child pulls a weed or a young bean plant carefully out of the soil and looks at root, stalk, leaf and flower, and takes apart a large flower such as a tulip. The child puts a white flower or a celery stalk in coloured water and sees the colour go up. The child looks at an animal and finds head, body, legs, tail, wings or fins, and tells what the animal does with them. A child of 4 points to leaf, flower and root and to the legs and head of an animal; a child of 7 says what a part is for, such as roots for taking up water and holding the plant in place, and wings for flying.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The word *elementaire* limits this to the basic parts, and the verb is observing: seeing the parts and what they are for. The wording speaks of the plant and the animal in general: it uses the general term parts (*onderdelen*) and names none, so which basic parts are observed is left open. It does not ask for explaining how a part works.

### Common mistakes

Not knowing the root as a part of the plant, because it is under the soil. Thinking the plant takes ready food from the soil through its roots, as people take food through the mouth. Calling the whole plant "flower", or naming a part without being able to say what it is for.
