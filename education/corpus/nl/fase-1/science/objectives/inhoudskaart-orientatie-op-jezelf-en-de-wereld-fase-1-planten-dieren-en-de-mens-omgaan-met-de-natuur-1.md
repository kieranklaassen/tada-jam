## Official wording

ontmoeten, verwonderen en beseffen van de grote diversiteit aan planten, dieren en schimmels (paddenstoelen)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

encountering, wondering at and realising the great diversity of plants, animals and fungi (mushrooms)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On a walk in the schoolyard or the park the child squats down by a snail, a beetle, a daisy or a mushroom, looks closely and calls another child to look too. With an adult the child lifts a stone or a piece of bark and sees woodlice run, and brings leaves, acorns or feathers to the nature table. The child tells in the circle what was found. A child of 4 looks and points with surprise; a child of 6 says that there were many different animals and plants and shows two that are not alike.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording joins three verbs: encountering, wondering and realising; the first two describe an experience and an attitude, not a skill. Its subject is the great diversity of three groups: plants, animals and fungi, with mushrooms in brackets. The statement does not ask for the names of species, for counting kinds, or for sorting into groups.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out what is small or does not move: they look at large animals and overlook insects, mosses and mushrooms. Many count a mushroom as a plant, and count as animals only those with four legs and fur.
