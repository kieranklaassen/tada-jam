## Official wording

ontdekken dat sommige dieren eieren leggen en dat de geboorte wordt voorafgegaan door de ontwikkeling in een ei

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering that some animals lay eggs and that birth is preceded by development in an egg

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a hen's egg carefully, feels how smooth it is, and sees one broken open. The child looks at eggs in an incubator, at frogspawn in a jar, or at a picture book about a chick, and tells what comes out of the egg. In play the child makes a nest and puts eggs in it. A child of 4 says that a bird or a chick comes out of an egg; a child of 6 can name more animals that lay eggs, such as a duck, a fish, a frog or a snail, and says that the young first grows inside.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says "some animals", so it does not cover all animals, and it names none. It holds two parts: that these animals lay eggs, and that the young develops in an egg before it is born. The statement sets no level of detail on what happens inside the egg, and it does not mention fertilisation or the difference between an egg that is eaten and an egg that hatches.

### Common mistakes

Thinking only birds lay eggs, and leaving out fish, frogs, snakes and insects. Thinking every egg from the shop holds a chick. Thinking the chick is complete in the egg from the start and only waits there, instead of growing.
