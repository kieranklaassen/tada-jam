## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and experiencing principles of movement and transmission such as the working of a rope with a winding axle (crane) and a hinge

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child turns the crank of a toy crane or a toy well and sees the rope wind round the axle and the load rise; the child turns back and sees the load go down. The child opens and closes a door, a box lid and a pair of scissors and finds the point where the parts turn. The child makes a hinge of tape between two strips of cardboard, or winds a string round a pencil to pull up a small basket. A child of 4 turns, opens and closes and watches what moves; a child of 7 can point to the axle or the hinge, say that turning in one place makes something move in another, and build a simple version.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is about principles of movement and of transmission, that is, passing a movement on from one part to another. "Such as" (*zoals*) introduces two examples: how a rope with a winding axle works, with a crane (*hijskraan*) in brackets, and a hinge. The verbs are investigating and experiencing, not explaining or building. The wording names no other principle of movement or transmission; the list after *zoals* is not given as complete, so the choice of further principles is left open.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the load goes up because the handle is turned hard, without seeing that the rope winds round the axle. Not seeing the hinge as the fixed line of turning, and expecting a door or lid to open on either side. Seeing each device as a separate thing and not noticing the same part, an axle or a hinge, in another device.
