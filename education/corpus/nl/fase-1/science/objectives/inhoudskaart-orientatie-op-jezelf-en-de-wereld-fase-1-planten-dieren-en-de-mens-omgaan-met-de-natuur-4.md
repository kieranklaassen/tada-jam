## Official wording

ervaren dat bomen (en andere planten) veranderen met de seizoenen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing that trees (and other plants) change with the seasons

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child goes to the tree in the schoolyard, looks up into it and tells what is seen: flowers, green leaves, yellow and brown leaves, or no leaves. The child kicks through fallen leaves and collects acorns and chestnuts in autumn, and looks for buds on a twig in spring. The child lays leaves and fruits on the nature table. A child of 4 says what the tree has today; a child of 6 remembers what it looked like before and says that the leaves fall in autumn and come back in spring.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experiencing, so the wording describes seeing and feeling the change, not explaining it. Trees are named first and other plants are added in brackets. The statement does not name the seasons or the changes, names no particular kinds of tree or plant and makes no exception for trees that stay green, and does not ask why leaves fall.

### Common mistakes

Thinking a bare tree in winter is dead. Thinking all trees lose their leaves, and not noticing those that stay green. Thinking the leaves fall only because the wind blows them off, or seeing the change as something that happens once and not every year.
