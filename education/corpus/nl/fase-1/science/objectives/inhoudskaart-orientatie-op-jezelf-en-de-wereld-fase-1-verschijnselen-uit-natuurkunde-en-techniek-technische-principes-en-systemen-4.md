## Official wording

werken met een (eenvoudige) werktekening of handleiding

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

working with a (simple) working drawing or manual

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays a building card next to the blocks or the bead board, takes the pieces shown and puts them down as in the picture. The child follows a row of pictures for a craft, such as fold, cut, stick, and looks back at the card after each step. The child holds the result next to the picture and says whether it is the same. A child of 4 copies a picture of three or four pieces with the colours and places right; a child of 6 follows a card with a few steps in order and corrects a piece that is in the wrong place.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names two things to work with: a working drawing (*werktekening*, a drawing that shows how to make something) or a manual; the bracketed word limits this to a simple one. It says "working with", so the child uses a drawing or manual that is given; the wording does not ask the child to make one. It does not say whether the manual has pictures or text, and it does not mention scale, measurements or symbols.

### Common mistakes

Skipping a step or doing the steps out of order, and noticing only at the end. Placing a piece mirrored or turned, because the child does not look at the build from the same side as the picture. Miscounting how many pieces long or high a part is in the picture.
