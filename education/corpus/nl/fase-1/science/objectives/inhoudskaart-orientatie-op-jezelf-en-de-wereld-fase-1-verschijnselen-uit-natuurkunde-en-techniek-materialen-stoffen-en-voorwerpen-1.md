## Official wording

verkennen en ontdekken van eigenschappen van materialen en stoffen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring and discovering properties of materials and substances

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At the water table and the sand table the child pours, sieves, squeezes and moulds, and finds that dry sand runs through the fingers and wet sand keeps its shape. The child feels wood, metal, cloth and plastic in a feeling box and says which is cold, soft, rough or smooth, and tries which things float in a tub of water. The child kneads clay and sees that it keeps the new shape. A child of 4 explores with the hands and names one property; a child of 6 sorts things into two groups, such as floats and sinks, and tells what was found.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording asks for exploring and discovering properties of materials and of substances; it names no property, no material and no substance, so the choice of each is left open. It does not mention measuring, and it does not say whether changes such as melting and freezing are meant. What objects are made of is in another statement of the card, Materialen, stoffen en voorwerpen / 2.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up the object and the material, for example saying a spoon sinks because it is a spoon, not because it is metal. Thinking heavy things always sink and light things always float, whatever their shape or material. Using one word such as "hard" for several properties: strong, stiff, heavy and not soft.
