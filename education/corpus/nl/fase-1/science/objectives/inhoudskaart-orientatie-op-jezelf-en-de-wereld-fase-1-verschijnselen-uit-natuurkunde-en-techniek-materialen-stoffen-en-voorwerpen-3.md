## Official wording

maken van een object naar eigen ontwerp (idee en inzicht) met materialen en voorwerpen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

making an object to one's own design (idea and insight) with materials and objects

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the building corner or at the craft table the child says what the child is going to make, such as a house for a toy animal or a car, and picks blocks, boxes, rolls, glue and tape for it. The child builds, sees a part fall over or come loose, and tries another way. The child shows the thing and tells what it is. A child of 4 often decides what it is while building; a child of 6 has a plan before starting, keeps to it and can say why a material was chosen.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording asks for making an object to the child's own design, with materials and objects; the brackets name idea and insight next to the word design. The card gives no example of a material or of an object to make. The statement does not ask for a drawing beforehand, for moving parts, or for a product that meets a set requirement, and it names no tools.

### Common mistakes

Starting without an idea and naming the result afterwards, so that there is no design to compare it with. Choosing materials by colour or liking and not by whether they are strong, stiff or light enough. Copying another child's build or an example instead of using an own idea, or stopping at the first failure instead of changing the build.
