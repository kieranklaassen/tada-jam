## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that plants and animals are food for other plants and animals and for humans (food cycle)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child watches a bird pull a worm out of the grass, a caterpillar eat a leaf, or a snail eat lettuce in a tank, and tells who eats what. The child lays pictures in a row: leaf, caterpillar, bird, or grass, cow, and milk and meat for people. The child names what on the child's own plate comes from a plant and what from an animal. A child of 4 says what a familiar animal eats; a child of 7 lays a row of three or four and can say that the animal at the end depends on the plant at the start.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording states that plants and animals are food for other plants and animals and for humans, and the bracket adds the word food cycle (*voedselkringloop*). It asks for a realisation; it does not ask for drawing a chain or a cycle, and it names no plants or animals. The wording counts plants among those for whom plants and animals are food, and does not say how. The words food chain, food web, predator and prey are not in the statement.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that an animal that eats another animal is bad or mean. Leaving people out: thinking that human food comes from the shop and not from plants and animals. Seeing only single pairs, such as cat eats mouse, without linking them, so that the plant at the start is left out.
