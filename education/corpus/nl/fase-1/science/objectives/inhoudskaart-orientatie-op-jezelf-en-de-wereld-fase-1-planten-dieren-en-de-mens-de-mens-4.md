## Official wording

ontdekken en benoemen van eigen duidelijke uiterlijke kenmerken en de overeenkomsten of verschillen met andere mensen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering and naming one's own clear outward characteristics and the similarities or differences with other people

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks in a mirror and tells what the hair, eyes and skin look like, and whether the child wears glasses or has a tooth missing. Two children put their hands or feet next to each other, or stand back to back, and say what is the same and what is different. The child chooses crayons that match the child's own hair and eyes for a drawing of the face. A child of 4 names an own feature when asked ("I have brown hair"); a child of 6 compares with another child ("we both have blue eyes, but she is taller").

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is limited to the child's own features that are outward and clear, that is, easy to see, and to how they are alike or different from those of other people. It says "similarities or differences" and names no features. The card does not say whether the other people are children or adults. The statement does not ask why features are alike or different, and it does not mention heredity.

### Common mistakes

Naming clothes or belongings, such as a red jumper or a new bag, as features of the body. Seeing only differences and leaving out what is the same, or only what is the same. Treating a feature that changes, such as hair length, as fixed, and a fixed one, such as eye colour, as something that will change with age.
