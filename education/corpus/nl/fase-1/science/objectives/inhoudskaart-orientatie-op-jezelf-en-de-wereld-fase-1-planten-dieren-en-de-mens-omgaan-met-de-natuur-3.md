## Official wording

waarnemen en verkennen van de omgeving met alle zintuigen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

observing and exploring the surroundings with all the senses

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the schoolyard or the park the child strokes a tree trunk, squeezes mud, smells a flower, listens for a bird and looks closely at an ant. The child closes the eyes and says what is heard, or feels a thing from outside behind the back and says what it is. The child tastes only what an adult hands out. A child of 4 uses hands, nose and ears when an adult invites it; a child of 6 tells what was felt, smelt and heard and uses words such as soft, hard, wet and prickly.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says "all the senses", so it is not limited to looking, and it names none of the senses. Its object is the surroundings (*omgeving*); the sub-heading it stands under is about dealing with nature, but the wording itself does not say natural surroundings only. It asks for observing and exploring, not for recording or explaining, and it states no condition for tasting or touching.

### Common mistakes

Using only the eyes and naming what a thing is, without touching, smelling or listening. Saying "nice" or "dirty" instead of describing what is sensed, such as rough, cold or sweet. Putting things in the mouth, or touching plants and animals that sting or prick, because the child does not yet tell safe from unsafe.
