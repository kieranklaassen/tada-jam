## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring outward characteristics of plants, of animals such as shape, colour, build and hairiness

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child collects leaves and sorts them by shape, edge or colour, and feels which are smooth and which are hairy. The child looks at animals or clear photos and describes them: striped or plain, fur, feathers, scales or a bare skin, long or short legs, a round or a long body. The child groups toy animals or pictures by one feature and then by another. A child of 4 sorts by one clear feature such as colour; a child of 7 describes one plant or animal by several features and finds a feature that two of them share.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is limited to outward characteristics, those that can be seen or felt from outside. "Such as" (*zoals*) introduces four examples: shape, colour, build and hairiness; the list is not given as complete. The source prints a comma, not "and", between plants and animals, and prints a colon at the end with nothing after it; the gloss leaves the colon out. The statement does not ask for naming species or for a fixed way of sorting.

### Common mistakes

Changing the feature halfway through sorting, for example starting by colour and going on by size. Describing what an animal does, or whether the child likes it, instead of how it looks. Looking at one striking feature only, so that two animals of the same colour are called the same.
