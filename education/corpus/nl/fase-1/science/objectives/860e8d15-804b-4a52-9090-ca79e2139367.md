## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child joins two pieces of cardboard or wood in different ways and tries which holds: glue, tape, a knot in a string, a nail, a screw, a bolt with a nut. With an adult close by, the child hammers a nail into soft wood, turns a screw with a screwdriver and tightens a nut on a bolt by hand. The child pulls at each joint and takes apart the ones that can be undone. A child of 4 glues, tapes and turns large nuts on a toy workbench; a child of 7 chooses a joint that fits the job and can say which joints can be undone and which cannot.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experimenting: trying out, not mastering. The brackets give examples (*bijv.*): glue, *knopen*, nails, screws, and bolts and nuts; the list is not given as complete. The Dutch word *knopen* can mean knots or buttons, and the wording does not show which. The text names no tools, states no safety condition for nails, screws or hammering, and does not say which materials are joined.

### Common mistakes

Using far too much glue and expecting it to hold immediately, before it has dried. Turning a screw or a nut the wrong way, or hitting a screw with a hammer as if it were a nail. Thinking a joint that holds when left alone will also hold when it is pulled or loaded.
