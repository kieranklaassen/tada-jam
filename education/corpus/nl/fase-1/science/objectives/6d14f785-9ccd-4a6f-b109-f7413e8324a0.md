## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discussing that people and some animals give birth to live young and that birth is preceded by a period of being carried by the mother

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child talks in the group about a baby brother or sister, or about a pregnant mother, cat or guinea pig, and tells what was seen: the belly became bigger and then the baby or the young was born. The child looks at pictures of a pregnant animal and of the mother with her young and puts them in order. The child asks questions and listens to the answers of the teacher and of other children. A child of 4 tells that a baby grows in the mother's belly; a child of 7 can name animals that are born alive and animals that come out of an egg, and says that the young is carried for a time before birth.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing (*bespreken*): the wording describes a conversation, not an investigation. It covers people and "some animals", names no animals, and holds two parts: live birth, and a period before it in which the mother carries the young. This statement touches on human reproduction, and the text sets no level of detail: it does not say how a pregnancy starts, how long it lasts, or how the birth goes.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the baby is in the stomach, where the food goes. Thinking the baby was always there, or was fetched or bought complete, with no period of growing. Thinking all animals carry their young in the belly, or that all animals lay eggs.
