## Official wording

waarnemen van de elementaire onderdelen van de plant en van het dier en de functie ervan

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

observing the elementary parts of the plant and of the animal and their function

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With an adult the child takes a plant out of its pot and sees the roots under the soil, feels the stalk and the leaves and smells the flower. The child looks at the class pet or a bird outside and points to its head, legs, tail, wings or ears. The child says what a part does when asked, such as "the bird flies with its wings". A child of 4 points to flower, leaf and stalk and to legs and head; a child of 6 names the root as well and gives a use for some parts.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The word *elementaire* limits this to the basic parts, and the verb is observing: seeing the parts and what they are for. The wording speaks of the plant and the animal in general: it uses the general term parts (*onderdelen*) and names none, so which basic parts are observed is left open. It does not ask for explaining how a part works.

### Common mistakes

Not knowing the root as a part of the plant, because it is under the soil. Thinking the plant takes ready food from the soil through its roots, as people take food through the mouth. Calling the whole plant "flower", or naming a part without being able to say what it is for.
