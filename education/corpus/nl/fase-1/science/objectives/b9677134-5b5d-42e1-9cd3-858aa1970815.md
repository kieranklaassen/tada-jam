## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring and applying simple construction principles for a stable and sturdy product (constructions) such as stacking, fitting into each other, laying in bond (*in-verband-leggen*, overlapping the joints as in brickwork), among other things with construction materials

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stacks blocks into a tower and finds that a wide base and straight stacking keep it up. The child builds one wall with each block across the joint of the two below and one wall of separate columns, pushes against both and feels which holds. The child clicks or slots parts of a construction set into each other and makes a bridge that carries a toy car. A child of 4 stacks, sees the tower fall and tries again; a child of 7 chooses on purpose to overlap the blocks, widen the base or add a support, and tests the build with a push or a load.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is limited to simple construction principles that make a product stable and sturdy; the bracket adds the word constructions. "Such as" (*zoals*) introduces three examples: stacking, fitting into each other and laying in bond. The abbreviation *o.a.* (among other things) makes construction materials one means among others. The wording names no other construction principle; the list after *zoals* is not given as complete, so the choice of further simple principles is left open. The statement does not mention load or measuring, and it does not say what the product is.

### Common mistakes

Building tall on a narrow base, and putting large or heavy blocks on top of small ones. Stacking blocks in straight columns so that the joints line up and the wall splits between the columns. Thinking a build is sturdy because it stands, without testing it with a push or a load.
