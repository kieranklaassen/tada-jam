## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering and naming one's own clear outward characteristics and the similarities or differences with other people (children)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks in a mirror and says what is seen: colour of the eyes, colour and kind of hair, freckles, glasses, a missing tooth. The child stands next to a classmate and compares: who is taller, who has curls, whose hands are bigger, what is the same. The child draws a self-portrait in the right colours, or marks the child's own height on a strip of paper on the wall. A child of 4 names one or two own features such as hair colour; a child of 7 names several and says for each whether a classmate has the same or a different one.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is limited to the child's own features that are outward and clear, that is, easy to see, and to how they are alike or different from those of other people; the bracket adds children. It says "similarities or differences" and names no features. The statement does not ask why features are alike or different, and it does not mention heredity.

### Common mistakes

Naming clothes or belongings, such as a red jumper or a new bag, as features of the body. Seeing only differences and leaving out what is the same, or only what is the same. Treating a feature that changes, such as hair length, as fixed, and a fixed one, such as eye colour, as something that will change with age.
