aangeven waarvan voorwerpen zijn gemaakt en de relatie tussen de vorm en de functie onderzoeken

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

indicating what objects are made of and investigating the relation between the form and the function

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes things from a basket, such as a wooden spoon, a metal spoon, a plastic cup and a woollen mitten, taps and feels them and says what they are made of. The child shows what each is for and tries it: pours water into a cup and onto a flat board and sees which holds it, rolls a ball and a block. A child of 4 says "wood" or "iron" for familiar things and shows their use; a child of 6 can say what about the shape makes the thing work, such as "the cup has a side, so the water stays in".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording holds two parts: saying what objects are made of, and investigating how the form of an object relates to its function. It names no objects and no materials. It does not ask why a material was chosen, and it does not mention how objects are made. Properties of materials are in another statement of the card, Materialen, stoffen en voorwerpen / 1.

### Common mistakes

Naming the object or its colour when asked for the material. Calling all metal "iron" and all see-through material "glass". Explaining the shape by looks ("because it is nice") and not by what the object has to do.
