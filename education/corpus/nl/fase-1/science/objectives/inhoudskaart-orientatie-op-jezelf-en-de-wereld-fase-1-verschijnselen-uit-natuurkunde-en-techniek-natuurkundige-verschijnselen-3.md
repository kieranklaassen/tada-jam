## Official wording

beseffen dat natuurkundige verschijnselen veel bieden, maar ook gevaarlijk kunnen zijn

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that physical phenomena offer a lot, but can also be dangerous

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child feels the warmth of the sun or of a radiator from a distance and says that it is pleasant, and hears from an adult that the stove and the kettle are hot and are not touched. The child tells in the circle what a lamp, water or fire is good for and when it can hurt. The child shows what to do: stay away from the socket, do not look into the sun, cover the ears when a sound is too loud. A child of 4 follows the rule and repeats it; a child of 6 can say what is useful and what is dangerous about the same thing, such as fire that warms and burns.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording states two sides of physical phenomena: that they offer a lot, and that they can also be dangerous. It names no phenomenon and no danger, and it asks for a realisation, not for handling a risk. It states no safety condition and no rule of conduct. A neighbouring statement, Natuurkundige verschijnselen / 2 on the card, names light, sound, temperature, force and electricity as its own subjects; this statement does not say that it means the same five.

### Common mistakes

Seeing a thing as only good or only dangerous, not both: fire is bad, water is safe. Judging danger by what can be seen, so that a hot plate that does not glow, a socket or a very loud sound is taken as harmless. Being able to repeat a rule without knowing the reason, and not applying it to a new case.
