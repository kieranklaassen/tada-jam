## Official wording

onderzoeken van de vorm en functie van de zintuigen van de mens

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the form and function of the human senses or sense organs (*zintuigen*, which means both)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at eyes, ears, nose, tongue and skin in a mirror or on a classmate and describes them: two eyes at the front, an ear on each side, a nose with two holes. The child tests what each one does: guesses an object in a feeling bag by touch, names a sound with the eyes closed, smells jars with the nose pinched and then open, tastes pieces of fruit. The child says which part of the body was used each time. A child of 4 uses the sense and names the part; a child of 7 can link the shape to the use, such as the outer ear catching sound or two eyes seeing wider than one.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is limited to humans and asks for two things: form and function. The Dutch word *zintuigen* covers both the senses and the sense organs, and the wording does not separate them. It does not say how many there are and does not name them. It does not mention the senses of animals or the brain, and it does not say whether the inside of the eye or ear is meant.

### Common mistakes

Linking taste to the mouth as a whole or to the teeth, not to the tongue, and not noticing that smell takes part in tasting. Thinking that touch is only in the hands, not in the skin of the whole body. Naming the part but not what it does, or naming a wrong part ("I hear with my head").
