## Official wording

beseffen dat planten, dieren en de mens in hun leefomgeving water, voedsel en een plek nodig hebben om te leven

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that plants, animals and humans in their living environment need water, food and a place in order to live

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child waters a plant in class and sees that a plant that got no water hangs limp. The child gives the class pet or a pet at home food and fresh water and helps to clean its cage, and tells what the child's own body needs each day. The child looks where a snail, a bird or a dandelion lives outside and what it finds there. A child of 4 gives water and food and sees what happens; a child of 7 can say that plants, animals and people all need water, food and a place, and can name what a given animal eats and where it lives.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names three needs: water, food and a place. It puts plants, animals and humans in one sentence, and it asks for a realisation, not for an explanation of how a plant or a body uses water or food. The statement does not mention air, light or warmth, and it does not say what counts as food for a plant.

### Common mistakes

Thinking plants are not alive because they do not move, so that they need nothing. Thinking plants eat soil, or that water is their food. Thinking wild animals are cared for by people in the same way as pets, so that someone gives them food and a place.
