treating plants and animals with care

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child waters the class plants when the soil feels dry, feeds the class pet the right amount and gives it clean water. The child holds a small animal low and with two hands, strokes it gently, and puts a beetle or a snail back where it was found. Outside, the child walks around flowers and does not pull leaves or branches off. A child of 4 does this with an adult next to the child and copies the adult's gentle hands; a child of 7 does a care task alone when it is the child's turn and can say what the plant or animal needs and what would hurt it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording describes a way of acting, with care, towards plants and animals; it is an attitude shown in behaviour, not knowledge. It does not say which plants or animals, or which care tasks, and it names no setting and no length of time, so these choices are left open. It states no safety or hygiene condition for handling animals.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out that an animal or a plant has needs of its own: they squeeze or chase an animal out of affection, pick up what should be left alone, or give far too much water or food. Many are careful with a furry animal and not with an insect, a spider or a plant.
