## Official wording

onderzoeken van de vorm en functie van de zintuigen van de mens

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating the form and function of the human senses or sense organs (*zintuigen*, which means both)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts a hand in a feeling bag and says what is inside without looking, closes the eyes and points to where a bell rings, smells a jar and tastes a piece of apple. After each try the child points to the part of the body that was used: eyes, ears, nose, tongue, skin of the hands. The child looks at these parts in a mirror and tells what they look like. A child of 4 uses each sense and names eyes, ears, nose and mouth; a child of 6 can say "I smell with my nose" and "I feel with my skin".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is limited to humans and asks for two things: form and function. The Dutch word *zintuigen* covers both the senses and the sense organs, and the wording does not separate them. It does not say how many there are and does not name them. It does not mention the senses of animals or the brain, and it does not say whether the inside of the eye or ear is meant.

### Common mistakes

Linking taste to the mouth as a whole or to the teeth, not to the tongue, and not noticing that smell takes part in tasting. Thinking that touch is only in the hands, not in the skin of the whole body. Naming the part but not what it does, or naming a wrong part ("I hear with my head").
