## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experiencing and investigating weather phenomena and the relation with the seasons (it rains, the wind blows, the sun shines)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child goes outside and feels rain on the hands, wind against the face and sun on the skin, and says which it is. The child puts out a pot to catch rain, holds up a ribbon or a small windmill to see how hard the wind blows, and looks the next day whether the puddles have dried. Each day the child puts a picture of the day's weather on a weather chart, and after some weeks looks which pictures were chosen most. A child of 4 feels and names rain, wind and sun; a child of 7 can say which weather comes often in which season, such as frost in winter.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets name three weather phenomena: rain, wind and sunshine. The wording asks for experiencing and investigating them and for their relation with the seasons; it does not ask for measuring in units or for instruments. The cluster is called weather and climate, but this statement does not mention climate. It does not name the seasons. The term weather phenomena (*weersverschijnselen*) is general, and apart from the three in the brackets the wording names no particular ones.

### Common mistakes

Naming the weather of this moment as the season ("it is winter because it rains"). Thinking a kind of weather belongs to one season only, for example that the sun does not shine in winter or that it never rains in summer. Thinking the wind is made by the moving trees.
