## Official wording

realiseren dat een levend wezen steeds voortkomt uit een ander levend wezen van dezelfde soort

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), SLO open data: curriculum-inhoudslijnen (inhoudslijnen po) (commit d2ec46f), inh_cluster ojw/pdm/3; SLO open data: curriculum-basis (doelen, doelniveaus, niveaus) (commit 5adf7a4), doelniveau f56fdf37-19c7-4a8c-890c-f2953af3b121.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that a living being always comes from another living being of the same kind or species (*soort*, which means both)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child matches young animals to their parents with pictures or toy animals: lamb with sheep, calf with cow, duckling with duck, tadpole with frog. The child takes seeds out of an apple, a sunflower or a bean pod, sows them and sees which plant grows from them. The child tells from whom a kitten, a chick or the child itself comes. A child of 4 matches a young animal that looks like its parent; a child of 7 also matches young that look different, such as caterpillar and butterfly, and can say that a bean seed gives a bean plant and no other plant.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording states one idea: a living being comes from another living being, and that one is of the same kind. The word *steeds* means always, so the wording gives no exception. The statement does not say how this happens and sets no level of detail on reproduction; eggs and live birth are in other statements (`ojw/pdm/3/05/fase1` and `ojw/pdm/3/06/fase1`). It names no plants or animals.

### Common mistakes

Thinking small animals such as worms, flies or snails come from mud, rain or rubbish and have no parents. Thinking a plant grows out of the soil by itself, without a seed or a parent plant. Not linking a young animal to its parent when it looks different, such as a tadpole to a frog.
