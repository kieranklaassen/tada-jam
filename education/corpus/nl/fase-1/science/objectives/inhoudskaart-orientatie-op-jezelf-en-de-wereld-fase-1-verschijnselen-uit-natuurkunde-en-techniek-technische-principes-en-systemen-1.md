## Official wording

verkennen en toepassen van eenvoudige constructieprincipes voor een stabiel en stevig product zoals stapelen, in elkaar passen, in-verband-leggen o.a. met constructiematerialen

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

exploring and applying simple construction principles for a stable and sturdy product such as stacking, fitting into each other, laying in bond (*in-verband-leggen*, overlapping the joints as in brickwork), among other things with construction materials

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the building corner the child stacks blocks as high as possible, sees the tower lean and fall, and starts again with the big blocks at the bottom. The child fits parts of a construction set into each other, and lays blocks so that each lies over the gap between the two below. The child pushes gently against the build to find out whether it stays up. A child of 4 stacks and fits by trying; a child of 6 says why the build stays up, such as "the big ones are at the bottom", and uses that in the next build.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is limited to simple construction principles that make a product stable and sturdy. "Such as" (*zoals*) introduces three examples: stacking, fitting into each other and laying in bond. The abbreviation *o.a.* (among other things) makes construction materials one means among others. The wording names no other construction principle; the list after *zoals* is not given as complete, so the choice of further simple principles is left open. The statement does not mention load or measuring, and it does not say what the product is.

### Common mistakes

Building tall on a narrow base, and putting large or heavy blocks on top of small ones. Stacking blocks in straight columns so that the joints line up and the wall splits between the columns. Thinking a build is sturdy because it stands, without testing it with a push or a load.
