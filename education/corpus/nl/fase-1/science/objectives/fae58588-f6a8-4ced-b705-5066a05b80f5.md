## Official wording

realiseren dat de aanwezigheid en positie van de zon dag, nacht en de seizoenen veroorzaken

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that the presence and position of the sun cause day, night and the seasons

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child notices where the sun is in the morning and in the afternoon, without looking straight at it, and sees that the child's own shadow on the playground is long early in the day and short at noon. The child tells that it is light when the sun is in the sky and dark when it is not, and that in winter it is already dark at dinner time. With a lamp and a ball the child sees one side of the ball lit and the other side dark. A child of 4 links the sun with day and the dark with night; a child of 7 can say that the sun stands higher and stays up longer in summer than in winter.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording asks for a realisation, not an explanation: that the sun being there, and where it stands, causes day, night and the seasons. The statement does not mention the earth turning or going round the sun, the tilt of the earth, or shadows. It speaks of the seasons as a group and does not name them.

### Common mistakes

Thinking night comes because the sun goes to sleep, goes behind clouds or hills, or is replaced by the moon. Thinking it is summer because the sun is closer and winter because it is further away. Thinking it gets dark because people go to bed.
