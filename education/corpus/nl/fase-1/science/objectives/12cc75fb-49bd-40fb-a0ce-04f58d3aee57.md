## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tries out what a body part can do: bends the arm at the elbow, turns the head, stands on one leg, grips a ball with and without the thumb. The child feels the heartbeat with a hand on the chest after running and feels the chest go up and down when breathing. The child tells what a part is used for: legs for walking and jumping, teeth for biting and chewing, hands for holding. A child of 4 shows what a part does by doing it; a child of 7 can say in words what a part is for and where the body bends.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The word *globale* limits this to how body parts work in broad outline: what a part does, not how it does it. The wording asks for exploring, not for explaining. It uses the general term body parts (*lichaamsdelen*) and names no particular parts; it does not say whether parts inside the body are meant, so the choice of parts is left open. The senses have their own statement, `ojw/pdm/1/03/fase1`.

### Common mistakes

Naming a part without being able to say what it is for. Thinking an arm or a leg can bend anywhere along its length and not only at the joints. Giving a part one use only, such as hands for holding, and leaving out its other uses.
