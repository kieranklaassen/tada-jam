## Official wording

ontdekken van de aanwezigheid van maan en sterren in de nacht (heelal)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering the presence of the moon and stars in the night (universe)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks out of the window at home before bedtime, or up at the sky on a dark winter morning on the way to school, and points to the moon and the stars. In class the child tells what was seen and draws or sticks a moon and stars on dark paper. A child of 4 says "moon" and points; a child of 6 can tell that the moon looked round one evening and thin on another.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is about discovering that the moon and stars are there at night; the bracket adds the single word universe (*heelal*). The statement names no phases of the moon, no planets and no constellations, and it does not ask for an explanation. The sun is the subject of another statement of the card, Weer, klimaat en hemellichamen / 4.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the moon is there only at night and is gone in the daytime, although it is often visible by day. Thinking the stars go away or are switched off in the morning. Thinking the moon follows the child while the child walks or rides in a car.
