## Official wording

herkennen en benoemen van onderdelen van het eigen lichaam

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and naming parts of one's own body

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child points to the nose, an ear, a knee or the back when the part is named, and says the name when an adult points to a part. The child sings a body song and touches each part at its word, and looks in a mirror to find the parts of the face. A child of 4 names head, hands, feet, belly and the main parts of the face; a child of 6 also names parts such as elbow, shoulder, heel and neck.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is limited to the child's own body. It asks for recognising and naming parts; it does not say which parts or how many, and does not say whether parts inside the body are meant. How body parts work is in another statement of the card, De mens / 2.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up parts that lie near each other or have a matching part elsewhere on the body, such as elbow and knee, wrist and ankle, or chin and cheek. Using one word for a whole region, such as "leg" for the foot or "arm" for the hand. Leaving out the neck, shoulders or trunk when drawing or naming a person, so that the arms and legs are attached to the head.
