## Official wording

experimenteren met eenvoudige verbindingen (bijv. lijm, knopen, spijkers, schroeven, bouten en moeren)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

experimenting with simple connections (e.g. glue, *knopen* (knots, or buttons), nails, screws, bolts and nuts)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At the craft table the child sticks paper and boxes together with glue and finds that it holds only after it has dried. The child threads a string and tries to tie a knot, turns big plastic bolts and nuts on a toy workbench, and with an adult next to the child hammers a nail into soft wood. The child pulls at what was joined to find out whether it holds. A child of 4 glues, sticks and turns; a child of 6 tries more than one way for the same job and says which held best.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is experimenting: trying out, not mastering. The brackets give examples (*bijv.*): glue, *knopen*, nails, screws, and bolts and nuts; the list is not given as complete. The Dutch word *knopen* can mean knots or buttons, and the wording does not show which. The text names no tools, states no safety condition for nails, screws or hammering, and does not say which materials are joined.

### Common mistakes

Using far too much glue and expecting it to hold immediately, before it has dried. Turning a screw or a nut the wrong way, or hitting a screw with a hammer as if it were a nail. Thinking a joint that holds when left alone will also hold when it is pulled or loaded.
