## Official wording

realiseren dat een levend wezen steeds voortkomt uit een ander levend wezen van dezelfde soort

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that a living being always comes from another living being of the same kind or species (*soort*, which means both)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts toy animals or pictures in families: the piglet with the pig, the foal with the horse, the chick with the hen. The child picks pips out of an apple or peas out of a pod and tells which plant they came from. The child says that the child came from a mother and that a puppy comes from a dog. A child of 4 matches young and parent when they look alike; a child of 6 can say that a cat always gets kittens and never puppies.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording states one idea: a living being comes from another living being, and that one is of the same kind. The word *steeds* means always, so the wording gives no exception. The statement does not say how this happens and sets no level of detail on reproduction; eggs and live birth are in other statements (Groeien, bloeien en voortplanten / 5 and / 6 on the card). It names no plants or animals.

### Common mistakes

Thinking small animals such as worms, flies or snails come from mud, rain or rubbish and have no parents. Thinking a plant grows out of the soil by itself, without a seed or a parent plant. Not linking a young animal to its parent when it looks different, such as a tadpole to a frog.
