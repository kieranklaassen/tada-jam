## Official wording

ontdekken en verwonderen over: licht (licht en donker), geluid (volume, toonhoogte), temperatuur (warmte), kracht (van magneten, van water) en elektriciteit (lamp en zoemer)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discovering and wondering about: light (light and dark), sound (volume, pitch), temperature (heat), force (of magnets, of water) and electricity (lamp and buzzer)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child plays with a torch in a darkened corner and sees the child's own shadow become bigger and smaller. The child shakes and strikes instruments to make loud, soft, high and low sounds, feels warm and cold with the hands, and picks up paper clips with a magnet. At the water table the child lets water from a jug push a water wheel round, and with an adult presses a switch that makes a small lamp light or a buzzer sound on a battery. A child of 4 repeats many times what is surprising; a child of 6 tells what was done and what happened, such as "when I hit hard, it is loud".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names five subjects, each with brackets: light (the brackets name light and dark), sound (volume and pitch), temperature (heat), force (of magnets and of water) and electricity (a lamp and a buzzer). The verbs are discovering and wondering; the wording does not ask for explaining, measuring or stating a rule. It states no safety condition for heat or electricity. Apart from the magnets, the lamp and the buzzer in the brackets, it names no apparatus or instrument.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up loud with high and soft with low when describing a sound. Thinking a magnet pulls all metal, or all shiny things. Thinking a shadow is a thing of its own that belongs to the body, not a place where the light is blocked.
