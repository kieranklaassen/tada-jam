## Official wording

realiseren dat de aanwezigheid en positie van de zon dag, nacht en de seizoenen veroorzaken

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that the presence and position of the sun cause day, night and the seasons

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sees the sun through the classroom window in the morning and on the other side of the building in the afternoon, without looking straight at it. The child says that it is day when the sun shines and night when it is dark, and tells what the child does by day and by night. A child of 4 links the sun with daytime and the dark with night; a child of 6 can tell that in winter it gets dark early and that in summer it is still light at bedtime.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording asks for a realisation, not an explanation: that the sun being there, and where it stands, causes day, night and the seasons. The statement does not mention the earth turning or going round the sun, the tilt of the earth, or shadows. It speaks of the seasons as a group and does not name them.

### Common mistakes

Thinking night comes because the sun goes to sleep, goes behind clouds or hills, or is replaced by the moon. Thinking it is summer because the sun is closer and winter because it is further away. Thinking it gets dark because people go to bed.
