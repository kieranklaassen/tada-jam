## Official wording

vergelijken van onderdelen van de mens met onderdelen van dieren

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing parts of the human being with parts of animals

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child strokes a rabbit or a guinea pig in class, or looks at a large picture of an animal, and points to its eyes, ears, nose and legs, then to the same parts on the child's own body. The child says what the animal has that the child does not have, such as a tail, fur, a beak or wings, and moves like the animal on hands and feet. A child of 4 finds the eyes, ears and mouth of the animal; a child of 6 says what is the same and what is different ("I have two legs, the cat has four").

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording asks for comparing parts of humans with parts of animals; it does not say which parts or which animals. It does not say whether parts inside the body are meant, and it does not ask for sorting animals into groups such as mammals or birds.

### Common mistakes

Counting only four-legged animals with fur as animals, so that a bird, a fish or an insect is not compared at all. Calling every part of an animal by the human name, such as hands for a dog's front paws or a nose for a bird's beak, without seeing the difference. Comparing by size only ("the horse's leg is bigger") and leaving out shape and use.
