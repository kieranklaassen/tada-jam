exploring and discovering properties of materials and substances

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child handles wood, metal, plastic, paper, cloth, clay, sand and water and finds out what each is like: hard or soft, rough or smooth, heavy or light, bends or breaks, lets light through or not. The child tests which things float and which sink, which a magnet picks up, what soaks up water, and what happens to sugar or sand stirred into water. The child sorts the things by what was found. A child of 4 feels, pours, squeezes and says hard, soft or wet; a child of 7 tests one property on several materials, says beforehand what the child expects and then says what the result was.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording asks for exploring and discovering properties of materials and of substances; it names no property, no material and no substance, so the choice of each is left open. It does not mention measuring, and it does not say whether changes such as melting and freezing are meant. What objects are made of is in another statement, `ojw/nattech/02/fase1`.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up the object and the material, for example saying a spoon sinks because it is a spoon, not because it is metal. Thinking heavy things always sink and light things always float, whatever their shape or material. Using one word such as "hard" for several properties: strong, stiff, heavy and not soft.
