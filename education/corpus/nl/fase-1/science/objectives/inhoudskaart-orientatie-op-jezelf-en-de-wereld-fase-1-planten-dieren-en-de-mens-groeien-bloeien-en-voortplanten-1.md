## Official wording

beseffen dat planten, dieren en de mens in hun leefomgeving water, voedsel en een plek nodig hebben om te leven

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

realising that plants, animals and humans in their living environment need water, food and a place in order to live

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child fills the watering can and waters the plants on the window sill, and sees that the soil of a forgotten plant is dry and that its leaves hang. The child helps to feed the fish or the rabbit and to give it clean water, and says that the child also eats, drinks and has a home. In play the child builds a pen or a nest for a toy animal and puts food and water in it. A child of 4 does the caring together with an adult; a child of 6 can say what would happen to the plant or the animal without water or food.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names three needs: water, food and a place. It puts plants, animals and humans in one sentence, and it asks for a realisation, not for an explanation of how a plant or a body uses water or food. The statement does not mention air, light or warmth, and it does not say what counts as food for a plant.

### Common mistakes

Thinking plants are not alive because they do not move, so that they need nothing. Thinking plants eat soil, or that water is their food. Thinking wild animals are cared for by people in the same way as pets, so that someone gives them food and a place.
