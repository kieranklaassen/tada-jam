## Official wording

vergelijken van onderdelen van de mens met onderdelen van dieren

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

comparing parts of the human being with parts of animals

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a pet, a farm animal or a clear photo and finds the parts that the child also has: eyes, ears, nose, mouth, legs. The child puts a hand next to a dog's paw or next to a picture of a bird's wing and says what is the same and what is different: a hand has fingers, a paw has pads and claws, a bird has wings where the child has arms. The child tells what the animal does with a part and what people do with theirs. A child of 4 points out the same parts on the animal; a child of 7 names differences such as fur, feathers, tail and beak and links some of them to what the animal does.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording asks for comparing parts of humans with parts of animals; it does not say which parts or which animals. It does not say whether parts inside the body are meant, and it does not ask for sorting animals into groups such as mammals or birds.

### Common mistakes

Counting only four-legged animals with fur as animals, so that a bird, a fish or an insect is not compared at all. Calling every part of an animal by the human name, such as hands for a dog's front paws or a nose for a bird's beak, without seeing the difference. Comparing by size only ("the horse's leg is bigger") and leaving out shape and use.
