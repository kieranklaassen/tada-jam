## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking note of some common plants and animals in one's own surroundings with name, some characteristics and the living conditions

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child walks around the school and the neighbourhood and looks at the plants and animals that live there, for example a daisy, a dandelion, a nettle, a chestnut tree, a blackbird, a sparrow, a snail, a worm or a ladybird. The child hears the name, says it, looks at what makes the plant or animal easy to know again, and sees where it lives and what it eats. The child matches a found leaf or a bird that was seen to a picture on a simple search card (*zoekkaart*). A child of 4 knows a few by name, such as daisy, duck and snail; a child of 7 names more, gives one or two features of each and says where it is found, such as under stones or in the pond.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb *kennisnemen van* means taking note of or being told about; it asks less than recognising or naming from memory. The wording is limited to "some" plants and animals that are common in the child's own surroundings, and for these it names three things: the name, some characteristics and the living conditions. It gives no list of species and no number, so which plants and animals are meant depends on where the child lives.

### Common mistakes

Using one name for a whole group, such as "bird" for every bird, "flower" for every plant in bloom, or "bug" for every small animal. Knowing farm and zoo animals from books better than the animals of the child's own street, and naming those instead. Knowing a name without knowing anything about where the plant or animal lives or what it needs.
