discovering the presence of the moon and stars in the night (universe)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at the sky on a dark winter afternoon or evening and points to the moon and to stars. The next morning the child says what was seen and draws it: a round moon, a thin curved moon, many small points of light. The child looks again on other evenings and notices that the moon does not always look the same. A child of 4 points and names the moon; a child of 7 can say that the moon and stars are far away in space and are also there when clouds hide them.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording is about discovering that the moon and stars are there at night; the bracket adds the single word universe (*heelal*). The statement names no phases of the moon, no planets and no constellations, and it does not ask for an explanation. The sun is the subject of another statement, `ojw/rojh/2/02/fase1`.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the moon is there only at night and is gone in the daytime, although it is often visible by day. Thinking the stars go away or are switched off in the morning. Thinking the moon follows the child while the child walks or rides in a car.
