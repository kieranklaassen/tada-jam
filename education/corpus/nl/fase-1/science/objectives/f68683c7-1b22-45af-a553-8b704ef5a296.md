working with a (simple) working drawing or manual

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds from a card or booklet that shows a model step by step: looks at the picture, finds the same pieces, puts them in the same place and compares the build with the picture. The child follows a picture recipe or a folding sheet, one step after the other, and points to the step the child is at. The child checks at the end whether the result matches the last picture. A child of 4 copies a picture of a few blocks that lies in front of the child; a child of 7 follows a booklet of several numbered steps alone and finds the step where something went wrong.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording names two things to work with: a working drawing (*werktekening*, a drawing that shows how to make something) or a manual; the bracketed word limits this to a simple one. It says "working with", so the child uses a drawing or manual that is given; the wording does not ask the child to make one. It does not say whether the manual has pictures or text, and it does not mention scale, measurements or symbols.

### Common mistakes

Skipping a step or doing the steps out of order, and noticing only at the end. Placing a piece mirrored or turned, because the child does not look at the build from the same side as the picture. Miscounting how many pieces long or high a part is in the picture.
