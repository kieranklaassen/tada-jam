## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

being open to the feelings and emotions of others (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child looks at a classmate who is crying, stops playing, and asks "what is wrong?" or fetches the teacher. With a picture book the child points at a character's face and says how that character feels and why. A child of 4 notices crying or laughing and reacts to it, often by fetching an adult; a child of 7 notices quieter signs, such as a child who stands apart, and asks before helping.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The wording is about being open to what others feel, which is an attitude; it does not say naming, explaining or comforting. It names no feelings and no situations.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children of this age most often leave out that another person can feel differently from them in the same situation: a child who likes loud play assumes everyone does. They also overlook feelings that are not shown loudly, and they offer what would comfort themselves in place of asking.
