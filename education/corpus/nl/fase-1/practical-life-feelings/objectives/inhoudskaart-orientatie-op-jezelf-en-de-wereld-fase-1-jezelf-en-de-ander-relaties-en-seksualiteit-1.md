## Official wording

(her)kennen van kenmerken en uitingen van vriendschap

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing or recognising (*(her)kennen*, both verbs written as one) characteristics and expressions of friendship

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says who he or she likes to play with and what they do: build together, laugh, wait at the door, share. With hand puppets the teacher shows two short scenes and the child says which puppet acts as a friend. The child gives a friend a drawing or keeps a seat free for the friend. A child of 4 calls the child he or she plays with now a friend; a child of 6 names two or three things friends do for each other and sees them in a story.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The Dutch *(her)kennen* joins knowing (*kennen*) and recognising (*herkennen*); the statement does not choose. It names characteristics and expressions of friendship without listing any. It does not mention making friends or quarrels.

### Common mistakes

Children end a friendship in words after each quarrel ("you can't come to my party") and are friends again an hour later. They think a friend must play only with them. They call anyone who gives them something a friend.
