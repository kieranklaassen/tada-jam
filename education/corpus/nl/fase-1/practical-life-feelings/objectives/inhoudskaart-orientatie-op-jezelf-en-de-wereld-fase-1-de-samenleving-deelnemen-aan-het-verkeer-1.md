## Official wording

veilig gedragen op straat en als (lopende en fietsende)deelnemer aan het verkeer

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

behaving safely in the street and as a (walking and cycling) participant in traffic

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On a class walk a child holds a partner's hand, stays on the pavement behind the teacher, and stops when the teacher stops. At a crossing the child stands still at the kerb, looks both ways with the group, and walks across without running. On the playground the child rides a tricycle or bicycle along painted lanes and stops for a child on foot. A child of 4 stops and waits when an adult says so; a child of 6 stops at the kerb without being told and says what he or she looks and listens for.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card prints no space after the closing bracket ("fietsende)deelnemer"); the gloss gives what is plainly meant. The statement names the street and taking part in traffic, and the brackets name walking and cycling. It names no rules, signs or kinds of road and states no safety condition such as adult supervision.

### Common mistakes

Children run into the road after a ball or towards a parent on the other side without stopping. They think a driver sees them whenever they see the car. They think a zebra crossing or a green light makes crossing safe without looking.
