## Official wording

(her)kennen van kenmerken en uitingen van vriendschap

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing or recognising (*(her)kennen*, both verbs written as one) characteristics and expressions of friendship

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child tells who his or her friend is and what they do together: playing, sharing a snack, waiting for each other, comforting. With a picture book or with dolls the child points out what a friend does and what a friend does not do. The child makes a drawing for a friend or asks "do you want to play?". A child of 4 names as a friend the child he or she plays with today; a child of 7 names what makes someone a friend, such as helping and keeping a promise, and recognises it in a story.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The Dutch *(her)kennen* joins knowing (*kennen*) and recognising (*herkennen*); the statement does not choose. It names characteristics and expressions of friendship without listing any. It does not mention making friends, quarrels or the end of a friendship. The content-line document prints a footnote on this cluster's heading: "Scholen zijn verplicht aandacht te besteden aan het thema relaties en seksualiteit, dat valt onder kerndoel 38, maar mogen zelf kiezen welke leerlijnen en tussendoelen per bouw zij formuleren." (in English: schools are obliged to give attention to the theme of relationships and sexuality, which falls under core goal 38, but may choose for themselves which learning lines and intermediate goals they formulate for each *bouw*, a stage of several school years).

### Common mistakes

Children take "friend" to mean whoever they are playing with at the moment, and say "you are not my friend any more" after one disagreement. They think a friend must always do what they want. They think a friend can have only one friend, and take a friend's play with another child as the loss of the friendship.
