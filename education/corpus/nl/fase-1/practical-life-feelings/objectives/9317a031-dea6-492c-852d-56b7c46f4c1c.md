## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child greets the teacher in the morning, says "thank you" and "sorry", waits for a turn, tells the truth about who knocked over the tower, and leaves another child's work alone. The child helps to make the class rules and points at the picture of a rule when it is needed. A child of 4 follows these rules with an adult near and with reminders; a child of 7 follows them more often when no adult is watching and can say whom a rule is for.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The statement is about behaving, and about where the behaviour comes from: respect for values and norms. It names no values or norms and does not say what "generally accepted" (*algemeen aanvaarde*) covers or who decides that.

### Common mistakes

Children think a rule holds only while the adult who made it is present. They apply a rule strictly to others and report them to the teacher, and do not apply it to themselves. They think a rule from home or school holds everywhere in the same way, and are confused when another place has another rule.
