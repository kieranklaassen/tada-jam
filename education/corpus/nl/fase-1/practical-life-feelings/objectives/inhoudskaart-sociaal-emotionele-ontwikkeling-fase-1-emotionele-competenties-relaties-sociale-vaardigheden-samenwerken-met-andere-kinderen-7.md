## Official wording

houden aan afspraken en regels, bijv. op je beurt wachten

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - fase 1 (kleuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

keeping to agreements and rules, e.g. waiting for your turn

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child hangs its name card at the sand table and, when four cards already hang there, picks another activity. At a board game it waits until the die is passed to it. A child of 4 keeps to a rule when an adult or a picture reminds it; a child of 6 keeps to known rules without a reminder and reminds others of them.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. Waiting for your turn is given as an example. The statement does not say which agreements or rules. It sits under working together with other children. Exploring agreements and rules is `Zichzelf staande houden of aanpassen in een groep / 1`, and waiting for a turn is also `Beheersen van eigen gevoelens / 9`.

### Common mistakes

A child knows the rule and breaks it when its wish is strong. A child keeps to a rule only while the teacher watches. A child applies the rule to others and not to itself.
