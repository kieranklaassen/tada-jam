## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the circle children tell with whom they live: with one parent, with two, in two homes, with grandparents, with or without brothers and sisters. A child tells about a change, such as a new baby, a move, or a parent who now lives elsewhere, and says how that was. The child draws his or her home and the people in it. A child of 4 names who lives at home now; a child of 7 can tell what was different before and how people felt about the change.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is discussing, and three things are discussed: different forms of living together, that this can change over time, and what this means for people. The Dutch *samenleven* can mean living together in a household and living together in a society; the statement does not choose and names no forms. It sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

Children assume that everyone lives as they do and that a household never changes. They think a change at home, such as a parent moving out, happened because of something they did. They think a person who no longer lives in the house is no longer family.
