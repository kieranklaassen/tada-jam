## Official wording

benoemen welke eigenschappen van zichzelf kunnen helpen bij het oplossen van moeilijke situaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - fase 1 (kleuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming which of one's own characteristics can help in solving difficult situations

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says "I am good at waiting, so I can let her go first" or "I dare to ask the teacher". The group makes a card for each child with something the child is good at when there is a problem. A child of 4 names what it can do ("I can share"); a child of 6 names a trait, such as patient, brave or funny, and a situation where it helped.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement asks for naming and is about the child's own characteristics. It names none. It sits under thinking about the consequences of one's own actions.

### Common mistakes

A child names a skill that has nothing to do with the situation ("I can run fast"). A child cannot name anything about itself and repeats what the teacher said. A child knows the word for a trait and cannot tell when it used it.
