discussing (one's own) behaviour in traffic

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a walk to the gym or the library a child tells what the group did at the crossing and what he or she did. The child looks at a photo or a table-top street with toy cars and figures, says what a figure does well or wrongly, and moves it to show a better way. A child of 4 tells one thing he or she did on the way to school, such as holding a hand; a child of 7 can say what he or she would do differently next time and why.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing. The brackets around "one's own" (*eigen*) leave open whether the talk is about traffic behaviour in general, the child's own, or both. The statement names no situations and no rules.

### Common mistakes

Children describe the rule and not what they did ("you have to look") when asked about their own behaviour. They blame the other party in every example and do not consider what the walker or cyclist could have done. They judge behaviour by the outcome: nothing happened, so it was safe.
