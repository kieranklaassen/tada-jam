## Official wording

plezier hebben in het uitvoeren van een (nieuwe) taak

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - fase 1 (kleuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking pleasure in carrying out a (new) task

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child gets a new task, such as weaving a paper mat, starts at once, hums while it works and shows the result. It asks whether it may do the task again tomorrow. A child of 4 enjoys the doing and stops when the pleasure stops; a child of 6 also enjoys getting better at it and finishing it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The brackets add "new": any task is covered and a new one is named in particular. The statement is about pleasure and names no kind of task.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the start: a child holds back from a new task because it fears it cannot do it, and enjoys it once it has begun. Pleasure drops when the task is too easy or too hard.
