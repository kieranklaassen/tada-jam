## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children want the same tricycle; each says what he or she wants, and with the teacher they agree on turns with a sand timer. A child who wants to build a tower and a child who wants a road in the same space divide the blocks or join the two plans. A child of 4 needs an adult to put both wishes into words and to offer two solutions to choose from; a child of 7 can say what the other child wanted and proposes a solution before an adult helps.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is experiencing, and two things are experienced: that conflicts can arise from conflicting interests, and that you can resolve them together. The statement names no kinds of conflict and no method for resolving one, and does not say whether an adult helps.

### Common mistakes

Children think a conflict means the other child is mean, and do not see that both wanted something reasonable. They think a conflict ends only when one child wins or an adult decides. They agree to a solution to end the quarrel and do not keep to it.
