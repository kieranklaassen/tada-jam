## Official wording

rekening houden met de ander

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

taking the other person into account

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child steps aside for a classmate who carries a tray, waits until the tap is free, and passes the fruit bowl on before taking a second piece. In the building corner the child asks before using blocks from another child's building. A child of 4 does this when an adult points at the other child; a child of 6 looks around before starting and sometimes offers a turn without being asked.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement is five words long in Dutch and names no situations, no behaviour and no persons. "The other" (*de ander*) is singular and general; the text does not say whether a classmate, an adult or anyone is meant.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often do not notice the other child at all while they carry out their own plan. They take account of a friend and not of a child they do not know well. They also think it means the other always goes first.
