realising that the living environment is influenced by technology (*techniek*) (science and technology)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child walks through the school and points at things that people made to solve a problem: a tap, a light switch, a zip, a bicycle bell, a traffic light. The child tries what a room is like with the light off or how to carry water without a tap, and says what the thing does for people. A child of 4 names a made thing and what it is for; a child of 7 can say what would be different at home or in the street without it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is realising, not explaining how a thing works. The Dutch *techniek* covers made things and the skill of making and using them; the source's own brackets name science and technology. The statement names no devices and does not say whether the influence is good or bad. The influence of digitalisation is the subject of `ojw/ds/1/04/fase1`.

### Common mistakes

Children take "technology" to mean only screens and things with a plug, and do not count a zip, a wheel or a pair of scissors. They think made things have always been there and do not think that someone designed them. They do not link a thing to the change it brings, for example a street lamp and being able to walk outside after dark.
