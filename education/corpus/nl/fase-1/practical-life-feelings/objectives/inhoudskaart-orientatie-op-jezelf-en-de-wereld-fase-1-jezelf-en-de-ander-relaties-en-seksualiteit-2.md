## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child shows a family photo or drawing and names the people on it. In the circle the child hears that classmates have one parent, two mothers, two fathers, a father and a mother, two homes, or grandparents who care for them. In the home corner the child plays a family with roles the players choose. A child of 4 names his or her own people; a child of 6 can say that families and couples differ and names two forms he or she knows.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing. The statement says "different forms of relationship" and names none; it sets no level of detail. It stands under the sub-heading relationships and sexuality (*Relaties en seksualiteit*).

### Common mistakes

Children think a family has to look like theirs. They think two people who live together must be married or must be a father and a mother. They use "brother", "aunt" or "friend" loosely and are confused about who is related to whom.
