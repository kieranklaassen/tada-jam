## Official wording

vertellen wat de oorzaken en gevolgen (kunnen) zijn van conflictsituaties

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - fase 1 (kleuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

telling what the causes and consequences of conflict situations (can) be

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a quarrel over the bicycle a child tells the teacher "I wanted it and he had it, so I pushed, and then he cried". Looking at a picture of two children who pull at one toy, it says what happened before and what may happen next. A child of 4 tells one cause, mostly what the other child did; a child of 6 tells a cause and a consequence and includes what it did itself.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The word in brackets widens the statement from what the causes and consequences are to what they can be. Telling is asked, so the child puts it into words. The statement does not say whether the conflict is the child's own, and it names no number of causes or consequences.

### Common mistakes

A child names what the other child did as the only cause. A child tells the last thing that happened, such as the hitting, as the cause and leaves out what came before it. A child mixes up cause and consequence when it tells them in order.
