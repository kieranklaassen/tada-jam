demonstrating good manners

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says "good morning" at the door, "thank you" when it gets its fruit, and "may I pass?" at a crowded coat rack. It holds a hand in front of its mouth when it coughs and waits until someone has finished speaking. A child of 4 does this when an adult prompts or models it; a child of 6 does it unprompted in familiar situations.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement does not say which manners are good manners. It asks for showing them, not for explaining them. It names no situation.

### Common mistakes

A child says "sorry" or "thank you" as a formula without looking at the other. A child uses manners with adults and not with children. A child treats the manners of its own home as the only right ones.
