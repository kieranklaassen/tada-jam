discussing what being in love means and in which ways people express it

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the circle or after a story a child says what he or she thinks being in love is and how you can tell that someone is: wanting to be near someone, making a drawing, holding hands, giving a kiss. The child hears that classmates name other signs and that some children have felt it and others have not. A child of 4 mostly repeats what he or she has seen adults or story characters do; a child of 7 can tell being in love apart from being friends and names more than one way people show it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing, and two things are discussed: what being in love means, and the ways in which people express it. The statement names no ways and sets no level of detail. It stands under the heading relationships and sexuality (*Relaties en seksualiteit*). The content-line document prints a footnote on this cluster's heading: "Scholen zijn verplicht aandacht te besteden aan het thema relaties en seksualiteit, dat valt onder kerndoel 38, maar mogen zelf kiezen welke leerlijnen en tussendoelen per bouw zij formuleren." (in English: schools are obliged to give attention to the theme of relationships and sexuality, which falls under core goal 38, but may choose for themselves which learning lines and intermediate goals they formulate for each *bouw*, a stage of several school years).

### Common mistakes

Children mix up being in love and being friends, and say they will marry a parent, a sibling or a best friend. They think being in love is for adults only, or something to laugh at, and tease a child who mentions it. They think that if one person is in love, the other must be too.
