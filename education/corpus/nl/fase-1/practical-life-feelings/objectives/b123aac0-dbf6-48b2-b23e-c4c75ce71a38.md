## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child points to a face picture that fits how he or she feels at the start of the day and says "I am angry" or "I am sad" in place of hitting or hiding. The child goes to a quiet corner, squeezes a cushion, takes slow breaths or asks an adult for a hug, and comes back to play. A child of 4 names happy, angry, sad and scared with help and needs an adult to calm down; a child of 7 can say what caused the feeling and more often picks a way to calm down without help.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts: recognising one's own feelings and dealing with them. It names no feelings, no number of feelings and no ways of dealing with them. It is about the child's own feelings; the feelings of others are the subject of `ojw/ja/1/02/fase1`.

### Common mistakes

Children name every unpleasant feeling "angry" or "sad" and have no word for disappointed, jealous or nervous. They take a feeling in the body, such as a stomach ache, for illness and not for worry. They think a strong feeling will not end, or that having the feeling is itself naughty.
