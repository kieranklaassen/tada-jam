## Official wording

openstaan voor gevoelens en emoties van anderen (b)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

being open to the feelings and emotions of others (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child sees a classmate fall, goes over, looks at the face and fetches a tissue or the teacher. While a story is read the child looks at the pictures and says "she is scared" or "he is happy now". In doll play the child comforts a doll that is said to cry. A child of 4 reacts to clear signs such as tears and laughter; a child of 6 sees a frown or a turned-away body and asks "are you sad?".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card marks this as an aspect of citizenship (b). The wording is about being open to what others feel, which is an attitude; it does not say naming, explaining or comforting. It names no feelings and no situations.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out that the other child feels something different from what they feel: a child who enjoys chasing does not see that the chased child is frightened. They also laugh at crying because it makes them uneasy, and they hand over their own favourite cuddly toy as comfort without asking.
