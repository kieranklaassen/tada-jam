## Official wording

weten welke gevolgen je gedrag heeft

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - fase 1 (kleuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

knowing what consequences your behaviour has

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child tells "if I throw sand, it gets in her eyes and she cries" and "if I help tidy, we can go outside sooner". After an incident it looks at the other child's face and says what its action did. A child of 4 sees the direct, visible consequence when an adult points to it; a child of 6 predicts a consequence before it acts and includes how the other will feel.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement asks for knowing. It does not say whether consequences for the child itself or for others are meant, and it names no behaviour. Explaining the consequence of a particular choice is `Nadenken over keuzes/keuzemogelijkheden / 3`.

### Common mistakes

A child sees the consequence for itself, such as a punishment, and not the consequence for the other. A child says "I did not mean to" and thinks that this removes the consequence. A child knows the consequence when asked and does not think of it in the moment.
