## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discussing different forms of living together, that this can change over time and what this means for people (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child tells who lives in his or her house and draws the house with those people at the windows. In the circle children tell about a change at home: a baby brother, a move, a grandparent who came to live with them, parents who live apart. The child hears how a classmate felt about such a change. A child of 4 tells who is at home now; a child of 6 tells what was different before and says something about how it was for him or her.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card marks this as an aspect of citizenship (b). The verb is discussing, and three things are discussed: different forms of living together, that this can change over time, and what this means for people. The Dutch *samenleven* can mean living together in a household and living together in a society; the statement does not choose and names no forms. It sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

Children think every house holds the same kind of family as theirs. They think that what is now will stay, and are upset by a change. They think they caused a change at home by being naughty.
