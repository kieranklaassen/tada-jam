## Official wording

zichzelf handhaven binnen de eigen groep en opkomen voor jezelf

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

holding one's own within one's own group and standing up for oneself

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child whose blocks are taken says "those are mine, give them back" and holds out a hand. The child walks up to two classmates in the sandpit and asks "can I join in?", and says in the circle what he or she wants to choose. A child of 4 says "no" or "stop" and goes to the teacher if it goes on; a child of 6 uses a sentence that says what he or she wants, repeats it once, and then asks for help.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts: holding one's own within one's own group, and standing up for oneself. It names no situations and no words to use. "One's own group" (*de eigen groep*) is not filled in: it can be the class or a smaller group of children. The statement does not mention standing up for someone else.

### Common mistakes

Children hit, bite or grab to get a thing back. They give up the toy and say nothing, and stay away from the group afterwards. They go to the teacher at once without having said anything to the other child.
