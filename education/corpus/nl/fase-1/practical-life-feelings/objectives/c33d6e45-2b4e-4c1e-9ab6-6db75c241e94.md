## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In the circle a child says what he or she thinks about a class matter, such as which book to read or a rule for the building corner, and hears that another child thinks differently. The child tells why, lets the other finish, and hears from the teacher that everyone may say what they think. A child of 4 says "I think ..." and "I don't want that" and listens to one other child; a child of 7 can give a reason for an opinion and can say that another child may hold a different one.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is discussing. The statement puts the term in quotation marks and names the right only; it does not mention the limits of that right, the law or other rights. It sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

Children take "I may say what I think" to mean "I get what I want", and are upset when the group decides otherwise. They take disagreement as unkindness or as the end of a friendship. They repeat the opinion of a friend or of the first speaker and present it as their own.
