## Official wording

wachten op zijn/haar beurt

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - fase 1 (kleuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

waiting for his/her turn

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child stands in line at the tap and steps forward when the child before it is done. At a board game with a die it watches the others' moves and takes the die when it is passed. A child of 4 waits through another child's turn with a reminder; a child of 6 waits through the round and knows whose turn it is.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names no length of waiting and no number of children. Waiting for your turn is also the example in `Samenwerken met andere kinderen / 7`.

### Common mistakes

A child grabs the object when its wish is strong, although it knows whose turn it is. A child stops paying attention while waiting and misses its turn. A child takes a turn and does not pass the object on.
