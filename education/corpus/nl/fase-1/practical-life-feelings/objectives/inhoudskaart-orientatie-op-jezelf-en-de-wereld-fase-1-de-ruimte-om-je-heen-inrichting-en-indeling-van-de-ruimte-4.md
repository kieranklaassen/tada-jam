## Official wording

onderzoeken en bespreken van elementen die positief bijdragen aan de zorg voor het milieu

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

investigating and discussing elements that contribute positively to care for the environment (*milieu*)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child lifts the lid of the rain barrel in the school garden, sees the rainwater that ran in from the roof and lets some run from its tap into a watering can for the plants. The child looks into the box where used paper is collected to be made into new paper, and into the compost bin, where fruit peel turns into dark, crumbly earth for the garden, and the children tell each other what they found and what each thing is for. A child of 4 stirs the compost with a stick, finds a worm and talks about it with the others; a child of 6 opens the tap of the rain barrel, watches the water run and talks with the others about where it came from.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording joins two verbs, investigating and discussing, and names no act of care that the child carries out. Their object is elements that contribute positively to care for the environment: the wording names no element, does not say what kind of thing an element is, and names no measure. The Dutch word is *milieu*, the environment as something that is cared for, and the wording does not say what that care consists of.

### Common mistakes

Children think that a thing put in a bin is gone, and do not ask what happens to it afterwards. Many think that water simply comes out of the tap, and do not link it with rain. When they talk about what they looked at, they tell what they did, such as opening the lid, and leave out what the thing is for.
