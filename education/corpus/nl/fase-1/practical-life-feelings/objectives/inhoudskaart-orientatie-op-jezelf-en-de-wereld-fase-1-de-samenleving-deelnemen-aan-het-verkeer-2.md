## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discussing (one's own) behaviour in traffic

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Back in class after a walk a child tells what he or she did at the kerb and what the group did. On a floor mat with roads the child moves a toy figure and a toy car, shows how the figure crosses, and says what goes wrong when it runs. A child of 4 tells one thing, such as "I held hands"; a child of 6 tells the steps in order and says what he or she will do next time.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing. The brackets around "one's own" (*eigen*) leave open whether traffic behaviour in general, the child's own, or both are discussed. The statement names no situations and no rules.

### Common mistakes

Children say the rule they have heard instead of what they did. They say a way of crossing was safe because nothing happened. They talk about what cars and other people did wrong and leave out their own part.
