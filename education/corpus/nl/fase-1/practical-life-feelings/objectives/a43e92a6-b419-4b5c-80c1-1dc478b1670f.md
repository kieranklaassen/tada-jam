experiencing similarities and differences in people's behaviour

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child watches how classmates come in each morning: one runs in, one stays close to a parent, one goes straight to the puzzles. In the circle children show how they greet someone or what they do when they are happy, and a child sees which ways are like his or her own and which are not. A child of 4 says "she does it like me" or "he does it differently"; a child of 7 can describe how two people act differently in the same situation and offers a reason.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The source prints *verschillende* ("different") where *verschillen* ("differences") is plainly meant, and the gloss gives what is meant. The verb is experiencing, not judging or explaining. The statement names no kinds of behaviour and no groups of people. Views and habits are the subject of `ojw/ja/1/03/fase1`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out similarities and report only what is different. They call behaviour that differs from their own "naughty" or "silly". They take one act as the whole person ("he always pushes").
