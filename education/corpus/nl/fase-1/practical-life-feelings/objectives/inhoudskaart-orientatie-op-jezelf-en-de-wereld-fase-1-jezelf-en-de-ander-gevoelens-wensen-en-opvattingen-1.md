## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

recognising and dealing with one's own feelings

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child hangs his or her name card under the happy, angry, sad or scared face on the feelings board and says a word about it. When angry the child stamps on the spot agreed for that, squeezes a soft ball or sits with a cuddly toy in the quiet corner until calm. A child of 4 shows the feeling with the whole body and needs an adult to name it; a child of 6 names the feeling and often what caused it, and goes to the quiet corner without being sent.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts: recognising one's own feelings and dealing with them. It names no feelings, no number of feelings and no ways of dealing with them. It is about the child's own feelings, not those of others.

### Common mistakes

Children say "angry" for tired, hungry, jealous or disappointed. They treat a feeling and an act as one thing: angry means hitting. They say "nothing" or "fine" when asked, because finding the word while upset is hard.
