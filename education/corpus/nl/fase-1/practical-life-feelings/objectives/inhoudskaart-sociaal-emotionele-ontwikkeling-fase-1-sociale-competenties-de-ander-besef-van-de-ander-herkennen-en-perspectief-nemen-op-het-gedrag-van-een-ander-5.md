## Official wording

benoemen wat de bedoeling van een ander kan zijn

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - fase 1 (kleuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

naming what another person's intention can be

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child is bumped in the corridor and says "maybe he did not see me", where earlier it would have said "he hit me". Looking at a picture of a child who takes a block from a tower, it says "maybe she wants to help build" or "maybe she wants to break it". A child of 4 judges by what happened to it; a child of 6 gives a possible intention and can think of a second one.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says "can be": a possible intention is named, not a certain one. The statement asks for naming. It does not say who the other is.

### Common mistakes

A child takes every bump or loss for done on purpose. A child judges by the result (the tower fell) and not by the intention. A child takes its first guess about the intention for a fact.
