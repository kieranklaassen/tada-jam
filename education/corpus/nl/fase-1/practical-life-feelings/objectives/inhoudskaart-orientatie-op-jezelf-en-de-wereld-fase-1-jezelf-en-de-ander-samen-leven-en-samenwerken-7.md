## Official wording

gedragen vanuit respect voor algemeen aanvaarde waarden en normen (b)

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Oriëntatie op jezelf en de wereld - kleuters (fase 1), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

behaving from respect for generally accepted values and norms (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says good morning at the door, waits until a classmate has finished speaking, says sorry after bumping into someone and helps to pick up what fell. The child points at the rule picture on the wall ("we walk inside") when someone runs. A child of 4 keeps to a rule when an adult reminds him or her at that moment; a child of 6 keeps to known rules much of the day and says why a rule is there.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The card marks this as an aspect of citizenship (b). The statement is about behaving, and about where the behaviour comes from: respect for values and norms. It names no values or norms and does not say what "generally accepted" (*algemeen aanvaarde*) covers or who decides that.

### Common mistakes

Children keep to a rule only when the teacher is looking. They report every classmate who breaks a rule and break it themselves. They say sorry as a word that ends the matter, and repeat the act.
