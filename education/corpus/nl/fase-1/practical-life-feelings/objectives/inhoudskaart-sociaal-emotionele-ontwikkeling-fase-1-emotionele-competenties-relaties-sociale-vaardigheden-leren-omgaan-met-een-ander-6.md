## Official wording

vertrouwen hebben in een ander

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - fase 1 (kleuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

having trust in another

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child lets another child hold the other end of the bench they carry, or gives its hand to a partner when the group walks in a line. It tells the teacher something that worries it, and leaves its building with a friend while it goes to the toilet. A child of 4 trusts the familiar adult; a child of 6 also relies on classmates in a shared task.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement does not say who the other is, a child or an adult, and names no situation. Trust in one's own ability is a separate statement, `Realiseren van gestelde (eigen) doelen / 7`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out the difference between people: some trust nobody outside the family, others trust anyone. After one broken promise a child may refuse to rely on that child again.
