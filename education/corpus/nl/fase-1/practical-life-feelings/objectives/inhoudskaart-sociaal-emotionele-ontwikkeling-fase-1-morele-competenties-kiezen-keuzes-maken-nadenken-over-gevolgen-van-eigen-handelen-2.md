## Official wording

bedenken welke oplossingen er zijn als zich een moeilijke situatie of probleem voordoet

Source: SLO (Stichting Leerplanontwikkeling), Inhoudskaart Sociaal-emotionele ontwikkeling - Jonge kind - fase 1 (kleuters), page 1.

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

thinking of which solutions there are when a difficult situation or problem arises

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children want the same swing, and a child says "we can take turns, or count to twenty, or I go on the slide first". With the teacher the children list what you can do when someone will not let you join. A child of 4 thinks of one solution, often fetching the teacher; a child of 6 thinks of two or three and says which it would try.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording says "solutions" in the plural. It does not say whether the child also carries one out, and it does not say whose problem it is.

### Common mistakes

A child knows one solution and repeats it when it fails. A child offers a solution that is good only for itself ("I get it"). A child thinks of solutions when calm and of none during the problem.
