investigating a positive contribution to care for the environment

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child sorts the class's waste into paper, fruit peel and the rest and looks at how full each bin gets in a week. The child turns off the tap and the light, uses both sides of a sheet or helps to water the school garden, and tells what changed. A child of 4 does one such task with the teacher and says what it was for; a child of 7 can compare two ways of doing something and say which one leaves less waste.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is investigating, and its object is "a positive contribution" in the singular, not a list of duties. The statement does not say whose contribution is meant (the child's, the class's or other people's) and names no example. It names no topic such as waste, water or energy.

### Common mistakes

Children think that something put in a bin is gone, and do not ask where it goes next. They think a contribution only counts when it is large, so they overlook small repeated acts. They mix up tidy and good for the environment, for example by throwing away paper that could be used again.
