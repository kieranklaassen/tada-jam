## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

discussing what being in love means and ways in which people express it

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story in which two characters are in love, a child says how you can tell: they look at each other, hold hands, give something, want to sit together. The child says whether he or she has ever been in love, or has seen it in a big sister, a parent or a film. A child of 4 repeats what he or she saw and may say "I am in love with mummy"; a child of 6 says that being in love feels different from liking a friend and names a few ways of showing it.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is discussing, and two things are discussed: what being in love means, and ways in which people express it. The statement names no ways and sets no level of detail. It stands under the sub-heading relationships and sexuality (*Relaties en seksualiteit*).

### Common mistakes

Children use "in love" for anyone they like, including parents and pets. They giggle or tease when the word comes up, and a child who said something then says no more. They think being in love means you must marry.
