## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child plays shop: chooses from the shelf, asks what something costs, hands over coins and waits for the item and the change. With an adult the child takes one item from the shelf of a real shop, puts it on the counter and pays. The child chooses between two things when there is money for one and says why. A child of 4 hands over money and takes the item in play; a child of 7 can compare two prices on labels and say that the money is gone once it is spent.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The verb is developing. The statement names no shop, product, amount of money or way of paying, and does not mention advertising. This goal states no number range.

### Common mistakes

Children think more coins always means more money, whatever the coins are. They think paying is a ritual and that the money comes back afterwards, because they see change returned. They want the first thing they see and do not compare.
