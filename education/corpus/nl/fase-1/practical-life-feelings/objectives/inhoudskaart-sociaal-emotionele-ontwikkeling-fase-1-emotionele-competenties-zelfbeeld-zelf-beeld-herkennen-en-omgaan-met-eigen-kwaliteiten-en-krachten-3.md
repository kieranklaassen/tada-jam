developing self-confidence

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child climbs one rung higher than last week and calls "look!". It tells something in the circle, starts a new task without first asking whether it is right, and shows its work. A child of 4 gains confidence from doing again what worked before and from an adult who watches; a child of 6 starts something new and says "I think I can do this".

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1, for the youngest children (*groep* 1 and 2, about ages 4 to 6), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement names no situation and no sign by which self-confidence shows. Trust in one's own ability is also `Realiseren van gestelde (eigen) doelen / 7`.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. Children most often leave out trying: a child asks for help before it has started, or waits until others have gone first. A loud child is not always a confident one, and a quiet child is not always unsure.
