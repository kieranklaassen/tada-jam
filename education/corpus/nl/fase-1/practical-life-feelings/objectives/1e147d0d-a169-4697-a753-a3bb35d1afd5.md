zichzelf handhaven binnen de eigen groep en opkomen voor jezelf

## English gloss

_The pack's gloss, not an official translation._

holding one's own within one's own group and standing up for oneself

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child says "I was playing with that" when a toy is taken, says "stop, I don't want that", and asks to join in with a group that is already playing. The child says in the circle what he or she wants to do and keeps to a choice when another child pushes for something else. A child of 4 says "no" or "stop" and fetches an adult when that does not work; a child of 7 says what he or she wants and why, without hitting or shouting, and tries a second time before fetching an adult.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The statement has two parts: holding one's own within one's own group, and standing up for oneself. It names no situations and no words to use. "One's own group" (*de eigen groep*) is not filled in: it can be the class or a smaller group of children. The statement does not mention standing up for someone else.

### Common mistakes

Children stand up for themselves by hitting, grabbing or shouting, or they do nothing and cry. They fetch an adult before saying anything to the other child. They take standing up for themselves to mean always getting their way.
