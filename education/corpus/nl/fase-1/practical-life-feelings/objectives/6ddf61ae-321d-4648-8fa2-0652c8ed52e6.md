discovering that everyone is part of different groups in which different roles are filled (b)

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child lists the groups he or she belongs to: the family, the class, the swimming lesson, the children in the street. The child says what he or she is in each: the youngest at home, the helper of the day in class, the goalkeeper on the field. With photos or drawings the child sorts people into groups and finds that one person fits in several. A child of 4 names the family and the class and one task in each; a child of 7 can say that the same person, such as the teacher, is also a parent or a neighbour somewhere else.

### Limits

This statement describes what a school offers in fase 1 (*groep* 1 to 3, about ages 4 to 7), not what a child must be able to do, and nothing says in which year. The wording ends with the printed marker (b), the curriculum institute's sign for an aspect of citizenship (*burgerschap*). The verb is discovering. The statement says "everyone", so it covers the child and other people. It names no groups and no roles.

### Common mistakes

Children think a person is one thing only: a teacher lives at school and cannot be someone's mother. They are surprised or upset to meet a person outside the usual place, such as the teacher in the supermarket. They take a role as fixed, for example that the oldest child always decides, and do not see that roles change between groups.
