## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child's work here is with decimals down to the thousandths place: reading them, writing them and comparing them. In this part the child reads and writes such decimals in three forms: in digits, as number names, and in expanded form, where each digit is multiplied by the value of its place (for example, 347.392 as a sum that runs from 3 × 100 down to 2 × (1/1000)).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads 0.305 aloud as 'three hundred five thousandths', writes the numeral on hearing the name, and lays out place-value disks for each digit. The child writes a decimal as a sum, one term per digit, and puts a scattered sum back together into one number. The child says what the zero in 0.305 is doing.

### Limits

Decimals go to the thousandths place and no further. Three forms are named: expanded form, number names and base-ten numerals. In the statement's example the expanded form writes the decimal places with unit fractions such as 1/10. The statement sets no upper limit on the whole-number part.

### Common mistakes

Reading the digits after the point as a whole number with no place name ('point three hundred five') and then treating 0.305 as larger than 0.35. Dropping a zero that holds a place, writing three hundred five thousandths as 0.35. Naming the places with whole-number names, 'thousands' for thousandths.
