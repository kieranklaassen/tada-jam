## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Using the properties of flat shapes, the child sorts them into an ordered scheme of groups and sub-groups, where each sub-group sits inside a wider group.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out cards for quadrilateral, trapezoid, parallelogram, rectangle, rhombus and square, and arranges them as a tree or as nested rings, with the widest group at the top or on the outside. For each link the child says which property is added: 'a rectangle is a parallelogram with right angles'. Given a new cut-out shape, the child measures its sides and angles and places it at the most specific spot that fits. The child does the same with triangles, sorting by sides and by angles.

### Limits

Only two-dimensional figures are covered. The statement does not list which figures or which properties to use. It does not settle definitions that textbooks give differently, such as whether a trapezoid has exactly one pair of parallel sides or at least one.

### Common mistakes

Sorting into separate boxes with no overlap, so a shape can be only one thing. Placing a shape by its appearance instead of by measuring sides and angles. Building the scheme upside down, with the most special shape (the square) as the widest group.
