## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child connects volume with multiplying and adding and solves volume problems from everyday life and from mathematics. In this part the child sees that volumes add: for a solid made of two right rectangular prisms that do not overlap, the child finds the volume of each part and adds the two, and uses this way of working on problems from real situations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds an L-shaped block from two box-shaped stacks of cubes, works out each stack with a multiplication, and adds the two results. On a drawing of an L-shaped swimming pool or a two-step podium, the child draws a line to split it into two boxes, labels the edges of each, and calculates. The child splits the same solid a second way and checks that the total is the same.

### Limits

The solid is made of two right rectangular prisms that do not overlap. The statement does not mention solids of three or more parts, or finding a volume by taking away a missing piece. The neighbouring parts of this standard use whole-number edge lengths; this part states no number range of its own.

### Common mistakes

Using a measurement of the whole figure for a part, for example the full length for both boxes, so the shared region is counted twice. Not working out an unlabelled edge of one part by subtracting. Multiplying the two part volumes instead of adding them.
