## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child finds how much space a solid takes up by counting the unit cubes that fill it. The cubes may be cubic feet, cubic inches or cubic centimetres, or a unit the child makes up for the purpose.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child fills a tissue box with centimetre cubes layer by layer and counts them, often counting one layer and then the number of layers. The same box is measured again with larger wooden blocks or sugar cubes as a home-made unit, and the child notes that the number changes with the unit. For a cubic foot, the child tapes together a frame from rulers and estimates how many would fit in a cupboard.

### Limits

Volume is found by counting cubes, not yet by formula; formulas belong to a neighbouring statement at this grade. The named units are cubic centimetres, cubic inches, cubic feet and units the child improvises. The statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

Counting the squares on the outer faces instead of the cubes, which counts edge cubes twice and misses those inside. Writing the answer with a length or area unit ('24 cm' or '24 square cm'). Mixing two sizes of cube in one count.
