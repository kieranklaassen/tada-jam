## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child divides whole numbers and finds whole-number quotients, with a dividend of at most four digits and a divisor of two digits. The strategies draw on how multiplication and division are related, on properties of operations, on place value, or on a mix of these. The child shows and explains the calculation with area models, rectangular arrays or equations, alone or together.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

For 1,656 ÷ 24 the child asks 'how many 24s make 1,656?', takes away easy multiples such as 50 × 24 and 10 × 24, and adds up the partial quotients. The child draws a rectangle with one side 24 and area 1,656 and fills in the other side piece by piece. The child checks by multiplying the quotient by 24.

### Limits

The dividend has at most four digits and the divisor has two. The quotient is a whole number. Strategies and models are named; the statement does not name the standard long-division algorithm. It leaves open whether one or several of the named strategies and models are used.

### Common mistakes

Dropping a zero in the quotient, for example writing 38 instead of 308 when a place has nothing to divide. Estimating a quotient digit too high or too low with a two-digit divisor and not correcting it. Treating the digits of the divisor separately, dividing by 2 and then by 4 instead of by 24.
