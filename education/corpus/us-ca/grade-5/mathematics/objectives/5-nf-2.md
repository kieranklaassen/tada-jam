## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child solves word problems in which fractions of one and the same whole are added or subtracted, including fractions whose denominators differ, for example with an equation or a visual fraction model that stands for the problem. The child estimates in the head, using benchmark fractions and a sense of how large fractions are, and judges whether an answer is reasonable. As an example, the child spots that 1/2 + 2/5 = 3/7 must be wrong because 3/7 is less than 1/2.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a problem such as 'Ana walked 3/4 of a mile and then 2/3 of a mile' and draws two bars of the same length, cut into quarters and thirds, before writing an equation. Before calculating, the child says 'each is more than a half, so the total is more than 1'. After calculating, the child compares the answer with that estimate.

### Limits

All fractions in a problem refer to the same whole. Unlike denominators are included, and like denominators are not excluded. The statement gives no list of benchmark fractions; its example uses 1/2. Models and equations are given as examples of ways to represent a problem, not as a requirement. The cluster heading presents the adding and subtracting as work done by way of equivalent fractions.

### Common mistakes

Adding across, numerators together and denominators together, which is the error in the statement's own example. Adding fractions of different wholes, such as half of a small pizza and half of a large one, as if the halves were the same size. Skipping the estimate, so an answer smaller than one of the parts being added goes unnoticed.
