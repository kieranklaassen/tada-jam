## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reads a fraction as its numerator divided by its denominator (a/b means a ÷ b). The child solves word problems where one whole number is divided by another and the answer is a fraction or a mixed number, for example with an equation or a visual fraction model that stands for the problem. The statement's examples: 3 divided by 4 gives 3/4, since 4 times 3/4 is 3 and 4 people sharing 3 wholes equally get 3/4 each; and a sack of rice weighing 50 pounds, shared equally by 9 people, where the child also names the two whole numbers the answer falls between.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shares 3 paper strips among 4 people by cutting each strip into quarters and dealing them out, and sees that each person holds three quarters. For 50 ÷ 9 the child gives each person 5 whole pounds, has 5 pounds left, and shares those as ninths, arriving at 5 5/9. The child says 'between 5 and 6' before working out the exact answer.

### Limits

The numbers being divided are whole numbers, and answers are fractions or mixed numbers. The statement sets no number range. Models and equations are given as examples of ways to represent a problem. Naming the two whole numbers on either side of the answer appears in an example, not as a separate requirement.

### Common mistakes

Reversing the division: reading 3/4 as 4 ÷ 3, or always dividing the larger number by the smaller. Believing a smaller number cannot be divided by a larger one. Writing what is left as a remainder ('5 remainder 5') and not as a fraction of a share.
