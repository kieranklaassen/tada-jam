## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child explains two patterns that powers of 10 produce: how many zeros the answer has when a number is multiplied by them, and where the decimal point sits after a decimal has been divided or multiplied by one of them. The child writes powers of 10 with whole-number exponents.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child multiplies 34 by 10, 100 and 1,000 on a place-value chart, sliding the digits left one, two and three columns, and says why the zeros appear. The child does the same with 3.4 and with dividing, watching the digits move right, and explains it as each digit becoming ten times or one tenth as much, not as 'adding zeros'. The child writes 1,000 as 10³ and matches the exponent to the number of places moved.

### Limits

Exponents are whole numbers and the base is 10 only. Dividing by a power of 10 is named for decimals. The statement sets no range for the numbers or the exponents and does not mention negative exponents or powers of other bases.

### Common mistakes

Applying 'add a zero' to a decimal, so 3.4 × 10 is written as 3.40. Moving the decimal point in the wrong direction when dividing. Reading 10³ as 10 × 3 = 30.
