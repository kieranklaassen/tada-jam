## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child carries out all four operations on decimals as far as hundredths. The child works with drawings or concrete models and with strategies that rest on how addition and subtraction are related, on properties of operations, on place value, or on a mix of these. The child ties a written method to the strategy used and explains the reasoning.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child adds 2.75 and 1.4 with place-value disks, trading ten tenths for a one, then writes the same sum in columns with the decimal points aligned. For 0.3 × 0.4 the child shades 3 columns of a hundredths grid, then 4 rows across them, and counts 12 hundredths. For 2.4 ÷ 0.6 the child asks how many groups of 6 tenths are in 24 tenths, and says each step aloud.

### Limits

Decimals go to hundredths, and all four operations are included. Work is done with models or drawings together with the named strategies, and is tied to a written method; the statement does not name a standard algorithm for decimals. It does not say whether 'to hundredths' bounds only the numbers worked with or the answer as well.

### Common mistakes

Lining up the last digits instead of the decimal points when adding or subtracting, so 2.75 + 1.4 is worked as 2.75 + 0.14. Multiplying tenths by tenths and writing tenths: 0.3 × 0.4 = 1.2. Expecting multiplication to make larger and division to make smaller, and so rejecting a correct answer such as 2.4 ÷ 0.6 = 4.
