## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child plots points in the first quadrant of a coordinate plane to picture a problem, whether it comes from everyday life or from mathematics itself. The child also says what a point's coordinate values mean in the situation the problem describes.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child keeps a table of two linked measurements, such as the day and the height of a bean plant in centimetres, and marks each pair as a point on squared paper. Pointing at one mark, the child says what it tells: 'on day 4 the plant was 6 centimetres tall'. On a grid map of a room, the child marks where the door and the desk are and reads how many units apart they are along one axis.

### Limits

Points lie in the first quadrant only, so neither coordinate is negative. The statement sets no upper number range and does not say whether coordinates are whole numbers only. Problems may come from real situations or be purely mathematical.

### Common mistakes

Reading the pair in the wrong order, so the day is taken for the height. Naming the two numbers of a point without saying what they stand for in the situation. Assuming one square always stands for 1 when the axis is numbered in steps of 2, 5 or 10.
