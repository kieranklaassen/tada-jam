## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Building on what the child already knows about division, the child carries out two kinds of division: a unit fraction divided by a whole number, and a whole number divided by a unit fraction. In this part the child solves problems from real situations that call for either kind, the whole number never being zero when it is the divisor, for example with equations and visual fraction models that stand for the problem. The statement's examples ask how many 1/3-cup portions 2 cups of raisins make, and what each of 3 people gets when half a pound of chocolate is shared equally among them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child scoops raisins out of a 2-cup jug with a 1/3-cup measure and counts 6 scoops. The child breaks half a bar of modelling clay into 3 equal lumps and works out that each is 1/6 of a bar. Before calculating, the child decides which kind of problem it is: 'how many of these fit?' or 'share this among so many'.

### Limits

Only two kinds of division appear: a whole number divided by a unit fraction, and a unit fraction divided by a whole number that is not zero. Problems come from real situations. A footnote on the stem of this standard, and so covering this part, says that at this grade no child has to divide a fraction by another fraction. The statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

Setting the division up the wrong way round: writing 3 ÷ 1/2 for half a pound shared among 3. Multiplying when the problem is about sharing. Answering 6 instead of 1/6 for the sharing problem, by treating both kinds of problem alike.
