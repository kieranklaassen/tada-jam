## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Building on what the child already knows about multiplication, the child multiplies a whole number or a fraction by a fraction. In this part the child works out the area of a rectangle whose sides are fractions of a unit by covering it with small squares whose sides are a suitable unit fraction, and shows that multiplying the two side lengths gives the same area. The child multiplies fractional side lengths to get rectangle areas and draws a product of fractions as the area of a rectangle.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On squared paper where a 4-by-4 block of small squares stands for one square unit, the child draws a rectangle 3/4 of a unit long and 1/2 of a unit wide, counts the 6 small squares inside it, and says each is 1/16 of the unit square, so the area is 6/16. The child then multiplies 3/4 × 1/2 = 3/8 and checks that 6/16 is the same amount. Given a product such as 1/2 × 3/5, the child draws the rectangle that shows it inside a unit square.

### Limits

The side lengths are fractions, and the tiles are squares whose side is a unit fraction. The statement gives no list of denominators and does not say whether side lengths written as mixed numbers are included. Only rectangles are named.

### Common mistakes

Counting the small tiles and giving that count as the area, without saying what fraction of a square unit each tile is. Thinking a tile with side 1/4 of a unit has an area of 1/4 of a square unit instead of 1/16. Adding the side lengths, or finding the perimeter, when asked for the area.
