## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes simple expressions that set down a calculation with numbers, and says what a numerical expression means without working out its value. As examples: 'take the sum of 8 and 7 and multiply it by 2' is written as 2 × (8 + 7), and the child sees, with no need to compute the sum or the product, that 3 × (18932 + 921) is triple 18932 + 921.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears 'subtract 4 from 20, then divide by 2' and writes (20 − 4) ÷ 2, placing parentheses where the spoken order needs them. Shown two expressions on cards, the child says which is larger and why, without calculating. The child reads an expression back as words: '5 times the sum of 6 and 9'.

### Limits

The expressions are simple and use numbers only; the statement does not mention letters standing for unknowns. Interpreting is done without working out the value. The statement's examples use one pair of parentheses and at most two operations; it sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

Writing the operations in the order they are spoken and leaving out the parentheses, so 'add 5 and 3, then multiply by 4' becomes 5 + 3 × 4. Reversing 'subtract 4 from 20' into 4 − 20. Calculating both expressions to compare them when their structure already gives the answer.
