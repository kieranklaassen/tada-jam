## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Building on what the child already knows about multiplication, the child multiplies a whole number or a fraction by a fraction. In this part the child reads (a/b) × q in two matching ways: split q into b equal parts and take a of them, or carry out the chain of operations a × q ÷ b. As examples, the child shows with a visual fraction model that (2/3) × 4 is 8/3 and makes up a story that fits, and does likewise for (2/3) × (4/5), which is 8/15; in general, multiplying a/b by c/d gives ac/bd.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

For 2/3 × 4, the child draws 4 bars, cuts each into thirds, shades two thirds of each, and counts 8 thirds. For 2/3 × 4/5 the child shades 4 of 5 columns of a rectangle, then 2 of 3 rows across them, and counts 8 of 15 small parts. The child tells a story for each, such as 'there are 4 kilograms of clay and we use two thirds of it'.

### Limits

One factor is a fraction; the other is a whole number or a fraction. The statement gives no list of denominators. Mixed numbers are not named in this part; a neighbouring statement at this grade brings them in for problems from real situations. The two worked equations are the statement's examples.

### Common mistakes

Expecting the product to be larger than both factors, and so doubting 2/3 × 4/5 = 8/15. Looking for a common denominator before multiplying, as in addition. For a whole number times a fraction, multiplying both the numerator and the denominator by the whole number: 2/3 × 4 = 8/12.
