## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child rounds a decimal to whichever place is asked for, relying on an understanding of place value to do it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child marks 3.746 on a number line between 3.7 and 3.8, finds the halfway point 3.75, and says which end is nearer. The child does the same for the nearest hundredth and the nearest whole number, each time naming the two candidates first. With a measured length such as 12.385 metres, the child says it to the nearest tenth of a metre.

### Limits

Any place may be the target, whole-number places and decimal places alike. The statement itself sets no limit on the number of decimal places; neighbouring statements at this grade read and write decimals to thousandths. The statement names an understanding of place value as the basis for rounding.

### Common mistakes

Deciding from the last digit of the number instead of the digit right after the target place. Rounding in a chain from the right (3.746 to 3.75 to 3.8) and getting a different answer from rounding once. Going wrong when a 9 has to carry, as in 2.97 to the nearest tenth.
