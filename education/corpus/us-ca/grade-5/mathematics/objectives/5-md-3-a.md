## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sees volume as a property that solid shapes have and understands the ideas behind measuring it. This part names the measuring piece. A unit cube is a cube with every side 1 unit long; the volume it has counts as one cubic unit, and volumes are measured with such cubes.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a centimetre cube, measures an edge with a ruler, and names it 'one cubic centimetre'. The child sets it beside a one-inch cube and says which takes up more space. Filling a small box with the cubes, the child says the size of the box in cubes and contrasts this with covering only its bottom with squares.

### Limits

The statement defines the unit and sets no number range. It does not yet ask the child to work out a volume; counting cubes and using formulas come in neighbouring statements at this grade. Volume is treated as a property of solid figures only.

### Common mistakes

Confusing volume with area: calling a flat square a cubic unit, or counting only the faces that can be seen. Saying a unit cube has a volume of 6 because it has 6 faces. Calling any cube a unit cube whatever its size, without checking that its edge is 1 of the chosen unit.
