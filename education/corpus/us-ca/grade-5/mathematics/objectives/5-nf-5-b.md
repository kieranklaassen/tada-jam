## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands multiplication as scaling, that is, as making a quantity larger or smaller. In this part the child explains why a number grows when it is multiplied by a fraction above 1 (multiplying by a whole number above 1 being the case already known) and why it shrinks when the fraction is below 1. The child also connects the rule for equivalent fractions, (n × a)/(n × b) = a/b, with what happens when a/b is multiplied by 1.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a 12-centimetre strip and makes strips 3/2 and 2/3 as long, lays them beside the original, and explains: '3/2 is one whole strip and half of another, 2/3 is only part of one strip'. The child multiplies 3/4 by 2/2, gets 6/8, and explains that 2/2 is 1, so the amount is unchanged though the numbers look different.

### Limits

Three explanations are asked for: multiplying by a fraction above 1, multiplying by a fraction below 1, and multiplying by 1 written as n/n. The number being multiplied is not restricted to whole numbers; the cluster heading places this work under multiplying and dividing fractions. The statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

Stating the rule without a reason ('it just gets smaller'). Thinking that 2/2 × 3/4 = 6/8 has made the fraction bigger because both of its numbers grew. Assuming a fraction always means less than 1, so that multiplying by 5/4 is expected to shrink the number.
