## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sees how the places of a number with several digits relate. Moving a digit one place to the left makes it worth ten times what it was; moving it one place to the right makes it worth a tenth of what it was.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out place-value disks or base-ten blocks and trades ten of one kind for one of the next larger kind, and one for ten of the next smaller. On a place-value chart the child writes 5 in the tens column, slides it to the hundreds, and says 'ten times as much'; slides it back to the tens and on to the ones, and says 'one tenth as much' each time. In 444 or 0.33, the child says how the value of one digit compares with the same digit beside it.

### Limits

The statement speaks of multi-digit numbers in general and sets no number range. Neighbouring statements at this grade work with decimals down to thousandths, so the relationship is used for places to the right of the decimal point as well as for whole-number places. The comparison is between a place and the one next to it.

### Common mistakes

Believing the pattern stops at the decimal point, so tenths are not seen as one tenth of ones. Mixing up tens with tenths and hundreds with hundredths because the names sound alike. Expecting a 'oneths' place, as if the places mirrored around the decimal point instead of around the ones place.
