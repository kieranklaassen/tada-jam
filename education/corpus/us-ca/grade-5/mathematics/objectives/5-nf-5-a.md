## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands multiplication as scaling, that is, as making a quantity larger or smaller. In this part the child judges, without working the multiplication out, how big a product is next to one of its factors by looking at how big the other factor is.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Shown 3/4 × 836, the child says straight away 'less than 836, because it is only three quarters of it', and for 2 1/2 × 836 'more than twice 836'. The child sorts cards with products into 'smaller than the second number', 'the same' and 'bigger' without calculating. With a strip of paper, the child cuts a second strip 1 1/2 times as long and a third strip 1/2 as long and lays them side by side.

### Limits

The comparison is made without carrying out the multiplication, and no exact answer is asked for. The statement sets no number range and does not itself say which kinds of numbers the factors are. The cluster heading places it under multiplying and dividing fractions, and the neighbouring part speaks of fractions greater than 1 and less than 1.

### Common mistakes

Keeping the belief from whole numbers that multiplying always makes a number bigger. Calculating the product anyway and then comparing. Deciding from the size of the digits in a fraction (5/8 has large digits, so it must enlarge) instead of asking whether the factor is above or below 1.
