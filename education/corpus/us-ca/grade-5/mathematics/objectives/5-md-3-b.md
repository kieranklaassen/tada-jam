## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sees volume as a property that solid shapes have and understands the ideas behind measuring it. This part says how the number arises: when n unit cubes fill a solid exactly, leaving no spaces and not overlapping, the volume of that solid is n cubic units.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child packs a small open box with unit cubes, pressing them together so no space is left and none sticks out, then counts them and says 'this box is 24 cubic units'. The child builds two different solids from the same 12 cubes and says both have the same volume. Shown a loosely filled box with spaces, the child says why the count is not yet the volume.

### Limits

The cubes must fill the solid with no gaps and no overlaps. The count n is a number of whole cubes; the statement gives no number range. It does not limit the solid to a box shape, though the neighbouring statements on calculating volume use right rectangular prisms.

### Common mistakes

Counting only the cubes visible on the outside of a stack and missing the hidden ones inside. Counting cubes at edges and corners more than once when counting face by face. Accepting a filling with gaps, or with cubes of mixed sizes, as a measure.
