## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child connects volume with multiplying and adding and solves volume problems from everyday life and from mathematics. In this part the child fills a right rectangular prism whose sides are whole numbers of units with unit cubes, and shows that the count equals the product of the three edge lengths, which is also the base area times the height. The child also pictures a product of three whole numbers as a volume, for example to show the associative property of multiplication.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds a box shape 4 cubes long, 3 wide and 2 high, counts the 24 cubes, and then checks that 4 × 3 × 2 gives the same number. The child counts one layer (12) and multiplies by the number of layers, then turns the block on its side and counts layers the other way to see that (4 × 3) × 2 and 4 × (3 × 2) match. Given 3 × 5 × 2 as a written product, the child builds the block that shows it.

### Limits

The solid is a right rectangular prism and its side lengths are whole numbers. The statement does not mention fractional edge lengths and sets no upper size. The associative property is given as an example of what a product of three factors can show, not as a separate requirement.

### Common mistakes

Adding the three edge lengths instead of multiplying them. Multiplying only two edges and giving the area of one face as the volume. Counting the cubes on the visible faces of a drawn prism instead of thinking in layers.
