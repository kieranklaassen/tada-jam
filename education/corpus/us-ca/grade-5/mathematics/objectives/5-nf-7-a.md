## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Building on what the child already knows about division, the child carries out two kinds of division: a unit fraction divided by a whole number, and a whole number divided by a unit fraction. In this part the child gives meaning to a unit fraction divided by a whole number other than zero and works out the quotient. As an example, the child makes up a story for (1/3) ÷ 4, shows the quotient with a visual fraction model, and explains from the link between division and multiplication that the answer is 1/12, since 4 × (1/12) is 1/3.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a third of a paper strip and shares it equally among 4 people by folding it into 4, then lays the small piece along the whole strip and finds that 12 of them fit. The child tells a story such as 'a third of a pan of cornbread is shared by 4 friends'. The child checks with multiplication: 4 pieces of 1/12 make 1/3 again.

### Limits

A footnote limits this standard: at this grade no child has to divide a fraction by another fraction. The footnote adds that children who can multiply any two fractions are able to work out ways of dividing them by reasoning from how the two operations are connected. In this part the number being divided is a unit fraction and the divisor is a whole number that is not zero. The statement gives no list of denominators.

### Common mistakes

Answering (1/3) ÷ 4 with the whole number 12, having multiplied 3 by 4 without seeing that the 12 names the size of the pieces (twelfths). Mixing up (1/3) ÷ 4 with 4 ÷ (1/3). Expecting a smaller answer to have a smaller denominator, when a smaller piece has a larger one.
