## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes a whole number from 2 to 50 as its prime factors multiplied together. For example, the child finds which primes divide 24 and writes 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 for 24.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child draws a factor tree for 36, splitting it into 4 × 9 and then each branch again until every end is a prime, and writes the primes as one multiplication. With 24 counters, the child makes rectangles to find factor pairs and keeps splitting any side that is not prime. The child multiplies the primes back together to check.

### Limits

The number is a whole number from 2 to 50. This standard is a California addition: California added it to the common standards. The statement does not ask for exponent notation; its example writes the repeated factor out in full.

### Common mistakes

Stopping at a factor that is not prime, such as writing 24 as 4 × 6 or 2 × 2 × 6. Treating 1 as a prime and including it, or treating 9, 15, 21, 27, 39 or 49 as prime because they are odd. Listing each different prime once (2 and 3 for 24) instead of every factor needed to make the number.
