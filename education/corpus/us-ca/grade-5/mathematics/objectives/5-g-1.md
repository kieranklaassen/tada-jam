## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sets up a coordinate system from two number lines, the axes, that cross at right angles so that the zero of both sits at the crossing point, named the origin. Any point in the plane is then fixed by its coordinates, an ordered pair of numbers. The child understands that the first of the two numbers gives the distance to move from the origin along one axis and the second gives the distance along the other, and that each coordinate takes the name of its axis (the x-coordinate goes with the x-axis, the y-coordinate with the y-axis).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child draws two number lines at right angles on squared paper, marks 0 where they cross, and numbers both. Given (3, 5), the child puts a finger on the origin, moves 3 squares along the x-axis, then 5 squares up, and marks the point. Shown a marked point, the child reads off its two numbers and says them in order, x first.

### Limits

The statement itself names no quadrant and no number range, and neither does its cluster heading. The cluster heading gives the purpose: points are plotted in order to solve problems, both from real situations and from mathematics. The neighbouring statement at this grade keeps plotted points to the first quadrant, where both coordinates are zero or greater. The statement does not mention negative coordinates.

### Common mistakes

Swapping the two numbers: moving up first and then across, so (3, 5) is marked where (5, 3) belongs. Counting the origin as 1 instead of 0, which places the point one square short. Marking points in the spaces between grid lines instead of where two lines cross.
