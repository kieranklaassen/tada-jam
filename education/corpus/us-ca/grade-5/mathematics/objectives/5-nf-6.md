## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child solves problems from real situations that call for multiplying fractions and mixed numbers, for example with an equation or a visual fraction model that stands for the problem.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads 'a recipe needs 2 1/2 cups of flour and we make 3/4 of the recipe' and draws 2 1/2 bars, cutting each into quarters and shading three quarters. The child writes 3/4 × 2 1/2, changes the mixed number to 5/2 or multiplies the whole and the half separately, and says the answer with its unit. The child checks that the answer is less than 2 1/2 because the multiplier is less than 1.

### Limits

Problems come from real situations and involve multiplication. Both fractions and mixed numbers appear. The statement gives no list of denominators. Models and equations are given as examples of ways to represent a problem.

### Common mistakes

Multiplying the whole-number parts and the fraction parts separately and stopping there: 2 1/2 × 1 1/2 = 2 1/4. Choosing addition or subtraction because 'of' is not recognised as multiplication. Giving an answer without its unit, or with a unit that does not fit the question.
