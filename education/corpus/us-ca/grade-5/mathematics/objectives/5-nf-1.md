## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child adds and subtracts fractions whose denominators differ, mixed numbers included. The way to do it is to swap each fraction for an equivalent one so that the denominators match, which gives a sum or difference equal to the original one. As an example, 5/4 + 2/3 is rewritten as 15/12 + 8/12, which is 23/12; in letters, the sum of a/b and c/d has numerator ad + bc and denominator bd.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays fraction strips for 1/2 and 1/3 end to end, finds that sixths fit both, and exchanges them for 3/6 and 2/6 before joining. On paper the child rewrites both fractions over a shared denominator, adds or subtracts the numerators, and keeps the denominator. With 3 1/4 − 1 2/3 the child either regroups a whole into twelfths or turns both into fractions greater than one.

### Limits

The denominators differ, and mixed numbers are included. The cluster heading names equivalent fractions as the strategy, which matches the method the statement sets out. The statement gives no list of allowed denominators. Its general rule uses the product of the two denominators as the shared one; it does not ask for the least common denominator or for answers in simplest form.

### Common mistakes

Adding numerators and adding denominators: 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5. Changing the denominator to the shared one but leaving the numerator as it was. With mixed numbers, subtracting the smaller fraction part from the larger whichever way round they stand, instead of regrouping a whole.
