## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child draws a line plot for a set of measurements taken to fractions of a unit (halves, quarters, eighths). The child then answers questions about the data in a line plot with the fraction operations of this grade. As an example: identical beakers hold different measured amounts of liquid, and the child works out how much would be in each one if all the liquid were shared out evenly among them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures a handful of pencils or leaves to the nearest eighth of an inch and marks an X above each length on a number line marked in eighths. Reading the plot, the child finds the longest and the shortest and subtracts them, or adds all the lengths. With six identical cups holding different amounts of water, the child adds the amounts and shares the total into six equal parts, then pours to check that the cups come out level.

### Limits

The measurements are in halves, quarters and eighths of a unit. The operations are those this grade's fraction statements cover: adding and subtracting with unlike denominators, multiplying fractions, and dividing only with unit fractions and whole numbers. The beaker problem is the statement's example, not a required task.

### Common mistakes

Spacing the marks on the number line unevenly, or leaving out values that have no data so the scale is broken. Reading the number of X marks as the measurement instead of how many times it occurred. Adding each distinct value once when totalling, forgetting that a value with three X marks counts three times.
