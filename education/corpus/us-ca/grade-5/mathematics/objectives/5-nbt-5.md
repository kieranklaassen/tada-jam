## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child multiplies whole numbers of more than one digit by the standard algorithm, and does so with fluency.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes 347 × 26 in columns, multiplies by the 6 ones, then by the 2 tens with that row shifted one place, and adds the two rows. The child says what each carried digit stands for and checks the answer against an estimate such as 350 × 25. The work is done on paper, steadily and without counting aids.

### Limits

The numbers are whole numbers with more than one digit, and the method is the standard algorithm. The statement sets no limit on the number of digits. It asks for fluency and gives no time limit. Decimals are multiplied under a different statement at this grade.

### Common mistakes

Forgetting the place shift for the tens row, so 347 × 20 is worked as 347 × 2. Adding a carried digit before multiplying instead of after, or forgetting to add it at all. Skipping the step for a zero inside a factor, as in 304 × 26, so the later rows sit in the wrong places.
