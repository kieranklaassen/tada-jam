## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Building on what the child already knows about division, the child carries out two kinds of division: a unit fraction divided by a whole number, and a whole number divided by a unit fraction. In this part the child gives meaning to a whole number divided by a unit fraction and works out the quotient. As an example, the child makes up a story for 4 ÷ (1/5), shows the quotient with a visual fraction model, and explains from the link between division and multiplication that the answer is 20, since (1/5) × 20 is 4.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out 4 whole paper strips, cuts each into fifths, and counts 20 pieces. The child tells a story such as '4 litres of juice are poured into glasses of 1/5 litre each; how many glasses are filled?' The child checks with multiplication: 20 glasses of 1/5 litre make 4 litres.

### Limits

The number being divided is a whole number and the divisor is a unit fraction. The statement sets no number range and gives no list of denominators. A footnote on the stem of this standard, and so covering this part, says that at this grade no child has to divide a fraction by another fraction.

### Common mistakes

Expecting division to make the number smaller, and so answering 4/5 instead of 20. Dividing 4 by 5 because 5 is the only other whole number written. Finding the 20 pieces in the drawing but naming the answer '20 fifths' or '20/5' instead of 20.
