## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child connects volume with multiplying and adding and solves volume problems from everyday life and from mathematics. In this part the child uses two formulas for a rectangular prism, base area times height (V = b × h) and length times width times height (V = l × w × h), to work out the volume of right rectangular prisms whose edges are whole numbers of units, as part of solving such problems.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures the length, width and height of a shoebox to the nearest whole centimetre, writes V = l × w × h, and multiplies. For a fish tank whose floor area is already known, the child multiplies that area by the height of the water. The child writes the answer with a cubic unit and checks it against a rough count of layers.

### Limits

The solids are right rectangular prisms whose edge lengths are whole numbers. Two formulas are named: length times width times height, and base area times height. The statement does not mention other solids such as cylinders or pyramids, and does not mention fractional edges.

### Common mistakes

Reading b in V = b × h as one edge of the base instead of the area of the base. Leaving the answer in square units or with no unit. Mixing units, for example multiplying a length in feet by a width in inches.
