## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child's work here is with decimals down to the thousandths place: reading them, writing them and comparing them. In this part the child decides which of two such decimals is greater, or that they are equal, by reasoning about what the digit in each place is worth, and writes down the outcome with the signs <, = or >.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lines up 0.47 and 0.409 on a place-value chart, compares tenths first, then hundredths, and stops at the first place where the digits differ. The child shades two thousandths grids or places both numbers on a number line to check. The child writes 0.47 > 0.409 and reads the sentence aloud.

### Limits

Two decimals are compared at a time, each going no further than the thousandths place. The comparison rests on place value, and the outcome is written with the symbols for greater than, equal to and less than. The statement does not mention putting a longer list in order.

### Common mistakes

Taking the decimal with more digits as the larger one, so 0.409 is judged greater than 0.47. The opposite error: taking the shorter decimal as larger because 'thousandths are small'. Not seeing 0.5 and 0.500 as equal.
