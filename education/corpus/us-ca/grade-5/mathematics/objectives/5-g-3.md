## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that whatever is true of every flat shape in a group is also true of every shape in the smaller groups inside it. As an example: squares are a kind of rectangle, and every rectangle has four right angles, so every square has them too.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts cut-out quadrilaterals into hoops that sit inside one another: a hoop for parallelograms, inside it one for rectangles, inside that one for squares. Holding up a square, the child lists what it must have because it is a rectangle and because it is a parallelogram: four right angles, two pairs of parallel sides. The child tests a claim such as 'every rhombus has opposite sides parallel' by naming the wider group a rhombus belongs to.

### Limits

Only two-dimensional figures are covered. The statement gives rectangles and squares as its example and no list of the categories to be used. Solids are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Treating squares and rectangles as separate kinds, so 'a square is not a rectangle'. Running the rule backwards: because squares have four equal sides, saying all rectangles do. Judging by how a shape looks or how it is turned, so a square standing on a corner is called a diamond and no longer a square.
