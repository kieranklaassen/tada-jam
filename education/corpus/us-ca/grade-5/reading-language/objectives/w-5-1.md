## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes pieces that give an opinion about a text or a topic, with information and reasons behind the point of view. At the start the child makes clear what topic or text the piece is about and says what their opinion is, and arranges the ideas in logical groups, a structure that serves the purpose of the writing. The reasons come in a logical order, and facts and details back them. Words, phrases and clauses such as specifically and consequently connect the opinion to the reasons. A closing section or statement that refers to the opinion ends the piece.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a letter to the principal arguing that the library should open at lunch. The first paragraph says what the letter is about and what the child thinks. Each following paragraph gives one reason with a fact or example, and the child adds "specifically" or "consequently" where a reason connects to the opinion. The child rereads the draft, moves a reason that is in the wrong paragraph, and checks that the last paragraph returns to the opinion.

### Limits

The piece is about a topic or a text. Reasons are to be backed by facts and details. Two example linking words are given; no list is set. The statement sets no length and no number of reasons. It does not mention answering an opposing view.

### Common mistakes

Children state an opinion and repeat it in other words instead of giving reasons. They give reasons with no fact or detail behind them. They end with a new idea, or stop after the last reason with no closing statement.
