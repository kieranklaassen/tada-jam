## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child describes the effect that the point of view of a narrator or speaker has on the way events are told.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a scene told by one character, then retells the same scene aloud as a different character would tell it. The child points out what the narrator knows, cannot know, likes and dislikes. The child says how that changes which events are told and which words are used for them.

### Limits

The statement covers narrators and speakers. It asks the child to describe the influence of point of view. It does not use the terms first person and third person, and it does not ask the child to rewrite the text.

### Common mistakes

Children name the point of view ("first person") and stop, without saying how it shapes the telling. They believe everything a narrator says, including a narrator who is biased or does not know the whole story. They confuse the narrator with the author.
