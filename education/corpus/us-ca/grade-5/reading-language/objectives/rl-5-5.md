## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child explains how several chapters, scenes or stanzas in a row fit with one another and so give a particular story, play or poem its overall structure.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes one line about what each chapter of a short novel does: introduces the problem, makes it worse, brings the turning point, resolves it. The child explains why chapter 3 has to come before chapter 4. For a poem the child says what each stanza adds and how the last stanza answers the first.

### Limits

The units are chapters, scenes and stanzas. The statement is about how the parts make up the whole of one particular work, not about comparing works. It names no plot terms such as climax.

### Common mistakes

Children summarise each chapter and do not say what it does for the whole. They treat stanzas as separate small poems. They cannot say what would be lost if a scene were removed or moved.
