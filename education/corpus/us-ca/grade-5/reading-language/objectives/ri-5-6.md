## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child analyses several accounts of one event or topic and notes the important ways in which the points of view behind them are alike and different.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a soldier's diary entry and a textbook paragraph about the same battle. The child lists what each account includes and leaves out, and says who is telling it and what that person could see or wanted. The child names one point where the accounts agree and one where they differ.

### Limits

"Multiple" means more than one account; no number is set. The accounts are about the same event or topic. The statement asks for important similarities and differences in point of view; it does not ask the child to judge which account is correct.

### Common mistakes

Children list differences in facts and do not connect them to who is telling the account. They assume a firsthand account tells everything that happened. They take "point of view" to mean only first person or third person.
