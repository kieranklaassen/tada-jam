## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Using specific information from a technical, scientific or historical text, the child explains how two or more people, events, ideas or concepts in it are related or act on one another.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a history passage and explains how a new tax led to a protest, pointing to the sentences that connect the two. In a science text the child draws arrows from "less rainfall" to "fewer plants" to "fewer deer" and says in words what each arrow means. The child explains how two people in a biography worked together or disagreed.

### Limits

The text types are historical, scientific and technical. At least two things must be related to each other. The explanation rests on specific information in the text. The statement does not name kinds of relationship such as cause and effect.

### Common mistakes

Children describe each person or event separately and never state the link between them. They read two events that follow each other as cause and effect when the text does not say so. They explain the link from what they already know about the topic instead of from the text.
