## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out what general academic words and subject-specific words and phrases mean when they occur in a text about a topic or subject area of grade 5. The statement points to Language standards 4 to 6 of the same grade for further expectations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a science chapter, stops at "erosion", and uses the sentences and the diagram around it to say what it means in this chapter. The child notices that "table" in a mathematics text means rows and columns of numbers, not furniture. The child keeps a list of the chapter's key terms and explains each one in their own words.

### Limits

The words come from texts on grade 5 topics or subject areas. The statement gives no word list and names no strategy; for more detail it refers to Language standards 4 to 6 of the grade. California adds to this statement, which ends with the California mark; the wording does not show which of its words are California's.

### Common mistakes

Children apply the everyday meaning of a word that has a special meaning in the subject ("volume", "product", "cell"). They skip a term printed in bold without reading the definition given in the same sentence. They can repeat a glossary definition but cannot use the word when talking about the text.
