## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child quotes a text accurately in two situations: when explaining something the text states directly, and when explaining an inference drawn from it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says that a character in a novel is afraid and reads out the exact line that shows it. Asked why the character lied, the child gives an inference and copies the words from the chapter that support it, inside quotation marks, exactly as printed.

### Limits

This record sits in the literature strand; the same wording stands in the informational-text strand as a separate record. Two uses of quotation are named: for what the text says outright and for what the reader infers. No number of quotations is set.

### Common mistakes

Children retell the plot instead of quoting the words that prove their point. They quote a long passage when one sentence holds the evidence. They give an inference about a character's feelings that comes from their own experience and has no support in the text.
