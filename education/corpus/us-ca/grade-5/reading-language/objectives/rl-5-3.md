## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Using specific details from a story or play, such as the way characters deal with each other, the child compares and contrasts at least two of its characters, two of its settings or two of its events.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes two characters from a novel and fills two columns: what each wants, how each speaks to the other, what each does when things go wrong. For every entry the child notes the page it comes from. The child then says or writes one way the two are alike and one way they differ, and does the same with two settings or two events.

### Limits

At least two items are compared: characters, settings or events. The texts are stories and dramas; poems are not named in this statement. Details must be specific and come from the text. How characters interact is given as an example of such a detail.

### Common mistakes

Children describe each character in turn and never set the two side by side. They compare outward features such as hair or age and leave out actions and motives. They list only differences.
