## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

For analysis, reflection and research, the child uses evidence taken out of informational or literary texts. With literature, the child applies the reading standards of grade 5; the example quoted is the standard on comparing characters, settings or events of a story or play through specific details. With informational texts the child does the same; the example quoted is the standard on explaining which reasons and evidence an author gives for which points.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a paragraph claiming that two characters in a novel handle fear differently, and backs each half of the claim with a detail or quotation from the book. In a response to an article, the child names the author's point and writes out the reason and the evidence the author gave for it.

### Limits

Evidence comes from texts, literary or informational. Each lettered part quotes one reading standard as an example; the parts refer to the grade 5 reading standards for literature and for informational text in general, not only to the ones quoted. The statement sets no length and no number of pieces of evidence.

### Common mistakes

Children state an analysis and support it with their own opinion or experience instead of the text. They insert a quotation and do not say how it supports the point. They retell the text at length in place of analysis.
