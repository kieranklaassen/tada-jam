## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes texts that inform or explain: each examines a topic and makes the information and ideas clear to a reader. The opening names the topic clearly and gives a general observation and a focus; information that belongs together is grouped in a logical way, and headings or other formatting, illustrations and multimedia are added where they help a reader understand. The body of the text is made of concrete details, facts, quotations and definitions, or other examples and information that relate to the topic, and the child writes it in precise language with the vocabulary of the subject. Words, phrases and clauses such as especially and in contrast connect ideas, both inside one category of information and from one category to another. At the end comes a section or statement that relates to the explanation or information given.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a report on how volcanoes erupt. The child starts with a sentence that says what the report covers, sorts notes into sections with headings such as "Inside a volcano" and "Kinds of eruption", and adds a labelled drawing. The child uses the terms "magma" and "vent" and defines each the first time, joins two sections with "in contrast", and ends with a paragraph that sums up the explanation.

### Limits

Formatting, illustrations and multimedia are included only when they help comprehension; headings are the one example of formatting given. Development may draw on any of several kinds of material (quotations, facts, definitions, concrete details, other examples); the statement does not require all of them. Two example linking phrases are given. The statement sets no length and no number of sections.

### Common mistakes

Children write everything they know about the topic in the order they remember it, with no grouping. They copy sentences from a source instead of developing the topic in their own words. They add opinions ("Volcanoes are awesome") or end without any closing statement.
