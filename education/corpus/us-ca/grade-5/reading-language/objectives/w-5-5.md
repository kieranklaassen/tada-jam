## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Helped and guided by adults and peers, the child improves a piece of writing and makes it stronger where it needs it. The ways named are planning, editing, revising, rewriting and attempting a different approach. A note says that when the child edits for conventions, the result should show control of Language standards 1 to 3 for every grade up to grade 5, grade 5 included.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child makes a plan for a story as a list or a web before writing. After a classmate says the middle is confusing, the child rewrites that part and reads it to the classmate again. The child then checks the draft line by line for tense, commas and spelling. When a draft does not work at all, the child starts again from a different opening.

### Limits

Support from peers and adults is part of the statement; the child is not expected to do this alone. Five ways of improving writing are named, joined by "or", and used as needed. A note links editing to the Language standards on conventions of this grade and of the grades before it.

### Common mistakes

Children treat revising as recopying neatly or fixing spelling, and leave the content unchanged. They resist cutting anything they have written. They accept a peer's comment without understanding it, or ignore every comment.
