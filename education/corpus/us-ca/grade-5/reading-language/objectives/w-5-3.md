## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes narratives: accounts of experiences or events, true or made up, told with effective technique, a clear order of events and descriptive detail. The opening sets up a situation and brings in a narrator, characters or both, so that the reader knows who and what the story is about, and the events then follow one another in a natural way. Techniques such as pacing, description and dialogue develop the events and experiences or show how characters respond to situations. The child manages the order of events with varied transitional words, phrases and clauses, and makes events and experiences precise with sensory details and concrete words and phrases. The ending follows from the events or experiences told.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a story about getting lost at a fair. The first lines say who is telling it and where they are. The child writes what the characters say to each other, slows down at the frightening moment with details of sound and smell, moves time forward with "A few minutes later", and ends with what happened as a result of the search. On rereading, the child replaces "it was scary" with what the narrator saw and felt.

### Limits

The narrative may be real or imagined. Dialogue, description and pacing are named as examples of technique. A narrator, characters or both are introduced. The statement sets no length.

### Common mistakes

Children list events joined by "and then" and give the important moment no more space than the rest. They name feelings in general words instead of showing them with concrete detail. They stop at "The end" or "It was all a dream", an ending that does not follow from the events.
