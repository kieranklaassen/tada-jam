## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes as a regular habit, both over longer stretches that leave time for revision, reflection and research, and over short ones such as one sitting, or one or two days. The writing serves a range of tasks, purposes and audiences that belong to particular subjects.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On one day the child writes a half-page science observation in a single sitting. Over two weeks the child works on a history report: reading and taking notes, drafting, leaving it for a day, and revising. Across a month the child's folder holds pieces written for different subjects and different readers.

### Limits

Both longer and shorter time frames are required. A shorter frame is described as a single sitting or one to two days; for the longer frame no length is given, only that it leaves time to research, reflect and revise. Tasks, purposes and audiences belong to subject disciplines. The statement sets no page counts and no number of pieces.

### Common mistakes

Children treat every piece as a one-sitting task and hand in a first draft. They write readily in language arts lessons and very little in science or history. On a long piece they forget the plan after the first day and start again from the beginning.
