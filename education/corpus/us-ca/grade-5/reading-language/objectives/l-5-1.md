## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows control of standard English grammar and usage in both writing and speech. The child explains what conjunctions, prepositions and interjections do, both in general and inside a given sentence, and uses paired (correlative) conjunctions such as neither/nor and either/or. The child forms the perfect verb tenses and uses them, for example I will have walked, I have walked, I had walked. The child picks a verb tense to show different times, orders of events, states and conditions, and spots and fixes a change of tense that does not belong.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a draft paragraph aloud, hears that it starts in the past tense and changes to the present halfway, and rewrites the verbs so they agree. The child points to "although" or "under" in a sentence and says what job the word does there. The child writes a sentence about something that was finished before another past event, using "had", and joins two choices with "either ... or".

### Limits

Both writing and speaking count. Three word classes are to be explained (conjunctions, prepositions, interjections) and one kind of conjunction is to be used (correlative); the statement gives no list of words for any of them. The perfect tenses are shown with one past, one present and one future example. A footnote says that, from grade 3 on, an asterisk marks a skill that will probably need more work in later grades as writing and speaking become more demanding; in this record the asterisk stands on fixing wrong changes of tense.

### Common mistakes

Children change tense in the middle of a story without noticing, most often from past to present. They use the simple past where the past perfect is needed ("When I arrived, she left" for "she had left") and form the perfect with the wrong verb form ("have went", "had ate"). With correlative pairs they mix the halves ("neither ... or").
