## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child summarises the points made by a speaker or a media source and explains the reasons and evidence behind each claim. The child also identifies logical fallacies and analyses them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to a classmate's speech for a later bedtime and writes down each claim, and next to it the reason or fact the speaker gave. The child marks a claim that had no support. The child also marks a place where the speaker said "everyone knows" or criticised the people who disagree instead of giving a reason.

### Limits

The source is a speaker or a media source. Every claim is to be linked to its reason and evidence. The statement names no particular fallacies. The stored wording is the export's as corrected from the adopted document at two places. California adds to this statement, which ends with the California mark; the wording does not show which of its words are California's.

### Common mistakes

Children summarise what the talk was about and leave out the claims. They accept a claim because the speaker sounds confident or is someone they like. They call any statement they disagree with a fallacy.
