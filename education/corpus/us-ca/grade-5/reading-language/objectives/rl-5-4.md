## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out what words and phrases mean in the way a text uses them, and this covers figurative language like similes and metaphors. The statement points to Language standards 4 to 6 of the same grade for further expectations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads "her words were ice" in a story, says that no ice is involved, and explains what the phrase tells about how the character spoke. The child reads a familiar word used in an unusual sense, rereads the sentences around it, and says what it means here.

### Limits

Meaning is worked out as the words are used in the text, not in isolation. Metaphors and similes are the named examples of figurative language. For more detail the statement refers to Language standards 4 to 6 of the grade. California adds to this statement, which ends with the California mark; the wording does not show which of its words are California's.

### Common mistakes

Children take a metaphor literally. They give a word's most common meaning when the text uses a different one. They identify a simile correctly and stop there, without saying what it means in the passage.
