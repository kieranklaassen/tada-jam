## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out, or makes sure of, what an unfamiliar word or phrase, or one with several meanings, means in grade 5 reading and subject content, and picks whichever of several strategies suits the case. One is context: clues in the text such as a comparison or a cause-and-effect link. Another is word parts: common Greek and Latin roots and affixes suited to the grade (the example words are photosynthesis and photograph). A third is reference materials in print and digital form, such as glossaries, thesauruses and dictionaries: the child consults them to learn how a word is pronounced, to settle exactly what a key word or phrase means, and, in every content area, to find other words that could be chosen.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child meets "arid" in a science passage, rereads the sentences around it, and says what it must mean because the text contrasts it with a rainforest. The child sees the shared part "tele" in "telescope" and "telephone" and says what it adds to each word. The child looks a word up in a glossary or an online dictionary, reads the pronunciation aloud, and picks the meaning that fits the sentence.

### Limits

Words and phrases come from grade 5 reading and subject content. Affixes and roots are limited to common Greek and Latin ones suited to the grade; two example words are given and no list. Reference materials may be print or digital. California adds to the line on reference materials, which ends with the California mark; the wording does not show which of its words are California's. The statement sets no number of words to learn.

### Common mistakes

Children take the first dictionary meaning without checking it against the sentence. They guess from one familiar part of a word and miss the rest (reading "inflammable" as "not flammable"). They skip an unknown word instead of using the sentences around it.
