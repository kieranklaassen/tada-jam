## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child produces writing that is clear and coherent, texts of several paragraphs among it, and develops and organises it to suit the task, the purpose and the audience. A note in the statement says that what is expected for each type of writing at this grade is set out in writing standards 1 to 3.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child is asked to explain a class rule to new students and writes three paragraphs: what the rule is, why it exists, and what to do. The child rereads it as a new student would, removes a sentence that is not about the rule, and changes words the reader would not know. Writing a note to a friend on the same subject, the child chooses a shorter form and a different tone.

### Limits

Writing includes texts of more than one paragraph. Fit is judged against three things: task, purpose and audience. A note leaves the detail of each writing type to writing standards 1 to 3 of the grade. California adds to this statement, which ends with the California mark; the wording does not show which of its words are California's.

### Common mistakes

Children write one long block with no paragraph breaks, or start a new paragraph at random. They write for themselves and leave out what a reader who was not there needs to know. They use the same form and tone for every task.
