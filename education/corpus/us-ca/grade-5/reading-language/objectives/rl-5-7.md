## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child analyses what visual and multimedia elements add to a text's meaning, tone or beauty. Its bracketed examples are a graphic novel, then a multimedia presentation of fiction, then folktale, myth and poem, with nothing to mark whether those last three stand as examples by themselves or are further kinds of work that a multimedia presentation presents.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a page of a graphic novel and says what the dark colours and the close-up panel add that the words alone do not say. The child listens to a recorded reading of a poem with music, then reads the poem silently, and says how the music changed the mood.

### Limits

Three things an element can add to are named: meaning, tone and beauty. The bracketed items are examples, not a closed list. They are five items separated by commas with no grouping marked, so it is open whether the poem, the myth and the folktale are texts in their own right or, like fiction, things a multimedia presentation is of. The record sits in the literature strand.

### Common mistakes

Children describe what a picture shows and do not say what it adds to the text. They treat illustrations as decoration and skip them. They cannot separate tone, the feeling the work gives, from the events shown.
