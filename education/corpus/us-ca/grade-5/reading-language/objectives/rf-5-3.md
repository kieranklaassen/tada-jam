## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child has the phonics and word-analysis skills expected at this grade and uses them when decoding words. In particular, the child reads unfamiliar words of several syllables accurately, inside a text and standing alone, by putting together everything they know about letter-sound correspondences, about syllable patterns, and about morphology such as affixes and roots.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child meets "unpredictable" for the first time, splits it into un, pre, dict, able, says each part, and blends the parts into the word. The child reads a list of long words aloud with no sentence around them, then reads words of the same kind inside a paragraph. When a first try does not sound like a word, the child moves the syllable break and tries again.

### Limits

The words are unfamiliar and have more than one syllable. They are read both inside a text and standing alone. The statement asks for all letter-sound correspondences to be in use, so no subset of sounds is singled out. Roots and affixes are an example of morphology; no list is given. The statement is about reading words accurately, not about spelling them.

### Common mistakes

Children guess a long word from its first syllable and the context instead of reading every part ("constitution" for "construction"). They leave out a middle syllable. They break a word at the wrong place and do not try a second break when the result is not a word they know.
