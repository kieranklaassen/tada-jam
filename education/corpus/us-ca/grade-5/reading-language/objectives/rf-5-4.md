## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reads accurately and fluently enough to understand what is read. The child reads text at grade level knowing why they are reading it and understanding it. Reading grade-level poetry and prose aloud, the child is accurate, keeps a fitting rate and reads with expression, and this holds over repeated readings. The child uses the surrounding text to check the words they have read and what they have understood, corrects themselves where either is wrong, and rereads when that is necessary.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a page of a grade 5 novel aloud at a talking pace, pausing at commas and changing voice for a question. The child reads the same poem a second and a third time and sounds smoother each time. The child reads "He wound the bandage", hears that the first pronunciation does not fit, goes back, and reads it again correctly.

### Limits

Texts are at grade level. Oral reading covers both prose and poetry and is described over successive readings, not one first reading. The statement sets no words-per-minute figure. The opening line names comprehension as what accuracy and fluency are for.

### Common mistakes

Children read fast with few errors but cannot say what the passage was about. They read aloud in a flat voice that ignores punctuation. They read on after a misread word that makes no sense instead of going back.
