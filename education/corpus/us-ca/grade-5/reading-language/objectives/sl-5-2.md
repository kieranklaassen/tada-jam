## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

After a written text has been read aloud to them, or after taking in information from different media and formats, the child gives a summary of it. Three ways of presenting such information are named as included among those media and formats: spoken, shown visually, and given as quantities.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The teacher reads a news article aloud once, and the child then says its main points in three or four sentences without looking at the text. The child watches a short video with a chart in it and tells a partner what the chart showed and what the speaker said about it.

### Limits

The source is either a text in writing that is read aloud, or information that comes in varied media and formats. Visual, quantitative and oral presentation are named as included among these, so other media and formats are not excluded. The statement sets no length for the source or the summary and does not say whether the summary is spoken or written.

### Common mistakes

Children retell the last thing they heard and forget the beginning. They report details from a video and leave out the main point. They read numbers off a chart without saying what the numbers show.
