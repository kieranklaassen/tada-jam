## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child compares two or more texts by the overall structure in which each sets out its events, ideas, concepts or information, for example cause and effect, problem and solution, time order, or comparison. The child says how the structures are alike and how they differ.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads two articles about the same flood: one tells the events hour by hour, the other states the problem and then the ways the town solved it. The child labels each article's structure and underlines the signal words that show it, such as "first" and "next" or "as a result". The child explains how the two articles are organised differently.

### Limits

At least two texts are compared. The comparison is about overall structure. Four structures are given as examples (problem/solution, cause/effect, comparison, chronology); the list is not closed.

### Common mistakes

Children compare what the texts say instead of how they are organised. They name a structure from a single signal word in one sentence, although the text as a whole is built another way. They confuse cause/effect with problem/solution.
