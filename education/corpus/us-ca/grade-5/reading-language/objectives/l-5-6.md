## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child learns general academic words and subject-specific words and phrases suited to the grade, and uses them accurately. Among them are words that mark a logical relationship such as contrast or addition; the examples given are moreover, although, similarly, however, in addition and nevertheless.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a science explanation and uses "evaporate" and "condense" with their exact meanings. The child joins two facts that disagree with "however" or "although", and adds a further reason with "in addition". In a discussion the child says "similarly" when linking a classmate's point to their own.

### Limits

Words are limited to those suited to the grade; six example linking words are given, and no word list or count. Two kinds of word are named: general academic words, and words that belong to one subject. Both learning the words and using them accurately are asked for.

### Common mistakes

Children use a contrast word where there is no contrast ("It rained; however, we got wet"). They treat "although" like "however" and write half a sentence ("Although it was late"). They use a new academic word in a sentence that shows they know only roughly what it means.
