## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When the child explains what a text states outright, or what they infer from it, they quote the text's words accurately.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads an article about volcanoes and answers "How do scientists know an eruption is coming?" by copying the exact sentence that says so, inside quotation marks. For a question the article does not answer directly, the child states an inference and points to the quoted words that led to it. The child checks the quotation against the page word by word.

### Limits

This record sits in the informational-text strand; the same wording stands in the literature strand as a separate record. Accurate quotation is asked for in two cases: explicit statements and inferences. The statement sets no number of quotations and does not mention citing a page or a source.

### Common mistakes

Children paraphrase loosely and put quotation marks around the paraphrase, or change words while copying. They quote a sentence that is on the topic but does not support the point they are making. They state an inference with no words from the text behind it.
