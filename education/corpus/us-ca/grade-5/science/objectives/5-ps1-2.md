## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student measures quantities and graphs them to give evidence that the total weight of matter stays the same whatever kind of change happens when substances are mixed, cooled or heated. The clarification's examples of changes are mixing that forms new substances, dissolving, and changes of phase.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student weighs a cup of water and a spoonful of salt apart, stirs them together, and weighs the mixture. The student weighs ice in a closed bag, lets it melt, and weighs it again. The student puts the before and after weights in a table, draws paired bars, and compares them.

### Limits

The assessment boundary does not ask students to tell mass from weight. The changes named in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Expecting the weight to drop when something dissolves or melts. Leaving the container open during a change that gives off gas, then concluding that weight was lost. Counting the container in one weighing and not in the other.
