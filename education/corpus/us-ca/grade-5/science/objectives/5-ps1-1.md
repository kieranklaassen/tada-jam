## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student develops a model describing matter as made up of particles so small they cannot be seen. The clarification's examples of evidence for such a model are salt water evaporating, sugar dissolving in water, air squeezed in a syringe, and a basketball swelling as air is pumped in.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student blocks the tip of a syringe full of air, pushes the plunger, and feels it give and spring back. The student stirs sugar into water until it cannot be seen, and leaves salt water in a dish until crystals are left. The student draws dots to show where the sugar, salt or air is before and after.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out explaining evaporation and condensation at the scale of atoms, and leaves out saying what the unseen particles are. The four pieces of evidence in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Thinking dissolved sugar has gone because it cannot be seen. Thinking air is nothing and has no particles. Drawing the particles of a liquid inside a continuous background of that same liquid, as if the substance sits between its own particles.
