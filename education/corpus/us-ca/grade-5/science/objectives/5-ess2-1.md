## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student develops a model, built around an example, that describes ways in which Earth's atmosphere, biosphere, geosphere and hydrosphere, or some of them, act on one another. The clarification says each of the four is a system and a part of the whole Earth system, and that the hydrosphere includes ice. Its examples: mountain ranges affecting clouds and winds; the atmosphere affecting ecosystems and landforms through climate and weather; and the ocean affecting climate, the shape of landforms, and ecosystems.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student builds a tray model with a clay mountain, pours "rain" from a watering can, and watches where the water runs and what it carries off. The student draws a diagram with two labelled boxes, such as ocean and air, and arrows between them that say what passes or changes. The student explains the example aloud using the names of the two systems.

### Limits

The assessment boundary keeps each assessed interaction to a pair of systems. The clarification counts ice as part of the hydrosphere. The influences listed in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Sorting things into the four systems and stopping there, without showing how one affects another. Placing ice, clouds or soil in the wrong system. Drawing arrows without saying what each arrow means.
