## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student backs up an argument that the material a plant needs in order to grow comes mainly from water and air. The clarification stresses that plant matter is mostly from those two, and not from the soil.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student weighs a pot of dry soil, grows a bean plant in it for some weeks with only water added, then dries and weighs the soil again and weighs the plant. The student grows a seedling with its roots in water and no soil at all. The student uses the weights and the soil-free plant as evidence in a written argument.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. The clarification stresses air and water over soil. The wording says mainly, not only.

### Common mistakes

Believing plants eat soil and that soil is their food. Finding it hard to accept that air, which seems to weigh nothing, adds to what a plant is made of. Calling fertiliser "plant food" and treating it as the source of the plant's bulk.
