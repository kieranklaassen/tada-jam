## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student backs up an argument that Earth's gravity pulls objects downward. The clarification explains that "down" is a local word for the direction toward the centre of the round Earth.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student drops balls of different sizes, lets go of a weight on a string, and pours water, and records which way each goes. The student sticks small figures all around a globe and draws an arrow for "down" at each one. The student writes a claim and cites these observations as evidence.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out any mathematical description of the force of gravity.

### Common mistakes

Thinking "down" is one fixed direction in space, so that people on the far side of the globe would fall off. Believing there is no gravity above the air. Thinking gravity acts only while an object is falling, not while it rests on a table.
