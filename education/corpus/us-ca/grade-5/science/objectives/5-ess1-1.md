## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student backs up an argument that the sun appears brighter than other stars because of how near it is to Earth compared with them. The clarification says a star's absolute brightness depends on several factors, and that relative distance from Earth, one factor in how bright a star appears, is the one this expectation deals with.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a dark hall, the student holds one flashlight close and has a partner hold an identical one at the far end, and compares how bright each looks. The student moves the near light back step by step and notes how its brightness seems to change. The student uses these observations as evidence in a short written argument about the sun and the other stars.

### Limits

The assessment boundary keeps to relative distance. It leaves out the sizes of stars and the other things that affect how bright a star appears, and gives stellar mass, age and stage as cases.

### Common mistakes

Believing the sun is the biggest or most powerful star, and that this is why it looks brightest. Thinking stars are small because they look small. Not counting the sun as a star.
