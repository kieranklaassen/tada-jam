## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student develops a model that describes how matter moves between the environment, plants, animals and decomposers. The clarification stresses one idea: plants take matter that is not food (water, air, and broken-down material in soil) and make it into food. Its examples of systems are the Earth, ecosystems and organisms.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student draws a pond or a garden with a plant, a snail, a bird and fungi, and adds arrows showing what each takes in and gives back. The student keeps a compost bin or a sealed jar of leaves and soil and records how the leaves break down over weeks. The student traces one path around the drawing aloud, from soil and air to plant to animal and back to soil.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out explanations at the level of molecules. The three systems named in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Leaving decomposers out, so dead matter has nowhere to go in the model. Drawing arrows only for what eats what, and omitting air, water and soil. Thinking matter disappears when something rots.
