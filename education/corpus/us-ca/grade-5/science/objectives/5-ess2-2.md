## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student describes how much fresh water and how much salt water Earth's various reservoirs hold, graphs those amounts, and uses them as evidence of how water is spread over the planet.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student starts with 100 small squares standing for all of Earth's water and sets aside the few that stand for fresh water, then splits those again between ice, ground water and surface water. The student draws a bar graph of the amounts from a data table. The student states what the graph shows about where most of the water is.

### Limits

The assessment boundary lists the reservoirs that count: glaciers, polar ice caps, ground water, rivers, lakes and oceans. It leaves out water in the atmosphere. The department's grade document differs here: it asks for percentages as well as amounts, and it sets water in general beside fresh water where the export names salt water and fresh water.

### Common mistakes

Believing most fresh water is in lakes and rivers. Thinking fresh water is a large share of all water. Drawing bars with an uneven scale, or misreading a graph in which the ocean bar is far taller than the rest.
