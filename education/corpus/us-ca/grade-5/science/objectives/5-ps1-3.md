## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student observes and measures in order to tell which material is which from its properties. The clarification's examples of materials are liquids, minerals, metals, and powders such as baking soda; its examples of properties are solubility, response to a magnet, how well the material conducts heat or electricity, reflectivity, hardness and colour. It says density is not meant as a property to identify materials by.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student is given four unlabelled white powders or a set of metal strips and tests each one: scratches it, holds a magnet to it, puts it in a circuit with a bulb, stirs it into water. The student fills in a table of results and matches each unknown to a named material on a reference card.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out density and does not ask students to tell mass from weight. The clarification also says density is not an identifying property here. The materials and properties it lists are examples.

### Common mistakes

Identifying a material from one property, such as colour, when several materials share it. Guessing from appearance instead of testing. Thinking all metals are attracted to a magnet.
