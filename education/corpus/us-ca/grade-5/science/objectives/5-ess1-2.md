## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student puts data into graphs and charts that show patterns: how shadows change in length and direction through a day, the cycle of day and night, and which stars show in the night sky in which season. The clarification's examples of patterns are Earth's position and movement relative to the sun, and certain stars that can be seen only in some months.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student stands a stick upright in the yard, marks the tip of its shadow every hour, measures each length, and plots length against time of day. The student records sunrise and sunset times over some weeks in a table and draws a bar for each day. The student marks on a year chart the months in which a chosen constellation is visible in the evening.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out what causes the seasons. The patterns in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Expecting a shadow to point toward the sun. Plotting the points but not describing the pattern they show. Believing the stars themselves go away in some seasons.
