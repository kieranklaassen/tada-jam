## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student carries out an investigation to find out whether new substances form when two or more substances are mixed. The clarification's example of a mix that gives no new substance is sand with water; its examples of mixes that do are vinegar with milk and vinegar with baking soda.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student mixes pairs of household substances in clear cups and watches for bubbles, a colour change, a new solid, or a change in temperature felt through the cup. The student tries to get the starting substances back, for example by letting sand settle and pouring the water off. The student records for each pair whether something new formed and what the sign was.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. The three mixtures in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Counting dissolving as making a new substance because the solid can no longer be seen. Taking any colour change, such as two paints blending, as proof of a new substance. Thinking the bubbles are air that was already in the liquid.
