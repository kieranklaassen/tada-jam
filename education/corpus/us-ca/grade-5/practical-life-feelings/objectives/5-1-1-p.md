## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names things a person can do for their own health that work to lower the chance of getting sick or hurt. The examples given are washing hands, enough sleep, brushing and flossing teeth, protecting hearing, staying safe in the sun, and ergonomics.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old matches each habit to what it prevents: hand washing to colds and stomach infections, ear protection to hearing loss, a hat and sunscreen to sunburn, flossing to gum disease. The child adjusts a chair and a screen so the back is straight and the feet reach the floor, and carries a heavy school bag on both shoulders. The child says how many hours of sleep they get and whether it is enough.

### Limits

The six items in brackets are examples, not a full list. The statement asks the child to name strategies; carrying them out is covered by the practice statements of this content area (5.7.1.P to 5.7.3.P). It gives no number of hours of sleep and no other measure.

### Common mistakes

Children name the habit without knowing what it prevents. Many think illness comes only from germs and injuries only from accidents, so slow harm such as hearing loss from loud music or back strain is left out. Some think doing a thing once is enough, such as one application of sunscreen for a whole day.
