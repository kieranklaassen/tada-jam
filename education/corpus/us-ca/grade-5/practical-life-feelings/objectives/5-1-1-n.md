## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells what the groups of foods are and how much of each one a person is advised to eat.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old sorts the foods from yesterday's lunch and dinner into groups such as grains, vegetables, fruits, dairy and protein foods, and names each group. The child fills a plate or a set of measuring cups to show how much of each group is advised for a day. The child says which group takes up the most room and which the least.

### Limits

The statement does not name the groups or give the amounts, and it does not say whose recommendations are meant. Giving reasons for the different amounts is 5.1.4.N.

### Common mistakes

Children put a mixed dish such as pizza into a single group. Many place a food by its name or its look, so potato chips go with vegetables and fruit-flavoured drinks with fruit. Some confuse the amount advised for a whole day with what belongs on one plate.
