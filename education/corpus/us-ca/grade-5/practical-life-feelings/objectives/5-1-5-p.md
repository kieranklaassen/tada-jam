## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child says what makes an event a danger to someone's life. Poisoning, an asthma attack and a heart attack are the examples given.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old sorts cases into 'life in danger now' and 'needs care, but not an emergency': a person who cannot breathe, who has collapsed or who swallowed a cleaning product goes in the first pile, a grazed knee or a mild headache in the second. The child gives the signs that put each card in its pile, such as not breathing, not waking up, or lips turning blue. The child states the definition in their own words.

### Limits

The three items in brackets are examples, not a full list. The statement asks for a definition, not for first aid; getting help is covered by neighbouring statements (5.3.2.P, 5.4.1.P, 5.5.2.P).

### Common mistakes

Children judge by how much blood or crying there is, so a quiet person who has collapsed seems less serious than a loud child with a small cut. Many think only adults have emergencies. Some call every injury an emergency.
