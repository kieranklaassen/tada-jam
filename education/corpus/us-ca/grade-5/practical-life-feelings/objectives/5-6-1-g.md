## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names what a person can do to gain, and then keep, a body image that is realistic and healthy.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old writes a short list of actions: name three things their body can do, compare themselves with real people and not with edited pictures, stop joining in when classmates mock bodies, talk to a trusted adult when they feel bad about their looks. The child picks one action to try this week. The child describes themselves in plain, true words, without insulting themselves and without exaggerating.

### Limits

The statement belongs to the goal-setting standard and asks the child to name steps, not yet to carry them out or track them. It asks for an image that is both accurate and healthy, and lists no steps. How media and culture shape body image is a separate statement (5.2.1.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children name steps that change the body, such as dieting or building muscle, instead of steps that change how they see it. Many think a healthy body image means believing they look perfect. Some list wishes with no action they can take.
