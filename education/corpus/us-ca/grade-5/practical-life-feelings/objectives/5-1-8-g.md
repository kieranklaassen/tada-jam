## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that there is more than one way to show friendship, to show affection, and to show that one is attracted to someone.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old lists what people do when they like someone: kind words, a note, sharing, spending time, a hug, holding hands, helping. The child compares what they do for a best friend, for a grandparent and for someone they are attracted to, and notices that families and cultures differ. The child says which of these feel comfortable to them.

### Limits

The statement names three feelings and asks only that the child knows each can be shown in several ways. It lists none. Showing them in a healthy and respectful manner is the matching skill statement (5.4.2.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children think liking someone always looks the same, so they miss quiet signs such as helping or listening. Many confuse friendship with attraction and tease a boy and a girl who are simply friends. Some assume that what is normal in their own family, such as a kiss on the cheek, is welcome to everyone.
