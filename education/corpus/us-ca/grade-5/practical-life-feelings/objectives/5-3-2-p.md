## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names the people who are able to help with a health concern or with a condition that could put a life in danger. A seizure and an asthma episode are the examples given.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old lists who helps where: a teacher or the school nurse at school, a parent at home, a coach at practice, the emergency number anywhere. The child says which adult keeps a classmate's inhaler or knows the plan for a seizure. The child points out the nearest adult and the nearest telephone in three places where they spend time.

### Limits

The two items in brackets are examples. The statement asks the child to know who can help, not to give first aid. Asking for help is a separate skill statement (5.4.1.P), and deciding when medical help is needed is another (5.5.2.P).

### Common mistakes

Children think only of a parent and know nobody to ask when the parent is not there. Many do not know that any person, including a child, may call the emergency number. Some name a friend of the same age as the helper.
