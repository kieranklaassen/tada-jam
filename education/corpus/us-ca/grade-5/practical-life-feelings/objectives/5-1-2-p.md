## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child explains what happens in the immune system when bacteria or viruses get into the body, and what this does to a person's health.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old describes a cold step by step: a virus gets in through the nose, it multiplies, white blood cells fight it, fever and a runny nose show the fight, and the body recovers and remembers the virus. The child says one difference between the two kinds of germ, for example that antibiotics work on bacteria and not on viruses. The child says why rest, food and vaccines help the body defend itself.

### Limits

The statement names two kinds of germ and one body system. It does not name diseases, vaccines or medicines, and sets no depth for the workings of immunity.

### Common mistakes

Children think all germs are the same and that antibiotics cure a cold. Many believe the symptoms are the germ itself, not the body's response. Some think all bacteria are harmful.
