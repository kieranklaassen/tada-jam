## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sees that what people eat depends partly on their family and their culture.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old tells what their family eats on an ordinary evening and at a celebration, and who decides and cooks. The child compares this with two classmates and finds one food that all three eat and one that only one family eats. The child says where a family dish comes from: a country, a religion, a grandparent's recipe.

### Limits

The statement names two influences, family and culture, and is about food only, not physical activity. It asks for recognising them and says nothing about which way of eating is better.

### Common mistakes

Children take their own family's meals as what everyone eats and call other food strange. Many think culture means only holidays and miss everyday habits such as meal times and who eats together. Some believe they choose all their food themselves.
