## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child explains how the reproductive system of the human body is built, names its main parts, and says what the system and its parts do.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old labels a plain line drawing of the male and of the female reproductive organs with their correct names. The child points to a part and says in a sentence what it does, for example where egg cells are stored or where sperm cells are made. The child uses the anatomical words, not slang.

### Limits

The statement asks for the main parts, how they are arranged and what they do; it does not list which parts count as main. The life-cycle statement (5.1.1.G) names birth and reproduction; it does not name pregnancy. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children confuse parts whose names sound alike, such as the uterus and the urethra. Many think urine and menstrual blood leave a girl's body through the same opening. Some know slang words only and do not connect them to the correct names.
