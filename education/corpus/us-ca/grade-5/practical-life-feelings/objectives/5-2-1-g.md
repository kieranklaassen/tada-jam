## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child explains how people's views of gender roles, of their own bodies, and of what counts as attractive come to be shaped by the media, by culture, and by other influences.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old looks at advertisements, a film poster and a toy catalogue and points out what kind of body is shown and what boys and girls are shown doing. The child compares this with real people they know and says what is missing or changed, such as edited photos. The child names another influence, such as family, friends or tradition, and says what it has taught them to find good-looking.

### Limits

The statement names two influences and leaves the rest open. It asks for an explanation of the influence, not for the child to change their own view. Steps toward a healthy body image are a separate statement (5.6.1.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children take pictures in the media as unedited and typical. Many say 'adverts do not affect me' while repeating the ideals the adverts show. Some see the media as the only influence and overlook family and friends.
