## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names what changes in puberty: in the body, in feelings, and in how a young person gets along with others.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old sorts cards with changes into three piles: body, feelings, and friends and family. The child puts 'growing taller' and 'hair under the arms' with the body, 'mood swings' with feelings, and 'wanting more privacy' on the social pile. The child adds one change of their own to each pile.

### Limits

The statement names three kinds of change and asks the child only to name them. It does not list the changes or say how many. How they affect thinking and behaviour is a separate statement (5.2.3.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children list body changes and leave out the emotional and social ones. Many think a change belongs to one sex only, for example that only boys' voices change or only girls grow body hair. Some expect every change to arrive at once.
