## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child says what a sexually transmitted disease (STD) is. The definition takes in HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) and AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old says in one or two sentences that an STD is an infection passed from one person to another through sexual contact. The child writes out what the letters HIV and AIDS stand for and says that HIV is a virus and AIDS is the illness it can cause when it is not treated. The child sorts HIV with the STDs and keeps colds and flu in another group.

### Limits

The statement asks for a definition and names HIV and AIDS as the two that must be covered. It names no other disease and asks nothing here about prevention or treatment; how HIV passes between people is the next statement (5.1.5.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children treat HIV and AIDS as two names for one thing. Many think any illness that spreads between people is an STD, or that an STD spreads like a cold. Some believe only adults or only certain groups of people can get one.
