## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells how a person can avoid catching a serious disease that passes from one person to another through blood.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old says what to do when a classmate has a nosebleed or a cut knee: do not touch the blood, fetch an adult, and let the adult use gloves. The child says never to pick up a needle or syringe found on the ground and never to share anything that pierces skin, and that a cut should be covered with a plaster. The child washes hands with soap after helping with first aid.

### Limits

The statement belongs to the standard on practising behaviours but asks only for a description. It names no disease; HIV is named in neighbouring statements (5.1.4.G, 5.1.5.G). It does not list the ways. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children want to help a bleeding friend with bare hands. Many think such diseases spread through air or touch, so they avoid the person instead of the blood. Some think a small amount of dried blood or a found needle is harmless.
