## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells what good it does a person to eat a balanced diet, one that follows today's dietary guidelines grounded in research.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old names what a balanced diet does for the body: energy for school and play, growth of bones and muscles, fewer illnesses, a healthy heart later in life. The child links a benefit to a food group, for example calcium in dairy foods and strong bones. The child compares a day of meals with a food guide and says what that day gives the body and what is missing.

### Limits

The statement points to current guidelines that rest on research and does not name them or list the benefits. It asks for description only.

### Common mistakes

Children name only 'being strong' or 'not getting fat' and leave out energy, growth and protection from illness. Many think balanced means equal amounts of every food. Some believe one healthy food makes up for the rest of the day.
