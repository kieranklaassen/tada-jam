## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that people differ from one another in how they grow and mature, in how they look, and in gender roles.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old looks at pictures of children of the same age and names differences without ranking them: height, build, skin, hair, who has started to change and who has not. The child gives examples of people whose work or interests some call 'for boys' or 'for girls', such as a father who cooks or a girl who plays football. The child says that none of these differences makes a person better or worse.

### Limits

The statement names three areas of difference and asks the child to know they exist, not to explain their causes. It sets no list of differences. That puberty itself varies widely is stated separately (5.1.9.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children take their own body or the tallest classmate as the standard and read any difference as something wrong. Many treat gender roles as fixed rules about what boys and girls may do. Some confuse a difference in looks with a difference in health.
