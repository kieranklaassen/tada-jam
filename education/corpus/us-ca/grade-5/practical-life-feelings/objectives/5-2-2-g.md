## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells what part heredity plays in the way a person grows and develops.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old compares their own height, eye colour, hair and build with parents, grandparents or siblings, or does the same for a family in a story. The child says which features are shared within the family and which, such as a scar or a haircut, are not inherited. The child says that the age at which a parent started puberty gives an indication of their own timing.

### Limits

The statement asks for a description of the effect of inherited traits on a growing child. It does not mention genes, DNA or the mechanism of inheritance. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children expect to become an exact copy of one parent, usually the parent of the same sex. Many think the body is decided by inheritance alone and leave out food, sleep and activity, or the reverse. Some think a trait that a parent does not show cannot come from a grandparent.
