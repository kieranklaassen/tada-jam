## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells how a person's life runs from start to finish: people are born, grow up, grow old and die, and by having children they begin a new life.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old puts pictures of a baby, a child, a teenager, an adult and an old person in order and says what changes at each step. The child draws the stages as a circle and marks where a new baby starts the circle again. Asked about a grandparent or a pet that died, the child says that dying is the last stage of every life.

### Limits

The statement names five stages and asks only for a description. It sets no depth for any stage; how the reproductive system works is a neighbouring statement (5.1.2.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children often draw a line that stops at death and leave out that having children starts a new life. Many skip old age and move from adult straight to death. Some think people die only through accident or illness, not as a part of every life.
