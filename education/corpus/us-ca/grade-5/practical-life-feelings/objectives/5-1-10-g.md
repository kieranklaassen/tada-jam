## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names the body-care habits that puberty makes necessary and the matters of health and safety that come with it. The statement's examples are deodorant, taking showers, athletic supporters, and using sanitary products.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old matches each change to a care habit: more sweat to a daily shower and deodorant, oilier skin to washing the face, a first period to pads and how to change and throw them away, sport to a supporter or a sports bra. The child packs a small bag with what they would need on a school day. The child says why each item is needed.

### Limits

The four items in brackets are examples, not a full list. The statement asks the child to name the habits, not to carry them out; making a hygiene plan is a separate statement (5.6.2.G). It does not say which safety matters are meant beyond the examples. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children keep the washing routine of a younger child and do not notice body odour on themselves. Many think deodorant replaces washing. Some think menstrual products are a subject for girls only and learn nothing about them.
