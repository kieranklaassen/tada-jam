## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows how to say no in a way that keeps their own limits safe.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old stands straight, looks at the other person and says 'No, stop, I don't want that' in a firm voice. When the other person continues, the child repeats the no, steps away and goes to tell a trusted adult. In role play the child practises this with a friend who keeps insisting, an older child and an adult.

### Limits

The statement asks the child to show the skill, not only to describe it. It does not list the refusal skills or the situations. That every person has a right to boundaries is a knowledge statement in this lane (5.1.7.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children say no while smiling or giggling, so the no is not believed. Many give in after the second or third request, or think refusing a friend or an adult is rude. Some forget the last step, telling an adult.
