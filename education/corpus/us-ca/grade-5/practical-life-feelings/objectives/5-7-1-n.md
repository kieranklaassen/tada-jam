## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names ways of picking snacks that are good for health, going by today's guidelines that rest on research.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old names practical checks for picking a snack: look for a fruit, a vegetable or a whole grain first, read the sugar line on the label, drink water with it, take a portion and put the package away. The child applies the checks to the snacks in a lunch box or on a shop shelf and says which pass. The child plans three snacks for the coming week.

### Limits

The statement points to current guidelines that rest on research and does not name them. It asks for ways of choosing; telling more nutritious snacks from less nutritious ones is 5.1.6.N.

### Common mistakes

Children take the claim on the front of the package as the guideline. Many know the checks and still choose by habit or by what friends eat. Some think a healthy snack must be one they dislike.
