## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When the child shows friendship, affection or attraction, the child does so with respect for the other person and in a healthy manner.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old asks before hugging, gives a compliment about what someone did instead of about their body, and writes a kind note instead of teasing. The child accepts a 'no thanks' without sulking or insisting. In role play the child replaces an unkind way of getting attention, such as pulling hair or spreading a rumour, with a respectful one.

### Limits

The statement names three feelings and asks the child to act, not only to know. It does not define what is healthy or respectful. Knowing that these feelings can be shown in several ways is the matching knowledge statement (5.1.8.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children show liking by teasing, chasing or grabbing and read the other child's protest as part of the play. Many do not ask first and assume a hug is always welcome. Some tell the class whom a friend secretly likes.
