## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells which ways HIV passes from one person to another and which kinds of contact cannot pass it on.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old sorts cards into two piles. In the pile 'can pass HIV' go contact with infected blood, sex, shared needles, and passing from a mother to her baby; in the pile 'cannot' go hugging, sharing a desk, a drinking fountain, coughing and mosquito bites. The child gives the reason for one card in each pile.

### Limits

The statement covers both sides: the routes that spread the virus and the everyday contact that does not. It lists no routes itself and asks only for a description. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children think HIV spreads like a cold, through coughing, touching, sharing food or using the same toilet. Many believe mosquitoes carry it. Some think a person can tell who has the virus by looking.
