## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Given drinks and snacks, the child tells which ones nourish the body more and which less.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old lays out water, milk, soda, fruit juice, an apple, crackers and candy and sorts them into two rows. The child compares pairs, for example plain milk with a milkshake, and says what makes one the better choice: less added sugar, more vitamins, more fibre. The child checks a guess against the label.

### Limits

The statement covers drinks and snacks only, not meals, and gives no rule for what makes one more nutritious. It asks for telling them apart; ways of choosing a snack are 5.7.1.N.

### Common mistakes

Children trust words on the package such as 'fruit' or 'natural' and class fruit-flavoured drinks with fruit. Many judge by taste, so anything sweet is bad and anything plain is good. Some sort each item as healthy or unhealthy and miss that the task is a comparison.
