## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out why friendship with a person who has HIV or AIDS puts the child in no danger.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old lists what friends do together: sit side by side, share a ball, eat lunch, hug, play. For each one the child checks it against the routes by which HIV spreads and concludes that none of these passes the virus. The child then says in their own words why a classmate with HIV can be a friend like any other.

### Limits

The statement belongs to the decision-making standard and asks for reasons, not only the conclusion. It builds on the neighbouring statement about the spread of HIV (5.1.5.G). It names only HIV and AIDS. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children say there is no danger and cannot give a reason. Many still believe that sharing food, a toilet or a hug carries a risk. Some forget that the one rule that remains, not touching another person's blood, applies with every friend.
