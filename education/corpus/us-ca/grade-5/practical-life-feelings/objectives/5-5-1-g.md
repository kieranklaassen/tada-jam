## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells why it matters for a person to know where their own limits lie.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old thinks about what feels fine and what does not: who may hug them, who may read their diary, which jokes about them are not fine. The child says what can go wrong when a person has not thought about this, such as going along with something and feeling bad later. The child says that knowing the limit beforehand makes a decision quicker.

### Limits

The statement belongs to the decision-making standard and asks why, not for a list of the child's own limits. It names no kinds of boundary. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children think a boundary only matters with strangers and not with friends or relatives. Many notice a limit only after it has been crossed. Some describe rules set by adults instead of their own limits.
