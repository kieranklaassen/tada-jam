## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child goes step by step through a decision to work out whether a situation calls for medical help.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old gets short cases: a paper cut, a twisted ankle that swells, a friend who is wheezing and cannot speak, a bee sting on someone with an allergy. For each the child asks the same questions in order: is the person breathing and awake, is it getting worse, can I handle it with a plaster or rest, do I need an adult, do I need the emergency number. The child says the decision and the reason.

### Limits

The statement asks for a process, not a memorised list of emergencies. It does not spell out the steps or say what kind of medical help. What counts as life-threatening is defined under 5.1.5.P.

### Common mistakes

Children wait to see whether a problem goes away because they fear overreacting. Many decide by how the person looks from outside and miss breathing trouble or an allergic reaction. Some call for emergency help for a minor injury that an adult nearby could handle.
