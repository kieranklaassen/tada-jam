## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names where trustworthy facts about products and services for a person's own health can be found.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old picks a product, such as a sunscreen or a toothpaste, and finds out about it from the label, a pharmacist, a school nurse or a dentist, and a public health website. The child compares that with what the advert claims. For a service, such as an eye test, the child says who to ask and where it is offered.

### Limits

The statement covers both products and services and asks only that the child can name valid sources. It gives no list of sources and no test of validity.

### Common mistakes

Children treat an advert or the front of the packaging as neutral information. Many trust a product because a famous person or a friend uses it. Some know where to ask about products and have no idea where to ask about services.
