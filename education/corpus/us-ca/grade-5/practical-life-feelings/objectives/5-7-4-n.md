## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows they can judge how active they themselves are.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old measures their own activity: counts minutes of movement in a day, takes a pulse at the wrist before and after running, and notes whether they could still talk while moving. The child rates each activity as light, moderate or hard. The child adds up the week and says whether it is a lot, some or little.

### Limits

The statement names no method of measuring and no target amount. It is about the child's own activity, not about comparing children.

### Common mistakes

Children count the whole length of a practice or a recess as active time, including standing and waiting. Many judge by whether they sweated. Some overestimate a week they recall from memory.
