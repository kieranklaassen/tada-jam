## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells apart places to learn about puberty that can be trusted from those that cannot.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old gets a set of sources: a health textbook, a school nurse, a rumour from the playground, an advert for a skin product, an anonymous video. The child sorts them into 'trust' and 'check first' and gives a reason for each, such as who wrote it, whether the person is trained, and whether something is being sold. The child takes one doubtful claim to a trusted adult to check.

### Limits

The statement is limited to puberty as the subject and asks the child to sort sources both ways. It gives no list of sources and no criteria. Knowing which adults to ask is a neighbouring statement (5.3.1.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children judge a source by how confident or popular it is, not by who is behind it. Many trust what an older child says because the older child has already had these changes. Some treat everything online as equally true, or equally false.
