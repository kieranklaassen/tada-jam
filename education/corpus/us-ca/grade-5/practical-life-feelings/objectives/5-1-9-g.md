## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child explains that wide differences between people in puberty and in how the body develops are normal.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old looks at a chart of the ages at which changes can begin and sees that two healthy children can start several years apart. The child explains to a worried classmate, in their own words, that starting early or late, or growing fast or slowly, is within the usual range. The child says who to ask when they are still unsure.

### Limits

The statement gives no ages or ranges. It asks for an explanation, not only for knowing the fact. Differences between people in growth and looks in general are a neighbouring statement (5.1.6.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children take the first or the last classmate to change as a sign that something is wrong with that classmate or with themselves. Many think boys and girls start at the same age. Some think that starting later means ending up smaller or less developed as an adult.
