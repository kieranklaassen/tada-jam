## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child talks about what puberty does to the way a young person thinks, feels and acts.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old talks in a small group about a short story in which an eleven-year-old answers a parent sharply, feels shy in the changing room or suddenly cares what classmates think. The child links each reaction to puberty: hormones and a changing body can bring mood swings, self-consciousness and a wish for privacy. The child says what the character could do or who the character could talk to.

### Limits

The statement asks for discussion, so the child speaks and listens and does not only list. It names three areas that are affected and gives no examples. Naming the changes themselves is a separate statement (5.1.3.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children describe body changes again and never reach feelings or actions. Many blame every bad mood on puberty, or none. Some think strong feelings mean something is wrong with them.
