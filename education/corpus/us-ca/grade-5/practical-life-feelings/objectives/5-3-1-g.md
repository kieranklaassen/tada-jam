## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child knows that questions about puberty can be taken to a parent, a guardian, or another adult the child trusts.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old writes down three adults they could ask about body changes, such as a parent, an aunt, the school nurse or a teacher. The child says what makes each one trustworthy and which question they would take to which adult. The child compares what such an adult would say with what a classmate or a video might say.

### Limits

The statement names parents and guardians and leaves open who else counts as a trusted adult. It is about knowing whom to ask; holding the conversation is a separate statement (5.4.1.G), and judging sources is another (5.3.2.G). A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children ask friends, older siblings or the internet first because asking an adult feels embarrassing. Many think only a parent of the same sex can be asked. Some assume adults do not want to be asked.
