## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that setting one's own limits toward other people (personal boundaries) is a right, and that every person holds it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old says which kinds of touch, closeness and questions are fine for them and which are not, for example 'I don't like being hugged without being asked'. The child listens when a classmate states a different limit and repeats it back. The child says that a younger child, a friend and an adult each hold the same right.

### Limits

The statement is about knowing the right exists and that it belongs to all people. Saying no to protect a boundary is a separate skill statement (5.4.3.G), and why knowing one's boundaries matters is another (5.5.1.G). The statement does not list kinds of boundaries. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children apply the right to themselves and forget that others hold it too, so they keep hugging or teasing a friend who said stop. Many believe an adult or an older child can overrule their limit about their own body. Some think a limit once given up cannot be set again later.
