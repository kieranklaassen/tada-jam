## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child talks with a parent, a guardian or another adult they trust about what changes in puberty, and does so with good communication skills.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A ten-year-old practises opening a conversation with a trusted adult: choosing a quiet moment, saying 'I have a question about my body', and asking it in clear words. The child listens to the answer, asks a second question when something is unclear, and says thank you. In role play the child tries this once as the one who asks and once as the adult.

### Limits

The statement names the listeners (a parent, a guardian, another adult the child trusts) and the subject (what changes in puberty). It does not say which communication skills count as effective. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children hint or joke and never ask the real question. Many use vague words such as 'stuff' or 'down there', so the adult cannot tell what they mean. Some stop after the first awkward moment and do not ask the second question.
