## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children are beginning, with adult support, to manage three things: habitual reactions (habits that do not help in the situation at hand), impulsive behaviour (doing something on a whim), and delaying gratification (not giving in to a reward that could be had at once). At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they manage the same three with less adult support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child, reminded by an adult, keeps both hands in the lap while a snack is passed around, or stops at the door before running indoors where walking is the rule. An older child waits for a turn on the slide without a reminder, raises a hand instead of calling out, and leaves a treat on the plate until everyone is served.

### Limits

The statements name three things to manage and explain each in brackets. They set no waiting time and no number of occasions. At the earlier age the ability is described as emerging. Adult support is part of both statements. What changes with age: the ability is no longer called emerging, and less adult support is needed.

### Common mistakes

A child can say the rule ("we wait") and still grab the item. Waiting fails more often when the reward stays in view. A habit from one place, such as shouting outdoors, is carried into a place where it does not fit.
