## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages children try to change the way they are tackling a problem after thinking over how their present way is working. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), they do this with adult support. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they need less of it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child pushing a puzzle piece into the wrong space stops when an adult asks "is that working?", looks at the shape and turns the piece. An older child pouring sand through a funnel that keeps clogging says "it's too wet" and fetches dry sand.

### Limits

The statements speak of attempts, so a successful change is not required at either age. They name no kind of problem and no number of tries. Adult support is part of both statements. What changes with age: only the amount of adult support, which is less at the later age.

### Common mistakes

A child repeats the same action with more force instead of changing it. Children blame the material ("it's broken") and do not look at what they did. A child leaves the problem instead of adjusting the approach.
