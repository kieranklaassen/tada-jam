## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children notice, with adult support, how their body reacts when they are physically active (thirst, sweat and a fast heartbeat are the examples given), and they show that they need to move, whether inside or outside. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they notice these reactions with less or limited adult support and are beginning to understand that it is healthy to be active. They also show their need to move, inside or outside, with more consistency and less help from an adult.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child stops after running, puts a hand on the chest when an adult says "feel your heart", asks for water, and on a rainy day jumps on the spot or says "I want to run". An older child says "my heart is going fast" or "I'm sweaty" without being asked, fetches the water bottle, and says that running makes the body strong.

### Limits

The body reactions listed are examples. Adult support is stated at both ages, as less or limited at the later age. The idea that activity is healthy appears only at the later age, and there as an emerging understanding. No amount or kind of activity is stated. What changes with age: less adult support is needed, a first understanding that activity is healthy is added, and the child signals the need to move more consistently.

### Common mistakes

A child takes a fast heartbeat or being out of breath as a sign that something is wrong. Children do not connect thirst or feeling hot with the running they have just done. A restless child shows the need to move by pushing or by climbing on furniture, not by saying so.
