## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children do their part in keeping a good relationship with their attachment figures (the adults who care for them, teachers possibly among them) and with other adults they know, and they are starting to be aware of how those adults feel, what they like and how they are. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), children do their part in good two-sided relationships and in cooperating with the same adults, and they take an interest in the adults' feelings, likes and well-being.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child brings a drawing to a teacher, helps to carry cups to the table when asked, and looks at the adult's face when the adult says "I'm tired today". An older child asks "are you feeling better?", saves a seat for a grandparent, offers the teacher the colour the teacher likes, and works with the adult to tidy the shelf.

### Limits

Both statements cover attachment figures, explained in brackets in the wording, and familiar adults; unfamiliar adults are not mentioned. Cooperation and mutual relationships are named only at the later age. Neither statement mentions adult support. What changes with age: the relationship becomes more mutual and cooperative, and a first awareness of how the adults feel and what they prefer becomes an interest in it.

### Common mistakes

A child assumes that the adult feels what the child feels and likes what the child likes. Children see the adult only as someone who gives help, and do not notice when the adult is busy, tired or unwell. A child shows care in a way that suits a child, such as offering a toy to a sad adult.
