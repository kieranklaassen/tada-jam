## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children show that they understand sharing and being fair to other people, whether those people are like them or unlike them, though an adult sometimes has to prompt them to share. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they share and treat others fairly and respectfully more consistently and with less prompting from an adult, and they point out unfairness when it happens in play or in daily activities.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child hands over half of the play dough when a teacher says "can Sam have some?", and gives a turn on the trike to a child from another group. An older child deals out the cards so that each player has the same number, waits for a turn, and says "that's not fair, she hasn't had a go" when someone is left out.

### Limits

The earlier statement speaks of understanding and itself says that some adult prompting is needed for sharing. Respect and speaking up about unfairness appear only at the later age. Adult prompting is part of both statements. No rule of fair division, such as equal shares, is stated. What changes with age: sharing and fair treatment become more consistent and need less prompting, and the child begins to name unfairness.

### Common mistakes

Children take "fair" to mean getting what they want, or getting the same as others whatever the need. A child shares only with friends and not with children who seem different. Children speak up about unfairness when they get less and stay silent when they get more.
