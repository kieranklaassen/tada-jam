## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children let an adult know when they feel unwell or uncomfortable or have a special health need, though how exact and how accurate the message is varies. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they let an adult know about these things consistently and can identify something that would help.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child tugs an adult's sleeve, holds the tummy and says "hurts", or says "I feel yucky" when the trouble is a sore throat. An older child says "my ear hurts on this side" or "I need my inhaler", and adds what would help, such as lying down or a drink of water.

### Limits

The statements cover three things: discomfort, feeling unwell, and a special health need. In both statements the message goes to an adult. The earlier statement itself allows that the message may be vague or inaccurate. Identifying a solution appears only at the later age. What changes with age: the child lets the adult know consistently, and can identify a solution.

### Common mistakes

A child places most complaints in the tummy, wherever the discomfort is. Children show discomfort by crying, clinging or going quiet, and say nothing. A child keeps playing and reports the problem only when asked.
