## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children identify basic emotions (surprised, sad and happy are the examples) and recognise how emotions show, in themselves and in other people. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they identify complex emotions as well as basic ones (pride and embarrassment are the examples) and still recognise how emotions show in themselves and in other people. They increasingly understand that there are different ways to express an emotion, and which behaviours go with emotions, in their own case and in that of others.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child points to the smiling face on a card and says "happy", says "I'm sad" when a parent leaves, and notices that a friend is crying. An older child says "he's embarrassed because he fell", makes a proud face in the mirror, and says that one friend goes quiet when angry while another shouts.

### Limits

The emotions listed are examples; the statements set no number of emotions. Complex emotions appear only at the later age. So does the understanding that an emotion can be expressed in more than one way and is linked with behaviour. Neither statement mentions adult support. What changes with age: complex emotions are added to basic ones, and the child understands more about the varied ways in which emotions are expressed.

### Common mistakes

Children confuse emotions that look alike on a face, most often fear with surprise, and anger with disgust or sadness. A child reads the emotion from the situation alone ("it's her birthday, so she is happy") and overlooks the face. Children believe that a person can feel only one emotion at a time.
