## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children handle daily routines and changes from one place to another (home to child care is the example) or from one activity to another (play to story time is the example), with adult support. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they know which routine is coming and handle changes of place (home to school is the example) or of activity (snack to small-group work is the example), with some adult support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child hangs up a coat on arrival after an adult points to the hook, and puts blocks in the bin when the clean-up song starts and an adult helps. An older child says "after snack we go outside", finishes the drawing when told that clean-up is soon, and moves to the rug after a brief reminder.

### Limits

The settings and activities named are examples. Anticipating routines appears only at the later age. Adult support is part of both statements: support at the earlier age, some support at the later age. No number of routines or transitions is stated. What changes with age: the child begins to anticipate routines, and the adult support needed is reduced to some.

### Common mistakes

A child keeps playing after the signal: stopping a liked activity is harder than starting the next one. Children manage a transition in its usual order and become upset when the order changes. A child knows what comes next and still needs help with the steps in between, such as putting things away.
