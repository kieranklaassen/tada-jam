## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children know part of the sequence for brushing teeth and carry out those steps while an adult supervises and instructs. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they know and carry out more of the steps and know at what times teeth are to be brushed, with limited supervision and instruction from an adult.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child holds the brush, lets an adult put on the paste, brushes the front teeth and spits, while the adult says what comes next and finishes the back teeth. An older child brushes front, back and chewing surfaces, spits and rinses the brush, and says when teeth are brushed, such as before bed.

### Limits

The statements do not list the steps, the number of steps, or a brushing time. The foundations document notes, beneath the two statements, that the routine uses a soft-bristled brush with fluoride toothpaste in an amount about the size of a pea, and two minutes of brushing that covers the outer, inner and chewing surfaces of the teeth; it gives a University of California child care health programme as its source. Adult supervision and instruction are part of both statements, limited at the later age; brushing with no adult present is not stated at either age. Knowing when to brush appears only at the later age, and the statement does not say which times. What changes with age: more steps are known and followed, knowing when to brush is added, and adult supervision and instruction become limited.

### Common mistakes

Brushing only the front teeth that show in the mirror. Chewing or sucking the brush instead of moving it over the teeth. Swallowing the paste instead of spitting it out.
