## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children have friendly exchanges with adults they know, most of all in places they know, and are beginning to be able to start such contact themselves. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they take more initiative and take part in more two-way exchanges with adults they know; asking for help, proposing something to do together and opening a conversation are the examples given.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child answers a teacher's question, hands the teacher a book to read, and smiles and chats more in the home classroom than in a new room. An older child walks up and says "guess what I did yesterday", asks "can you play shop with me?", listens to the answer and replies, or asks for help with a stuck zip.

### Limits

Both statements concern familiar adults; unfamiliar adults are not mentioned. The earlier statement stresses familiar settings and describes starting contact as an emerging ability. The three forms of initiative at the later age are examples. What changes with age: the child takes more initiative, and the exchanges become more two-way.

### Common mistakes

A child speaks to an adult only to ask for something and does not respond to the adult's reply. Children interrupt an adult who is busy with someone else and expect an answer at once. A child who talks freely with a known adult goes silent in a new place, which can be mistaken for lacking the skill.
