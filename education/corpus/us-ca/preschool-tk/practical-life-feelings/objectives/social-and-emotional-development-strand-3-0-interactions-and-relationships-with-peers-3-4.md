## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children pick one or two particular children, whom they single out as their friends, to play with, and their play with these friends is more complex than their play with others. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), their friendships become more two-sided, more exclusive and longer-lasting, and they cooperate and work at solving problems to a greater degree.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child looks for the same one or two children each morning, says "she's my friend", and plays a longer pretend scene with them than with anyone else. An older child keeps the same friend over many weeks, saves a place for the friend, takes turns choosing what to play, and works out with the friend how to share one scooter.

### Limits

"One or two" is stated as the number of special friends at the earlier age; the later statement gives no number. Neither statement mentions adult support. The later statement does not say with whom the stronger cooperation and problem-solving take place. What changes with age: friendships become more mutual, exclusive and lasting, and cooperation and problem-solving increase.

### Common mistakes

Children say "you're not my friend" in a short quarrel and mean only "I'm angry now". A child calls whoever they are playing with at the moment a friend. An exclusive friendship leads a child to refuse a third child a place in the play.
