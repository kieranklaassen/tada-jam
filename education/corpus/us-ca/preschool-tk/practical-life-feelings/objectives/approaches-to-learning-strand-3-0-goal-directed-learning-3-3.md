## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children work with other children to solve problems that come up in play and in learning activities; the joint effort is sometimes brief and sometimes lasts longer. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the joint problem-solving goes on for extended stretches, and children communicate with one another about ways to solve the problem and give help to another child who needs it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child and a friend push a heavy box that neither can move alone, and go separate ways once it has moved. An older child tells a friend "you hold the bottom and I'll put the roof on", listens to the friend's idea, and steadies the friend's side when it wobbles.

### Limits

Neither statement mentions adult support or a group size. No length of time is stated; the earlier statement expressly allows short joint efforts as well as longer ones. Communicating with other children about solutions, and helping them, appear only in the later statement. What changes with age: joint problem-solving lasts longer, and children communicate about solutions and help one another.

### Common mistakes

Children work side by side on the same problem without combining their efforts. One child takes over and the other watches. A disagreement about whose idea to use ends the joint effort.
