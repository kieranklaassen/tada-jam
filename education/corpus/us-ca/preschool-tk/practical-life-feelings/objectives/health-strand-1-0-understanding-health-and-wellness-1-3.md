## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children express a basic understanding of two things that people who give health care do: they help people stay healthy, and they look after people who are ill. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), children express more specific knowledge of how those people do both.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child playing doctor puts a toy stethoscope on a doll and says "the doctor makes you better". An older child says that the dentist counts and cleans teeth, that a nurse gives a shot so that you do not get sick, and that the doctor looks in your ears when they hurt.

### Limits

The statements name no particular professions, tools or procedures. Both sides of the role are in both statements: keeping people healthy and helping people who are ill. What changes with age: a basic understanding becomes more specific knowledge of how health care providers do this work.

### Common mistakes

Children think a doctor is only for someone who is sick or hurt, and do not see a check-up as part of staying healthy. A shot is understood as a punishment, or as the thing that causes the illness. Children take clothing or tools to define the job, so that anyone in a white coat is a doctor.
