## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages children keep to safety rules inside and outside, meaning every rule that guards a child against injury, danger or risk. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), they do so with an adult's support and prompting. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they need less support and guidance from an adult.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child walks indoors, holds the rail on the stairs and stays inside the fence when an adult gives a reminder. An older child waits until the slide is clear before going down, carries scissors with the points down, and tells a friend "we walk inside", with few reminders.

### Limits

The statements define a safety rule broadly and list no particular rules. Emergency routines and traffic rules have statements of their own in this strand. Adult support is part of both statements; following rules with no adult is not stated at either age. What changes with age: less adult support is needed, and the adult's part is called prompting at the earlier age and guidance at the later age.

### Common mistakes

A child follows a rule only while an adult is watching. Children know the rule in one place and do not apply it in a similar place, walking in the classroom and running in the hallway. A child can recite a rule and cannot say what danger it prevents.
