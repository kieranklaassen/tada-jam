## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children cope when an attachment figure leaves and they are separated (being dropped off at family child care or at preschool is the example), but now and then during the day they need extra help with the distress of being apart. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they cope with such partings and handle the distress of being apart with very little help or none.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child waves at the window, cries briefly, and settles on a teacher's lap with a family photo; later in the day the child asks for the parent again and needs a hug. An older child says goodbye with a set routine, such as two kisses and a wave, goes to play, and when sad looks at the photo or says "mum comes after snack".

### Limits

The drop-off settings are an example. The earlier statement itself allows that extra help is occasionally needed during the day. The later statement says minimal or no assistance. Neither sets how long the child is apart. What changes with age: the help needed with distress while apart falls from occasional extra help to little or none.

### Common mistakes

A child seems settled at the parting and becomes upset later, at rest time or when another child's parent arrives. Children fear that the parent will not come back, since they cannot yet judge how long the wait is. A child copes on usual days and not after a break such as a holiday or an illness.
