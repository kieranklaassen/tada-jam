## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children show interest in some of the things, people and activities close around them, both known and new. They look for information by using their senses, saying what they notice and asking simple questions. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the interest reaches a wider range of things, people and activities, and it shows in fuller exploring with the senses, descriptions with more detail and questions with more detail.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child picks up a new object, turns it over, shakes or smells it, says one thing about it ("it's bumpy") and asks "what's that?". An older child stays with the object for longer, holds it next to something known, describes several features such as colour, weight and sound, and asks how or why it does what it does.

### Limits

The statements set no number of objects, questions or topics. At the earlier age the interest is in "some" things in the child's near surroundings and the questions are simple. The later statement does not repeat the mention of near surroundings. What changes with age: the range of things that interest the child grows, and exploring, describing and asking all gain detail.

### Common mistakes

A young child asks a question and moves on before the answer comes. Describing often stops at one striking feature and leaves the rest out. A child may repeat "why?" to keep an adult talking, without attending to the answers.
