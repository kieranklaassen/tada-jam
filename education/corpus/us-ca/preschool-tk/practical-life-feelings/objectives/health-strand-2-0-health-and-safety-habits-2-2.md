## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages children keep up health habits whose purpose is to prevent infectious disease and infestation; lice are the example given. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), they do so when an adult instructs them and shows how. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they need only limited instruction and showing from an adult.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child coughs into the elbow after an adult shows how, takes a tissue when handed one and drops it in the bin, and keeps a hat on their own head when told. An older child reaches for a tissue, wipes the nose, throws the tissue away and washes hands with only a short reminder, and does not share a comb or a hat.

### Limits

The statements do not list the habits; lice is the one example of an infestation, and no disease is named. Handwashing and toothbrushing have statements of their own in this strand. Adult instruction and modelling are part of both statements. What changes with age: only the amount of adult instruction and modelling, which is limited at the later age.

### Common mistakes

A child covers a cough with the hand and then touches toys or food. Children think germs can be seen, so hands that look clean are taken to be clean. A used tissue goes into a pocket or onto the table instead of the bin.
