## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children show that they notice, and are curious about, ways in which people are alike and ways in which they differ. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they compare themselves with other people and, going by the likenesses and differences they perceive, express beliefs about others and about themselves.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child puts an arm next to a friend's and says "yours is darker", asks why a classmate uses a wheelchair, or points out that two children have the same shoes. An older child says "we both have little brothers, but she speaks Spanish at home", and says what they think follows from a difference, such as "boys like trucks".

### Limits

The statements name no particular traits. Expressing beliefs appears only at the later age; the statement says that children express such beliefs and does not say that the beliefs are accurate. Neither statement mentions adult support. What changes with age: awareness and interest become comparisons the child makes and beliefs the child expresses.

### Common mistakes

Children generalise from one person to a whole group ("girls can't run fast"). A child treats a difference as a fault, or says so aloud in a way that hurts. Children attend to visible features such as skin, hair and clothing and overlook likenesses in what people feel or like.
