## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children are beginning to understand that growing and feeling good are helped by eating many different foods. They pick foods they know, among them foods of their family and culture, and now and then are willing to try a new one. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they understand that varied eating helps the body to grow and feel good, and at meals they pick from a wider range of foods, again including those of their family and culture.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child takes the rice and beans eaten at home, says "food makes me big", and once in a while tastes a small piece of something new when another child does. An older child puts three or four different foods on the plate, a dish from home among them, and says that eating different foods helps the body grow.

### Limits

The statements name no foods, no food groups and no number of foods. Family and cultural foods are named in both statements as part of what children choose. Trying new foods is described at the earlier age as occasional. Neither statement labels any food as good or bad. What changes with age: an emerging understanding becomes an understanding, and the child chooses among more foods at meals.

### Common mistakes

Children believe that a large amount of one liked food does the same as many different foods. A new food is refused on its look or smell before it is tasted. A child sorts foods into "good" and "bad" and does not connect variety with growing.
