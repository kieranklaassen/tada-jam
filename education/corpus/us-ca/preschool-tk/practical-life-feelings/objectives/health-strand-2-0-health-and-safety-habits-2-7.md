## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children are beginning to be able to keep to safety rules for walking near traffic and for riding in vehicles, under an adult's instruction and supervision. The examples given are checking in both directions before stepping into the street and helping to fasten the straps of a car seat harness. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they are better able to keep to these rules and also understand them, with an adult's support and supervision; the examples are the same.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child stops at the kerb, holds an adult's hand, turns the head left and right when the adult says "look", and pushes one buckle of the car seat together with help. An older child stops at the kerb unprompted, looks in both directions and says "no cars", stays seated on the bus, helps with the harness, and says that the straps hold the body in a sudden stop.

### Limits

Adult supervision is part of both statements; crossing a street alone is not stated at either age. In the car seat example the child helps to buckle; the statement does not say that the child buckles alone. Both examples are examples, and no other rules are listed. Understanding the rules appears only at the later age. What changes with age: an emerging ability becomes an increased one, understanding is added, and adult instruction becomes adult support, with supervision at both ages.

### Common mistakes

A child turns the head both ways as a ritual and does not check for cars. Children think that a driver they can see can also see them. A child judges a street safe because it is quiet at that moment, or runs after a ball without stopping at the edge.
