## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children are beginning to be able to carry on with what they are doing, alone or in a group, when something distracts or interrupts them, but they often need an adult's support to bring their attention back. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the ability is still described as emerging, and children use it with less adult support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child drawing at a table looks up when another child drops a box, and returns to the drawing after an adult points to the paper. An older child at story time glances towards a noise in the hallway and turns back to the book without being told.

### Limits

Both statements call the ability emerging, so steady resistance to distraction is not stated at either age. The statements cover activities done alone and activities done in a group. No length of time is stated. What changes with age: less adult support is needed to keep or regain attention.

### Common mistakes

After an interruption a child starts a new activity instead of returning to the first. A child tries to deal with a distraction by reacting to it loudly ("stop that!"), which interrupts the activity anyway.
