## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children are coming to understand that each other person has thoughts, experiences and ways of behaving of their own. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they are beginning to understand the inner reasons (mental and psychological) for what people do, and that these reasons help to explain why people behave differently from one another.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child says "you like peas, I don't", or tells a friend about a trip the friend was not on and starts to realise that the friend does not know about it. An older child says "he took the truck because he thought it was his" or "she's hiding because she's shy", and gives a different reason when another child does the same thing.

### Limits

Both statements describe an understanding that is still forming: the earlier one speaks of developing it and the later one calls it emerging. The statements name no situations, and neither mentions adult support. What changes with age: from knowing that others have their own thoughts and experiences to beginning to explain behaviour by what goes on in a person's mind.

### Common mistakes

A child assumes that another person knows what the child knows, for example where a hidden object is. Children explain behaviour by the outward event only ("he cried because he fell") and leave out what the person thought or wanted. A child takes an accident to have been done on purpose.
