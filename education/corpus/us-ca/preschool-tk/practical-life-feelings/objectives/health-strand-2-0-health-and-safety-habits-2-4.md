## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children do some of the things that keep them safe in the sun, with an adult supporting and guiding them; drinking water and having sunscreen on are the examples given. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they act in sun-safe ways (the same examples are given) with less support and guidance from an adult.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child holds out arms and face for sunscreen, puts on a hat when an adult hands it over, and drinks when a cup is offered. An older child fetches the hat before going out, asks for sunscreen, moves into the shade to play and goes to the water bottle after a short reminder.

### Limits

Sunscreen and drinking water are examples; the statements do not list the actions, and hats and shade are not named in them. The earlier statement speaks of "some" actions and the later statement leaves that word out. Adult support and guidance are part of both statements. What changes with age: the word "some" is dropped, and less adult support and guidance is needed.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks sun protection is needed only when it feels hot, and not on a cool or cloudy bright day. Children take the hat off within minutes because it is uncomfortable. A child does not link feeling thirsty or flushed with having been in the sun.
