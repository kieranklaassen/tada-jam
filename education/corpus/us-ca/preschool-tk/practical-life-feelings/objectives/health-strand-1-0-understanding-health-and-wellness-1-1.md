## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children point out and give the correct names of a few parts on the outside of the body (the head, an elbow and the private parts are examples) and a few parts inside it (such as the heart, the brain and bones), and they know a little about what these parts do. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they correctly name several outside and inside parts and know in more detail what the parts do.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child touches an elbow or a knee when asked, says its name, and says that the heart "goes boom boom". An older child names more parts, feels the heartbeat with a hand on the chest, and says that bones hold the body up, that the brain is for thinking and that the heart moves blood.

### Limits

The statements give no count: "a few" at the earlier age and "several" at the later age. The parts listed are examples, and the examples of outside parts include the private parts, named correctly. At the earlier age "a few" applies to inside parts as well as outside parts. What changes with age: more parts are named, and knowledge of what they do gains detail.

### Common mistakes

Children believe the inside of the body holds only what they have seen or felt, such as food and blood. A child points to the right part and uses a family word or a wrong name for it. The brain is thought to be for thinking only, with no part in moving or feeling.
