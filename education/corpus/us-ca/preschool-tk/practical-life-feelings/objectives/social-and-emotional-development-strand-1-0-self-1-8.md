## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children show empathy: they feel along with what another person is feeling and show concern for what someone in distress needs. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they respond to another person's distress or need with sympathetic care and with help, comforting and assisting, though now and then they need an adult's support to do so.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child stops playing and watches when a friend cries, looks sad too, and pats the friend or calls an adult. An older child fetches a tissue or the friend's soft toy, asks "are you OK?", helps to pick up the spilled crayons, and gets an adult when the problem is too big.

### Limits

At the earlier age the statement speaks of feeling with others and showing concern, and does not mention helping. Comforting and helping appear at the later age, where the statement itself allows that an adult's support is occasionally needed. No adult support is mentioned at the earlier age. What changes with age: shared feeling and concern become comforting and helping.

### Common mistakes

A child offers what would comfort the child, such as their own blanket, and not what the other person wants. Children become so upset by another's crying that they need comfort themselves. A child laughs or stares at someone in distress out of unease, which looks like not caring.
