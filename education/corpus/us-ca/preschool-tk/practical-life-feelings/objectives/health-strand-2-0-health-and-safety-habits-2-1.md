## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children show that they know part of the sequence for washing hands. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they show that they know most of the steps, or all of them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child turns on the tap, wets the hands and rubs them briefly, and needs a reminder for soap or for drying. An older child wets the hands, takes soap, rubs palms, backs and between the fingers, rinses, dries and turns off the tap, in order, and can tell another child what comes next.

### Limits

The statements do not list the steps, say how many there are, or give a washing time. The foundations document notes, beneath the two statements, what the routine consists of: the water is turned on and the hands are wetted, soap goes on and is lathered for 20 seconds, the backs of the hands and the gaps between the fingers are cleaned, and then the child rinses, dries the hands and turns the water off; it gives a University of California child care health programme as its source. They are about knowing the steps; neither mentions adult support or says when hands are to be washed. What changes with age: from knowing part of the sequence to knowing most of it or the whole.

### Common mistakes

Skipping soap, or putting soap on and rinsing it off at once without rubbing. Washing only the palms and leaving out the backs of the hands and the gaps between fingers. Doing steps out of order, such as drying before rinsing, or playing in the water and losing the sequence.
