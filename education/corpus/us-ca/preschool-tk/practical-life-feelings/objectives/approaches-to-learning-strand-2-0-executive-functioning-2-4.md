## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children are beginning, with adult support, to change what they are doing and to consider something in a way they had not before. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they do both more consistently and need less adult support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child sorting buttons by colour switches to sorting by size after an adult shows how, or accepts a blue cup when the red one is taken. An older child whose block bridge keeps falling tries a wider base without prompting, or agrees to a new rule in tag when a friend proposes it.

### Limits

The statements set no number of changes and name no kind of task. At the earlier age the ability is described as emerging. Adult support is part of both statements. What changes with age: the shifts happen more consistently, and less adult support is needed.

### Common mistakes

Asked to sort a second way, a child keeps sorting by the first rule. A change of plan or routine is met with protest. A child insists that there is only one right way to use a material or to play.
