## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children do two things with adult support: they keep about one or two pieces of information in mind for a short time, and they act on that information while doing tasks and playing. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they keep about two or three pieces in mind for longer and act on them in tasks with several steps and in more complex play, with less adult support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child hears "get your shoes and bring them to the door" and does it while an adult repeats the second part, or remembers which of two cups hides a toy. An older child carries out a three-part errand, such as fetching a cup, filling it and carrying it to the table, or keeps track of who plays which role in a pretend scene, with fewer reminders.

### Limits

The amounts are stated as approximate ranges, not as examples: about one to two pieces of information at the earlier age and about two to three at the later age. No length of time is given beyond "short" and "longer". Adult support is part of both statements. What changes with age: the amount held rises by about one piece, it is held for longer, the tasks have more steps and the play is more complex, and less adult support is needed.

### Common mistakes

A child carries out the last thing heard and forgets the first. The instruction is lost when something interesting interrupts on the way. A child can repeat an instruction aloud and still not act on it.
