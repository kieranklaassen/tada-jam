## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children can tell which food is which for a small number of particular foods. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the foods they recognise are of a wider variety, and they know some of the food groups those foods belong to.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child picks the banana out of a bowl of fruit when asked, or says "apple" and "rice" on seeing them on a plate. An older child names many items at a market stall or in a lunch box, and puts a carrot with the other vegetables and a pear with the other fruit.

### Limits

The earlier statement says only "a few" and gives no count or list. The later statement speaks of "some" food groups and does not say which groups or how many. Neither statement mentions adult support, and neither says which foods are healthy. What changes with age: the child knows more foods and begins to know the groups they belong to.

### Common mistakes

A child names a food by its colour or by a familiar look-alike, calling every round red fruit an apple. Children do not recognise a known food in another form, such as grated carrot beside a whole carrot. Grouping follows what is eaten together or what the child likes, not the food group.
