## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children try, with adult support, to manage their feelings and their behaviour and to deal with stress. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they do manage feelings and behaviour, and they show strategies for dealing with the feelings and behaviour that stress brings on, with some adult support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child who is upset goes to an adult's lap, hugs a soft toy, or takes slow breaths when the adult breathes along. An older child walks to the quiet corner, squeezes a cushion, breathes slowly or says "I need a minute", and comes back to the group, sometimes after an adult suggests it.

### Limits

The statements name no strategies and no time for calming down. The earlier statement speaks of efforts, so success is not required at that age. Adult support is part of both statements: support at the earlier age, some support at the later age. What changes with age: efforts become actual regulation, the child has strategies to show, and adult support is reduced to some.

### Common mistakes

A child can name a calming strategy when calm and cannot use it when upset. Children hit, throw or run off before any strategy starts. A child believes that the feeling itself is wrong, and hides it instead of managing it.
