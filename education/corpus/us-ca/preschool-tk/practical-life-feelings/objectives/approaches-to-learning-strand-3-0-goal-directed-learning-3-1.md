## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children are beginning, with adult support, to choose a simple goal and follow it through; a plan of one or two steps is the example given. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they make and follow through plans of greater complexity, a plan of two or three steps being the example, with less adult support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child says "I'm going to make a road", gets the blocks and lays them in a line, while an adult asks "what do you need first?". An older child says "first I'll get paper, then draw my dog, then cut it out", and does the steps in that order.

### Limits

The numbers of steps (one to two, then two to three) are given as examples, not as limits. At the earlier age the ability is described as emerging. Adult support is part of both statements. What changes with age: plans become more complex, the ability is no longer called emerging, and less adult support is needed.

### Common mistakes

A child names a plan and then does something unrelated. Children start with the step that interests them most and skip the preparation, such as painting before putting on a smock. The goal changes halfway and the child does not notice that the first one was dropped.
