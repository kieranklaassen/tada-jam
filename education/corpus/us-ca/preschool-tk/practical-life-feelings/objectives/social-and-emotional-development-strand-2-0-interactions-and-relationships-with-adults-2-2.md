## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children turn to their attachment figures for security and support, such as comfort or help, so that their needs are met, above all when a situation is hard. The statement explains attachment figures as the adults who care for the child, and teachers can be among them. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), children foresee when they will need support and go to those adults for it more on their own initiative.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child who falls runs to the teacher with arms up, or brings a stuck jar and holds it out. An older child says "can you stay near me, the dog scares me" before walking past the dog, or asks for help before starting a hard task.

### Limits

The statements are about turning to attachment figures, which the wording explains in brackets; other adults and other children are not mentioned. Help and comfort are examples of support. Anticipating the need appears only at the later age. Neither statement says how often a child seeks support. What changes with age: the child foresees the need for support and takes more initiative in asking for it.

### Common mistakes

A child in difficulty cries or withdraws and waits to be noticed instead of going to the adult. Children ask for help with everything, including what they can do, or refuse help they need so as to appear capable. A child accepts comfort from the usual adult only, and not from another caregiver.
