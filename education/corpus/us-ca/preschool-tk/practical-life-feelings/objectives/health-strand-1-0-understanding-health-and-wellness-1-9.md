## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children realise, with adult support, that they are tired, and their tiredness shows in a range of behaviours; their ability to keep to sleep routines consistently is limited. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they show through a range of behaviours when they are tired, are beginning to understand that rest and sleep help to keep the body healthy, and keep to routines for sleep and rest more consistently.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child rubs the eyes, cries easily or lies on the floor, and agrees "I'm sleepy" after an adult says so; at nap time the child gets up from the mat several times. An older child says "I'm tired", fetches a blanket or goes to the quiet corner, lies down at rest time after the usual story, and says that sleep helps the body.

### Limits

The statements do not list the behaviours that show tiredness, and give no hours of sleep. Adult support is stated only for recognising tiredness at the earlier age. Linking sleep and rest with health appears only at the later age, as an emerging ability. Rest is named beside sleep only in the later statement. What changes with age: the adult support is no longer stated, the child begins to link sleep and rest with health, and the routines are followed more consistently.

### Common mistakes

A tired child becomes more active or louder and says "I'm not tired". Children see sleep as an end to play that adults impose, not as something the body needs.
