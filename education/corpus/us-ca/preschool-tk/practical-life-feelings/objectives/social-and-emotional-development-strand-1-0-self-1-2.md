## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children speak well of what they can do and like to show it, and they let it be known that they want adults they know, teachers among them, to think well of them. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they express confidence in what they can do and describe their strengths, which includes referring to what they could do in the past. They remain sensitive to how other children as well as known adults, teachers among them, see them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child calls "watch me!" before jumping off a step, says "I'm good at puzzles", and looks to the teacher for a reaction. An older child says "I couldn't zip my coat when I was little and now I can", shows a friend how to do it, and checks whether the other children are watching.

### Limits

The statements name no particular abilities. At the earlier age the wish to be seen well is tied to familiar adults; other children's views appear only at the later age. Reference to what the child could do in the past appears only at the later age. Neither statement mentions adult support. What changes with age: describing abilities becomes expressing confidence and naming strengths with reference to the past, and sensitivity to others' views widens from familiar adults to peers.

### Common mistakes

Children overrate what they can do, and say they are the best or can do everything. A child takes one failure in front of others as proof of being "bad at it" and refuses to try again. A child says "I can do it" and refuses help that is needed.
