## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages children show initiative in three ways: they begin activities themselves, they open contact with other people (helping someone is the example given), and they look for ways to solve problems. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), the activity given as an example is a simple pretend-play scene. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the example is a play scene with more detail and complexity, the child opens contact with others more often, and the child keeps looking for a solution with more persistence.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child fetches a doll and a cup and starts feeding the doll without being asked, hands a friend a dropped block, or tries a second way to get a lid off. An older child sets up a longer pretend scene with roles and props, offers help before anyone asks, and keeps trying different ways when the first ones fail.

### Limits

Play scenes and helping are given as examples, not as the only forms of initiative. Neither statement mentions adult support. No number of activities or attempts is stated. What changes with age: the play scenes a child starts gain detail and complexity, reaching out to others becomes more frequent, and the search for solutions lasts longer.

### Common mistakes

A child waits for an adult to suggest what to do or to fix a problem. Starting is more common than carrying through: a child sets out the props and then leaves. Help is sometimes given in a way the other child does not want, such as taking over the task.
