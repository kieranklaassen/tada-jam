## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children see, with adult support, that other people like different things and go about things in different ways, though they often find this hard to accept. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they understand and can explain that other people's ways and likes may differ from their own, and they accept this more consistently, with less adult support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child sees a friend start a tower with the small blocks and says "no, not like that", until an adult points out that both towers stand. An older child says "Mina draws first and colours after, I do it the other way", and lets the friend carry on.

### Limits

The statements name no kind of activity or preference. The earlier statement itself says that accepting other people's ways is often difficult at that age. Adult support is part of both statements. What changes with age: noticing becomes an understanding the child can explain, acceptance becomes more consistent, and less adult support is needed.

### Common mistakes

A child takes a different way of doing something to be a wrong way. Children assume that everyone likes what they like, and choose for a friend accordingly. A child states that people differ and still insists that a friend do it the child's way.
