## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children go to an adult for help when they and another child disagree or are in conflict and it needs settling. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they negotiate with the other child and more often use communication when a conflict arises; they turn to an adult for help in understanding what the other child needs or in settling the conflict.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child whose toy is taken runs to the teacher and says "he took it", or pulls the teacher over by the hand. An older child says "I had it first, you can have it when I'm done", offers a trade or a turn, and fetches an adult when the other child will not agree or when it is unclear what the other child wants.

### Limits

At the earlier age the one thing stated is asking an adult for help; settling a conflict with no adult is not stated. At the later age negotiating and communicating are added, and going to an adult remains part of the statement. No particular strategy, such as taking turns, is named. What changes with age: the child begins to negotiate and communicate with the other child, and goes to the adult also to understand what the other child needs.

### Common mistakes

A child grabs, pushes or hits before asking anyone for help. Children tell the adult what the other child did and leave out their own part. A child treats negotiating as repeating the demand more loudly, and offers nothing in return.
