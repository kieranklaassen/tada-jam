## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), once they have been taught and have practised, children are able to go through an emergency routine with an adult supporting and guiding them; an earthquake drill and a fire drill are the examples given. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), again after teaching and practice, they go through such routines more independently, with some guidance from an adult.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child stops playing at the alarm, takes an adult's hand or holds the walking rope, and follows the group to the meeting place. An older child stops at the alarm, lines up and walks out without running back for a toy, or gets under a table and covers the head in an earthquake drill, after a word from the teacher.

### Limits

Both statements place the ability after instruction and practice, so a routine the child has not been taught is outside them. Fire and earthquake drills are examples. An adult is part of both statements. What changes with age: the child follows the routine more independently, and adult support and guidance is reduced to some adult guidance.

### Common mistakes

A child goes back for a toy, a coat or a friend instead of leaving. Children freeze, cry or cover their ears at the alarm and do not move. A child treats the drill as play and runs or hides.
