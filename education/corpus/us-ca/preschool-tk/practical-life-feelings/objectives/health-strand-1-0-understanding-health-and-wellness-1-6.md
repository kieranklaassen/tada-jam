## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children are beginning to be able to let an adult know when they are hungry and when they are full. They expect the routines around meals but cannot wait long for the next one. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they let an adult know about being hungry or full more consistently, still expect the mealtime routines, and can wait somewhat longer for a meal.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child says "I'm hungry" or pulls an adult to the kitchen, pushes the plate away when full, goes to wash hands when the table is being set, and fusses when the food is slow to come. An older child says "my tummy is full" and stops eating, asks how long it is until lunch, and plays for a while after hearing "soon".

### Limits

The statements give no waiting time: the earlier one calls the capacity to wait limited, and the later one says only that the wait is a little longer. In both, the child communicates to an adult. Neither statement says what or how much a child should eat. What changes with age: letting an adult know about hunger and fullness becomes more consistent, and the child can wait somewhat longer for food.

### Common mistakes

A child confuses hunger with thirst, tiredness or boredom and asks for food in each case. Children say "I'm full" in order to leave the table and play, or keep eating a liked food past fullness. A child does not notice hunger while absorbed in play and then becomes upset suddenly.
