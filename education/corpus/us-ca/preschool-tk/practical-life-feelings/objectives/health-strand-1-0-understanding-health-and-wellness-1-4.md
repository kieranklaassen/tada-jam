## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children, with adult support, recognise body boundaries and communicate about them: touches they want (a friend's hug or a teacher's high five are examples) and touches they do not want (such as being pushed or hit, or touching that is inappropriate). They tend to follow what adults they trust have taught them about this. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they are able to recognise the same boundaries and communicate about them, and they follow and use that guidance more consistently and more independently.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child says "no" or steps back when another child grabs, goes to an adult about it, and with a reminder asks "can I hug you?" before hugging. An older child says "stop, I don't like that" in the moment, asks before touching a friend, accepts a "no", and tells a trusted adult about a touch that felt wrong.

### Limits

The touches listed are examples of the two kinds, wanted and unwanted. The foundations document notes, beneath the two statements, that the term inappropriate touches takes in any touch that leaves a child uncomfortable and does not serve to keep the child clean, healthy or safe. Adult support is stated at the earlier age and not at the later age. Both statements tie the child's conduct to the guidance of trusted adults; neither says that a child deals with an unwanted touch without an adult. What changes with age: the adult support for recognising and communicating about boundaries is no longer stated, and the child follows and uses adults' guidance more consistently and independently.

### Common mistakes

A child thinks that a touch from a friend or a liked adult has to be accepted. Children hug or climb on others without asking, and do not read a turned-away body as "no". A child laughs when tickled or pushed while disliking it, so others do not see the refusal.
