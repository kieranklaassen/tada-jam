## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), children take part in activities shared with other children and now and then join a cooperative effort with them, with adult support. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), they engage and cooperate with other children more actively and more on purpose, in everyday play and learning.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child plays beside others at the sand table, passes a spade when asked, and helps another child carry a mat when a teacher sets the task up. An older child says "let's build a zoo, you make the fence", shares out the animals, and keeps working with the others until the zoo is done.

### Limits

The earlier statement describes cooperation as occasional and states adult support. The later statement mentions no adult support. Neither gives a group size or a length of time. Resolving conflicts and friendship have statements of their own in this strand. What changes with age: interacting and cooperating become more active and intentional, across daily play and learning.

### Common mistakes

Children play next to each other with the same materials and take it for playing together. A child joins a group by taking a piece or changing the play, without asking or watching first. Cooperation stops as soon as two children want the same item or role.
