## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child now and then identifies a small number of well-known solid shapes by everyday names; calling a sphere a "ball" is the example. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child identifies a few well-known solid shapes; sphere, cube and cylinder are the ones named. A note for the later age says the child at times still uses everyday names, and gives tube, square box and ball as examples.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child handles balls, blocks and cans. A younger child sometimes calls them ball, box or can when talking about their shape. An older child picks the cube out of the block basket when asked for it, and says that a can or a paper-towel tube is a cylinder, though at other times still calls the same things box or tube.

### Limits

Earlier: occasionally, a few shapes, by informal names. Later: a few familiar shapes; sphere, cube and cylinder are given as examples and the list is left open. A note at the later age says informal names are sometimes still in use, with tube, square box and ball as its examples, so informal names appear at both ages. Neither statement asks the child to describe faces, edges or corners. Flat shapes are foundation 4.1. What changes with age: from identifying a few shapes occasionally, by everyday names, to identifying them, with everyday names still used some of the time.

### Common mistakes

Using a flat-shape name for a solid: calling a cube a square or a sphere a circle. Calling every box a cube. Not recognising a cylinder when it lies on its side or is very short or very tall.
