## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child says the number words in sequence, starting at one and reaching ten or beyond, and a small number of slips is accepted. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the sequence reaches thirty with the same allowance for slips, and the child can also begin at some number that is not one and carry on upward.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says the number words aloud with nothing to count: while climbing stairs, clapping, or waiting for a turn. A younger child chants to ten or a little past it. An older child keeps going through the teens and twenties to thirty, and when an adult names a starting number such as six, picks up the chant there and continues.

### Limits

Earlier: one to ten, or further. Later: one to thirty, plus counting on from a start other than one; the statement does not say how far that counting on has to go. A few errors are allowed at both ages. The statements ask only for saying the words forward: counting objects is foundation 1.2 and knowing how many is foundation 1.3. What changes with age: the range grows from ten to thirty, and the starting point stops being fixed at one.

### Common mistakes

Skipping or swapping the teens, most often thirteen and fifteen, whose names do not follow the pattern of the others. Stalling at a change of decade, for example saying "twenty-ten" after twenty-nine. When asked to start at a number other than one, going back to one, aloud or under the breath, to get a run at it.
