## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child identifies where objects and people are in space. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) this covers some positions, with in and on, under and over, up and down, and inside and outside given as the examples. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the list is longer: those same pairs plus near and far, next to, beside and between, and in front of and behind.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child puts the teddy under the chair when asked, or says the ball is in the box and the cat is outside. An older child stands between two friends, sets the cup in front of the plate, and says the dog is behind the sofa or that the park is far and the gate is near.

### Limits

Earlier: some positions; the four pairs are examples. Later: a longer list, introduced as what the positions include. The statements say "identify" and do not say whether by words, by pointing or by acting. Left and right appear in neither list. What changes with age: near and far, next to, beside and between, and in front of and behind are added.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up in front of and behind for things with no clear front, such as a ball, where the answer depends on where the child stands. Taking "between" to mean "next to". Placing an object correctly on request but using the opposite word when describing it.
