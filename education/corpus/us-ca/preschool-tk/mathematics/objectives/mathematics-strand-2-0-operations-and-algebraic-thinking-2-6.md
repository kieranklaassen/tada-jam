## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child notices and explores patterns around them and, helped by an adult, copies a simple repeating pattern; ABAB is the example given. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), still helped by an adult, the child explores, copies and continues several kinds of repeating pattern, with AABBAABB and ABCABC as the examples. The child also says which part of the pattern repeats (the pattern unit).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child points out stripes on a shirt or tiles on a floor and, with an adult beside them, lays red and blue beads under the adult's row so the two rows match. An older child adds the next blocks to the end of a row someone else began. The older child also touches "red, red, blue, blue" and says that is the bit that comes again.

### Limits

Repeating patterns only, at both ages; growing patterns are not mentioned. Earlier: noticing and exploring, and copying a simple pattern (ABAB) with adult support. Later: adult support is still named for exploring, extending and copying; units get longer (pairs, three different elements). Making up a pattern of one's own is foundation 2.7. What changes with age: extending is added, the units become more varied, and the child describes the unit.

### Common mistakes

Extending by repeating only the last item, so ABAB turns into ABABBB. Continuing AABB as ABAB, dropping the doubles. Naming a single item or the whole row as the part that repeats.
