## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child shows they understand that a group of things has smaller parts inside it, and that each part is smaller than the group as a whole. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child splits a group into two small groups in more than one way, and joins two small groups to form a bigger one; the statement's examples all use a group of 5.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child pulls a handful of beads out of a pile, sees that the pile they came from was bigger than the handful, and pushes them back. An older child holds five counters and splits them between two bowls as four and one, then again as three and two. The older child also pushes two little piles together and says how many that makes.

### Limits

Earlier: no numbers are named; it is an understanding of parts and whole. Later: two parts at a time, in more than one way; the examples use 5 only, and "small" is not given a number. No written number sentences or symbols are mentioned. What changes with age: from sensing that a whole has parts to making the splits and the joins with small sets.

### Common mistakes

Believing that a different split makes a different total, and counting everything again after each split to check. Finding one split and repeating it, or offering the same two parts in the other order as a new way. Losing sight of the whole once it is split, for example saying there are more red beads than beads.
