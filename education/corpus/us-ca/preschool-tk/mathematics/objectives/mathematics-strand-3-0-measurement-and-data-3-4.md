## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child shows data using objects, tally marks, or pictographs. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), the data fall into two groups and an adult helps. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), there can be more than two groups, and the child shows they understand that every single object, mark, or picture stands for one piece of data.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Each child in a group puts one block on the apple pile or on the banana pile to show which fruit they like, or an adult and child make one mark for every rainy day and one for every dry day. An older child places one picture card per person in one of three or more columns, and can point to a card and say whose it is.

### Limits

The forms named are objects, tally marks and pictographs. Earlier: two groups, with adult support. Later: two or more groups, adult support not mentioned, and each marker stands for exactly one data point. Bar graphs with a numbered scale and written numerals are not mentioned. Reading what the data show is foundation 3.5. What changes with age: more groups, less help, and the one-marker-for-one-thing idea is asked for outright.

### Common mistakes

Putting down two markers for one thing, or none. Spacing the markers unevenly or using markers of different sizes, so the longer row is not the one with more. Forgetting what a mark stands for once it has been made.
