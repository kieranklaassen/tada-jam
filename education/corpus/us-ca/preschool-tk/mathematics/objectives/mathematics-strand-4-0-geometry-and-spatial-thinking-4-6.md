## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child finds out by trial and error how things move through space and which places they fit into, for instance turning, flipping or sliding a piece into different positions until it goes into a hole. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child relies less on physically trying things out: to work a problem out the child may turn a piece, flip it over or slide it, for instance turning a piece the right way before putting it in the hole.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With a shape sorter or a jigsaw, a younger child pushes a piece at the hole, turns it, pushes again, flips it, and keeps going until it drops in. An older child looks at the hole, turns the piece in the hand first, and then places it, with fewer tries.

### Limits

Neither statement names shapes, a number of pieces, or an amount of turning. The later statement says the child relies less on trying things physically, not that trial and error is gone. The examples at both ages are about fitting an object into a hole. What changes with age: how much of the turning is worked out before the piece touches the hole.

### Common mistakes

Pushing harder when a piece does not fit and not turning it. Rotating a piece but never thinking to flip it over. Taking one piece to every hole in turn without looking at the shape of the hole.
