## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child looks at two sets of things and tells how they compare in number. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), the two sets are plainly equal or plainly unequal, the child may count or not, and the answer is "same" or "more". At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child counts both sets to compare them, and the words used extend to "less" and "fewer".

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two plates of grapes sit on the table. A younger child looks, or lines the grapes up in pairs, and says which plate has more or that they have the same, when the difference is easy to see. An older child counts each plate, compares the two numbers, and can also say which plate has fewer.

### Limits

Two groups only, at both ages. Earlier: the groups are clearly equal or clearly different, counting is optional, and the words are "same" and "more". Later: the comparison is made by counting, the condition that the difference be obvious is gone, and "less" and "fewer" are added. Neither statement sets a number range or asks how many more. What changes with age: from judging an obvious difference by eye to comparing by counting, with words for the smaller group too.

### Common mistakes

Judging by the space a group takes up: a spread-out row is called more than a bunched one, and a few big things more than many small ones. Hearing "less" as "more" and pointing to the bigger group. Counting both groups correctly and then not knowing which of the two numbers is the larger.
