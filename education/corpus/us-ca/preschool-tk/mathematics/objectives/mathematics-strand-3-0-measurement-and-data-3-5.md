## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), with an adult's help, a child notices that the data in two groups differ and describes which group has more or which has less. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the groups number two or more, and what the child describes and compares, still with an adult's help, is how many data points the groups hold. The group with more, or the group with less, is also something the child works out at that age.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child looks at two rows of blocks from a fruit vote, points to the longer row, and says more children like apples. An older child counts each of three columns of picture cards, says the numbers, and tells which column has more and which has less.

### Limits

Adult support is named at both ages, but at the later age not for everything. Earlier: two groups, and noticing a difference. Later: two or more groups; the support is named for describing and comparing the number of data points, and the separate sentence about working out which group has more or less names none. The outcome of the comparison is only more or less: not how many more, and not totals. Making the display is foundation 3.4. What changes with age: from seeing a difference between two groups to counting and comparing across more groups.

### Common mistakes

Judging by the length of a row when the markers are unevenly spaced. With three groups, naming the biggest and being unable to say which has less. Answering with their own preference and not with what the data show.
