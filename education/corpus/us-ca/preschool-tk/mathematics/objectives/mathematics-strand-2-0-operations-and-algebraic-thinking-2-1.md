## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child shows they know that putting things into a group makes its number go up and removing things makes it go down, whether one thing or several is moved. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child shows they know the precise effect for a single object in a small group: one put in or one removed makes the number differ by exactly one.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child has three crackers, is given another, and says there are more now; when a friend takes two blocks from the pile, the child says there are fewer. An older child has four toy cars, gets one more, and says five without counting them all again; after giving one away, says four.

### Limits

Earlier: the direction of the change only (more or fewer), for one object or several; the size of the change is not asked. Later: the change is exactly one, for adding or removing a single object, in a small group; the statement does not put a number on "small". Working out results of larger changes is foundation 2.3. What changes with age: from knowing which way the number moves to knowing by how much when one object moves.

### Common mistakes

Counting the whole group again from one after a single object is added, not yet trusting that the answer is the next number word. Finding one fewer much harder than one more, because it needs the number before. Saying the number is unchanged because the group looks about as big as it did.
