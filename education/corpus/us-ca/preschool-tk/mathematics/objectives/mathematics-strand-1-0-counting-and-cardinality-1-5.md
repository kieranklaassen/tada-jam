## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child sees a numeral in writing and says its name. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) this holds for a few of the numerals below 10; later (4 to 5½ years) for every numeral up to and including 10.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child points at a numeral on a front door, a clock, a page, or a wooden puzzle piece and says what it is called, or picks out the numeral an adult names from a few cards. A younger child knows a handful, often the one for their own age. An older child names any of them when they are shown out of counting order.

### Limits

Earlier: a few numerals, all below 10. Later: all numerals through 10, so the two-digit 10 is included. The statement does not say whether zero counts among them. It asks for recognising and naming only: not writing numerals, and not matching a numeral to an amount. What changes with age: from a few numerals to the full set through 10.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up numerals that look alike: 6 and 9, 2 and 5. Reading 10 as two separate numerals. Needing to count up from one to find a numeral's name, so that the numeral is known only in sequence.
