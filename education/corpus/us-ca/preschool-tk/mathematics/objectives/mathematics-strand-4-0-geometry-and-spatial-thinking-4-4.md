## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child uses flat or solid shapes to stand for separate parts of a picture or design; a circle placed in a corner as the sun is the example. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child puts different flat or solid shapes together so that they form a picture or design; the example is a house built from two blocks that are rectangular prisms and one that is a triangular prism.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child places shapes one at a time, each standing for one thing: a circle for the sun, a square for a house, with space between them. An older child pushes shapes together so they touch and make one thing: a triangle on top of a square for a house with a roof, or blocks stacked into a tower with a pointed top.

### Limits

Both flat and solid shapes count, at both ages. The result is a picture or a design. Neither statement asks the child to build one named shape out of others (two triangles into a square, for example) or to fill a given outline. What changes with age: from one shape for one thing to several different shapes joined into one thing.

### Common mistakes

Leaving gaps or overlaps, so the parts do not read as one object. Choosing a piece by its colour and not by its shape. Not turning a piece that would fit if it were turned.
