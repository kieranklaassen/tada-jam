## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages, a child compares flat shapes that differ in size and in which way they are turned, and decides if they are the same shape. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child also finds what is alike and what is different in the properties of two different shapes, meaning how many sides or vertices each has. For the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) only the first part is stated.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a small paper triangle against a large one and turns it until the corners point the same way, then says they are both triangles. An older child runs a finger along the edges of a triangle and of a square, counts three and four, and says both have straight sides but one has more.

### Limits

Flat shapes only. The same-shape judgement, across different sizes and orientations, is stated identically for both ages. The later statement adds a comparison of two different shapes, and limits the properties to the number of sides or vertices; angles, side lengths and symmetry are not mentioned. What changes with age: only that added comparison of properties.

### Common mistakes

Saying two shapes are different because one is bigger or turned another way. When counting sides, losing the starting place and counting one side twice. Mixing up sides and corners.
