## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child counts a set of things by matching a single number word to each thing, so that every object gets exactly one word. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) this is done with sets of five or more; later (4 to 5½ years) with sets of ten or more.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child touches, points to, or moves each thing while saying the next number word: one word, one block pushed across the table. A younger child does this with about five crackers or toy cars. An older child keeps word and touch together for ten or more, often by sliding each counted item into a separate pile.

### Limits

Earlier: five objects or more. Later: ten objects or more. Both are lower bounds; the statement gives no upper bound, does not say how the objects are arranged, and does not mention adult support. Knowing that the last word said is the total belongs to foundation 1.3. What changes with age: only the size of the set; the skill is the same.

### Common mistakes

Counting one object twice or missing one, most often when the objects are scattered and not in a row. Saying the words faster than the finger moves, so that words and touches fall out of step. Giving one object two touches for a two-syllable word such as "seven".
