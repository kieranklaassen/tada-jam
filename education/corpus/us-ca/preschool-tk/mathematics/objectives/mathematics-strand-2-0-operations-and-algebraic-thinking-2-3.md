## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child works out adding and taking-away problems that come up in daily life and involve objects. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the quantities are very small. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the quantities are bigger, with totals no higher than 10.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

At snack a younger child has two apple slices, gets one more, and says how many there are now by looking or by counting them all. An older child sets out four plates, hears that three more children are coming, and adds plates and counts, or counts on with fingers. When two of seven blocks roll off the table, the older child counts the ones still there.

### Limits

Earlier: a very small number of objects; no figure is given. Later: sums up to 10. At both ages the problems are about objects in everyday situations: not written sums, not symbols, and no recall of number facts is asked. What changes with age: the size of the numbers only.

### Common mistakes

Counting on and saying the starting number again as the first step, which gives an answer one too small (five and three counted as "five, six, seven"). In taking away, counting the removed objects together with the ones left. Answering with one of the numbers heard in the story, usually the last one.
