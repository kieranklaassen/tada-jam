## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child shows awareness that things can be compared in how long they are, how heavy they are, or how much they hold: the child notices such differences and communicates about them. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child compares two objects on one of these attributes and communicates what the comparison shows; placing the objects next to each other is given as an example of how.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child says their stick is longer, that the bucket is heavy, or that the big cup holds more juice. An older child sets two sticks together with one end level to see which reaches further, holds one thing in each hand to feel which is heavier, or pours water from one cup into another to see whether it all goes in.

### Limits

The attributes are length, weight and capacity. Earlier: awareness, shown by noticing and talking; no procedure is asked. Later: two objects; the statement does not say how they are compared, and putting them next to each other is its example, not a required method. No units, numbers or measuring tools appear in either statement; measuring length with objects is foundation 3.3 (later age only) and putting three or more objects in order is foundation 3.2. What changes with age: from showing awareness by noticing differences to comparing two objects.

### Common mistakes

Comparing lengths without lining up one end, and judging by whichever far end sticks out. Assuming the bigger object is the heavier one. Assuming the taller container holds more, whatever its width.
