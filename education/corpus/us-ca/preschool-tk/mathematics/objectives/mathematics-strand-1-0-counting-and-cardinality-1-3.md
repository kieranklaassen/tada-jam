## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child responds to being asked how many there are by counting the things. The child may say the final count word again afterwards, but does not yet reliably grasp that this final word stands for the size of the whole group. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child shows that understanding every time: the word said for the last thing counted tells how many there are altogether.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An adult asks how many spoons are on the table. A younger child counts them and may echo the last word ("four... four") without meaning it as the amount; asked again, the child often counts again. An older child counts, gives the last number as the answer, and treats that number as settled without starting over.

### Limits

The statement sets no number range. The neighbouring foundation 1.2 has children counting five or more objects at the earlier age and ten or more at the later age, which shows the size of set in play at this level. The earlier statement itself says the understanding is still forming; the later one asks for it consistently. What changes with age: from counting as a routine reply to the question, to knowing that the last word is the amount.

### Common mistakes

Counting the whole set again each time the question is repeated. Answering with the whole string ("one, two, three, four") and not with one number. Treating the last word as the name of the last object, for example pointing only at the final spoon when asked to show four.
