## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Nothing is expected at the earlier age (3 to 4½ years): the framework marks that band as having no foundation here. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), a child measures how long something is by placing real objects in a line, each touching the next, and sometimes needs an adult's help. A note in the statement says children at this age may not yet grasp that the units have to be the same length as each other.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lines blocks or paper clips along the edge of a book from one end to the other, counts them, and says the book is six blocks long. An adult sometimes helps: starting the row at the edge, keeping it straight, closing a gap.

### Limits

Later age only; the earlier band has no statement. Length only, not weight or capacity. The units are physical objects placed one after another; rulers and standard units are not mentioned. Adult support is sometimes needed. The note allows for a child mixing units of different lengths, so equal units are not yet expected. What changes with age: the skill appears only at the later age; before that, children compare (foundation 3.1) and order (foundation 3.2) without measuring.

### Common mistakes

Leaving gaps between the units, or letting them overlap. Mixing long and short units in one row and counting them as if they were alike, which the note anticipates. Starting short of the object's end or running past the other end.
