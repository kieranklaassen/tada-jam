## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child deals out objects so that each receiver gets an equal amount. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), there are two receivers and only a few objects; the examples given are four or six. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), there are somewhat more objects and there may be more than two receivers; the example given is nine objects for three.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child hands crackers to two dolls in turn, one to each, until none are left, then looks to see that both dolls have as many. An older child deals nine strawberries round three friends in the same way and compares the three piles, by lining them up or by counting.

### Limits

Earlier: two recipients and a small number of objects (four or six in the examples). Later: two or more recipients and a slightly larger number (nine among three in the example). Every example divides evenly, and neither statement mentions leftovers. No division symbol or number sentence is mentioned. What changes with age: more objects and more receivers.

### Common mistakes

Giving out handfuls and not checking that the shares match. Taking "fair" to mean that everyone has some. Judging the shares by how big the piles look and not by how many are in them.
