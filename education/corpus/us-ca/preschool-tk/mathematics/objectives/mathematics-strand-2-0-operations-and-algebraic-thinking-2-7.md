## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child makes a simple repeating pattern of their own with an adult's help; ABAB is the example. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child makes several kinds of repeating pattern (AABBAABB and ABCABC are the examples), or rebuilds a pattern that already exists out of other objects.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child lines up spoon, fork, spoon, fork while an adult asks what comes next. An older child threads two yellow beads, two green, two yellow, two green, or uses three colours in turn. The older child also hears clap, stomp, clap, stomp and lays the same pattern out as leaf, stone, leaf, stone.

### Limits

Repeating patterns only. Earlier: a simple pattern (ABAB), with adult support. Later: adult support is not mentioned; the two halves are joined by "or" (making varied patterns, or remaking an existing one with different objects). Neither statement says how long a pattern must be. Copying and extending someone else's pattern is foundation 2.6. What changes with age: the adult's help drops out of the statement, the units get more varied, and a pattern is carried over to new materials.

### Common mistakes

Laying a row with no repeat in it and calling it a pattern. Stopping after a single unit, so nothing has repeated yet. When remaking a pattern with other objects, matching colours or looks and not the order.
