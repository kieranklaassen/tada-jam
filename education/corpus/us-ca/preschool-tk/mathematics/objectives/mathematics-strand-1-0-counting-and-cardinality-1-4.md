## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child tells how many things are in a little group just by looking, with no counting. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the group is described as small, with one to four things given as an example of small; later (4 to 5½ years) the range is stated as one to five things.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child glances at the dots on a die, a few berries on a plate, or buttons in an adult's open hand, and says the number at once without pointing at each one. A younger child does this for two or three and sometimes four. An older child does it up to five.

### Limits

Earlier: a small collection; one to four is the statement's example, not a fixed bound. Later: one to five, given as the range. At both ages the condition is that the child does not count. The statement says nothing about how the objects are arranged. Larger sets are counted, which is foundation 1.2. What changes with age: the top of the range moves from about four to five.

### Common mistakes

Counting one by one anyway, even for two or three things. Guessing at four and five, or calling every small group "three". Knowing five only in the layout of a die and not when the same five things lie in a line.
