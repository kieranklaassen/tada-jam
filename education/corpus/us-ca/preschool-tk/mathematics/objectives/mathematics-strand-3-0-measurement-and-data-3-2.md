## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child puts objects in order by length or by another attribute; height and capacity are the others named as examples. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) this is done with a few objects, three being the example; later (4 to 5½ years) with slightly more, four or five being the example.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child lines up three sticks from shortest to longest, or stands three dolls in a row by height. An older child does the same with four or five straws or nesting cups, and when handed one more, finds the place where it belongs in the row.

### Limits

Earlier: a few objects (three in the example). Later: a slightly larger number (four or five in the example). Length is named; height and capacity are given as examples of other attributes. No units or numbers are involved, and neither statement says which direction the order runs. What changes with age: only how many objects are ordered.

### Common mistakes

Picking out the biggest and the smallest and placing the ones in between at random. Comparing each object only with its neighbour and without a shared baseline, so the tops step up while the bottoms are uneven. Being unable to slot a new object into a finished row and starting the whole row again.
