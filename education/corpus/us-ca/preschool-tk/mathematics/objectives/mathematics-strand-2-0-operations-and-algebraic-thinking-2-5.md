## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years), a child notices how objects are alike and how they differ in their attributes, and sorts them into at least two groups using a single attribute. At the later age (4 to 5½ years), the child sorts into at least two groups using one attribute or more than one, accurately and flexibly; the statement adds that with two attributes a child may sort on the first and afterwards on the second.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child puts buttons into piles by colour: the red ones here, the blue ones there. An older child sorts the same buttons again by size, and again by how many holes they have. For two attributes the older child sorts by colour first and then splits each colour pile into big and small.

### Limits

Earlier: one attribute, two or more groups. Later: one or more attributes, two or more groups; sorting by two attributes may be done in two steps, so holding both in mind at once is not required. Neither statement names particular attributes such as colour, shape or size. What changes with age: from one attribute to more than one, done accurately, and the ability to sort the same things a second way.

### Common mistakes

Changing the rule partway, for example starting by colour and drifting to shape. Arranging the objects into a picture or a line and not into groups. Holding on to the first way of sorting and being unable to sort the same objects another way.
