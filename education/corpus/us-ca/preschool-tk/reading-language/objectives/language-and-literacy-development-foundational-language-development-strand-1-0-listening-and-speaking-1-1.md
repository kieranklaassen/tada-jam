## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages a child knows what words mean and says them: names of things, words for what people and things do, and words for what things are like. These are words the child meets in daily life, for example in stories, while playing, or when talking with others. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) they are words met often; at the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child's range of such words keeps growing.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child points to the cup when asked, names the dog in a picture book, and says what it is doing ("running") or what it is like ("wet"). An older child uses more exact words for the same things: "mug" as well as "cup", "sprinting", "soaked", and picks up a new word from a story and uses it later in play.

### Limits

The statement sets no number of words and names no word list. Three kinds of words are named: words for objects, for actions, and for attributes. The words come from the child's everyday experience; play, conversation and stories are given as examples of where, not as a complete list. Both understanding and using are asked. What changes with age: from words met often to a wider and growing variety of words.

### Common mistakes

Using one word too broadly, such as "dog" for every four-legged animal, or too narrowly, such as "shoe" only for the child's own shoes. Understanding many more words than the child says; a child who cannot yet name a thing can often still point to it. Mixing up words that are learned as a pair, such as "yesterday" and "tomorrow".
