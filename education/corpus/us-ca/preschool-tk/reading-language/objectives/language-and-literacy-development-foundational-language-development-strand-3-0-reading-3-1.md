## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages a child shows interest in activities that involve reading and writing, or that relate to them, and gets involved in them. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child stays with them for longer and longer and does more of it independently.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child brings a book to an adult and asks for it to be read, looks at the pictures while listening, and joins in with a repeated line. An older child sits alone with a book and turns through all of it, "reads" it to a doll from memory, or spends a long stretch making a card with marks and letters.

### Limits

The statement names no particular activities and sets no number of minutes. The foundations document notes, beneath the two statements, that the activities meant take in writing, handling books, rhyming, chanting, singing, telling stories and being read aloud to. It is about interest and taking part, not about a skill the child performs correctly. Longer time and more independence are named only at the later age, both as growing, with no end point. What changes with age: the same interest, kept up for longer and with less adult help.

### Common mistakes

This is not a skill with a typical error. What varies is attention: a younger child often leaves a book after a few pages or wants the same book every time, and both are usual at this age. Interest often shows only when the child has something to do: turn the page, point, or join in.
