## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child's writing is scribble that looks like letters or characters and can be told apart from their drawings. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child writes a small number of letters or characters that can be recognized, and uses them to stand for an idea or a word.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child makes a row of small separate marks, loops and lines, beside a drawing, and says "that's the writing". An older child writes a few real letters, often ones from their own name, under a drawing or on a pretend shopping list, and says what they stand for.

### Limits

Earlier: letter-like scribble that differs from the child's pictures; no real letters are asked. Later: a few recognizable letters or characters; the statement does not ask that they be the right letters for the word, that words be spelled, or that the forms be neat. Matching letters to sounds is foundation 4.2, and the child's own name is foundation 4.5. No adult support is mentioned here. What changes with age: from marks that look like writing to a few real letters used to mean something.

### Common mistakes

Using the same few letters, usually from the child's name, for every word. Writing letters reversed, rotated, or scattered over the page with no line. Believing a big thing needs a long word and a small thing a short one, so "train" gets many marks and "caterpillar" few.
