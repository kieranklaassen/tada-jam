## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child takes part in talk that goes to and fro, both with grown-ups and with other children. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child gives a reply that fits the subject for one turn or more. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the conversations get longer and more complex, and the child keeps to the subject over several turns.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child is asked "What did you build?" and answers "A garage", which fits the question; the next reply may be about something else. An older child keeps going on the same subject: answers, adds something, asks the other child a question back, and answers again.

### Limits

Earlier: one on-topic turn or more, which is a lower bound. Later: several turns, with no number given. Partners are both adults and other children at both ages. The statement sets no length of time. What changes with age: from one fitting reply to staying on the subject across several turns in longer, more complex talk.

### Common mistakes

Answering with what the child wants to talk about, not with something that fits what was just said. Talking at the same time as the other person, or leaving no gap for a reply. Going silent after one answer, so the exchange stops unless the adult asks again.
