## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child knows a well-known song or rhyme when hearing it, or joins in with it. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child comes up with sounds or words that rhyme. A made-up word counts as much as a real one, and the rhyme does not have to be exact.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child claps along to a known nursery rhyme, does the hand actions, and fills in the last word when the adult stops before it. An older child is given "cat" and answers "hat", "bat" or "zat", or makes up a string such as "silly, billy, willy" while playing.

### Limits

Earlier: the songs and rhymes are familiar ones, and either knowing them or joining in is enough; the child is not asked to make a rhyme. Later: the child makes rhymes. The framework accepts a near-rhyme and an invented word as well as an exact rhyme with a real word. The statement sets no number of rhymes and does not mention adult support, letters or spelling. What changes with age: from taking part in known rhymes to making new ones.

### Common mistakes

Answering with a word that starts with the same sound ("cat", "cup") when asked for a rhyme. Answering with a word linked by meaning ("cat", "dog"). Repeating the same word back.
