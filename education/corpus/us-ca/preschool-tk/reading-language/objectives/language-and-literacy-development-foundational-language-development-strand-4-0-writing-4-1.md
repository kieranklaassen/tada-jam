## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child tries out different ways of holding a range of tools for writing and drawing, and different ways of placing their body while doing so. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child changes their hold and posture so as to control their drawing and writing better.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child holds a thick crayon in a fist, then in the fingers, tries a brush, chalk and a marker, and draws standing at an easel, kneeling, or lying on the floor. An older child shifts the fingers closer to the tip of a pencil, steadies the paper with the other hand, and sits so that the arm can move, in order to make a smaller or straighter mark.

### Limits

The statement names no particular grip as correct and no particular tool. Earlier: trying things out, with a variety of tools. Later: adjusting for more control; the wording says "increased" control and states no standard of neatness. Forming letters is not part of this statement; it comes in foundations 4.2, 4.4 and 4.5. What changes with age: from experimenting with holds and positions to changing them on purpose to gain control.

### Common mistakes

Gripping the tool very hard, or pressing so hard that the tip breaks or the paper tears. Holding the tool far from the tip, which makes the mark hard to steer. Letting the paper slide because the other hand is not holding it.
