## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child tells a story in words, about something that happened or something made up. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the story is short. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) it is longer and holds several details or a plot.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child tells in two or three linked sentences what happened at the park, or makes a toy bear "go to the shop and buy an apple". An older child tells a longer story with a beginning, a problem and an end, or retells a birthday with who came, what they ate and what broke.

### Limits

The statement sets no number of sentences or events. The story may be true or invented at both ages. At the later age the wording asks for several details or a plotline, not necessarily both. This statement sits in the listening and speaking strand: the child tells the story and is not asked to write it. What changes with age: from a short telling to an extended one with details or a plot.

### Common mistakes

Listing events joined by "and then" with no start or ending. Leaving out who the story is about or where it happens, as if the listener had been there. Telling events out of order.
