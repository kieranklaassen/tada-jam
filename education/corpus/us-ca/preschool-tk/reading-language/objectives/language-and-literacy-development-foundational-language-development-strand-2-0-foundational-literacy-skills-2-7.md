## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child shows how a book is handled and how print is laid out. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) this is at a basic level, for example knowing a book's title and cover and turning the pages all in one direction. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) it is more advanced, for example turning a single page at a time and knowing which way up print goes and which way it runs.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child takes a book, turns it so the cover faces up, points at the title when asked where the name of the book is, and turns pages from front to back, sometimes several at once. An older child turns one page at a time, turns an upside-down book the right way round, and runs a finger under a line of print in the direction it is read.

### Limits

All the behaviors named are given as examples, not as a full list. Earlier examples: pages turned in one direction, and the cover and title recognized. Later examples: turning one page at a time, and knowing print's orientation and direction. The statement does not say which direction; that depends on the language of the book. No reading of words is asked. What changes with age: from basic handling of the book to finer handling and to knowing how the print itself runs.

### Common mistakes

Holding the book upside down or starting from the back. Turning several pages at once and not noticing the skipped part of the story. Pointing at the pictures, not the print, when asked where the reading happens, or moving a finger along the line in the wrong direction.
