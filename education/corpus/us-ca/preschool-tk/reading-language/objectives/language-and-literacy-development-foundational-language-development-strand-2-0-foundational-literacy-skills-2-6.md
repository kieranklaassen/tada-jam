## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child knows that printed words carry a message and are there to be read. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child can tell what a few well-known pieces of print around them mean.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child points at the words under a picture and asks "What does it say?", or holds up a shopping list and pretends to read it out. An older child sees the stop sign, their own name on a coat hook, or the name on a cereal box, and says what it says or what it is for.

### Limits

Earlier: no reading of any word is asked, only knowing that print is for reading and carries a message. Later: a few pieces of print, and only familiar print from the child's surroundings; the statement names no examples and no number beyond "a few". The statement does not ask the child to sound out the letters. What changes with age: from knowing that print carries a message to knowing what a few familiar signs or labels say.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the picture is what gets read, and that the print is decoration. Knowing a logo only in its usual colors and shape, and not knowing the same word in plain type. Treating any row of letters as saying whatever the child wants it to say.
