## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages a child gives explanations, expresses their own opinion, and describes things to others. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) these are detailed; the wording for the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) does not ask for detail.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child says what a rock looks and feels like ("it's bumpy and gray"), says "I like the red one best", and gives a short reason ("it fell because I pushed it"). An older child adds more: tells the rock's color, its weight and where it was found, says why the red one is better, and goes through the steps of how the tower came down.

### Limits

The statement names no topic, no length and no number of details. Three kinds of communicating are named: describing, giving an opinion, and explaining. Adult support is not mentioned. What changes with age: only the word "detailed" is added at the later age.

### Common mistakes

Giving an opinion with no reason, or with the reason "because I do". Describing with one all-purpose word such as "nice" or "big". Retelling what happened in order without saying what caused it, when asked why.
