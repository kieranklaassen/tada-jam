## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This is about the first sound of spoken words, with help from an adult or from objects or pictures. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child finds which spoken words begin with the same sound. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child separates the beginning sound from the rest of one word and says that sound by itself.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child hears "ball" and picks, from a toy boat and a toy sock, the one that starts the same way, while the adult says the three words slowly. An older child is handed a toy sun, is asked what it starts with, and says "sss". The child works with what is heard and said; no letters are involved.

### Limits

Only the first sound of a word is covered; middle and last sounds are not mentioned. The work is in speech: the statement does not mention letters or print. At both ages the child has support, from an adult or from pictures or objects; doing it unaided is not asked. The statement sets no number of words or sounds. What changes with age: from matching words by their first sound to saying the first sound of one word by itself.

### Common mistakes

Matching by meaning instead of sound: pairing "cat" with "dog" because both are animals. Picking a word that rhymes ("ball" and "wall") when asked for one that starts the same. Saying the whole first syllable ("ba") or both consonants of a blend ("st" for "stop") when asked for the first sound.
