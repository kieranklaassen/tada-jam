## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child makes marks that stand for their name. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child writes their name with almost all of it right.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child makes a squiggle, a few letter-like shapes, or just the first letter in the corner of a drawing and says "that's my name". An older child writes the letters of their first name in order, perhaps with one letter reversed, one missing, or uneven sizes.

### Limits

Earlier: any marks the child means as their name; real letters are not required. Later: the word used is "nearly", so small errors are within the statement. The statement does not say first name or full name, uppercase or lowercase, and does not mention adult support or copying from a model. What changes with age: from marks that stand for the name to the name itself with small errors.

### Common mistakes

Reversing single letters such as S, N or J, or writing the whole name from right to left. Leaving out a letter in the middle, or putting two letters in the wrong order. Running out of room and placing the last letters above or below the others.
