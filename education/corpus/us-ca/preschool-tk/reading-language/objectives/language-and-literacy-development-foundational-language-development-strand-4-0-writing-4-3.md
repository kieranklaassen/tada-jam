## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child tells an adult what they are thinking so it can be written down, when the adult has offered that help. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child shows that they want longer thoughts and ideas put into writing, and gets an adult to help with it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child finishes a painting; the adult asks "Shall I write what it is?", and the child says "It's my dog in the rain" and watches the words being written. An older child comes to the adult with paper and says "Write that this is a map to my house, and you go past the big tree, and then you turn", telling several linked things to be written.

### Limits

At both ages an adult helps; the statement does not ask the child to write the words alone. Earlier: the adult makes the offer and the child joins in. Later: the wording has the child engaging the adult's help, and the thoughts are called extended; no length is stated. What changes with age: from accepting an adult's offer to seeking the adult's help, and from a thought to an extended one.

### Common mistakes

Speaking much faster than an adult can write and not waiting. Changing the message halfway, or forgetting how it started. Not yet linking the written marks to the exact words said: asked to "read it back", the child says something different each time.
