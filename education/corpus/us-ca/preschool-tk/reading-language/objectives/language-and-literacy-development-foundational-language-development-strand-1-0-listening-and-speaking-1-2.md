## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child knows and says words that name groups of things and that tell how the things in a group belong together. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) these are words the child meets often. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the words become more and more exact.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child puts the apple and the banana together and calls them "fruit", or says "a cat is an animal". An older child uses narrower group words: sorts toy animals into "farm animals" and "zoo animals", says that a robin is a kind of bird, and that a hammer and a saw are both tools.

### Limits

The statement names no particular categories and sets no number of them. It asks for both understanding and use. At the earlier age the words are ones the child commonly meets. What changes with age: the category words go from common, broad ones to more specific ones.

### Common mistakes

Naming each member but not the group: the child says "apple" and "banana" and has no word for "fruit". Treating a group word as the name of one member, for example saying a dog is not an animal because it is a dog. Putting things together by a link in a story (the dog goes with the bone) instead of by kind (the dog goes with the cat).
