## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child asks questions for two purposes: to find something out, and to check or clear up whether they have understood. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child asks questions. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child also asks a further question after getting an answer.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child holds up a shell and asks "What is this?", or asks "This one goes here?" before placing a puzzle piece. An older child hears the answer and asks again: told "It's a shell", the child asks "Who lived in it?" and then "Where did the snail go?".

### Limits

The statement names no question words and sets no number of questions. Two purposes are named: getting information, and making sure of what was understood. Follow-up questions appear only at the later age. What changes with age: the child goes from single questions to a question that builds on the answer just given.

### Common mistakes

Asking "why?" again and again as a way to keep the adult talking, without listening to the answer. Making a statement with a rising voice instead of forming a question ("Doggy go?"). Asking a next question that has nothing to do with the answer given.
