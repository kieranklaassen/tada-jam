## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This is about texts that give information. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child shows a basic grasp of such a text once they have met it a few times. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the grasp is deeper: the child asks questions, works out things the text does not say outright, and links the text to what they already know.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child, after hearing a book about trucks a few times, points to the digger and says what it does, or finds the page that shows the wheels. An older child hears that ducks have oily feathers and says "so they don't get wet", says "we saw one at the pond", and asks "Do they sleep on the water?".

### Limits

Earlier: a basic understanding, and only after meeting the text a few times. Later: three abilities are named as the means: linking to what the child already knows, inferring, and asking questions. The statement names no topics and no kind of text beyond "informational", and sets no length. It does not ask the child to read the text themselves. What changes with age: from knowing what a well-known text says to reasoning about it and asking beyond it.

### Common mistakes

Treating an information book as a story and asking what happens next or who the "good one" is. Repeating one striking fact and taking it for the whole subject. Stating something from their own experience that the text did not say, as if it came from the book.
