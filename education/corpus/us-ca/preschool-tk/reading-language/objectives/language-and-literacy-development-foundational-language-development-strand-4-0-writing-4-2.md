## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the framework prints no foundation, so nothing is expected here at that age. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child, helped by an adult, writes a small number of letters that can be recognized and that the child means to stand for the sounds those letters make.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child: the framework states nothing for this age. An older child wants to label a drawing of a sun, says the word slowly with an adult, hears "sss", and writes an S. The child may write M for "mom" or B for "ball": one letter for a sound they can hear, most often the first sound of the word.

### Limits

Earlier: no foundation is printed for this age; nothing is expected. Later: a few letters, with adult support; the letters need only be recognizable, and what counts is that the child means each one to stand for its sound. The statement does not ask for whole words, correct spelling, or any number of letters beyond "a few". It does not say uppercase or lowercase. The foundations document notes, beneath the two statements, that this foundation is for languages written with an alphabet only (Chinese characters, used for Mandarin and Cantonese, are not one), and that nothing is expected at the earlier age because writing a letter for a sound rests on several other skills, such as hearing the sounds in words, control of the hand and knowing which sound goes with which letter, that younger children are still building. What changes with age: the expectation exists only at the later age.

### Common mistakes

Writing a letter for the first sound only and stopping. Choosing a letter by its name instead of its sound, such as Y for the /w/ sound at the start of "went", or R for "are". Forming a letter reversed; the letter can still be recognized and the sound choice can still be right.
