## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child knows and says words that tell how big a thing is and where it is. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) this covers words like "on" and "tiny" and easy comparing words like "bigger". At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the words for telling and comparing size and place become more exact, like "between" and "longer".

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child puts the bear on the chair when asked, calls a pebble "tiny", and holds two sticks side by side to say which is bigger. An older child lays two ribbons next to each other and says which is longer, and puts the cup between the plate and the spoon when told to.

### Limits

The words in the statement are examples, not a word list: "tiny", "on" and "bigger" at the earlier age, "longer" and "between" at the later age. Comparing at the earlier age is called simple. The statement sets no number of words and asks for no measuring with numbers or units. Both understanding and using are asked. What changes with age: from general words and simple comparisons to more exact words for describing and comparing.

### Common mistakes

Using "big" for every kind of size, so a long ribbon and a tall tower are both just "big". Mixing up place words that depend on a point of view, such as "in front of" and "behind", and finding "between" hard because it involves two other things. Judging "bigger" by one direction only, usually height.
