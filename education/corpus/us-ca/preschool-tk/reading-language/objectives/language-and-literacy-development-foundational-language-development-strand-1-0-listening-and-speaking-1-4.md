## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child puts what they think into words with the usual shapes of words and the usual ways of building a sentence. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) these are the common forms. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child also uses rarer forms, and what the child says with them is more complex.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child says "I jumped", "two cats", "my shoe is wet", and asks "Where is it?" in a full short sentence. An older child joins ideas in one sentence with "because", "if" or "when" ("I put my coat on because it is cold"), and says things such as "the tower that fell down" or "she was pushed".

### Limits

The statement lists no particular word forms or sentence types and sets no sentence length. It is about the child's own talk, not about naming grammar rules. It does not say the forms must be free of errors. What changes with age: less common forms are added to the common ones, and the ideas expressed are described as complex.

### Common mistakes

Putting a regular ending on an irregular word: "goed", "runned", "mouses", "foots". Mixing up "he" and "she", or "him" and "he". Keeping statement order in a question: "Why he is crying?" in place of "Why is he crying?".
