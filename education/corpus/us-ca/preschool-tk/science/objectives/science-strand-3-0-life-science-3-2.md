## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child shows what they know about what bodies do, for example walking, breathing, sleeping and eating, in people and in other animals. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) this knowledge is just beginning. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child knows more and shows it by observing and describing in more detail.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child says the dog is panting, puts a hand on their own chest to feel it go up and down, and says "the baby is sleeping, the cat is sleeping too". An older child says that we breathe in air through the nose and mouth, that food goes down to the stomach, that a fish breathes in water, and that a bird walks on two legs and a dog on four.

### Limits

The four processes listed (walking, breathing, sleeping, eating) are examples and are the same at both ages. Both humans and other animals are covered; plants are not named in this statement. Earlier: emerging knowledge. Later: greater knowledge, shown through more detailed observation and description. Names of organs are not asked. What changes with age: the amount of knowledge and the detail of the description.

### Common mistakes

Thinking only animals that look like us do these things: a fish does not breathe, a worm does not eat. Thinking food stays in the stomach or simply vanishes. Thinking a sleeping body has stopped working altogether.
