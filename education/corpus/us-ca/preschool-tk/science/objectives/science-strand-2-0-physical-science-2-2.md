## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This takes place while the child plays and investigates together with others. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child notices light, shadows and sound and explores them through the senses and by handling materials and objects. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child handles a range of different things to explore how the properties of light, shadows and sound change, and describes those changes.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child taps a pot with a spoon, then a box, stands in a sunbeam, and waves a hand to see its shadow move. An older child moves a toy nearer to a flashlight and says the shadow gets bigger, or says that the tight rubber band sounds higher than the loose one and the big drum sounds lower than the small one.

### Limits

The title speaks of waves, but neither age statement uses the word; the child works with sound, light and shadows as they are seen and heard. Earlier: noticing and exploring; describing is not yet asked. Later: describing changes in properties; the statement does not list the properties (louder or softer, brighter or dimmer are our examples). Explaining how light or sound travels is not mentioned. The setting at both ages is play and shared investigations. What changes with age: from noticing and exploring to changing something on purpose and describing what changed.

### Common mistakes

Treating a shadow as a thing that belongs to the object and not linking it to the light: looking for the shadow on the side that faces the lamp. Mixing up loud with high and soft with low. Believing a shadow shows the object's colors or its face.
