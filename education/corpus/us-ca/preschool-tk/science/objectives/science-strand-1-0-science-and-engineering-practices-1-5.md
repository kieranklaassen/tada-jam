## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child says in a simple way what they think will happen, gives a simple reason for it, and, helped by an adult, finds out by trying it for real. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the predictions are more detailed and build on what the child has seen and done before. The older child makes a plan, helped by an adult, for checking a prediction, and is starting to be able to talk about why it turned out right or wrong.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child holds a cork over a tub of water, says "it will float because it's little", drops it in, and looks. An older child says "the big wooden block will float too, because the wooden spoon did", decides with the adult to test three things one at a time, and afterwards says why the guess about the coin was wrong.

### Limits

Adult support is stated for the checking at the earlier age and for making the plan at the later age; it is not stated for making the prediction. Earlier: predictions and reasons are both called simple, and the check is through concrete experience. Later: talking about why a prediction was right or wrong is called an emerging ability, so it is only beginning. The statement names no topics. What changes with age: predictions draw on earlier experience, the child helps plan the check, and begins to discuss the result.

### Common mistakes

Changing the prediction after seeing the result and saying "I knew that". Giving a reason that restates the guess ("it will sink because it sinks"). Reasoning from one feature only, most often size: big things sink, small things float.
