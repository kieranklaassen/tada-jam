## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child is starting to understand that digital devices and tools differ in what they are for, for example taking photos or videos, and uses digital tools with adult support for everyday needs and goals. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child knows a wider range of digital devices and tools and what each does, for example practicing or learning a new skill or looking up information, and uses them with less adult support for those same everyday purposes.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child holds a tablet with an adult and presses the button to take a photo of a block tower, or knows that the phone is for calling grandma and the camera is for pictures. An older child asks to look up what a hedgehog eats, picks the tablet for that and a kitchen timer for timing, and follows a short video to practice a new way of folding paper with the adult nearby.

### Limits

The uses listed are examples: taking photos or videos at the earlier age; looking up information and learning or practicing a skill at the later age. Adult support is stated at both ages, less of it at the later age; fully independent use is not asked. The statement names no particular devices and sets no amount of time. The purpose is the child's own everyday needs and goals. What changes with age: more kinds of devices known, and less adult help in using them.

### Common mistakes

Treating every screen as a touchscreen: swiping a television or a printed photo. Thinking a device knows things by itself, or that a person in a video call is inside it. Using one device for everything and not seeing that another tool fits the job better.
