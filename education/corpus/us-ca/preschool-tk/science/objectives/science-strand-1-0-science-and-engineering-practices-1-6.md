## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child runs experiments or investigations, alone or together with other children and grown-ups. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) these are simple and serve to test the child's ideas about what they have observed. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) they are more complex and the child keeps at them more persistently. The older child also takes what was seen and found in earlier explorations to come up with new questions and to test their hypotheses.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child thinks the heavy car goes faster, lets two cars go down a ramp, and watches which arrives first. An older child repeats the run several times, then changes the ramp's height or covers it with a towel, and asks a new question from what happened: "Will it be slower on the carpet too?".

### Limits

The statement names no subject matter and sets no number of trials. The child may work alone or with peers and adults at both ages. Earlier: simple investigations that test the child's own ideas. Later: more complex ones, more persistence, and new questions that come from earlier results. The statement does not ask for changing one thing at a time or for written results. What changes with age: complexity, persistence, and building one investigation on the last.

### Common mistakes

Changing several things at once (a different car and a different ramp) and then not knowing which one made the difference. Trying once and treating the single result as settled. Seeing only the result they expected and ignoring a run that gave the opposite result.
