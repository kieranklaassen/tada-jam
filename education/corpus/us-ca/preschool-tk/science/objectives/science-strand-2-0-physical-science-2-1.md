## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child explores objects and materials and says what they are like and what physical properties they have, for example their color, shape, size, weight, sound, smell and texture. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the materials are grouped as solid or not solid. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the description is more detailed and the materials are solids, liquids or gases.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child squeezes clay, pours water, knocks on wood and metal, and says "hard", "wet", "heavy", "it makes a loud sound". An older child says more about each: the sponge is light, soft, full of holes and gets heavy when wet; the water takes the shape of the cup; the air in the balloon can be felt when it is let out.

### Limits

The properties listed (color, shape, weight and the rest) are examples, not a checklist, and are the same at both ages. Earlier: materials are solid or nonsolid; the words liquid and gas are not used. Later: solid, liquid or gas, with more detail. The statement asks for describing, not for explaining why materials differ, and sets no number of materials. What changes with age: nonsolid is split into liquid and gas, and descriptions get more detailed.

### Common mistakes

Taking big for heavy: expecting a large foam block to weigh more than a small stone. Not counting air as a material at all, because it cannot be seen. Calling sand or a powder a liquid because it pours.
