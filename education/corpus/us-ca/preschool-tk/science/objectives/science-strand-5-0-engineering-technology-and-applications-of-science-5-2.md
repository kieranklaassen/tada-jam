## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This is about the way tools and designed solutions help people, the child and others, with what they need. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child notices and explores this with adult support, for needs and goals in daily life. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child explores it in more detail and, with adult support, works out different solutions for needs of their family and community.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child tries a step stool to reach the sink, uses tongs to pick up something small, and hears from an adult that grandpa's glasses help him see. An older child looks at how a ramp helps a wheelchair or a stroller, compares a spoon, a fork and chopsticks for picking up peas, and with an adult makes two different holders so the family's shoes stop piling up at the door.

### Limits

Earlier: noticing and exploring, with adult support. Later: exploring in more detail; adult support is stated for developing solutions, not for the exploring. The later statement asks for different solutions, so more than one, for needs of the child's family and community. No particular tools are named. What changes with age: from seeing how tools help to working out several solutions for other people's needs.

### Common mistakes

Seeing a tool only as the thing itself and not as an answer to a need: scissors are "for scissors". Designing for their own wish and not for what the other person needs. Thinking there is one right solution and stopping at the first.
