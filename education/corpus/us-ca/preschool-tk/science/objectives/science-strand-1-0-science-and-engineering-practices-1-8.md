## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child keeps a record of what was observed or found out, and shows what they noticed and how they understand a science idea through some form of representation. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) recording is done with adult support and the representations are simple: role-play, movement, models, drawings and other means. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the record holds more detail, is made with some adult support, and the representations are more elaborate, with diagrams and charts added to the list.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child draws the snail seen in the garden while an adult writes the child's words beside it, or crawls slowly across the floor to show how it moved. An older child draws the bean plant each week with its new leaves, sticks a mark on a wall chart for each sunny or rainy day, or builds a clay model of the snail with its shell and feelers.

### Limits

Earlier: adult support for recording; simple representations. Later: some adult support; more detail; more elaborate representations. The forms listed (role-play, movement, models, drawings, and at the later age also diagrams and charts) end with "other methods", so the list is open. Writing words or numbers is not required by the statement. What changes with age: charts and diagrams are added, detail grows, and adult help shrinks.

### Common mistakes

Drawing what they know a thing looks like (a sun with a face, a flower with five round petals) and not what is in front of them. Leaving out the part that changed, which was the point of the record. Treating a chart as a picture to decorate, without linking each mark to one thing observed.
