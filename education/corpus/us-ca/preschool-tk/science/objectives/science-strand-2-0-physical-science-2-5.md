## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Helped by an adult at both ages, a child shows they are aware of where things get the energy they need, and this covers living things as well as nonliving ones. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the awareness is that things need an energy source in order to work. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) it is an awareness of the different energy sources things need, and the child describes the changes they see those sources bring about.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child, talking with an adult, says the toy car stopped because its battery is empty, that the lamp has to be plugged in, and that "I need to eat so I can run". An older child names different sources: food for people and the dog, sun for the plant, batteries for the flashlight, wind for the pinwheel. The older child also says what changes: the pinwheel spins faster when the wind blows harder, and the plant kept in the dark went pale.

### Limits

Adult support is stated at both ages. Both living and nonliving things are covered at both ages. The statement names no particular energy sources; the ones in these notes are our examples. It asks for awareness and, at the later age, for describing observed changes; it does not ask what energy is or how it passes from one thing to another. What changes with age: from knowing that things need an energy source to telling different sources apart and describing what they change.

### Common mistakes

Thinking only things that move by themselves need energy, so a lamp or a plant needs none. Thinking plants take in food from the soil the way animals eat. Saying a toy "died" or "is tired" and meaning it literally, without linking it to the battery.
