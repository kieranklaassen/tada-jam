## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages a child spots a problem while playing or in their daily dealings and tries to solve it, either alone or working together with other children and grown-ups. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the solutions tried are simple. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) they take several steps.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child sees that the ball has rolled under the shelf and tries reaching with an arm, then with a stick. An older child whose ramp is too low for the car to reach the box fetches more blocks, raises the ramp, moves the box nearer, and tries again.

### Limits

The problems are ones met in play and in everyday dealings; the statement names none. The child may work alone or with peers and adults at both ages. Trying a solution is what is asked; the statement does not say the solution must work. What changes with age: from simple solutions to multistep ones.

### Common mistakes

Repeating the same try with more force (pushing the same block again) instead of changing something. Saying "it's broken" or asking an adult to fix it before saying what the problem is. In a several-step fix, doing the steps in an order that undoes an earlier one.
