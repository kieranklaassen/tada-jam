## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages a child watches and explores how plants, animals and people grow, and makes clear they understand that living things change as they get bigger and older. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the changes named are changes over time in size and in other abilities. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) this understanding is stronger, and the child also describes the transformations within one living thing's life cycle, for example being born, growing, reproducing and dying.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child stands against a height mark on the wall, looks at a baby photo and says "I couldn't walk then", and checks a bean sprout each day to see it get taller. An older child puts pictures of egg, caterpillar, chrysalis and butterfly in order, says the seed grows into a plant that makes new seeds, and says that old animals die.

### Limits

Humans, animals and plants are covered at both ages. Earlier: the changes named are in size and in other capacities, as time passes. Later: the life cycle of an individual; the stages given are examples (birth, growth, reproduction and death), and no particular species is named. The statement does not ask how reproduction works. What changes with age: describing life-cycle transformations is added at the later age.

### Common mistakes

Linking age to size alone: a tall child must be older than a short adult. Thinking a seed or an egg is not alive until it sprouts or hatches. Not seeing that the baby and the adult in two pictures are one and the same individual at two times.
