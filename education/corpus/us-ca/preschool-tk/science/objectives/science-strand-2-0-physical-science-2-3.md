## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages a child, while playing and investigating with others, explores with the senses how objects and materials change and describes the changes, for example a change in temperature, texture, shape or color. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child describes in more detail and also explains the changes, and a change of form joins the examples.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child watches an ice cube in a warm hand and says "it's getting little, it's water now", or mixes blue and yellow paint and says "green!". An older child says the ice melted because the hand is warm, that the wet sand holds its shape and the dry sand does not, and that the dough got hard because it was left out.

### Limits

The kinds of change listed are examples: color, shape, texture and temperature at both ages, with form added at the later age. Earlier: explore and describe. Later: describe in more detail and explain. The statement does not use the words melting, freezing, dissolving or reversible. It does not say the child's explanation must be the scientific one. What changes with age: explaining is added, and so is the example of a change of form.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that a thing which changed form is gone or has become a different thing: the melted ice "disappeared", the dissolved sugar is "not there". Describing only the end state and not what it was like before. Giving their own action as the cause without the condition ("because I put it there", not "because it was warm there").
