## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child knows that plants and animals have to be looked after, and is starting to understand that giving food and water helps people, animals and plants to grow and stay alive. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child describes what people, animals and plants need in order to grow and survive, for example shelter, sunshine, sleep, water and food.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child waters a plant with a small can, puts food in the pet's bowl with an adult, and says "it's thirsty". An older child says the plant by the window grows better because it gets sun, that the rabbit needs food, water and a hutch to sleep in, and that people need sleep as well as food.

### Limits

Earlier: care in general, with feeding and watering as the two things named; the understanding is called emerging. Later: needs for growth and survival; shelter, sunshine, sleep, water and food are examples, not a full list, and the statement does not say which need belongs to which living thing. Humans, animals and plants are named at both ages. What changes with age: from knowing that feeding and watering help to describing a wider set of needs.

### Common mistakes

Giving a plant far too much water, or food meant for people, on the idea that more care is better. Thinking plants need the same things as people in the same form, for example that a plant eats soil. Naming wants as needs: the dog needs toys, the child needs candy.
