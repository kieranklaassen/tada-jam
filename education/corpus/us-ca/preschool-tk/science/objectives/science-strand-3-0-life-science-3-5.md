## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child identifies where people and well-known plants and animals live, and makes clear that they understand that not all living things share one kind of habitat. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child sees that each habitat fits the particular needs of the living thing that lives in it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child puts the toy fish in the blue cloth "water", the bird in the tree, and the cow in the field, and says "fish live in water, we live in a house". An older child says the fish has to be in water because it breathes there, the cactus lives where it is dry because it keeps water inside, and the polar bear has thick fur for the cold.

### Limits

Earlier: people and familiar animals and plants; the child identifies where each lives and shows they know habitats differ. Later: the link between a habitat and the needs of what lives there. The statement names no particular habitats or species. The later wording does not repeat "familiar". What changes with age: from knowing where things live to knowing that the place fits what the living thing needs.

### Common mistakes

Naming where an animal was seen (the zoo, a book, the pet shop) as where it lives. Thinking any animal could live anywhere if it wanted to. Leaving plants out: not thinking of a plant as having a place it needs.
