## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child sets things or happenings side by side and says how they are alike and how they are not. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the comparison rests on properties that can be observed. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) it rests on physical properties and also on what things are for, and the child gives a more detailed account of what is alike and what differs.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child holds a shell and a stone and says "both hard, this one is shiny". An older child compares a spoon and a spade: both have a handle and both scoop, one is metal and small and for eating, the other is big and for digging.

### Limits

Earlier: properties the child can observe; function is not named. Later: physical properties and functions, with more detail. Both likenesses and differences are asked at both ages. The statement sets no number of objects or properties and names none. What changes with age: function is added as a ground for comparing, and the description gets more detailed.

### Common mistakes

Naming differences only and not saying what is the same. Describing each thing in turn ("this is red; this is big") without comparing them on the same property. Calling the larger thing heavier without lifting both.
