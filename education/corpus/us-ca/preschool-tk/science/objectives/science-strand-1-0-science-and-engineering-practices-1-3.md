## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages a child is curious about the things and happenings around them and asks about them. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the questions are simple. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child is better able to word questions that are specific and detailed.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child watches an ant and asks "What's it doing?" or "Why?". An older child asks "Where is the ant taking that crumb?" or "Why do the puddles go away when the sun comes out?".

### Limits

The statement names no topics and sets no number of questions. The subject is whatever the child meets in their surroundings. Curiosity is asked at both ages. Testing an idea is not part of this statement; that is foundations 1.5 and 1.6. What changes with age: from simple questions to specific, detailed ones.

### Common mistakes

Asking a bare "why?" without saying what about. Making a statement and meaning it as a question ("The ice is gone."). Asking, then walking away before any answer or a closer look.
