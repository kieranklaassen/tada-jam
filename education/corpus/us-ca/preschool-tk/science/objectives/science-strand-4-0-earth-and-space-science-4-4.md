## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This is about how what people do, and the resources they use, affect the environment and the community. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child notices this when an adult prompts and supports them, and joins in activities that care for the environment. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child investigates it with adult support, talks in simple terms about ways of caring for the environment, and joins in activities for its care.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child, with an adult pointing it out, sees litter on the playground and helps pick it up, turns off a dripping tap, or puts paper in the recycling bin. An older child looks with an adult at how much paper the group throws away in a day, says "we can draw on both sides", and helps water the garden with collected rainwater.

### Limits

Earlier: noticing, with adult prompting and support. Later: investigating, with adult support, and discussing in simple terms. Taking part in caring activities is asked at both ages. The statement names no particular resources, actions or environmental problems. What changes with age: from noticing when prompted to investigating and to talking about ways of caring for the environment.

### Common mistakes

Thinking trash is gone once it is in the bin or out of sight. Thinking water from the tap has no end and comes from nowhere. Seeing care for the environment as a rule to obey ("we mustn't") without linking it to what happens to plants, animals or people.
