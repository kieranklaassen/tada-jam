## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages a child looks at the clouds, stars, moon and sun, the natural things seen in the sky, and tells what they are like. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child also describes how these appear to change and the patterns in how they move.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child points up and says "the moon is round", "the clouds are white and moving", "the sun is too bright to look at". An older child says the sun is over the gate in the morning and over the tree in the afternoon, that the moon was a thin curve last week and is round now, and that clouds change shape and the stars show only when it is dark.

### Limits

Four things are named at both ages: clouds, stars, the moon and the sun. Earlier: observing and describing. Later: patterns of movement and apparent changes as well; "apparent" means how it looks from the ground, and the statement does not ask why the changes happen. Planets, the Earth's turning and the names of moon phases are not mentioned. What changes with age: from describing what is in the sky to describing how it moves and seems to change.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the sun and moon follow the child or move because the child walks. Thinking the moon comes out only at night and the sun "goes to sleep". Thinking the moon really changes shape, or that clouds are covering part of it.
