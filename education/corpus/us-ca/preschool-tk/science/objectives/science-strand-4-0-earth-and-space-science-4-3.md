## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the child notices when the weather changes and describes the change, and notices what weather and the change of seasons do to plants, to animals and to the child's own life. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child observes and describes weather changes and gives examples of what changing weather and seasons do to plants, animals and the child's own life.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child looks out and says "it's raining now, it was sunny before", and puts on boots because of the puddles. An older child says "when it's cold we wear coats and the pond has ice", "the leaves fall off in the fall", "the birds come back in spring", and "the plants droop when it hasn't rained".

### Limits

Three things are affected in the wording at both ages: the child's own life, plants, and animals. Earlier: noticing the effects. Later: giving examples of them. The statement names no kinds of weather and no seasons, and asks for no measuring or forecasting. Seasons differ by place, and the statement does not say which seasonal changes are meant. What changes with age: from noticing the effects of weather and seasons to giving examples of them.

### Common mistakes

Taking today's weather for the season: one warm day means it is summer. Linking weather to the child's own actions or wishes ("it rained because I forgot my coat"). Describing the weather now without saying what changed.
