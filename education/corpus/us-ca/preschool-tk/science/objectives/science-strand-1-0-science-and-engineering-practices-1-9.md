## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages a child uses mathematical thinking to put amounts to what they observe, to make sense of it, and to answer questions that come up in daily activities. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) this is done with adult support. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) it is done more precisely and with some adult support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child counts with an adult how many seeds came up, or lines up two leaves to see which is longer. An older child counts the ladybugs on each of two plants and says which has more, or measures a sunflower with a row of blocks and says how many blocks tall it is.

### Limits

The statement names no particular mathematics: no number range, no units, no kind of chart. Earlier: with adult support. Later: with some adult support and greater precision; the statement does not define the precision. The questions come from everyday activities. What changes with age: more precision and less adult help.

### Common mistakes

Answering "which has more?" by which group takes up more room, without counting. Counting some items twice or skipping some when the things are scattered or moving. Giving a number without saying what was counted or measured.
