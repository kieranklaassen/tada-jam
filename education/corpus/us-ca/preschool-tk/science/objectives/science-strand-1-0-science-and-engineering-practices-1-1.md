## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At both ages a child looks closely at things and happenings, explores them hands-on with the senses, and says what was noticed. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child's account has more detail.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child picks up a pine cone, turns it over, smells it, shakes it, and says "it's prickly". An older child does the same and tells more: "it's brown, the bits open out at the bottom, it smells like a tree, and it's lighter than the rock".

### Limits

The statement names no particular objects, events or senses and sets no number of details. Describing is part of it at both ages; observing in silence is not the whole of it. Tools are not mentioned here; they belong to foundation 1.7. What changes with age: only the amount of detail in what the child says.

### Common mistakes

Saying what the thing is ("a leaf") or whether they like it, in place of what they notice about it. Using one sense only, nearly always sight. Reporting what they expect or already know ("leaves are green") when the leaf in the hand is brown.
