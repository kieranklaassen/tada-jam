## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child explores earth materials and says what they are like, for example their texture, color, shape, weight and size. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) the materials named as examples are air, water, soil, rocks and sand. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) the child also compares materials with each other, telling how they are alike and unlike in such features.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child lets dry sand run through the fingers, squeezes wet soil, lifts a rock, and says "the sand is tickly, the rock is heavy and cold". An older child puts sand and soil side by side and says the soil is darker and holds together and the sand is lighter in color and runs through the fingers, or sorts rocks into smooth and rough.

### Limits

The characteristics listed (texture, color, weight and the rest) are examples, the same at both ages. Air, water, sand, soil and rocks are named as examples of earth materials at the earlier age; the later statement names no materials. Air and water count as earth materials here. Later: comparing and contrasting is added. The statement does not ask where the materials come from or how they form. What changes with age: from describing a material to comparing materials by their features.

### Common mistakes

Not counting air as a material, because it cannot be seen or held. Calling every small stone "a rock" and every loose material "dirt", without noticing how they differ. Thinking wet sand and dry sand are two different materials.
