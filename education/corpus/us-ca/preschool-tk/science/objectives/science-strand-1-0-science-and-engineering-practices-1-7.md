## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

A child identifies tools for observing and for measuring and uses them. At the earlier age (3 to 4½ years) this covers some tools and happens with adult support. At the later age (4 to 5½ years) it covers a wider range of tools, the child uses them more spontaneously, and some adult support is still part of it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child is handed a magnifying glass and shown how to hold it over a beetle, or pours sand into a measuring cup while an adult holds it steady. An older child fetches the magnifier, a balance scale, a ruler or a tape measure without being told, and uses it to look closer or to find which of two things is heavier or longer.

### Limits

The statement names no particular tools; any tool named in these notes is our example. Earlier: some tools, with adult support. Later: a greater variety, used more spontaneously, still with some adult support. Reading numbers off a scale and using standard units are not mentioned. What changes with age: more kinds of tools, more of the child's own initiative, less adult help.

### Common mistakes

Holding a magnifying glass against the eye or flat on the object, so nothing looks bigger. Starting a ruler or a row of blocks partway along the object, or leaving gaps between the blocks. Reading a balance scale backwards: taking the side that goes up as the heavier one.
