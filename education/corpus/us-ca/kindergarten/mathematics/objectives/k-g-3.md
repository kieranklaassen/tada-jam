## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child says of a shape whether it is flat, meaning two-dimensional and lying in a plane, or solid, meaning three-dimensional.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts a mixed tray into things that lie flat on the table, such as a paper circle or a drawn square, and things that can be held all the way round, such as a ball, a can or a box. The child runs a hand over each one and says "flat" or "solid".

### Limits

The statement asks only for the two-way split between flat and solid. The statement itself lists no particular shapes. The cluster heading it stands under names nine: circle, triangle, square, rectangle and hexagon, which are flat, and sphere, cylinder, cone and cube, which are solid. The statement does not ask the child to name the shapes; naming is a neighbouring statement.

### Common mistakes

Calling a solid by the name of one of its flat faces, such as "circle" for a ball or a can and "square" for a cube. Putting a drawing of a cube with the solids because of what it shows.
