## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child puts two objects against each other to compare them on a measurable feature they share, finds which one has more of it and which has less, and says what the difference is. The example given is two children whose heights are compared, with one described as taller or shorter.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands two pencils on the table with their ends level and says which is longer. The child holds a stone in each hand, or sets them on a balance, and says which is heavier. Two children stand back to back and a third says who is taller.

### Limits

Exactly two objects, compared directly with each other on one shared feature. No units, rulers or numbers are mentioned, and in the example the difference is put in words such as taller and shorter, not as an amount. Putting three or more objects in order is not in the statement.

### Common mistakes

Not lining up one end, so that the object sticking out further at the top is called longer. Judging weight by size. Saying "bigger" without naming the feature, or being able to say which is longer but not which is shorter.
