## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child links counting to how many there are, as part of grasping how numbers and amounts go together. In this part, while counting things the child says the number words in their fixed order and gives every thing exactly one word and every word exactly one thing.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child touches or moves each bean once while saying one number word per touch, sliding counted beans to one side so that none is touched again. Finger and voice stay in step: one touch, one word.

### Limits

This part sets no number range; the neighbouring statement on answering how many puts the top at 20 things in a row, grid or ring and 10 when scattered. It covers only the act of counting correctly. Knowing that the final word is the total, and that each next number is one more, are the other two parts.

### Common mistakes

Counting one thing twice or missing one, especially when the things are scattered or not moved aside. Voice running ahead of the finger, so that two words land on one thing or one word covers two. Spreading a two-syllable word such as "seven" over two things.
