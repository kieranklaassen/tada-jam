## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child talks about things found around them by saying which shapes they are, and says where those things are in relation to one another with position words, for example above, below, beside, behind, next to and in front of.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child walks around a room and says "the clock is a circle" or "the box is a cube". Asked to, the child places a bear behind a chair or a ball next to a block, and says where a thing is in a full phrase such as "the cup is above the plate".

### Limits

The statement itself names no particular shapes. The cluster heading it stands under names nine: circle, triangle, square, rectangle and hexagon, which are flat, and sphere, cylinder, cone and cube, which are solid. The position words the statement lists are given as examples, not as a closed list; left and right are not among them. The things described are real ones in the child's surroundings.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up "in front of" and "behind" when the thing has no obvious front, or when the speaker faces the child from the other side. Naming a solid by one of its faces, such as calling a ball a circle or a box a square. Pointing and saying "there" instead of using a position word.
