## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows addition and subtraction in many forms: by acting a situation out, by explaining in words, with fingers or objects, with claps or other sounds, with pictures drawn on paper or held in the mind, or with expressions or equations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds up three fingers on one hand and two on the other and brings the hands together. The child claps four times, then twice more, and says how many claps that was. The child acts out three birds on a branch with one flying off, or draws circles and crosses some out, and tells in words what happened.

### Limits

The statement sets no number range; the neighbouring statement on solving problems puts the top at 10. No single form is required: any of the listed ones counts. A footnote says a drawing may leave out detail so long as it shows the mathematics of the problem, and that this holds wherever the standards mention drawings.

### Common mistakes

Drawing the story (birds with feathers, a tree) and losing the amounts. For subtraction, drawing both numbers as separate groups, as if joining them, instead of removing some from one group. Reading the equals sign as "the answer comes next" and not as "is the same as".
