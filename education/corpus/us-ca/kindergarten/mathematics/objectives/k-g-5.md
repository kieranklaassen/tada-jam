## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child makes models of shapes seen in the world in two ways: by putting them together from parts, with sticks and balls of clay as the example given, and by drawing them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child pokes sticks into clay balls to make a triangle or a square, and with more sticks the frame of a box. The child draws the shape of a window, a wheel or a roof on paper, in sand or on a chalkboard.

### Limits

The statement names no particular shapes and does not say whether they are flat or solid. Sticks and clay balls are an example of parts, not a requirement. It sets no standard for how exact a drawing must be.

### Common mistakes

Leaving a shape open, with sides that do not meet at the corners. Rounding the corners in a drawing: children can usually draw a circle and a square before they can draw a triangle or a diamond. Picking sticks of unmatched lengths for a square and not noticing that it comes out lopsided.
