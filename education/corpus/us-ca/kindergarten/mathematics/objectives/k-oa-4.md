## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child is given a number and finds the second number that brings it up to 10 by addition, then puts that answer down as a drawing or as an equation. The given number may be any from 1 to 9. Objects or drawings are named as an example way of finding the answer.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts six counters on a ten-frame and counts the empty squares: four. The child holds up seven fingers and looks at the three still folded. The child then draws the frame with both parts marked or writes 6 + 4 = 10.

### Limits

The given number is from 1 to 9 and the total is always 10. Objects and drawings are an example method; the answer is recorded as a drawing or an equation. The statement does not ask for these pairs to be known from memory.

### Common mistakes

Answering with the number that was given, or with 10, instead of the missing part. Being off by one when counting up, by starting the count on the given number itself. Adding the given number to 10 instead of finding what is missing.
