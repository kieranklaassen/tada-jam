## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can recite the number sequence as far as 100, both one number at a time and in jumps of ten.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child chants the numbers in order while climbing stairs, clapping, or dropping beads into a jar one by one, and keeps going through the turn into each new decade. For tens, the child says ten, twenty, thirty and on up to one hundred, often while touching bundles or rows of ten things.

### Limits

The sequence stops at 100. Ones and tens are the only steps named; twos and fives are not in the statement. This statement is about saying the sequence: counting real things and writing numerals are neighbouring statements, each with a top of 20.

### Common mistakes

Stalling or going wrong at a decade change, such as saying "twenty-ten" after twenty-nine or looping back to twenty. Garbling the teen names, whose pattern is irregular. In tens, skipping a decade or not knowing what follows ninety.
