## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

For each number from 11 to 19, the child understands that it is made up of ten ones together with one to nine ones more. The child builds the number from those two parts and splits it into them again, with objects or drawings as an example way of working. Every such building or splitting is put down in a drawing or an equation; the example equation given writes 18 as 10 + 8.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child counts out fourteen cubes, fills a ten-frame with ten of them, and sees four left over. The child says "ten and four make fourteen", then draws the full frame with four dots beside it or writes 14 = 10 + 4.

### Limits

Only the numbers 11 to 19. The wording speaks of ten ones and some more ones; it does not describe the ten as a single unit. Objects and drawings are example methods, and the recording is a drawing or an equation.

### Common mistakes

Reading the 1 in 14 as just one, not as ten. Writing a teen number in the order it is heard, so that fourteen becomes 41. Counting all fourteen from one every time instead of trusting that the full frame holds ten.
