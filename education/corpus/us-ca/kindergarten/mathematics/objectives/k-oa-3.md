## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child breaks a number of 10 or less into two parts, and does so in more than one way for the same number, for example with objects or drawings. Each way is recorded with a drawing or an equation, such as 5 = 4 + 1 and 5 = 2 + 3.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shakes and spills five two-coloured counters and reads off "two red and three yellow", then spills again and gets "four and one". The child snaps a stick of seven cubes in different places and says the two parts each time, then draws the split or writes it as an equation.

### Limits

The number being split is 10 or less. It is split into two parts, not three or more. More than one split of the same number is expected, but the statement does not ask for all of them. In its example equations the total stands to the left of the equals sign.

### Common mistakes

Stopping after one split and not seeing that the same number has others. Treating 2 and 3 and 3 and 2 as unrelated. Taking an equation with the total first, such as 5 = 2 + 3, to be wrong or backwards.
