## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child gives a shape its right name whichever way it is turned and whether it is big or small.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child picks up a triangle, turns it so that a point faces down, and still calls it a triangle. The child sorts large and tiny cut-out squares, circles and triangles into piles by name, and names a long thin rectangle as readily as a short wide one.

### Limits

The statement itself does not say which shapes. The cluster heading it stands under names nine: circle, triangle, square, rectangle and hexagon, which are flat, and sphere, cylinder, cone and cube, which are solid. Being turned and being a different size are the two changes the statement names.

### Common mistakes

Rejecting a triangle that is long and thin or that sits on a point, because it does not look like the familiar one with a flat base. Calling a square turned onto its corner a diamond and denying that it is a square. Calling every four-sided shape a square.
