## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child links counting to how many there are, as part of grasping how numbers and amounts go together. In this part, the child knows that the final number word of a count is the size of the whole group, and that this size stays the same however the things are laid out and in whatever order they are counted.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After counting seven shells the child answers "seven" when asked how many, without counting again. An adult spreads the shells out or pushes them into a pile, and the child still says seven. The child counts the same shells again in a different order, for instance from the other end of the row or starting in the middle, and expects the same answer.

### Limits

This part sets no number range; the neighbouring statement on answering how many puts the top at 20 things in a row, grid or ring and 10 when scattered. It is about one group; comparing two groups is a separate statement.

### Common mistakes

Answering "how many?" by counting all over again instead of stating the last word said. Believing a spread-out row holds more than the same things pushed together. Taking the number word to belong to the last thing touched, and so handing over just that one when asked for five.
