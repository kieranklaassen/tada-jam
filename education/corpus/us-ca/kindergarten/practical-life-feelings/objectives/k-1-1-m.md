## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can name and recognise a range of different feelings.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a face, in a photo, a mirror, or on a classmate, and says the feeling: happy, sad, angry, scared, surprised, proud. They make the face and body of a feeling for classmates to name. They say how they feel right now and point to where they feel it in the body.

### Limits

The statement asks for many kinds and gives no list or count. It is about knowing and naming feelings; letting them out suitably is K.7.1.M.

### Common mistakes

Most five-year-olds have a few words (happy, sad, mad) and put every feeling under one of them. Scared and angry look alike to them, as do surprised and scared. They read the feeling from the situation ("it's her birthday, so she is happy") and not from the face.
