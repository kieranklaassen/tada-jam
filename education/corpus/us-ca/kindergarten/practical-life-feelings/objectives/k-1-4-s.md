## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell touches that are okay apart from touches that are not okay.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child names touches that feel safe and kind, such as a hug they want, a high five, or a parent helping them wash. They name touches that are not okay: hitting, a touch on the parts a swimsuit covers, any touch they are told to keep secret. They practise saying "stop" and going to tell a grown-up they trust.

### Limits

The statement does not define either kind of touch. The right to refuse a touch is the next statement (K.1.5.S), and asking an adult for help is K.1.11.S.

### Common mistakes

Children judge a touch by who gives it and assume a known or liked person cannot give a wrong one. They think a touch that does not hurt must be fine. A doctor's exam or a parent's help with washing is hard for them to classify.
