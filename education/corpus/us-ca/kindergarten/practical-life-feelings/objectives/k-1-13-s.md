## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can say why it matters to let a grown-up they trust know when they have seen a person with a weapon or been told that a person has one.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells what they would do on seeing a classmate show a knife, or on being told that a child brought a gun: leave, find a teacher or parent, and say what they saw or were told. They say why: the adult can make it safe before anyone is hurt. They practise the sentence "I need to tell you something about a weapon".

### Limits

Both seeing it and being told about it count. The statement asks for the reason for telling; showing how to tell is K.8.1.S. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children think telling is tattling and will get a friend into trouble. They keep quiet when the other child asks them to keep a secret or threatens them. Something only heard, not seen, seems not worth reporting.
