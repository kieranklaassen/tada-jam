## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can act out what they would do when a person they do not know comes up to them. Three settings are named: the street, a car, and the home.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With a teacher playing the stranger, the child steps back, says "No" loudly, and runs to a known adult. At a pretend front door the child does not open it and goes to get their grown-up. When the "driver" of a pretend car asks for directions or offers a ride, the child stays well back and walks away in the opposite direction.

### Limits

It is a role-play statement: the child acts, and three settings are named. The statement does not prescribe the right response. Knowing who a stranger is belongs to K.1.10.S.

### Common mistakes

Children stand still and say nothing, or they answer politely, since they are taught to be polite to adults. They walk up to the car window to hear better. A stranger with a puppy, sweets, or a story that a parent sent them is believed.
