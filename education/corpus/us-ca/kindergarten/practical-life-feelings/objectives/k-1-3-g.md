## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can say some things that human beings have in common and some things that set one person apart from another.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children stand side by side and tell what is the same about them (two eyes, both like to run, both five) and what is not (hair, height, the language spoken at home, a wheelchair). They sort class photos by one feature, then by another. They finish "We both ..." and "Only I ...".

### Limits

The statement asks for both sides, alike and unlike, and sets no number of either. It does not say which kinds of likeness count, so body, family, abilities, and likes are all open.

### Common mistakes

Children find differences far more easily than likenesses, and they name only what can be seen. Some treat "different" as "worse" or "wrong". A child may say two people are the same or different without being able to say in what way.
