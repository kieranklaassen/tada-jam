## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can say what medicine is for: the reasons a person takes it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells what happened the last time they were ill or hurt and what a grown-up gave them for it. They pair a cough, a fever, or a scraped knee with what helped: a syrup, a tablet, a cream. They say in their own words that medicine helps a sick body get better or keeps a person from getting sick.

### Limits

The statement asks only for reasons. It names no kinds of medicine and no number of reasons. That a medicine can also hurt, and that an adult must be in charge of it, are separate statements in this content area (K.1.2.A and K.1.3.A).

### Common mistakes

Many children think medicine works at once, or that more of it works better. Some call everything in the medicine cabinet medicine, including vitamins and plasters. Sweet-tasting syrup or chewable tablets are thought of as candy.
