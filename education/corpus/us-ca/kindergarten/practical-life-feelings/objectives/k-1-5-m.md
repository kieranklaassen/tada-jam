## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell when polite words fit and tries them out in such moments. The words named are "I'm sorry", "Excuse me", "Thank you", and "Please".

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child asks for the glue with "please" and answers "thank you" when it is passed. They say "excuse me" to get past someone or to interrupt a conversation, and "I'm sorry" after bumping a friend or knocking over a tower. In role-play with puppets they pick the phrase that fits what just happened.

### Limits

Exactly four phrases are named. The statement asks for two things: saying when each fits, and practising it. It does not mention other courtesies such as greetings.

### Common mistakes

"Sorry" is said as a formula to end trouble, without seeing what the other child felt. "Excuse me" is said while already pushing through or interrupting. Children say the words with adults who prompt them and leave them out with other children.
