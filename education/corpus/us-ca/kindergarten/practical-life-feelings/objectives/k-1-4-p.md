## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can give the reason that germs passing from one person to the next can make people ill.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells the chain in order: a sick person sneezes into a hand, touches a door handle, a second person touches it and then their mouth, and that person becomes ill. They act it out with glitter on the hands as the germs. They say that this is how a cold is passed from child to child in a class.

### Limits

The wording says the passing of germs can cause harm, not that it always does. It names no disease and no route. What a germ is belongs to K.1.3.P, and prevention to K.7.2.P.

### Common mistakes

Children think a person gets ill at the moment of contact and only from someone who looks sick. The step in between, an object such as a toy or a tap, is left out. Some conclude that a sick classmate is to be avoided as a person.
