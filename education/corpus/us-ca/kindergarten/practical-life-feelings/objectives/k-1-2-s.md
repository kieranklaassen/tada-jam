## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell when what is happening is an emergency.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at pictures or hears short stories and says which ones need help right away: a fire, a person who fell and does not get up, a car crash. They put a scraped knee or a spilled drink in the "not an emergency" pile and say who could help with those.

### Limits

The statement gives no list of emergencies. What to do next, asking an adult or calling 9-1-1, is covered by other statements in this content area (K.4.1.S and K.5.1.S).

### Common mistakes

Anything upsetting counts as an emergency to a five-year-old: a lost toy, a small cut, a broken glass. The reverse also occurs: a quiet danger, such as an adult who will not wake up, is not seen as urgent because nothing loud is happening.
