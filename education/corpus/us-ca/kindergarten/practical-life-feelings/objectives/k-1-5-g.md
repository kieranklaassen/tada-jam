## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can say what the parts of the body are called and what each one does.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child touches their own elbow, knee, or ear when asked and says what it is called. They show what it does: bend the knee to jump, blink the eyes, chew with the teeth, fill the chest with a deep breath. They put a hand on the chest to feel the heart after running.

### Limits

The statement sets no list: it does not say which parts, how many, or whether organs inside the body are included. The senses have a separate statement (K.1.6.G).

### Common mistakes

Children know outside parts long before inside ones and often place the heart or stomach wrongly. A part gets one job only ("legs are for walking"), and the brain is thought to be only for thinking, not for moving. Words for joints such as wrist, ankle, and elbow are swapped.
