## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can say that every person, the child included, is allowed to say no to being touched by someone else.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds up a hand and says "Stop, I don't like that" in a clear voice. They practise it with a classmate who tickles, pokes, or hugs without asking. They also stop when another child says no to them, and they ask before hugging.

### Limits

The right is stated for all people, so it holds for the child and for the people the child touches. No exceptions are written into the statement; it does not discuss medical care or an adult holding a child back from danger.

### Common mistakes

Children believe they must obey every adult, so refusing an adult's touch feels like breaking a rule. They apply the rule to themselves and forget it when they are the one touching. Some laugh while saying no, and the other child does not take it as a no.
