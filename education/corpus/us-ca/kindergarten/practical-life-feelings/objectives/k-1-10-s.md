## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell who counts as a stranger and knows ways of keeping away from one.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says that a stranger is anyone they and their family do not know, even a friendly one. They practise staying next to their grown-up, not walking away with someone, not taking a gift or a ride, and stepping back out of arm's reach. They tell which people are known to the family and which are not.

### Limits

The statement has two parts: knowing who is a stranger and knowing how to keep away. It gives no definition and no list of methods. Acting out what to do when one comes near is a separate statement (K.5.2.S).

### Common mistakes

Children picture a stranger as someone who looks frightening, so a smiling, well-dressed person does not count. Someone who knows the child's name or says they know the parents is taken as known. A person seen often, such as a neighbour the family has never spoken to, is hard for them to classify.
