## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child knows that being in an advert or on a shop shelf does not make a thing good for a child: some such things are not.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a cereal box or a drink advert and says what it is trying to make them want: the cartoon animal, the bright colours, the toy inside. They say that the people who made it want it to be bought. They ask a grown-up "Is this good for me?" before asking to buy it.

### Limits

The wording speaks of some products, not every one, so it does not claim that everything in a shop or an advert is bad. The statement sits in the nutrition area, but its wording covers products of any kind. It asks for awareness, not for reading labels.

### Common mistakes

Children under about seven do not see that an advert is made to sell; they take it as information or entertainment. They believe that anything a shop sells to children must be safe for children. A liked character on the pack is taken as a sign that the food is good.
