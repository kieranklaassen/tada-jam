## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell what their own body looks like.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks in a mirror and says the colour of their hair, eyes, and skin, whether their hair is straight or curly, and how tall they are next to a friend. They draw themselves and choose crayons that match. They stand against a wall chart and see where their head reaches.

### Limits

The statement is about the child's own body only and names no features. Comparing people with each other is the next statement (K.1.3.G); what sets a person apart from everyone else is in the mental and social area (K.1.4.M).

### Common mistakes

A child tends to name clothing or belongings ("I have a red shirt") in place of the body. Some tell what they like or can do, not how they look. Skin and eye colours get named with the nearest crayon word, which may not fit.
