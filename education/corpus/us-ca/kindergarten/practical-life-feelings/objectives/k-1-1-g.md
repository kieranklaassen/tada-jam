## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can say that anything alive gets bigger and, with time, becomes fully grown.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts pictures in order: seed, sprout, plant; egg, chick, hen; baby, child, adult. They compare their own baby photo, or last year's shoes, with now. They plant a bean and look each day at what has changed.

### Limits

The statement covers every living thing, not only people. It names no stages, no ages, and no body changes; nothing about puberty or reproduction is asked here.

### Common mistakes

Children often count things that move, such as cars or clouds, as alive and leave out plants. Many think growing only means getting taller, and that adults have stopped changing. Some expect a small animal of one kind to turn into a large animal of another kind.
