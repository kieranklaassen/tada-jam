## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child knows not to take medicine alone: a grown-up they trust has to be there and in charge of it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child finds a pill on the floor or a bottle on the table and brings a grown-up to it without touching or tasting. When ill, the child waits for the parent, the school nurse, or another known carer to measure the dose and hand it over. In role-play the child says no to a classmate who offers a tablet.

### Limits

The statement covers every medicine and allows no case where the child takes one alone. It does not list who counts as such an adult; other statements in this lane ask the child to name those adults (K.1.3.M).

### Common mistakes

A child may accept medicine from any adult, or from an older child, because that person is bigger. Chewable vitamins and flavoured syrups get treated as sweets. Some children think a medicine that was safe for them last week can be taken again without asking.
