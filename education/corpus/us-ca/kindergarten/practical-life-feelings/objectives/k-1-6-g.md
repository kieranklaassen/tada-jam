## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can say what all five senses are called and tell about each one.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child points to eyes, ears, nose, tongue, and skin and says what each takes in: seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching. They close their eyes and tell an object by feel, a fruit by smell, or a sound by ear. They say which sense told them the soup was hot or the bell rang.

### Limits

The statement fixes the number at five and does not list them; by common usage they are sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. It does not ask how a sense organ works inside, and it does not mention balance or any further sense.

### Common mistakes

Touch is linked to the hands only, not the whole skin. Taste and smell are confused, and children say they "feel" a smell or a sound. Some give the body part (nose) when asked for the sense (smell).
