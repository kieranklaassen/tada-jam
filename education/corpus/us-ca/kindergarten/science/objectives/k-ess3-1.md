## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child uses a model to show how the place where a plant or an animal lives fits what that living thing needs; people are included. The clarification's examples: grasses often grow in meadows because they need sunlight, and deer usually live in forests because they eat leaves and buds. It adds that living things and their surroundings together form a system.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child places toy animals and plant pictures onto a sand-tray or picture model of a forest, a meadow and a pond, and says why each belongs there: "the fish needs water", "the grass needs sun". The child moves a figure to a place that does not suit it and says what it would be missing there.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. The verb is to use a model; the expectation does not say the child must make the model. Humans are named as included. The deer and grass cases are examples.

### Common mistakes

Matching an animal to a place because a picture book showed it there, without naming the need the place meets. Thinking an animal lives somewhere because it likes it, not because its food, water or shelter is there. Leaving people out.
