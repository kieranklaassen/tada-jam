## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

From what they observe, the child describes patterns in what living things, people included, must have to stay alive. The clarification's examples of patterns: every living thing needs water; plants must have light; animals have to eat food while plants do not; and different kinds of animals eat different kinds of food.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child waters one bean plant and leaves another dry, or keeps one in a cupboard and one on the windowsill, and looks at both each day. The child feeds a class pet or watches birds at a feeder and says what each one eats. The child then says what was the same for all of them.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. Humans are named as included. The four patterns in the clarification are examples. The expectation is about patterns of needs; it does not mention how plants get their food.

### Common mistakes

Saying that plants eat soil or that water is their food. Listing wants, such as toys or sweets, as things a person needs to survive. Thinking a plant is not alive because it does not move.
