## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child makes a claim, backed by evidence, about how living things can alter the place around them to get what they need. This covers plants, animals and people. The clarification's examples are tree roots that can crack concrete and a squirrel that digs a hole in the ground to hide food.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On a walk the child finds a paving slab lifted by a root, an ant hill, or a bird's nest, and points to it as evidence that a living thing changed the place. The child says what the plant or animal needed, such as room to grow, a home, or a place to keep food. The child also names a change people made nearby, such as a path or a garden bed, and the need it serves.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. Humans are named as included among the animals. The two cases in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that only people change their surroundings and that plants change nothing. Stating the change without giving anything observed as evidence. Describing how a place affects the animal, when the expectation asks how the living thing changes the place.
