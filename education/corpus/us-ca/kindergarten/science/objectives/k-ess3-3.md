## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shares ideas for ways people can do less harm to the local environment: its land, water, air, or the other living things in it. The clarification's examples of harm to the land are felling trees to make paper and using up resources to make bottles; its examples of solutions are recycling bottles and cans and using paper again.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts the classroom's waste into paper, cans and bottles, and turns a used sheet over to draw on its blank side. The child draws or tells the class one thing people nearby could do, such as carrying a bottle that can be refilled, and says what it protects.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. It is marked as integrating engineering practices. It concerns the local environment, and the child communicates solutions; inventing or testing them is not mentioned. The impacts and solutions in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Naming a good deed without linking it to the harm it reduces. Thinking litter is the only way people affect a place. Believing recycled things vanish instead of being made into new things.
