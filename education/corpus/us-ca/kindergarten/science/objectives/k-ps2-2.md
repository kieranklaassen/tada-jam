## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child looks at the results of tests to decide whether a design does what it was meant to do: make an object go faster or slower, or change the way it is heading, by a push or a pull. The clarification's example problems are getting a marble or a similar object to knock other things over, travel a set distance, or keep to a chosen path; its example solutions are a ramp that speeds the object up and a structure that makes a ball or marble turn.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds a ramp from a board and blocks, lets a marble go, and checks whether it reaches the target cup. The child records each try with a tick or a cross, then looks at the marks and says whether the ramp works. The child sets a block wall at an angle and sees whether the ball turns toward the skittles.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out friction as the way a change of speed comes about. The expectation is marked as integrating engineering practices. The marble problems, the ramp and the turning structure are examples.

### Common mistakes

Judging the design from one successful try and ignoring the tries that failed. Changing the design between tries without noticing, so the results do not belong to one design. Saying it works because it was fun to build, not because it met the goal.
