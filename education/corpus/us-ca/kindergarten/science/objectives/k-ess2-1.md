## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child watches the weather where they live, tells others what they noticed, and uses those observations to describe how the weather repeats or changes over time. The clarification gives examples: describing a day in words such as rainy, warm, cloudy or sunny; counting how many days in a month were rainy, windy or sunny; and noticing patterns such as mornings usually being cooler than afternoons, or how many sunny and how many cloudy days different months have.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Each morning the child looks out of the window or steps outside, says what the sky and the air are like, and puts a picture card for that day on a class chart. At the end of the month the child counts the sun cards and the cloud cards and says which there were more of. The child touches a railing in the morning and again after lunch and says which time it felt warmer.

### Limits

The assessment boundary keeps number-based observations to whole numbers and to relative comparisons, for example warmer or cooler. The weather observed is local. The weather words, counts and patterns in the clarification are examples, not a required list.

### Common mistakes

Describing only today's weather and not looking for a pattern across many days. Treating one unusual day as the rule ("it rained on Monday, so Mondays are rainy"). Confusing the season with the weather of a single day.
