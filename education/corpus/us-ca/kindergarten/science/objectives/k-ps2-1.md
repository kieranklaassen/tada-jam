## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child plans and carries out a test that compares how an object moves when it is pushed or pulled harder or more gently, or when the push or pull comes from another direction. The clarification's examples include a person pushing something, pulling an object by a string tied to it, a person stopping a ball that is rolling, and two things that bump into and push each other.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child gives a toy car a gentle push and then a hard push along the floor and marks where it stops each time. The child pulls a block by a string toward themselves, then pushes it away, and says which way it went. The child rolls a ball to a friend, who stops it with a hand.

### Limits

The assessment boundary has the child vary either how strong the push or pull is, in relative terms, or its direction, never the two in one test. It leaves out pushes and pulls that act without touching, and gives magnets as the case.

### Common mistakes

Changing the strength and the direction together, so the cause of the difference cannot be told. Thinking a moving object stops by itself because the push has been used up. Not seeing that stopping a rolling ball is also a push.
