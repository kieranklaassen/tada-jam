## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When prompted and supported, the child finds simple ways in which two texts about one topic are alike and ways in which they differ, for example in their procedures, descriptions or pictures.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The adult reads two short books about dogs, and the child lays them side by side. The child says that both show a puppy, that one has photos and the other has drawings, or that only one tells how to wash a dog. The adult prompts with "What is the same?" and "What is different?"

### Limits

The child does this with prompting and support. The comparison is between two texts on one topic, and the likenesses and differences are basic ones. Pictures, descriptions and procedures are given as examples of where to look.

### Common mistakes

Naming only differences, or only likenesses. Comparing the books as objects (size, colour of the cover) and not what they say or show. Describing each book in turn without comparing the two on any one point.
