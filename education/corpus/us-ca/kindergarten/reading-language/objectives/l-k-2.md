## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When writing, the child follows standard English rules for capital letters, punctuation and spelling. The child starts each sentence with a capital and writes the word I as a capital. The child knows the marks that close a sentence on seeing them and can say what each is called. Hearing a short vowel sound or a consonant sound (phoneme), the child can write one or more letters for it, and can do so for most of these sounds. The child spells simple words the way they sound, using what they know about which letters go with which sounds.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a sentence about a drawing, such as "I see a cat.", starting with a capital and writing I as a capital. Looking at a page, the child points to a period, a question mark and an exclamation mark and names each. To write "sun" the child says the word slowly, listens for each sound and writes a letter for it.

### Limits

Capitals are asked for in two places only: the start of a sentence and the word I; names are not mentioned. Part b asks the child to know the end marks on sight and to name them; it does not list the marks. Part c covers most, not all, of the short vowel and consonant sounds, and it does not mention long vowels. Part d is about simple words spelled by sound, so "kat" for cat shows the skill; the statement does not mention conventional spelling of irregular words.

### Common mistakes

Writing only the first sound, or the first and last sounds, of a word and leaving out the vowel ("ct" for cat). Putting capital letters in the middle of words because the capital form is the one the child knows best. Leaving out the end mark, or putting a period at the end of every line instead of every sentence.
