## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Given prompting and support, the child can say what the main events of a story are, where and when it takes place (the setting) and who is in it (the characters).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story, the child picks out the figures or pictures of who was in it and sets them on a mat showing the place: the forest, the house, the pond. The child tells one or two big things that happened, such as "The goat crossed the bridge". The adult asks "Who was in it?", "Where were they?" and "What happened?"

### Limits

The child does this with prompting and support. Three things are named, within one story: major events, settings and characters. Minor events, and a problem and its solution, are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Naming a small detail as a major event ("He had a red hat"). Leaving the setting out, or giving the place where the book was read. Counting every animal or object in the pictures as a character.
