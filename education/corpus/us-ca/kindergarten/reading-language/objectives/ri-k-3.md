## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells how two pieces of information, ideas, events or people in a text are linked, when prompted and supported.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a book about plants, the child says that the seed got water and then it grew, linking two events with "because" or "then". The child may point to two pictures in the book and say what one has to do with the other. An adult prompts with "What happened after the rain?"

### Limits

The child does this with prompting and support. The link is between two things at a time, and both are in one text. The statement does not name the kinds of link (such as cause, order in time, or likeness).

### Common mistakes

Describing each of the two things separately without saying how they are linked. Linking two things only because they are on the same page. Saying "and then" for every link, including where one thing caused the other.
