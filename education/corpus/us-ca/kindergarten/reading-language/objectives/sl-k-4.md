## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child describes events, things, places and people they know well and, when prompted and supported, adds further detail.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child tells about their grandmother's kitchen or a trip to the park: what was there and what happened. The adult asks "What did it look like?" or "Who else was there?", and the child adds a colour, a size, a name or a sound. The child may hold the object being described and turn it while talking.

### Limits

The prompting and support attach to adding more detail, not to the describing itself. What is described is familiar to the child. The statement sets no number of details or sentences.

### Common mistakes

Naming the thing without describing it ("It's my dog"). Assuming the listener already knows the people and places, and so leaving out who or where. Listing events with "and then" and giving no detail about any of them.
