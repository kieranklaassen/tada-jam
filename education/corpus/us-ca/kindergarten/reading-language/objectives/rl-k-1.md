## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child puts questions about the important details in a text and responds to such questions, with prompting and support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After hearing a story, the child answers "Who found the mitten?" or "Where did the bear sleep?" by saying it or by pointing to the page. The child asks their own question about the story, such as why the fox ran away. The adult helps by turning back to the page or by starting the question with "who" or "where".

### Limits

The child does this with prompting and support. This record belongs to literature, so the text is a story, a drama or a poem; a record with the same wording covers informational text. It sets no number of questions and does not say whether the child reads or listens.

### Common mistakes

Answering from their own life instead of from the story ("my dog does that"). Telling something about the story when asked to ask a question. Recalling the ending only and no details from the middle.
