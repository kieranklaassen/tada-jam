## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When prompted and supported, the child says what a text is mainly about and tells again its important details.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After hearing a short book about bees, the child says "It's about bees" and then tells things the book said, such as what bees make and where they live. The child may turn back through the pages and use the pictures to remember. An adult prompts with "What was the whole book about?" and "What did we learn?"

### Limits

The child does this with prompting and support. The statement asks for the main topic and important details of one text, and it sets no number of details. It belongs to informational text, where the child names a topic; the matching statement for literature asks for a retelling of familiar stories instead.

### Common mistakes

Naming one detail as the topic ("it's about honey" for a book about bees). Telling what they already know about the subject and not what this text said. Retelling only the last page heard.
