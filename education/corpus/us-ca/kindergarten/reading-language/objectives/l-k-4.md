## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Using kindergarten reading and subject matter as the basis, the child works out, or makes clearer, what a word or phrase means when it is unfamiliar or has more than one meaning. The child finds a new meaning for a word already known and uses it correctly (for example, learning that to duck is an action after knowing a duck as a bird). The child also treats the commonest word endings and added word parts, such as -less, -ful, pre-, un-, re-, -s and -ed, as a hint to what an unfamiliar word means.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears "bat" in a story about a cave and in one about a ball game and says which bat is meant each time. The child acts out "duck" as a movement and points to a duck in a picture. Hearing "unhappy" or "refill", the child says what the small part at the front changes: not happy, fill again.

### Limits

The words and phrases come from kindergarten reading and subject content. The endings and word parts listed are examples of the most common ones, not a closed list. The statement does not say whether the child reads the words or hears them, and it sets no number of words.

### Common mistakes

Keeping to the one meaning already known and finding the sentence strange instead of looking for a second meaning. Treating letters that only look like an added part as one (hearing "un" in "uncle" as "not"). Missing what -ed signals and retelling a past event as happening now.
