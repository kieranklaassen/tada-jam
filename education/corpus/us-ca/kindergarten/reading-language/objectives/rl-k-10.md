## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When a group reads together, the child joins in actively, with a reason for the reading and with understanding of it. The child brings to mind what they already know about the events and information in the texts. From context and from the pictures, the child predicts things about a text.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On the rug with the group, the child looks at the cover of a story about a lost kitten and tells about a time they looked for something lost. Before a page is turned, the child says what the kitten will do next, pointing to a clue in the picture. The child joins in with a repeated line and says whether the guess turned out right.

### Limits

The reading is done in a group; reading alone is not asked. California adds both lettered parts: bringing prior knowledge to the text, and predicting from pictures and context. The statement sets no text level and does not say who reads aloud. This record belongs to literature; a record with the same wording covers informational text.

### Common mistakes

Telling a long personal story that is no longer about the book. Predicting what they want to happen, with no clue from the picture or the story so far. No longer guessing after one prediction turned out wrong.
