## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child knows this grade's skills in phonics and in analysing words, and uses them to decode words, whether a word is on its own or in a text. For each consonant letter, the child says its main sound, or many of its most common sounds, which shows a basic grasp of how one letter matches one sound. For the five main vowels, the child links the short sound and the long sound to their usual spellings (graphemes); a note inside this part says the child picks out the letters that stand for those vowels (Uu, Oo, Ii, Ee and Aa) and knows both sounds of each, and that harder long-vowel spellings are left to the phonics standards of grade 1. The child reads on sight the common words that occur most often (high-frequency words), for example does, do, are, is, my, she, you, to, of and the. When two words are spelled nearly alike, the child tells them apart by saying which letters are different and what sound each of those letters makes.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Shown a consonant letter card, the child says its sound: /m/ for m, /s/ for s. Shown the letter a, the child gives both sounds, the one in "cat" and the one in "cake". The child reads "the" or "you" on a card straight away, without sounding it out. With "cat" and "cot" side by side, the child points to the middle letters and says the two sounds.

### Limits

For consonants the child gives each letter's main sound, or a good number of its most common sounds; not every sound of every consonant is asked. For vowels the statement covers the five main vowel letters, each with one short and one long sound, linked to common spellings. California adds to part b (the line carries the state's mark for an addition), and its bracketed note leaves the harder long-vowel spellings to grade 1. The sight words listed (such as you, she, the) are examples of common high-frequency words, not a fixed list or count. California also adds to the opening line, which asks for decoding of single words and of words in a text; the record does not show which words of that line are the added ones. The adopted document notes above the table that, for kindergarten, the expectation in the areas the table lists is that a child shows awareness and competence that are increasing.

### Common mistakes

Saying a letter's name when asked for its sound, most often with letters whose name does not start with their sound (w, y, h). Giving only the short sound of a vowel, or mixing up short e and short i. Guessing a word from its first letter and so reading "cat" for "cot" or "can".
