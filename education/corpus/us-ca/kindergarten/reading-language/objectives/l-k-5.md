## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

With adults guiding and supporting, the child explores how words relate to one another and the small differences in what they mean. The child sorts everyday objects into groups, such as foods or shapes, and so comes to sense the idea each group stands for. For common adjectives and verbs, the child shows what a word means by pairing it with its opposite (antonym). The child links words to where they apply in real life (for example, pointing out the colourful places in the school). By acting them out, the child tells apart verbs for one general action that differ slightly in meaning, such as prance, strut, march and walk.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts toy foods in one basket and wooden shapes in another, then says what belongs in each. Asked for the opposite of "hot" or "open", the child says "cold" or "shut" and shows it with a cup or a door. The child walks across the room, then marches, then tiptoes, and says how each one felt different. On a walk through school the child points to things that are "bumpy" or "colourful".

### Limits

All of this is done with adult guidance and support. The categories (foods, shapes) and the verbs for walking are the statement's examples, not a fixed list. Part b covers common verbs and adjectives only. Part d names acting out as the way the child shows the difference. The statement sets no number of categories or words.

### Common mistakes

Sorting by a surface feature such as colour when the group is about what the thing is. Answering "not big" instead of "small" when asked for an opposite. Acting out verbs that are close in meaning all in one way, with no difference between them.
