## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reads the simple texts made for children who are just beginning to read, has a reason for reading them, and makes sense of what they say.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a short book with a few words on each page and reads it aloud, pointing under the words. The child sounds out short regular words, says the common words known by sight, and looks at the picture to check that the sentence fits. Afterwards the child tells what the book was about or answers a question about it.

### Limits

The texts are those written for beginning readers; the statement does not define them further, give a level, or set a reading speed or accuracy. It asks for a reason to read and for comprehension, so saying the words without following the meaning does not meet it. This is the only fluency statement at this level. The adopted document notes above the table that, for kindergarten, the expectation in the areas the table lists is that a child shows awareness and competence that are increasing.

### Common mistakes

Reciting a memorised book from the pictures without looking at the words. Reading each word correctly but so slowly that the meaning of the sentence is lost by its end. Guessing a word from the picture or first letter and not noticing that the sentence no longer makes sense.
