## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child asks about the important details of a text and answers questions about them, when prompted and supported.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After an adult reads a page about frogs aloud, the child answers "What do frogs eat?" by saying it or by pointing to the part of the picture that shows it. The child asks a question of their own about the page, such as where the frog sleeps. The adult helps by rereading a sentence or by starting a question for the child to finish.

### Limits

The child does this with prompting and support, not alone. This record belongs to informational text; a record with the same wording covers literature. The statement does not say whether the child reads the text or hears it read, and it sets no number of questions or details.

### Common mistakes

Telling something they know about the topic instead of answering from the text. Making a statement or telling a story when asked to ask a question. Focusing on one small detail that interests them and missing the important ones.
