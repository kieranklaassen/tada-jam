## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child checks that they have understood a text read aloud to them, or information given by voice or through other media. They do this through questions on the important details, both asking and answering them, and by asking for something to be made clear when they have not understood it. The child also understands spoken directions of one step and of two steps and carries them out.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a read-aloud or a short video, the child answers "What did the baker do first?" and asks a question about a part they missed. When something was unclear the child says "I don't get it" or "Can you say that again?" Told "Get your coat and line up at the door", the child does both, in that order.

### Limits

The source can be a text read aloud, spoken information, or other media; the child is listening or watching, not reading. California adds part a, and it is limited to spoken directions of one or two steps. The statement mentions no prompting or support.

### Common mistakes

Carrying out only the last step heard, or only the first, of a two-step direction. Staying silent when something was not understood instead of asking. Answering a question about details with a general comment about the whole text.
