## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When an author makes a point in a text and gives reasons for it, the child can find those reasons, with prompting and support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After hearing a book that says "Bees are important", the child answers "Why does the author say that?" with something the book said, such as that bees help flowers make seeds. The child turns to the page where that reason is given. The adult supplies the point and asks for the reason.

### Limits

The child does this with prompting and support. The child finds reasons that the author gives; judging whether the reasons are good is not asked. The statement sets no number of reasons or points. The lane has no matching statement for literature.

### Common mistakes

Giving their own reason or opinion instead of the one in the text. Repeating the point itself as the reason ("because they are important"). Offering any remembered fact from the book, whether or not it supports the point.
