## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child says what is alike and what is different about the adventures and experiences that characters have in stories the child knows well, when prompted and supported.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With two well-known stories open, the child says that both the little pig and the gingerbread man were chased, but one got away and the other was eaten. The child moves character figures to show what each one did. The adult prompts with "What happened to both of them?" and "What was different?"

### Limits

The child does this with prompting and support, and with familiar stories only. What is compared is what happens to characters and what they go through; comparing settings, authors or pictures is not asked. The statement does not say how many characters or stories.

### Common mistakes

Comparing how the characters look instead of what happened to them. Retelling each story in turn without saying what is alike or different. Finding only likenesses, or only differences.
