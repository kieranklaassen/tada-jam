## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell which common kind of text something is, for example a realistic text, a fantasy, a poem or a storybook.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts a pile of books and pages into "poems" and "stories", looking at how the lines are set out on the page and listening for rhyme. Hearing about a talking dragon, the child says it is make-believe; hearing about a girl losing a tooth, the child says it could really happen.

### Limits

No prompting or support is mentioned. The four kinds named (realistic text, fantasy, poems, storybooks) are examples of common types, not a full list. California adds to this statement (it carries the state's mark for an addition); the record does not show which words are the added ones. The child recognises a type; explaining its features is not asked.

### Common mistakes

Calling any text that rhymes a poem and any text with pictures a story. Judging a story as real because its pictures are photographs or look lifelike. Deciding that a realistic story is true and happened to a real person.
