## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

By mixing writing, dictating and drawing, the child makes an opinion piece. The piece lets its reader know which book, by title, or which topic the child is writing on, and states what the child thinks of it or prefers (for example, a sentence that starts by saying which book is the child's favourite).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child draws the cover of a book they like, copies or sounds out its title, and writes or dictates "I like it" underneath. An adult writes down the words the child says for the parts the child cannot yet write. The child reads the page back, pointing to the picture and the words.

### Limits

Writing, drawing and dictating may be combined, so the child does not have to write every word. Two things must be in the piece: what it is about (a topic, or a book by name) and an opinion or preference. A reason for the opinion is not asked for. The sentence about a favourite book is an example.

### Common mistakes

Naming the topic and stopping, with no opinion given. Giving the opinion without saying what it is about ("I like it"). Retelling the book instead of saying what they think of it.
