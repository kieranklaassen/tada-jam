## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows a grasp of how print is laid out and of its basic features. The child tracks words across a line starting at the left, down a page starting at the top, and from one page to the next. The child knows that each word said aloud is written with its own particular sequence of letters, and that printed words have spaces between them. The child knows every letter of the alphabet on sight, capital and small, and can say its name.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds a book the right way up and slides a finger under each line, starting at the left and returning to the left for the next line down, then turns the page. The child touches each printed word once while an adult reads it and shows the gap between two words with a fingertip. Shown letter cards in mixed order, the child names each letter, capital or small.

### Limits

Part d asks for all 26 letters in both forms, by name; letter sounds belong to a separate statement at this level. The reading direction is the one English uses: across from the left, then down, then on to the following page. The statement is about how print works, and it does not ask the child to read the words they follow. The adopted document notes above the table that, for kindergarten, the expectation in the areas the table lists is that a child shows awareness and competence that are increasing.

### Common mistakes

Confusing letters that look alike, such as b, d, p and q, or small l and capital I. Pointing once for each syllable instead of once for each word, so the finger gets ahead of the voice on long words. Starting a new line at the right-hand end.
