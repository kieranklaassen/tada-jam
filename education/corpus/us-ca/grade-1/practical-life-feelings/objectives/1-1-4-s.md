## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can spot things that could hurt someone in three settings: where they live, their school, and the community around them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a picture of a kitchen, a classroom or a street the child puts a finger on each danger: a pot handle sticking out over the edge, toys on the stairs, a spilled drink, scissors left open, a ball rolling toward the road. The child walks through the classroom with a partner and names two things someone could trip over or bump into.

### Limits

Three settings are named and no particular hazards; no number to find is given. This statement is about spotting. Naming what lowers the risk is the next statement (1.1.5.S).

### Common mistakes

Noticing only dramatic dangers such as fire and knives and missing everyday ones such as a wet floor or a bag left in a doorway. Thinking a hazard only matters for babies. Giving a rule ("no running") when asked to point at the thing that is dangerous.
