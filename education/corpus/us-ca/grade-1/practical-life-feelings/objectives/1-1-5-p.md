## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell what the signs are of a few everyday illnesses and health problems, long-lasting (chronic) diseases among them. The examples given are influenza, diabetes, allergies and asthma.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says how a bad cold or the flu feels and points on their own body: hot forehead, sore throat, cough, aching legs, tired. The child tells what a classmate with asthma may feel (tight chest, coughing, hard to breathe) and what an allergy can look like (sneezing, itchy eyes, a rash). With a doll as the patient the child tells the "doctor" what is wrong.

### Limits

The statement says "some" and gives no number. The four conditions are examples; the list mixes a short illness (influenza) with long-lasting ones. The child describes symptoms, not causes or treatment. Supporting classmates who have such conditions is a later statement (1.8.2.P).

### Common mistakes

Thinking every illness can be caught, asthma and diabetes included. Thinking a person who looks well cannot have a health problem. Saying "sick" for everything without saying what the body feels.
