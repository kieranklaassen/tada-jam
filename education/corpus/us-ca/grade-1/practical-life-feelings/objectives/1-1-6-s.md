## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can say why it matters to let a grown-up know when somebody is being bullied or is in danger.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a short story (a child is pushed at every recess; a friend climbs onto a shed roof) the child says whom they would tell and why: "A grown-up can stop it. We might not be able to." The child practises the words: "Mr Lee, Sam keeps getting pushed by the swings."

### Limits

Two cases are named: danger and bullying. The statement says "someone" and does not say whether that includes the child themself. It asks for the reason; ways of reporting come in a later statement (1.4.1.S), and weapons have their own (1.1.9.S).

### Common mistakes

Mixing up telling to keep a person from harm with telling to get a person into trouble. Believing a promise to keep a secret has to be kept even when somebody is getting hurt. Not seeing repeated leaving-out or name-calling as bullying because nobody was hit.
