## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell which kinds of touch are all right and which are not.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to short situations and shows thumbs up or thumbs down: a hug they want from a grandparent, a high five, a doctor's check with a parent in the room, a push, a touch on a part of the body a swimsuit covers, a touch someone says to keep secret. The child practises saying "Stop, I don't like that" in a strong voice and names an adult they would tell.

### Limits

The statement gives no definition and no list for either kind of touch. It is about telling the two apart. Saying no and getting away, and letting an adult know afterwards, are separate statements (1.1.13.S and 1.4.2.S).

### Common mistakes

Thinking only strangers touch in ways that are not all right. Thinking a touch that does not hurt must be fine, or that a touch from an adult they know has to be accepted. Believing that a touch they did not want was their own fault.
