## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can pick out the main parts of the body, on the outside and inside it, and give each its proper anatomical name.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child touches their own elbow, wrist, ankle, chest or spine when an adult says the word, and says the word when the adult points. On a body outline drawn on large paper the child places paper cut-outs of the brain, heart, lungs and stomach about where they belong. The child says one name for each part of a doll while dressing it.

### Limits

The statement does not list which parts count as major, and it leaves out no part of the body. It asks for names, not for what each part does. Both kinds are required: parts that can be seen and parts inside.

### Common mistakes

Using a baby word or a family nickname in place of the proper name. Placing organs wrongly on an outline, for example the heart far over on one side or the stomach down by the hips. Believing the inside of the body is an open space that holds only blood and food.
