## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can pick out product labels that warn of a hazard or tell the user to take care.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With empty, clean containers on a table (a cleaning spray, a medicine box, a glue tube, a juice carton) the child turns each one round and puts a finger on the warning label or symbol. The child sorts the containers into "has a warning" and "has no warning". The child says what they do with the first pile: "I do not open it. I ask a grown-up."

### Limits

This statement is about finding the labels; what such labels, signs and symbols mean is the next statement (1.1.3.S). It names no particular symbols, words or products. It does not say the child must read the words on a label.

### Common mistakes

Looking only at the front of a container and missing a small label on the back or side. Taking a bright colour or a picture of a lemon on a cleaning product to mean it is a drink. Assuming a container with no label they can find is harmless.
