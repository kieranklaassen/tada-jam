## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can name things people do to make getting hurt less likely on a bus or car ride. Buckling a seat belt is the example given.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a car the child climbs in, sits back, clicks the belt and tugs it to check. On chairs lined up as a bus the child stays seated facing forward, keeps hands and head inside, keeps their voice low so the driver can concentrate, and waits for the full stop before standing. The child tells a doll each of these things in turn.

### Limits

Two kinds of vehicle are named: a car and a bus. The seat belt is an example, not the whole list. Where to sit in a car with air bags is a separate statement (1.1.8.S), and helmets for bicycles and skates come later (1.5.4.S).

### Common mistakes

Putting the shoulder strap behind the back or under the arm. Unbuckling before the car has stopped. Thinking a bus is so big that standing up or kneeling on the seat cannot matter.
