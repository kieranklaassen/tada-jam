## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can name ways to refuse when their own safety is at risk. The examples given are putting it off, switching to another topic, getting away at a walk or a run, and a plain, firm "no".

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a role-play ("Come and see the puppy in my car." "Let's light your dad's lighter.") the child stands straight, says "No!" loudly, and walks quickly to an adult they trust. The child practises a putting-off line, "I have to ask my mum first", and a switching line, "Let's go on the swings."

### Limits

Four examples are given, as examples. The verb is to name the skills; the statement does not ask the child to show them. No particular situations are named.

### Common mistakes

Saying no quietly, with a smile or a giggle, so that it does not sound meant. Thinking it is rude, and so not allowed, to refuse an adult or an older child. Staying to argue when leaving is possible.
