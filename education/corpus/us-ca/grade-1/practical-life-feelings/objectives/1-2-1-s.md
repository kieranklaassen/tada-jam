## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell about things inside a person and things from outside that can bring about injury or violence, or keep them from happening.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a story about a push in the lunch queue, the child says what pushed the character toward it (being angry, being tired, a friend's dare, something seen in a film) and what could have held them back (a slow breath, a class rule, a friend saying "don't"). The child sorts cards into "inside me" (feelings, thoughts, wants) and "around me" (friends, family, screens, rules).

### Limits

Both kinds of influence are required, and both directions: leading to harm and preventing it. The statement names no particular influences. It covers injury as well as violence.

### Common mistakes

Naming only outside causes ("he made me do it"). Not counting their own anger or excitement as a cause. Thinking an influence is always bad, and missing the ones that protect.
