## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can work out why a person on a skateboard, on in-line skates or on a bicycle is safer with a helmet on.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child presses a thumb into the foam lining of a helmet and knocks on the hard shell, then says what each layer does when a head hits the ground. The child puts a helmet on level, low on the forehead, and fastens the strap so that two fingers fit under it. The child says, "My skull is hard but my brain is soft."

### Limits

Three activities are named: cycling, skateboarding and in-line skating. The statement is about the reasoning, not about fitting a helmet. Other gear that protects the body is a later statement (1.7.3.S).

### Common mistakes

Thinking a helmet is only for beginners or for going fast. Wearing it pushed back or with the strap open and expecting it to work. Believing a helmet stops a person from falling.
