## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can say what happens over time to living things: they get bigger and they change until they are fully grown.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays picture cards in order (a seed, a sprout, a full plant; or a baby, a child, an adult) and says what changed from one card to the next. The child holds a baby photo of themselves next to a mirror and names differences: taller, more teeth, able to walk and talk. Over a few weeks the child measures a bean plant with a strip of paper and tells how it looks different each time.

### Limits

The statement names no particular plants, animals or stages, and no number of stages. It asks the child to tell what happens, not to give causes. Naming body parts and naming habits that help a body develop are separate statements in this group (1.1.2.G and 1.1.3.G).

### Common mistakes

Thinking that getting older only means getting taller, and leaving out other changes such as new teeth or new abilities. Believing that plants are not alive because they do not move, so only animals and people count. Assuming the bigger of two living things is always the older one.
