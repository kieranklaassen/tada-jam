## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can name several different things people do that help a person grow and develop well.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts picture cards of daily habits into two piles: ones that help a body grow well (sleeping, eating vegetables and fruit, running outside, drinking water, washing, brushing teeth) and ones that do not. The child picks one card and says a sentence about it: "Milk helps my bones." At the end of the day the child tells two things they did that were good for their body.

### Limits

The statement asks for more than one kind of habit but names none and gives no number. The child names habits here; carrying them out is not asked in this statement. Sleep and rest have their own statement in this group (1.2.1.G).

### Common mistakes

Naming only food and leaving out sleep, movement and keeping clean. Thinking more of one good thing gives faster growing, for example that eating a lot today makes a person taller tomorrow. Treating "healthy" as "whatever an adult tells me to do" without linking the habit to growing and developing well.
