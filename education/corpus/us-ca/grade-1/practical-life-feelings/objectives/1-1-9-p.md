## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell when something is an emergency. The examples given are earthquakes, floods, fires, a person being taken away (abduction), and injuries.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears pairs of situations and puts each on one of two mats, "get help now" or "a problem, but not an emergency": a scraped knee and a friend who fell and will not wake up; burnt toast and flames in the kitchen; a lost pencil and the ground shaking. The child says what they noticed that decided it.

### Limits

Five examples are given, as examples. The statement is about recognising an emergency. What to do is a separate statement (1.5.1.S), and what to say is another (1.4.2.P).

### Common mistakes

Calling every upset an emergency, such as a lost toy. Missing a quiet emergency, such as an adult who will not wake up. Thinking it is an emergency only when there are sirens or flames.
