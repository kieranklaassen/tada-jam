## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell what makes one place safe to be in and another one not safe.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Walking round the playground or looking at a picture, the child points and says what they notice: "A teacher is watching here." "There is a fence next to the road." "Nobody can see me behind that shed." "The floor is wet." The child sorts photos of places into two piles and gives one reason for each choice.

### Limits

The statement names no particular places and no list of features. It asks for the features that make the difference, not for rules of behaviour. Spotting hazards in the home, in school and in the community is a neighbouring statement (1.1.4.S).

### Common mistakes

Calling a place fine because it is familiar or fun. Treating a place as always one or the other, when it depends on what is there: an adult nearby, water, traffic, light. Naming places without saying what it is about them that matters.
