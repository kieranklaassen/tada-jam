## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can give the reason that young people are best protected sitting in the rear of a vehicle that has air bags, and not in the front.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child seats a doll in the rear of a toy car and buckles it in, then says why it does not go in front: "The air bag comes out very fast and hard. It is made for a grown-up's body." Walking up to a parked car with an adult, the child points to the seat that is theirs.

### Limits

The statement is only about where to sit in vehicles that have air bags. It gives no age, height or weight at which the front becomes acceptable. It does not mention seat belts or booster seats; a seat belt is the example in a neighbouring statement (1.1.10.S).

### Common mistakes

Seeing the front seat as a reward for being big. Thinking an air bag is a soft pillow that can only help. Believing the front is fine for a short trip.
