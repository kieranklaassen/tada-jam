## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can state what makes weapons dangerous and explain it. The child can also say why a grown-up they trust must be told when the child sees a person with a weapon or hears that a person has one.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child says the steps in order: "I do not touch it. I move away. I tell a grown-up I trust." After a short story ("A boy on the bus says his cousin has a knife in his bag") the child says whom they would tell and why telling is right when they have only heard about it. The child says in their own words what a weapon can do to a person.

### Limits

The statement covers both seeing and hearing about a weapon. It does not list kinds of weapon. A footnote points to the Education Code and to the document's glossary, both for how the law defines a weapon. Judging the reasons for reporting is a later statement (1.5.3.S).

### Common mistakes

Mistaking a real weapon for a toy, or thinking a small one cannot do harm. Wanting to pick it up and carry it to an adult. Keeping quiet because they only heard about it, or because the person is a friend.
