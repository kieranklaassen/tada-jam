## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell how the people in one family differ: each has a part to play, duties to carry out, and needs of their own.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child draws the people they live with and says who cooks, who takes them to school, who feeds the pet, and what their own job is, such as setting the table or putting toys away. The child says what a baby needs that an older sister does not (carrying, feeding, naps) and what a grandparent may need help with. With dolls the child acts out a morning in which each doll does its own job.

### Limits

The statement does not say who counts as part of a family or how a family is made up, and it names no particular roles. Three things are asked about: roles, responsibilities and the needs of each person. The child describes them; taking on duties is not part of the statement.

### Common mistakes

Assuming every family has the same people and the same split of jobs as their own. Thinking only adults have duties, or that only babies have needs. Believing a job belongs to a person because of who they are (only mothers cook) and cannot be done by someone else.
