## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child decides which of two two-digit numbers is larger, or whether they are the same, by reasoning from what the tens digit and the ones digit each stand for. The child writes down the outcome with the signs for greater than, equal to and less than.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds 34 and 43 with ten rods and single cubes, looks at the rods first and says "43 has four tens and 34 has only three, so 43 is more". The child writes 34 < 43. For 52 and 57 the child sees that the tens are the same and compares the ones.

### Limits

Two numbers at a time, both with two digits. All three signs are named: greater than, equal to and less than. Three-digit numbers are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Comparing by the ones digit, or by the largest single digit, and saying 29 is more than 31 because 9 is large. Writing the greater-than sign where the less-than sign is meant. Deciding correctly aloud and then being unable to say which digit settled it.
