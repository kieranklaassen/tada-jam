## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child solves word problems by adding and subtracting within 20. The problems are about comparing, putting together, taking apart, adding to and taking from, and the unknown number may be in any position. Ways named as examples are showing the problem with objects, with drawings, or with an equation in which a symbol stands for the unknown.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears "Some birds sat on a fence. Four more came. Now there are eleven. How many were there at first?" The child acts the story out with counters, draws circles for the birds, or writes an equation with a box for the missing number. The child gives the answer as a sentence about the birds.

### Limits

Numbers stay within 20. Five kinds of situation are named, and the unknown may be the starting amount, the amount of change or the result. Objects, drawings and equations are examples of ways, not required methods. An accompanying reference points to a table in the glossary of the standards; that table is not part of this record.

### Common mistakes

Choosing the operation from a single word, such as adding whenever the story says "more", which fails in comparison problems. Being unable to begin when the starting amount is the unknown. In a comparison, answering with the larger amount and not the difference.
