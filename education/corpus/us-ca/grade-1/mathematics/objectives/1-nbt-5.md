## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Starting from any two-digit number, the child works out in the head the number that is ten greater and the number that is ten smaller, with no counting, and says how the answer was reached.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Told "47", the child answers "57" for ten more and "37" for ten less, and explains "the tens go up or down by one and the ones stay". With rods and cubes the child adds or removes one ten rod to check. On a hundred chart the child moves one row down or one row up.

### Limits

The starting number has two digits. Only a single step of 10, up or down, done mentally and without counting. The statement does not say whether results above 99 or below 10 are included. The cluster heading groups this statement with adding and subtracting by means of place value and the properties of the operations.

### Common mistakes

Counting on by ones, often on fingers, and ending one too high or one too low. Changing the ones digit, so that ten more than 47 becomes 48. Stalling when ten less leaves a single digit, as with 15, or when ten more would pass 99.
