## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child solves word problems in which three whole numbers are added and the total is 20 or less. Ways named as examples are showing the problem with objects, with drawings, or with an equation in which a symbol stands for the unknown.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears a story about four red, six blue and three green marbles and sets out three groups of counters. The child joins the groups and counts, or notices that four and six make ten and adds the three. The child writes the three numbers with plus signs between them and a box for the total.

### Limits

Three addends, whole numbers, with a total no greater than 20. The statement asks for addition only; its cluster heading speaks more widely of problems that involve adding and subtracting. Objects, drawings and equations are examples of ways, not required methods.

### Common mistakes

Adding only two of the three numbers. Losing count when counting all three groups by ones. Not noticing a pair that makes ten, and so counting everything from one.
