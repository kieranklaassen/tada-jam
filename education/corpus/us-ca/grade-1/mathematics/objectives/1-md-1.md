## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child arranges three things according to how long they are. The child also finds out which of two things is longer without putting them side by side, by comparing each with a third thing.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lines up three pencils with one end of each at the table edge and places them from shortest to longest. To compare a table in one room with a rug in another, the child cuts a string as long as the table, carries it to the rug and says which of the two is longer.

### Limits

Three objects for ordering and two for the comparison through a third. No units or numbers are involved in this statement; measuring with length units belongs to the neighbouring statement. The statement and its cluster heading speak of length only, not of weight or capacity.

### Common mistakes

Not lining up the objects at one end, and judging by which far end sticks out most. Placing the first two correctly and then putting the third at the end of the row without checking it against both. Losing track of which object the string stands for, so that the answer names the string and not the table or the rug.
