## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that in a two-digit number one digit tells how many tens and the other how many ones. As a special case in this part, the child sees each number from 11 through 19 as one ten together with some ones, anywhere from one up to nine of them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds 14 with one bundle of ten sticks and four loose sticks, or fills one ten-frame and puts four counters on a second one. The child says "fourteen is ten and four" and writes 14, pointing to the 1 for the bundle and to the 4 for the loose sticks.

### Limits

This part concerns the numbers 11 to 19 only, each with exactly one ten. The number 10 and the tens numbers up to 90 are the other two parts.

### Common mistakes

Showing 14 as one stick and four sticks, reading the 1 as "one" and not as one ten. Writing 41 for fourteen, because the spoken word says the four first. Not seeing the ten inside eleven and twelve, whose names do not sound like the other teen numbers.
