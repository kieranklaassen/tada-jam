## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child uses properties of the operations as ways of adding and subtracting. One example swaps the order of the addends (the commutative property): a child who knows that 8 + 3 makes 11 also knows the total of 3 + 8. The other regroups three addends (the associative property): for 2 + 6 + 4 the child first joins the 6 and the 4 into a ten and then adds the 2 to reach 12.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child holds three cubes in one hand and eight in the other, swaps hands and sees that the total is unchanged, and so counts on from eight and not from three. With piles of two, six and four counters, the child pushes the six and the four together first and says "ten and two".

### Limits

A footnote says children do not have to use the formal names of the properties. Both examples are additions, although the statement names subtracting too. Neither the statement nor its cluster heading sets a number range; the examples stay below 13.

### Common mistakes

Swapping the numbers in a subtraction and expecting the same answer. Always adding from left to right without looking for a pair that makes ten. Recounting everything from one after the order is swapped, not trusting that the total stays the same.
