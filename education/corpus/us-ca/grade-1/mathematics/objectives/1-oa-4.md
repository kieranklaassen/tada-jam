## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that a subtraction can be treated as an addition with one addend missing. The example given is solving 10 - 8 by looking for what must be added to 8 to reach 10.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts eight counters on a ten-frame and counts the empty squares: "eight and two more is ten, so ten take away eight is two". The child hides part of a train of ten cubes under a hand, sees eight and says how many are hidden.

### Limits

Neither the statement nor its cluster heading sets a number range; the one example stays within 10. The neighbouring statements on adding and subtracting work within 20.

### Common mistakes

Seeing subtraction only as taking away, so counting back eight steps from ten and miscounting. Counting the starting number when counting up, saying "eight, nine, ten" and answering three. Not seeing that a missing-addend sentence and the matching subtraction ask the same question.
