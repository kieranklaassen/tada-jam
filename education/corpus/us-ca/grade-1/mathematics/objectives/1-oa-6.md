## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child adds and subtracts within 20 and is fluent within 10. The strategies named as examples are: counting on; making a ten (for 8 + 6, moving 2 over from the 6 to reach 10, then adding the 4 that is left to get 14); breaking a number apart to pass through ten (for 13 - 4, taking off 3 to reach 10 and then 1 more to get 9); using the link between adding and subtracting (whoever knows that 8 + 4 is 12 also knows what 12 - 8 is); and turning a sum into an easier or already known one (6 + 7 seen as double 6 and 1 more, which is 13).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child solves 8 + 6 on two ten-frames by moving two counters to fill the first frame and reading "ten and four". For 13 - 4 the child removes the three loose counters and then one from the full frame. The child answers sums and differences within 10, such as 4 + 5 or 9 - 3, at once and without counting objects.

### Limits

Adding and subtracting reach to 20; fluency is asked for only within 10. The strategies are listed as examples, not as a required set. The statement does not define fluency by a time limit.

### Common mistakes

Counting on from the first number and saying that number as the first step, which gives an answer one too small. When making ten, moving two over from the six and then adding the whole six again. When subtracting through ten, taking off the first part and then adding the rest.
