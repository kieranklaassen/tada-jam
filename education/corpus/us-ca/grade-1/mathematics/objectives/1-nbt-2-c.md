## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that in a two-digit number one digit tells how many tens and the other how many ones. As a special case in this part, the child knows that 10, 20, 30 and so on up to 90 stand for one ten, two tens and so on up to nine tens, each with no ones.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out five ten rods and counts them two ways: "ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty" and "one ten, two tens, three tens, four tens, five tens". The child writes 50 and says that the 0 means there are no loose ones.

### Limits

This part concerns the tens numbers from 10 to 90 only. The number 100 is not included. The number 10 as a bundle and the numbers 11 to 19 are the other two parts.

### Common mistakes

Confusing number names that sound alike, such as fifty and fifteen. Answering "five" when asked how many cubes five ten rods hold. Treating the 0 in 50 as meaning nothing at all, and writing 5 for fifty.
