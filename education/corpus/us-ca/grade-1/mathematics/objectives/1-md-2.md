## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child states how long a thing is as a count of whole units, found by placing several copies of a shorter thing, which serves as the unit, in a line with each touching the next. The child understands that the measurement is how many equal-sized units cover the length when none overlap and no space is left between them. Only cases where a whole number of units fits exactly are included.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays paper clips of one size along a pencil, each touching the next, and counts them: "six clips long". The child checks that no clips overlap, that no space is left between them, and that the row starts at one end of the pencil and stops at the other.

### Limits

Whole numbers of units only: the statement itself restricts measuring to objects that a whole number of units covers exactly. The unit is a shorter object that is copied many times. Rulers and standard units such as inches or centimetres are not mentioned. The statement sets no number range.

### Common mistakes

Leaving spaces between the units or letting them overlap. Mixing units of different sizes, such as large and small clips, in one measurement. Starting the row before the end of the object or letting it run past the other end, and counting those units too.
