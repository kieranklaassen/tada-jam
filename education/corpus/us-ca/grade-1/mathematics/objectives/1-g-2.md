## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child joins flat shapes, or joins solid shapes, into one combined shape, and then makes new shapes out of that combined shape. The flat shapes named are triangles, squares, rectangles, trapezoids, quarter-circles and half-circles. The solids named are cubes, box-shaped prisms, and cones and cylinders of the upright kind with a round base.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child pushes two quarter-circle tiles together into a half-circle, then sets that half-circle on top of a rectangle to make a new outline. With blocks, the child stands a cone on a cylinder and then uses two such towers as the ends of a larger building.

### Limits

Only the flat shapes and solids named in the statement are covered. A footnote says the formal names of shapes are not something children have to learn; its example is the full geometric name of the box shape. The statement sets no number of pieces. Cutting shapes into equal shares belongs to the neighbouring statement.

### Common mistakes

Leaving gaps between pieces or letting them overlap. Not thinking to turn or flip a piece, and giving up when it does not fit as first placed. Taking the combined shape apart again instead of using it as one piece for the next shape.
