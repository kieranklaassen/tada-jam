## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands what the equal sign means and judges whether an equation that has adding or subtracting in it is true or false. The examples to judge are two different sums set equal (4 + 1 = 5 + 2), one pair of addends written in swapped order on the two sides (5 + 2 = 2 + 5), a number set equal to a difference (7 = 8 - 1), and a number set equal to itself (6 = 6).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds each side of an equation card with cubes on two mats: five and two on one mat, two and five on the other, and says "same amount, true". For a card with 4 + 1 on one side and 5 + 2 on the other, the child builds five and seven and says "false". The child sorts a set of equation cards into a true pile and a false pile.

### Limits

Equations with adding and subtracting. Neither the statement nor its cluster heading sets a number range; the examples use numbers below 10. The examples include a single number on each side, an operation to the right of the sign, and operations on both sides.

### Common mistakes

Reading the equal sign as "the answer comes next", and so calling 7 = 8 - 1 backwards or wrong. Rejecting 6 = 6 because nothing is being worked out. Calling the card with 4 + 1 and 5 + 2 true, because 4 + 1 is 5 and a 5 comes right after the sign.
