## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child adds within 100; the cases named include a two-digit number plus a one-digit number, and a two-digit number plus a multiple of 10. The child works with concrete models or drawings and with strategies that rest on place value, on properties of the operations, or on how adding and subtracting are related, connects what was done to a way of writing it down, and says why it works. The child understands that two-digit numbers are added tens with tens and ones with ones, and that at times a new ten has to be made.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child builds 37 and 25 with ten rods and single cubes, pushes the rods together and the cubes together, trades ten cubes for a rod and reads 62. For 46 + 30 the child adds three rods and sees that the cubes are unchanged. The child draws sticks and dots for the same sums, writes the matching number sentence and tells a classmate what was done.

### Limits

Sums stay within 100. Models, drawings and the three kinds of strategy are the named ways of working. The statement does not mention fluency, and it does not mention a column procedure. Of subtraction with two-digit numbers, the neighbouring statement covers only taking one multiple of 10 from another.

### Common mistakes

Adding a single digit to the tens place, so that 37 + 5 becomes 87. Writing both column totals side by side, so that 37 + 25 becomes 512. Dropping the newly made ten and answering 52 for 37 + 25.
