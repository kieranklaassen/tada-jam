## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that in a two-digit number one digit tells how many tens and the other how many ones. As a special case in this part, the child sees ten single ones gathered together as one unit, which is named a "ten".

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child counts out ten sticks, wraps a rubber band around them and from then on counts that bundle as "one ten" without recounting the sticks. The child trades ten single cubes for one ten rod, and a rod back for ten cubes.

### Limits

This part concerns the number 10 only. The numbers 11 to 19 and the tens numbers up to 90 are the other two parts. Only two-digit numbers are covered; hundreds are not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Opening the bundle and recounting it by ones each time, not yet trusting that it holds ten. Answering "one" when asked how many sticks are in the bundle. Reading the digits of 10 as "one" and "nothing", without seeing one ten.
