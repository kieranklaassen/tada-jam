## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child uses what they have seen of the stars, the moon and the sun to describe patterns that let them say what will happen next. The clarification's examples: the moon and the sun seem to come up in one part of the sky, cross it and go down; and stars other than the sun can be seen at night and not in the daytime.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stands in the same spot in the morning, at noon and in the afternoon and points to where the sun is, or marks the end of a stick's shadow with chalk each time. In the evening with an adult, the child looks for the moon and the first stars and draws what is there. The next day the child says where the sun will be at lunchtime before checking.

### Limits

The assessment boundary keeps star patterns to one fact: stars are seen at night and not in the day. The sun and moon patterns in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Believing the sun really travels around the Earth each day; the clarification says it appears to move. Thinking the stars go away in the daytime. Thinking the moon is only ever in the sky at night.
