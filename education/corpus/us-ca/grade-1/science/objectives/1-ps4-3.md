## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child plans and carries out a test to find out what happens when things made of different materials are put in the way of a beam of light. The clarification's example materials are opaque ones such as cardboard, transparent ones such as clear plastic, reflective ones such as a mirror, and translucent ones such as wax paper.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shines a flashlight at a wall and holds a card, a clear lid, a sheet of baking paper and a small mirror in the beam, one at a time. The child says whether the wall goes dark, stays bright, goes dim, or whether a spot of light appears somewhere else. The child sorts the things into piles by what they did to the light.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out the speed of light. The four kinds of material in the clarification, and the item given for each, are examples.

### Common mistakes

Calling everything see-through "clear" and not separating materials that let all the light pass from those that let only some pass. Thinking a shadow is something the object sends out, not a place the light did not reach. Thinking only mirrors send light back.
