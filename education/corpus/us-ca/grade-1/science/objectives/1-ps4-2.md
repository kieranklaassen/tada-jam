## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child observes and then gives an account, based on what was seen, that a thing is visible only when it is lit. The clarification's examples of observations are a video of someone exploring a cave by flashlight, a box with a pinhole, and a room with no light at all. It adds that the light may come from an outside source or from the object itself.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks through a small hole into a closed shoebox with a toy inside and sees nothing, then opens a flap to let light in and sees the toy. The child sits in a darkened room, switches a flashlight on and off, and says what can be seen each time. The child names things that give off their own light, such as a lamp or a candle flame.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. The clarification counts two kinds of illumination: light from another source, and light the object gives off itself. The three settings for observation in the clarification are examples. The department's grade document differs here: its expectation sentence is about objects that are in the dark, a qualifier the export's sentence does not have, so the wording this record follows speaks of objects in general.

### Common mistakes

Believing eyes can see in complete darkness after a while; most rooms children call dark still have some light. Thinking sight goes out from the eyes to the object. Thinking shiny or white things can be seen with no light.
