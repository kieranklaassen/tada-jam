## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

With tools and materials, the child designs and builds a device that sends a message across a distance by means of sound or light. The clarification's examples are a drum-beat pattern, a "telephone" made of paper cups and string, and a light used for signalling.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children stand at opposite ends of the yard and agree on a code, such as two flashes for "come" and one for "stop", then send it with a flashlight. They build a cup-and-string line, pull it tight, and whisper into it. They change the device when the message does not get through and try again.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out the technical workings of communication devices. The expectation is marked as integrating engineering practices. The device uses light or sound; the three devices in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Building a device but agreeing on no code, so the signal carries no message. Letting the string hang slack or touch something, then deciding the idea does not work. Testing at arm's length, where the voice carries without the device.
