## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Using materials, the child designs something that solves a problem people have by copying the way an animal or a plant uses its outside parts to stay alive, grow and get what it needs. The clarification's examples: gear or clothing that protects cyclists, modelled on animal scales, acorn shells and turtle shells; steadier structures modelled on plant roots and animal tails; keeping intruders out with something like quills or thorns; and noticing intruders with something like ears and eyes.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks closely at a pine cone, a snail shell or a thorny stem and says what the part does for its owner. The child then builds something from card, tape and foam that does a similar job for a person, such as a hard cap, modelled on a shell, that protects an egg. The child says which plant or animal part the idea came from.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. It is marked as integrating engineering practices. The parts to copy are external ones. The human problems and the plant and animal parts in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Copying how the animal looks instead of what its part does. Choosing a part and then having no human problem for it to solve. Naming an inside part, such as the heart, when the expectation is about outside parts.
