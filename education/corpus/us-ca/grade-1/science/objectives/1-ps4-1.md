## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child plans and carries out tests that give evidence for two things: a material that vibrates can make sound, and sound can set a material vibrating. The clarification's examples for the first are a plucked string pulled tight and a tuning fork; for the second, an object held close to a ringing tuning fork and a sheet of paper held close to a speaker that is playing.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child stretches a rubber band over a box, plucks it, watches it blur and hears it, then pinches it and hears the sound stop. The child puts fingers on their own throat while humming. The child sprinkles rice on plastic wrap stretched over a bowl, bangs a pan nearby, and watches the grains jump.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. It asks for both directions: vibration making sound, and sound causing vibration. The objects in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the sound comes only from the hitting or plucking and not from the shaking that follows. Saying nothing is moving because the vibration is too small or too fast to see. Showing only that vibration makes sound and leaving out the reverse.
