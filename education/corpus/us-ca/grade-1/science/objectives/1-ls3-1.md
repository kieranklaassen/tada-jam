## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child observes and then gives an account, based on what was seen, that young animals and plants resemble their parents without being identical to them. The clarification's examples: features that plants or animals have in common; a dog of a given breed that looks like its parents yet differs from them; and leaves of one kind of plant that have the same shape but vary in size.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays photos of a mother cat and her kittens side by side and points to what matches (ears, tail, fur pattern) and what differs. The child collects several leaves from one tree, stacks them, and sees the same outline in different sizes. The child says in their own words that the young are the same kind as the parent but not the same in every detail.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out three things: inheritance, hybrids, and animals that go through metamorphosis. The dog and leaf cases are examples.

### Common mistakes

Expecting a young animal to be an exact copy of one parent. Thinking a difference in size or colour means a different kind of animal. Giving an opinion ("they look alike") without pointing to a feature that was observed.
