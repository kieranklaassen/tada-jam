## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child puts questions about a speaker's words, and answers questions about them, with the aim of learning more or of clearing up a point the child did not understand.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a classmate or a visitor has talked about a pet, the child raises a hand and asks for more ("What does it eat?") or for an explanation ("What is a hutch?"). When an adult asks what the speaker said, the child answers from what was heard.

### Limits

The source is a person speaking. Questions have one of two purposes: getting more information, or clearing up something not understood. Both asking and answering are covered. No support condition is stated.

### Common mistakes

Telling a story of the child's own ("I have a dog too") instead of asking a question. Asking something the speaker has already answered. Not noticing that a part was not understood, so that no question is asked.
