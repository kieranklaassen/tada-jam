## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reads accurately and fluently enough that the reading supports understanding. The child has a purpose when reading on-level text, and understands it. Read aloud again and again, such text comes out accurate, at a suitable pace and with expression. The child checks a word, or what was understood, against the context, corrects a slip without being told, and rereads when that is needed.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a short book aloud to an adult, and reads it again on later days; each time fewer words are misread, the pace is closer to that of talking, and the voice rises for a question or changes for a character. When a word read aloud does not make sense in the sentence, the child stops, looks again, goes back to where the sentence began and reads it once more. Afterwards the child tells what the text was about.

### Limits

The statement calls the text on-level and does not define the term: it does not say whose level or which level is meant. It gives no words-per-minute figure and no level scale. Accuracy, rate and expression are asked for on repeated oral readings. The opening line ties accuracy and fluency to comprehension. Rereading is expected when needed, not every time.

### Common mistakes

Reading fast without attention to meaning, and reading on past a word that made no sense. Reading word by word in a flat voice, without a pause at a full stop or a rise for a question. Guessing a word from its first letter and not checking the guess against the sentence.
