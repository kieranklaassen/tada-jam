## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When writing or speaking, the child follows the grammar and usage rules of standard English. The child prints every letter in its capital and its small form. Nouns of three kinds are used (possessive, proper and common), and in a basic sentence the verb matches a noun that is singular or plural (for example "We hop" beside "He hops"). The child uses pronouns, namely personal ones as subject and as object, possessive ones and indefinite ones (my, me and I, their, them and they, everything and anyone are the examples); shows past, present and future with verbs (the example sentences tell of walking home yesterday, today and tomorrow); and uses determiners (demonstratives and articles are the examples) along with common adjectives, common conjunctions (such as because, so, or, but, and) and common prepositions (such as toward, beyond, during). When prompted, the child makes complete sentences, both simple and compound, and expands them; the four kinds named are statements (declarative), questions (interrogative), commands (imperative) and exclamations (exclamatory).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child prints capital and small letters by hand on lined paper. Talking or writing about a picture, the child says who did what and when, changing "jump" to "jumped" or "will jump" to fit the time, and "jump" to "jumps" to fit who is doing it. The child joins two short sentences with a joining word such as "and" or "because". Asked to, the child turns a statement into a question, or adds words that tell where, when or what kind so that the sentence grows.

### Limits

The standard applies to both writing and speaking. Part a is about printing; the statement does not mention cursive or typing. The word lists in brackets are examples, not complete lists. Adjectives, conjunctions and prepositions are the frequently met ones, and noun-verb matching is asked for in basic sentences. Sentences are simple or compound, of the four kinds, and are made in response to prompts. California adds to part d, the pronoun part. The record's wording shows only the mark of a California addition at the end of that line. The adopted document shows the addition to be a short bracketed insertion after the first pronoun kind; the pronoun kinds and their examples are not themselves additions.

### Common mistakes

Reversing letters such as b and d, or mixing capital and small letters inside one word. Putting the regular ending on an irregular word ("goed", "mouses"). Saying "me" where the subject form is needed ("Me and Sam went"), or leaving the -s off the verb after he or she.
