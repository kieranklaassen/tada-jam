## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In writing, the child follows standard English rules for capital letters, punctuation and spelling. People's names and dates get capital letters, and every sentence closes with a punctuation mark. A date is written with its comma, and so is a list of single words, where a comma stands between one word and the next. The child spells correctly the words that follow common spelling patterns and the irregular words that come up often. A word not yet taught is spelled by its sounds, with the help of phonemic awareness and of the spelling conventions the child knows.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a short note by hand: a capital for a friend's name and for the month, a full stop or question mark after each sentence, commas between the items of a short list. For a word never seen in print, the child says it slowly and writes a letter or a letter group for each sound heard.

### Limits

The standard is about writing only. Capitals are named for dates and for people's names. Commas are named for dates and for a series of single words; the statement does not mention a series of phrases. Correct spelling is expected for words with common patterns and for frequent irregular words; for untaught words, a spelling that follows the sounds is what the statement asks. The statement gives no word list and does not name the end marks.

### Common mistakes

Leaving out the mark that closes a sentence, or putting a full stop at the end of each line instead of each sentence. Capitals placed inside words or missing from names. Spelling a frequent irregular word by sound ("sed" for said, "wuz" for was), or leaving one sound out of a consonant blend ("jup" for jump).
