## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child finds, in an informational text, the reasons with which the writer backs up the points made.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a short text which says, for example, that people should wear helmets, the child says what the writer wants readers to think and then points to or tells the sentences that say why. The child may underline a point in one color and its reasons in another.

### Limits

The reasons are the ones the author gives; the statement does not ask the child to judge the reasons or to add reasons of their own. No number of reasons and no support condition are stated. The lane's frame lists the literature counterpart, RL.1.8, as a placeholder with no standard.

### Common mistakes

Giving the child's own reason for agreeing instead of the reason in the text. Repeating the point in other words as if that were a reason. Taking any fact in the text for a reason, including one that does not support the point.
