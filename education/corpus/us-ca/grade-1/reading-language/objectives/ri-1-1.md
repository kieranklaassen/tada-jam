## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child comes up with questions on the important details of an informational text and also gives answers to such questions.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a short non-fiction book about frogs, the child says what they still want to know ("What do tadpoles eat?") and puts a finger on the page or sentence that answers an adult's question. The child starts questions with who, what, where, when, why and how.

### Limits

The statement covers both asking and answering. Questions are about key details of the text; the statement does not mention inferences. It states no support condition and no number of questions. The kind of text comes from the strand, informational text; the neighbouring RL.1.1 asks the same of literature.

### Common mistakes

Giving a statement or a personal story when asked to pose a question. Answering from what the child already knows about the subject instead of from what the text says. Asking about a small thing in a picture that has little to do with what the text is about.
