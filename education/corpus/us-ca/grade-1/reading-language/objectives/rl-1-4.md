## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In stories or poems, the child picks out the words and phrases that hint at a feeling or that call on one of the senses. A cross-reference points to Language standards 4 to 6 of grade 1 for further expectations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Listening to a poem about rain, the child raises a hand at words such as "drip", "cold" and "shivering", then says which sense or feeling each one goes with. The child underlines such words in a printed poem, and acts out or draws what the words make them see, hear or feel.

### Limits

Texts are stories or poems. The words and phrases to find are of two kinds: ones that suggest feelings and ones that appeal to the senses. The statement does not ask the child to explain how the words do this, and states no support condition. California adds to this statement: the state's mark stands after the cross-reference to the grade 1 Language standards 4 to 6 (L.1.4, L.1.5 and L.1.6 in this lane).

### Common mistakes

Picking only words that name a feeling outright ("sad", "happy") and missing words that suggest one ("slumped", "tears"). Picking a word because it is long or unfamiliar, not because of a feeling or a sense. Naming the feeling the child has about the story instead of a word from the text.
