## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out, or makes clearer, what an unfamiliar word or phrase means, or which meaning a word with several meanings has, in grade 1 reading and subject matter, and picks among several strategies as the case requires. Three strategies are listed. One is taking the rest of the sentence as a clue to a word or phrase. Another is taking a common affix as a clue to a word. The third is picking out common root words together with their inflected forms (the example is look, with looking, looked and looks).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Meeting a new word in a sentence, the child reads to the sentence's last word and says what the new word probably means from the other words. The child covers an ending or a beginning such as -ed, -ing, un- or re- with a finger, reads the root that is left, and says what the added part changes. Given word cards, the child groups jump, jumps, jumped and jumping as one family.

### Limits

Words and phrases come from grade 1 reading and content. Context is at the level of the sentence; the statement does not mention a glossary or a dictionary. Affixes and root words are the frequently met ones, and the statement names no particular affix. The forms named with root words are inflectional ones, and the endings -s, -ed and -ing appear only in the example.

### Common mistakes

Guessing a word's meaning from its first letters or from the picture without checking that the guess fits the sentence. Keeping to the one known meaning of a word that has several (bat as the animal only). Seeing an inflected form where a word only happens to end in the same letters (-ing in "king", -ed in "bed").
