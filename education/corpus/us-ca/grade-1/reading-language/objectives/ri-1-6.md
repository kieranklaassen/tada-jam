## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells apart two sources of information in a text: what the words say and what the pictures or other illustrations show.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Looking at a page about a fire engine, the child says one thing learned from the photo ("it has a long ladder") and one thing learned only from the sentences ("it carries water"). The child sorts fact cards into two piles: found in the picture, found in the words.

### Limits

The statement sets pictures and other illustrations on one side and the words of the text on the other. It asks for telling the two sources apart; drawing on both to describe key ideas is the neighbouring RI.1.7. No support condition is stated.

### Common mistakes

Saying "the book says" for something that only the picture shows. Reporting something from the picture when asked what the words say, most often when the child cannot yet read all the words. Assuming that picture and words always carry the same information.
