## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes a narrative that tells of at least two events in a fitting order, adds a few details on what took place, marks the order of events with time words, and comes to some kind of close.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes about something that happened, such as losing a tooth: what happened first and what happened next, with words like "first", "then" and "after that", a detail or two on each event, and a last sentence such as "Then I went to bed." The child may first put three drawings in order and write a sentence under each.

### Limits

Two events are the minimum. The events are told in order, the order is signaled with temporal words, and some details and some sense of closure are included. The statement does not say whether the narrative is real or imagined, sets no length, and states no support condition.

### Common mistakes

Writing one event only, or a string of events joined by "and then" with no details. Telling events out of order, or leaving out a step the reader needs. Stopping when the events are told, with no closing sentence, or writing "The end" in place of one.
