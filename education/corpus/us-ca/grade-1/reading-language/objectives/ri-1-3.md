## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Given an informational text, the child describes how two of its people, happenings, ideas or items of information are linked to each other.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes two things from a text, such as rain and puddles, or a farmer and a baker, and says how they are linked: one causes the other, one comes before the other, the two are alike, or one needs the other. The child may draw an arrow between two pictures and say the link aloud with "because", "then" or "both".

### Limits

The connection is between two items. The items may be individuals, events, ideas or pieces of information, and both come from the same text. The statement does not name kinds of connection such as cause or order, and states no support condition.

### Common mistakes

Describing each of the two things separately without saying how they are linked. Saying only that the two are "in the same book". Treating two things that happen one after the other as cause and effect when the text does not say so.
