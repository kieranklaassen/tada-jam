## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows understanding of how print is organized and of its basic features. One part is listed, and it is about the sentence: the child recognizes what marks a sentence out, for example its first word, its capital letter and the punctuation that closes it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a short printed passage, the child puts a finger on the first word of each sentence, points to its capital letter, and moves the finger along to the mark that closes the sentence. The child counts how many sentences are on a page and says where one stops and the next starts.

### Limits

Only one part is listed: the features of a sentence. First word, capital letter and closing punctuation are given as examples of those features. The statement asks for recognizing them in print, not for writing them; punctuation in the child's own writing is in the neighbouring language standard L.1.2. The statement does not name particular punctuation marks.

### Common mistakes

Treating each line of print as one sentence, so that a sentence continuing on the next line is counted as two. Taking any capital letter, such as the one on a name in mid-sentence, for the start of a new sentence. Pointing to a single word when asked to show a sentence.
