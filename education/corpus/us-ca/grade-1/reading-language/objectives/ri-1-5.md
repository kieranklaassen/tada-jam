## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child knows a range of text structures (sequence is the example) and of text features (for example icons, electronic menus, glossaries, tables of contents and headings), and uses them to find important facts or information in a text.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With a non-fiction book, the child turns to the table of contents to find the page on what whales eat, reads the headings to find the right part of the page, and looks up a hard word in the glossary at the back. On a tablet or computer the child taps a menu or an icon to reach the wanted information. In a text told in order, such as steps for planting a seed, the child finds what comes first, next and last.

### Limits

The named structure (sequence) and the named features (icons, electronic menus, glossaries, tables of contents, headings) are examples; the statement gives no full list. The purpose is finding key facts or information. No support condition is stated. California adds to this statement: it carries the state's mark, but the stored wording does not show which words are the addition.

### Common mistakes

Turning the pages of the whole book from the front instead of going to the table of contents or the headings. Confusing the table of contents (at the front, in page order) with the glossary (at the back, in alphabetical order, giving meanings). Reading a heading as one more sentence of the text and not as a label for what follows.
