## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

From wide reading across many kinds of text, the child explains the main ways in which a storybook differs from a book that gives information.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts a pile of books into storybooks and information books and says why each goes where it does: this one has characters and things that happen to them, that one has facts, photos, labels or a table of contents. The child holds up one of each and tells two ways in which they are different.

### Limits

The contrast is between two kinds of book: ones that tell stories and ones that give information. The differences asked for are major ones; the statement names none and gives no number. The child explains the differences, which is more than sorting. The explanation draws on the child's own wide reading of different text types. No support condition is stated.

### Common mistakes

Sorting by photos against drawings alone, so that an information book with drawings goes with the stories. Calling a book a story because it has animals in it. Sorting correctly but being unable to say why.
