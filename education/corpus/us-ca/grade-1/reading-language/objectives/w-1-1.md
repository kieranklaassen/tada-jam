## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes an opinion piece with four elements: it names the book or introduces the topic being written about, says what the child's opinion is, gives a reason for that opinion, and comes to some kind of close.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes three or four sentences by hand, for example: "This is about owls. Owls are the best birds. They can see at night. That is why I like owls." The child may first say the opinion and the reason aloud to a partner, and then write.

### Limits

One reason is the minimum; the statement asks for a reason, in the singular. The ending asked for is some sense of closure, not a developed conclusion. The piece is about a topic or about a book. The statement sets no length and states no support condition; adult support for strengthening writing is in the neighbouring W.1.5.

### Common mistakes

Stating the opinion with no reason, or giving "because I like it" as the reason. Retelling the book instead of giving an opinion on it. Stopping after the reason, with no last sentence.
