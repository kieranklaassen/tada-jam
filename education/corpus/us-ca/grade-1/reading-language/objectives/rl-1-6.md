## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

At different places in a text, the child says whose voice is narrating, that is, who the teller of the story is there.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

While a story is read aloud, the child holds up a puppet or a name card for whoever is speaking at that moment: a character, or the narrator. Reading a page with speech marks, the child says "now the mouse is talking" or "now the storyteller is talking".

### Limits

The question is who tells the story, and it is asked at more than one point of the text. The statement does not use the terms first person or third person, and states no support condition.

### Common mistakes

Naming the author, or the adult reading aloud, as the one telling the story. Assuming the main character is always the teller. Missing a change of speaker where the text does not give "said" and a name.
