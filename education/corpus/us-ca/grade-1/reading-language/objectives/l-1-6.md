## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child puts to use the words and phrases picked up from talking with others, from reading, from hearing texts read aloud and from responding to texts. This includes common conjunctions that mark a simple relationship between ideas; because is the example given.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After hearing a book read aloud, the child talks about it or retells part of it and brings in a new word from the book. The child gives a reason for something with "because", in talk or in a written sentence.

### Limits

The statement names no word list and no number of words. The sources of new words are conversation, reading, being read to and responding to texts. Conjunctions are the frequently met ones that signal simple relationships; "because" is an example, not the only one. The statement does not say whether the use is spoken or written.

### Common mistakes

Repeating a new word without knowing what it means, or putting it in a sentence where it does not fit. Saying "because" with no reason after it ("because I do"). Joining every clause with "and then" where another joining word is needed.
