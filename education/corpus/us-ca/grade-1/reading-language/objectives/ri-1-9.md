## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Given two texts about one topic, the child finds simple ways in which they are alike and ways in which they differ, for example in their procedures, descriptions or pictures.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With two short books about butterflies open side by side, the child says one thing both tell and one thing only one of them tells, and compares the pictures: one has photos, the other drawings. The child may place fact cards in two overlapping rings, with shared facts in the overlap.

### Limits

There are two texts, both on the same topic. The similarities and differences asked for are basic ones. Illustrations, descriptions and procedures are examples of where to look, not a required list. No support condition is stated.

### Common mistakes

Naming only differences, or only likenesses. Comparing features that carry no information, such as which book is bigger or longer. Describing each text in turn without setting one against the other.
