## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows a grasp of the sound structure of speech: single sounds (phonemes), syllables and whole spoken words. Every part is about spoken words of one syllable. Hearing such a word, the child tells whether its vowel sound is long or short. The child blends given phonemes, consonant blends included, and says the one-syllable word they make. The child picks out and says the first sound, the vowel sound in the middle and the last sound of a word, and also breaks a word into all of its phonemes, one by one and in order.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

All of this is done by ear and voice, without letters. The child hears "cap" and "cape" and says which has the long vowel. An adult says /s/ /t/ /o/ /p/ with gaps and the child says "stop". The child hears "ship" and says its first, middle and last sound, or pushes one counter forward for each sound while saying /sh/ /i/ /p/.

### Limits

Every part is limited to spoken words of one syllable, and no part asks for letters or print. Blending includes consonant blends. Isolating covers three positions: the first sound, the middle vowel and the last sound. Segmenting means the full sequence of single phonemes. The statement lists no particular vowels, consonants or blends, gives no example words and sets no number of sounds per word. Syllables are named in the opening line, but no part asks for work on words of more than one syllable.

### Common mistakes

Counting letters instead of sounds, so that "ship" gets four parts and "box" three. Keeping a consonant blend as one piece when segmenting (/st/ /o/ /p/). Adding a vowel after a consonant said alone ("buh", "tuh"), so that the blended word comes out wrong.
