## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that negative and positive numbers work as a pair for describing quantities whose values or directions are opposite; the examples given are electric charge, credits and debits, height above and below sea level, and temperature above and below zero. The child writes real-life quantities with positive and negative numbers and explains what 0 stands for in each case.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a thermometer that goes below zero and marks 5 degrees above and 5 degrees below as +5 and −5. The child records money earned and money owed in a small ledger with signs, and marks heights above and depths below sea level on a vertical line. For each setting the child says what 0 means: the freezing point of water on the Celsius scale, a balance of no money, sea level.

### Limits

The four settings are given as examples. The statement is about meaning and about writing quantities down; it does not ask for calculating with negative numbers. It sets no number range and does not restrict the numbers to integers. The cluster heading adds that the numbers the child already knows are being extended to the whole system of rational numbers.

### Common mistakes

Treating 0 as "nothing there" instead of a chosen reference point (0 degrees is not "no temperature"). Reading the minus sign only as "take away", so that −5 alone has no meaning. Dropping the sign when writing down a quantity below the reference point.
