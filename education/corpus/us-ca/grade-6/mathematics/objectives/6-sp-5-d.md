## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sums up a numerical data set with its context in view. One way of doing so, the one named in this part, is connecting which measures of center and variability are chosen to two things: the shape of the distribution, and the setting in which the data were collected.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child looks at a dot plot of pocket money in which one value lies far above the rest, works out both the mean and the median, and sees that the mean has moved toward the far value. The child says which of the two numbers better describes a typical student and why, and picks a measure of variability to go with it. For a set that is roughly symmetric, the child notes that the mean and the median are close together.

### Limits

The statement gives no rule for which measure fits which shape; it asks the child to connect the choice to the shape and to the setting. The measures to choose from are named in 6.SP.5.c. The stem introduces its parts with "such as", so the four parts are examples, not a closed list. The cluster heading names the purpose of this group: describing distributions and giving summaries of them.

### Common mistakes

Reporting the mean every time because it is the most familiar measure, whatever the shape of the data. Not noticing that one extreme value moves the mean a lot and the median only a little. Giving a choice without a reason from the shape or from the setting.
