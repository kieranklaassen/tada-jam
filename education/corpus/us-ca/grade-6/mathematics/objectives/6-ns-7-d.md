## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands how rational numbers are ordered and what absolute value means for them. In this part the child tells apart two things: comparing absolute values, and stating which of two numbers comes first in order. As an example, the child recognises that when an account balance is below −30 dollars, the debt is more than 30 dollars.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child places −50 and −30 on a number line, says "−50 is less than −30" from their positions, and then says "but 50 dollars is the larger debt" from their distances to 0. The child sorts statement cards into "about order" (colder, lower, less than) and "about size" (bigger debt, further from sea level). The child writes both −50 < −30 and |−50| > |−30| for one situation.

### Limits

This part is about keeping the two kinds of comparison apart; it asks for no calculation. An account balance is the statement's example. The stem speaks of rational numbers, and the statement sets no number range. The cluster heading adds that the numbers the child already knows are being extended to the whole system of rational numbers.

### Common mistakes

Carrying the order over to the size: calling a balance of −50 dollars "a smaller debt" because −50 is the lesser number. Carrying the size over to the order: writing −50 > −30. Saying "bigger" or "smaller" without saying whether the number or its distance from 0 is meant.
