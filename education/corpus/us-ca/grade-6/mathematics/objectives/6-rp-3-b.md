## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reasons with ratios and rates to solve problems from real life and from mathematics, for example with the help of equations, double number line diagrams, tape diagrams or tables of equivalent ratios. In this part the child solves problems about unit rates, among them problems on constant speed and on the price of one unit. As an example: 4 lawns were mowed in 7 hours; the child finds how many lawns that rate gives in 35 hours and states the rate of mowing.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child compares two packs of juice boxes by working out the price of one box in each pack. The child times a walk over a measured distance, finds the distance per second, and predicts how far the same pace covers in a minute. For the lawn problem, the child draws a double number line of hours and lawns, marks 7 hours against 4 lawns, and steps along it to 35 hours.

### Limits

Unit pricing and constant speed are named as kinds that are included, not as the only kinds. The statement sets no number range; in its example the rate, 4/7 of a lawn per hour, is not a whole number. The footnote on 6.RP.2, a neighbouring statement, says that unit rates expected at this grade stay with non-complex fractions. The stem gives its tools as examples of ways to reason. The cluster heading adds that understanding of ratio ideas comes together with this problem solving.

### Common mistakes

Giving the inverse rate (hours per lawn) when lawns per hour is asked, or mixing the two rates in one solution. Adding the difference instead of scaling: 28 more hours, so 28 more lawns. Comparing the prices of two packs without bringing them to the same number of units.
