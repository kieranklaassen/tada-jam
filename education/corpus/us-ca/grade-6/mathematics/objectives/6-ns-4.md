## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

For two whole numbers that are each 100 or less, the child finds the greatest common factor; for two whole numbers that are each 12 or less, the least common multiple. With the distributive property the child rewrites a sum of two whole numbers from 1 to 100 that share a factor as that factor times a sum of two whole numbers that share none. As an example, 36 + 8 is written as 4 (9 + 2).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lists the factors of 36 and of 8 on two strips, circles the numbers that are on both strips, and picks the largest, 4. The child builds 36 + 8 as a rectangle of tiles 4 high, with one part 9 wide and the other 2 wide, and writes 4 (9 + 2). For the least common multiple of 4 and 6, the child writes out the multiples of each until the same number appears in both lists.

### Limits

For the greatest common factor, both numbers are whole and at most 100. For the least common multiple, both numbers are whole and at most 12. The rewriting with the distributive property uses whole numbers from 1 to 100, and the two numbers left in the bracket share no factor. The statement names no method such as listing or prime factorisation. The cluster heading adds no further limit: it groups fluent calculation with multi-digit numbers together with common factors and multiples.

### Common mistakes

Mixing up the two ideas and giving a multiple when a factor is asked, or the reverse. Taking out a common factor that is not the greatest, such as 2 (18 + 4), and stopping there. Giving the product of the two numbers as the least common multiple (24 for 4 and 6).
