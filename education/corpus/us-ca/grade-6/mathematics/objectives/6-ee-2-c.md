## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reads, writes and works out the value of expressions that use letters in place of numbers; in this part the child finds what an expression is worth once each variable is given a particular value, including expressions taken from formulas that are used for problems from real life. Where no brackets fix the order, the child carries out the operations, powers with whole-number exponents among them, in the agreed order (the Order of Operations). As an example, for a cube whose side s is 1/2 the child gets the surface area from A = 6s² and the volume from V = s³.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child is given 3x + 2 and a card that says x = 4, lays the 4 over the x, and works out 14. The child measures the side of a small cube, puts that number into the two cube formulas, and compares the volume with a count of unit blocks. With 2 + 3 × 4², the child does the power, then the multiplication, then the addition, and writes one line for each step.

### Limits

The statement sets no number range for the values put in; its own example uses the fraction 1/2 as a side length. Exponents are whole numbers. The agreed order of operations applies wherever no brackets say otherwise. The cube formulas are the statement's example of formulas from real-life problems, not a list. The cluster heading adds that this work takes the arithmetic the child already knows and carries it over to algebraic expressions. The stored wording is the export's as corrected from the adopted document at two places, restoring two raised exponents.

### Common mistakes

Working strictly from left to right, so 2 + 3 × 4 gives 20. Reading 3x with x = 4 as 34 instead of 12. Working out 6s² as (6s)², or (1/2)³ as 3/2.
