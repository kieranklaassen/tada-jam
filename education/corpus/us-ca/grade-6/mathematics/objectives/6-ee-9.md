## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

For a real-life problem in which two quantities change together, the child gives each a variable and writes an equation that states one of them, treated as the dependent variable, by means of the other, treated as the independent variable. The child studies how the two variables are related with tables and graphs and connects both to the equation. As an example, for something moving at a steady speed the child lists pairs of times and distances, graphs them, and writes d = 65t for how distance depends on time.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child rolls a toy car or walks at a steady pace, notes the distance at each second in a two-column table, and plots the pairs as points on grid paper. The child says which quantity is chosen freely (time) and which follows from it (distance), then writes a rule such as d = 2t and checks it against each row. The child points to the same pair in the table, on the graph and in the equation.

### Limits

The statement covers two quantities and one equation, in a problem from real life. Three forms are named: the equation, the table and the graph. Motion at a steady speed is the statement's example, not a limit, and the statement sets no number range. It does not use the words slope or function. The cluster heading names the same subject, how a dependent and an independent variable are related, and adds no further limit.

### Common mistakes

Swapping the roles of the two variables, for example writing t = 65d for a speed of 65. Plotting a pair with its two numbers in the wrong order. Treating the table, the graph and the equation as three separate tasks and not seeing that they show the same pairs.
