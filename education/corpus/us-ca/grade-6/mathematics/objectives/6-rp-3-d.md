## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reasons with ratios and rates to solve problems from real life and from mathematics, for example with the help of equations, double number line diagrams, tape diagrams or tables of equivalent ratios. In this part the child converts between units of measurement by reasoning with ratios, and handles the units correctly, changing them as needed, whenever quantities are multiplied or divided.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures a table in inches and converts the length to feet with a ratio table that starts from 12 inches to 1 foot. The child writes the unit beside every number in a calculation, such as 3 hours × 60 minutes per hour, and crosses out the unit that appears both above and below to see that minutes remain. The child checks whether the answer should be a larger or a smaller number than the starting one.

### Limits

The statement names no particular units and no system of measurement, and it sets no number range. It does not say whether conversions stay inside one system or go from one system to another. The stem gives its tools as examples of ways to reason. The cluster heading adds that understanding of ratio ideas comes together with this problem solving.

### Common mistakes

Multiplying when dividing is needed (36 inches becomes 432 feet), because the size of the unit is not considered. Dropping the units during the calculation and attaching the wrong unit at the end. Treating a unit such as kilometres per hour as one label instead of one unit divided by another.
