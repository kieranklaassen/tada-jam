## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that a ratio a:b, where b is not zero, has a unit rate a/b that goes with it, and uses the language of rates in a ratio situation. The examples given: 15 hamburgers for $75 is a rate of $5 for one hamburger; and a recipe that takes 4 cups of sugar and 3 cups of flour has 3/4 cup of flour to every cup of sugar.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out 15 toy burgers and 75 one-dollar counters, shares the counters equally among the burgers, and says "5 dollars per burger". The child draws a double number line for flour and sugar and finds the amount of flour that sits opposite 1 cup of sugar. The child uses the words "per", "for each" and "for every 1" and says which quantity is the 1.

### Limits

A footnote limits this: at this grade, what is expected for unit rates goes no further than non-complex fractions. A complex fraction is one that has a fraction in its numerator or its denominator. The second number of the ratio is not zero. The statement sets no other number range. The cluster heading adds that the ratio ideas understood here are for reasoning through problems, which 6.RP.3 covers. The stored wording is the export's as corrected from the adopted document at one place, restoring one symbol.

### Common mistakes

Dividing the wrong way round and giving hamburgers per dollar when dollars per hamburger is asked. Giving the unit rate without units, or with the two quantities swapped (3/4 cup of sugar for each cup of flour). Thinking a unit rate must be a whole number and rounding 3/4 to 1.
