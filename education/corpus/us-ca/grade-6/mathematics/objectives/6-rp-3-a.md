## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reasons with ratios and rates to solve problems from real life and from mathematics, for example with the help of equations, double number line diagrams, tape diagrams or tables of equivalent ratios. In this part the child builds tables of equivalent ratios for quantities measured in whole numbers, fills in values that are missing from a table, and plots the pairs from the table as points on the coordinate plane. The child also compares ratios by means of tables.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child mixes juice in the ratio 2 cups of syrup to 3 cups of water, writes the pair in a table, then doubles and triples both numbers to make more rows. Given a table with a gap, the child finds the multiplier from a complete row and fills the gap. The child plots each row as a point on grid paper. To compare two mixtures, the child extends both tables until one of the two quantities is the same in both.

### Limits

The quantities in the tables are measured in whole numbers. The stem gives its tools (tables, tape diagrams, double number lines, equations) as examples of ways to reason, not as a required set. The statement does not say which quadrant is used. The cluster heading adds that understanding of ratio ideas comes together with this problem solving.

### Common mistakes

Making new rows by adding the same number to both quantities (2:3, then 3:4) instead of multiplying both. Swapping the two coordinates when plotting a row. Comparing two ratios by looking at one quantity only, without first making the other quantity equal.
