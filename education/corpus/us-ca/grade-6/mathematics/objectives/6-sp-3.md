## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child recognises the different jobs of two kinds of single number for a numerical data set: a measure of center sums up all the values in one number, and a measure of variation tells in one number how much the values differ from each other.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes two sets of five number cards with the same middle value, one set closely bunched and one widely scattered, and lays each set along a number line. The child gives one number for about where a set sits and another for how spread out it is, such as the distance from the lowest card to the highest. The child explains why the first number alone cannot tell the two sets apart.

### Limits

The data are numerical. The statement names no particular measure of center or of variation; the named ones are in 6.SP.5.c. It asks the child to recognise what each kind of number does, not to calculate one. The cluster heading names the aim of this group: a growing understanding of variability in statistics.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that the mean or the median must be one of the data values, or must be the value that occurs most often. Believing that two data sets with the same center are alike. Treating a measure of variation as another kind of average.
