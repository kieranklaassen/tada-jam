## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sets up and solves equations to answer problems from real life and from mathematics. The equations have two shapes, px = q and x + p = q, and in every case p, q and x are rational numbers that are not negative.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads "a ribbon got 3/4 metre longer and now measures 2 metres", writes x + 3/4 = 2, and finds x by asking what must be added to 3/4 to reach 2. For "4 equal bags weigh 10 kilograms together", the child writes 4x = 10 and divides 10 by 4. The child shows on a balance scale or a tape diagram that doing the same thing to both sides keeps the equation true, and puts the answer back into the equation to check it.

### Limits

Two forms are in scope: a number added to the unknown, and a number multiplying the unknown. All three numbers are rational and not negative, so fractions and decimals are included and negative numbers are not. The statement does not mention equations that need two steps. The cluster heading adds that the equations and inequalities of this group have one variable.

### Common mistakes

Repeating the operation that is seen instead of undoing it, for example solving x + 5 = 12 by adding 5. For px = q, subtracting p instead of dividing by it. Changing one side of the equation only, or dividing the wrong way round when p is a fraction.
