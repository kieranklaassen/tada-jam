## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out the area of triangles (right-angled ones and others), of special quadrilaterals and of polygons. The way of working is named: put shapes together into a rectangle, or cut a shape apart into triangles and other pieces. The child uses these methods on problems from real life and from mathematics.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child cuts a paper rectangle along a diagonal and gets two equal right triangles, each with half the area of the rectangle. The child cuts a triangle off one end of a paper parallelogram, slides it to the other end, and gets a rectangle with the same base and height. For an L-shaped or irregular polygon on grid paper, the child draws lines that split it into rectangles and triangles, finds each area, and adds them.

### Limits

The statement names a method (putting together and cutting apart), not formulas, and it does not list which quadrilaterals count as special. Circles are not mentioned. No limit is set on side lengths. The cluster heading names three subjects for the problems of this group: area, surface area and volume. This statement is the one about area.

### Common mistakes

Multiplying base by height for a triangle and leaving out the halving. Using a slanted side as the height instead of the perpendicular distance. Adding the side lengths, which gives the perimeter, when the area is asked.
