## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands what a ratio is and describes how two quantities are related using the language of ratios. The examples given: in a zoo's bird house there is 1 beak for every 2 wings, so wings to beaks is 2:1; and one election candidate got close to three votes for each vote another candidate got.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts a handful of red and blue counters and says "for every 2 red there are 3 blue", then writes 2:3 and "2 to 3". The child builds the same ratio with a larger handful by repeating the group of 2 red and 3 blue. The child says which quantity each number belongs to and notices that 3:2 describes the counters the other way round.

### Limits

The statement relates two quantities; ratios of three quantities are not mentioned. It sets no number range. Its second example uses a word for "almost", so a ratio may describe a situation approximately. The cluster heading adds that the ratio ideas understood here are for reasoning through problems, which 6.RP.3 covers.

### Common mistakes

Reversing the order, for example writing 1:2 for wings to beaks. Comparing by subtraction ("there is 1 more blue than red") where a comparison by multiplication is meant. Reading a part-to-part ratio as a fraction of the whole, so 2:3 is taken to mean that two thirds of the counters are red.
