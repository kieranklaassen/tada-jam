## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child performs all four operations on decimals with several digits, in each case by that operation's standard algorithm, and does so fluently.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes 12.5 + 3.75 in columns with the decimal points lined up, fills the empty place with a zero, and adds. For 2.4 × 0.35, the child multiplies as with whole numbers and then places the decimal point by counting decimal places, checking against an estimate of a little less than 1. For 7.2 ÷ 0.3, the child multiplies both numbers by 10 so that the divisor is a whole number and then divides.

### Limits

The statement covers addition, subtraction, multiplication and division of decimals with several digits, each by its standard algorithm, at the level of fluency. It sets no limit on the number of digits or decimal places. Negative decimals are not mentioned. The cluster heading adds no further limit: it groups fluent calculation with multi-digit numbers together with common factors and multiples.

### Common mistakes

Lining up the last digits instead of the decimal points when adding or subtracting. Misplacing the decimal point in a product or a quotient, which gives an answer ten times too large or too small. Expecting multiplication always to give a larger number and division a smaller one, so that 7.2 ÷ 0.3 = 24 is doubted.
