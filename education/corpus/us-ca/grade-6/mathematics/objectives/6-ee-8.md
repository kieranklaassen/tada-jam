## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes an inequality shaped like x < c or x > c for a condition or limit that a problem, from real life or from mathematics, puts on a quantity. The child recognises that an inequality of this kind is solved by infinitely many numbers and shows those solutions on a number line diagram.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads "you must be taller than 120 centimetres to ride", writes h > 120, and names several heights that fit, including ones between whole numbers such as 120.5. On a number line the child draws an open circle at 120 and an arrow to the right. The child tests one number from each side of the circle to check the direction of the arrow.

### Limits

Only the two strict forms are named, greater than and less than; the statement does not mention "greater than or equal to" or "less than or equal to". One variable is compared with one fixed number, and the statement sets no range for that number. The cluster heading adds that the equations and inequalities of this group have one variable.

### Common mistakes

Listing only whole numbers as solutions and thinking the list ends. Reading the symbol backwards and marking the wrong side of the number line. Filling in the circle at the boundary number, which wrongly includes it.
