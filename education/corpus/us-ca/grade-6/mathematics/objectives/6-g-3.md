## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Given the coordinates of its corners, the child draws a polygon on the coordinate plane. Where a side joins two points that share their first coordinate, or share their second, the child finds its length from the coordinates. The child uses both skills on problems from real life and from mathematics.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child plots (1, 2), (6, 2), (6, 5) and (1, 5) on grid paper, joins them in order with a ruler, and names the rectangle. The child finds the length of the bottom side by subtracting 1 from 6 and checks by counting squares. The child uses the side lengths to find the area of a garden bed drawn as a plan on the grid.

### Limits

Lengths are found only for sides whose two endpoints share one coordinate, which are the horizontal and vertical sides; slanted sides are not in scope. The statement itself does not say which quadrants are used; 6.NS.8, another statement of this grade, covers points in all four quadrants. The cluster heading adds what the problems of this group are about: area, surface area and volume.

### Common mistakes

Plotting a corner with its two coordinates swapped. Counting grid points instead of the spaces between them, which gives a length that is one too large. Joining the corners out of order, so that sides cross.
