## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands how rational numbers are ordered and what absolute value means for them. In this part the child understands that the absolute value of a rational number is how far the number is from 0 on the number line, and reads absolute value as the size of a quantity in a real-life situation, whether the quantity is negative or positive. As an example, for an account that stands at −30 dollars, the child writes |−30| = 30 to say how large the debt is in dollars.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts a finger on −30 on a number line and measures the distance back to 0, then does the same for 30 and finds that the two distances are equal. The child writes |−30| = 30 and |30| = 30. For a balance of −30 dollars the child says "the debt is 30 dollars", and for a position of −12 metres "12 metres below the surface".

### Limits

Absolute value has two readings here: distance from 0 on the number line, and the size of a real-life quantity, whichever sign the quantity has. The stem speaks of rational numbers, and the statement sets no number range. Calculating with absolute values is not mentioned in this part. The cluster heading adds that the numbers the child already knows are being extended to the whole system of rational numbers.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that absolute value "changes the sign", so that |30| is written as −30. Thinking that |−30| is less than |5| because −30 is less than 5. Keeping the sign and the word together, as in "a debt of −30 dollars".
