## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that each rational number is one point of the number line, and stretches the number lines and coordinate axes known from earlier grades so that points on a line and in the plane can have negative coordinates. In this part the child sees that numbers with opposite signs lie on opposite sides of 0, that taking the opposite twice returns the starting number (for instance −(−3) is 3), and that the opposite of 0 is 0.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child folds a paper number line at 0 and sees 3 land on −3. The child walks 3 steps to the right of a 0 mark on the floor, moves to the mirror position 3 steps to the left, and moves back again to show that the opposite of the opposite is the starting place. The child marks pairs such as 2 1/2 and −2 1/2 at equal distances on either side of 0.

### Limits

The stem speaks of rational numbers, so fractions and decimals and their negatives are included, not only integers. The statement sets no number range. This part is about opposites and signs; placing points in the plane belongs to parts b and c. Calculating with negative numbers is not mentioned. The cluster heading adds that the numbers the child already knows are being extended to the whole system of rational numbers.

### Common mistakes

Reading −(−3) as −3, on the idea that two minus signs make a number "more negative". Thinking that 0 has no opposite. Placing a negative mixed number on the wrong side of its integer, for example −2 1/2 between −2 and −1.
