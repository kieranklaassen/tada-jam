## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reads, writes and works out the value of expressions that use letters in place of numbers. In this part the child names the pieces of an expression with the mathematical words term, factor, coefficient, sum, product and quotient, and can treat a piece, or several pieces together, as one object. As an example, the child calls 2 (8 + 7) a product with two factors, and sees the bracket (8 + 7) both as one thing and as a sum that has two terms.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes an expression written on a strip, such as 3 (x + 5), and circles each part in a different colour, saying "3 is one factor, x + 5 is the other factor, and inside the bracket x and 5 are terms". The child puts a hand over the bracket and treats what is hidden as one number. The child points to the 7 in 7n and calls it the coefficient.

### Limits

Six words are listed: sum, term, product, factor, quotient and coefficient. The word difference is not in the list. The statement sets no limit on how long an expression is or which numbers it holds. The cluster heading adds that this work takes the arithmetic the child already knows and carries it over to algebraic expressions.

### Common mistakes

Confusing term and factor: calling 8 and 7 the factors of 8 + 7. Not seeing a bracket as one object, so 2 (8 + 7) is described as "2 times 8, plus 7". Saying that a lone x has no coefficient, or that its coefficient is 0.
