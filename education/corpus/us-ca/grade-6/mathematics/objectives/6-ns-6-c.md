## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that each rational number is one point of the number line, and stretches the number lines and coordinate axes known from earlier grades so that points on a line and in the plane can have negative coordinates. In this part the child finds where integers and other rational numbers belong on a number line diagram, whether it runs across or up and down, and does the same for pairs of such numbers on a coordinate plane.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child marks −4, 1.5, −3/4 and 2 1/3 on a horizontal number line and then on a vertical one drawn like a thermometer. The child reads the value of an unlabelled mark by counting the equal parts between two integers. On a four-quadrant grid the child plots points such as (−2.5, 3) and (1/2, −1) and reads the coordinates of points that are already drawn.

### Limits

Both directions of number line are named, horizontal and vertical. The numbers are integers and other rational numbers, so fractions and decimals of either sign are included. The statement sets no number range. The cluster heading adds that the numbers the child already knows are being extended to the whole system of rational numbers.

### Common mistakes

Counting a negative fraction in the wrong direction, so that −3/4 is placed to the right of 0 or −1 1/4 between −1 and 0. Swapping the two coordinates of a pair. Putting the negative numbers above 0 on a vertical number line.
