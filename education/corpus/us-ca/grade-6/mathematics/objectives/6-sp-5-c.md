## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sums up a numerical data set with its context in view. One way of doing so, the one named in this part, is giving numbers for center (the mean, the median, or both) and for variability (the mean absolute deviation, the interquartile range, or both). It also includes describing any overall pattern in the data and any values that depart strikingly from it, with reference to the setting in which the data were collected.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts 11 data cards in order, takes the middle card as the median, and finds the middle of each half to get the two quartiles and the distance between them. For the mean, the child evens out stacks of cubes until all stacks have the same height. For the mean absolute deviation, the child finds how far each value is from the mean and averages those distances. The child then says what is typical, how much the values vary, and which value lies far from the rest, with a possible reason from the setting.

### Limits

The measures named are the median and the mean for center, and the interquartile range and the mean absolute deviation for variability; the wording "and/or" leaves open which of them are given. The mode and the simple range (highest value minus lowest) are not named. The statement sets no size for the data set. The stem introduces its parts with "such as", so the four parts are examples, not a closed list. The cluster heading names the purpose of this group: describing distributions and giving summaries of them.

### Common mistakes

Finding the median without ordering the values first, or taking one of the two middle values when the count is even. Dividing the sum by the wrong count when finding the mean, for example leaving out a value of 0. For the mean absolute deviation, keeping the negative differences, so that the differences add up to zero.
