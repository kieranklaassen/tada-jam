## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands how rational numbers are ordered and what absolute value means for them. In this part the child writes order statements for rational numbers that come from real-life settings, and reads and explains such statements. As an example, the fact that −3°C is the warmer of two temperatures is written as −3°C > −7°C.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child compares two winter temperatures, two depths below sea level, or two account balances, and writes the comparison with > or <. The child then says it in the words of the setting: "warmer", "lower down", "has less money". The child explains why the diver who is deeper has the lesser elevation number.

### Limits

Three actions are named: writing, interpreting and explaining. The settings are from real life; temperature is the statement's example. The stem speaks of rational numbers, and the statement sets no number range. The cluster heading adds that the numbers the child already knows are being extended to the whole system of rational numbers.

### Common mistakes

Matching the word "more" in the story to the greater number without thinking about direction, for example writing −7 > −3 because 7 degrees below zero is "more cold". Leaving out the units, or comparing two quantities given in different units. Reading −3°C > −7°C as "−3 is colder".
