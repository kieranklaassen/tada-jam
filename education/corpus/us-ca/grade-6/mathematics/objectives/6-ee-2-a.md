## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reads, writes and works out the value of expressions that use letters in place of numbers. In this part the child turns a described calculation into a written expression, where the calculation is carried out on numbers and on letters that represent numbers. As an example, the instruction to take y away from 5 becomes 5 − y.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears "take y away from 5" or "three more than n" and writes 5 − y or n + 3 on a slip of paper. The child matches word cards to expression cards and reads an expression such as 4x aloud as "four times x". The child lays a number card over the letter to see that the expression is a calculation that waits for a number.

### Limits

The statement names no particular operations and sets no number range; its example uses subtraction and one letter. Naming the parts of an expression and working out its value belong to the sibling parts 6.EE.2.b and 6.EE.2.c. The cluster heading adds that this work takes the arithmetic the child already knows and carries it over to algebraic expressions.

### Common mistakes

Writing "subtract y from 5" in the order it is heard, as y − 5. Treating a letter as a label for an object (a for apples) instead of a number. Reading 4x as a two-digit number in the forties instead of 4 times x.
