## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that the data gathered for a statistical question form a distribution, and that three things describe a distribution: its overall shape, its spread and its center.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child collects one number from each classmate, such as hours of sleep, and stacks a sticky note for each value above a number line. The child describes the whole pile: where most notes sit, how far the notes stretch from the lowest value to the highest, and whether the pile has one peak, a long tail on one side, or a gap. The child compares two such piles using the same three descriptions.

### Limits

The statement asks for understanding and for description by center, spread and overall shape; it names no particular measure. The named measures (median, mean, interquartile range, mean absolute deviation) are in 6.SP.5.c. The statement sets no size for the data set. The cluster heading names the aim of this group: a growing understanding of variability in statistics.

### Common mistakes

Looking only at single values (the child's own value, the highest value) and not at the data set as a whole. Describing the center and leaving out spread and shape. Reading the tallest stack as the largest value instead of the most common value.
