## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands how rational numbers are ordered and what absolute value means for them. In this part the child reads an inequality as a statement of where two numbers lie relative to each other on a number line diagram. As an example, −3 > −7 says that on a line that runs from left to right, −3 lies to the right of −7.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child places two number cards, such as −3 and −7, on a number line and says "−3 is further right, so −3 is greater". The child writes the matching inequality with the open side of the symbol toward the number that lies further right. Given a written inequality, the child points to the two positions and says which is on the left and which is on the right.

### Limits

The stem speaks of rational numbers, so fractions and decimals of either sign are included. This part ties inequality to position on a number line; real-life settings are in part b, and absolute value is in parts c and d. The example uses a line that runs from left to right. The statement sets no number range. The cluster heading adds that the numbers the child already knows are being extended to the whole system of rational numbers.

### Common mistakes

Judging by the digits and saying that −7 is greater than −3 because 7 is greater than 3. Taking "greater" to mean "further from zero". Turning the inequality symbol the wrong way when both numbers are negative.
