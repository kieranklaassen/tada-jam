## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child solves problems from real life and from mathematics by plotting points anywhere in the four quadrants of the coordinate plane. This includes finding the distance between two points that share their first coordinate, or share their second, by using the coordinates and absolute value.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child plots places on a town map drawn on a four-quadrant grid, such as a school at (−3, 4) and a library at (5, 4). To find how far apart they are, the child notes that the second coordinates match and adds the distances of −3 and 5 from the y-axis, |−3| + |5| = 8, then checks by counting squares. For two points on the same side of an axis, the child subtracts the smaller absolute value from the larger.

### Limits

Points may lie in any of the four quadrants. The statement names, as included, finding distances between points that share one coordinate, which lie on one horizontal or vertical line; slanted distances are not mentioned. The statement sets no number range and does not restrict coordinates to integers. The cluster heading adds that the numbers the child already knows are being extended to the whole system of rational numbers.

### Common mistakes

Subtracting the absolute values when the two points lie on opposite sides of an axis, which gives 2 instead of 8 for −3 and 5. Giving a negative number as a distance. Counting grid points instead of the spaces between them.
