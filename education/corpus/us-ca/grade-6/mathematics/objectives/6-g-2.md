## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

For a right rectangular prism whose edges have fraction lengths, the child gets the volume by filling the prism with small cubes whose edge is a suitable unit fraction, and shows that this gives the same volume as multiplying the three edge lengths. The child then uses the two formulas, V = b h and V = l w h, to find volumes of such prisms in problems from real life and from mathematics.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a box that measures 2 1/2 by 1 1/2 by 1 units and packs it with cubes whose edge is 1/2 unit, counting 5 × 3 × 2 = 30 small cubes. The child works out that 8 small cubes make one unit cube, so the volume is 30/8, which is 3 3/4 cubic units. The child then multiplies 2 1/2 × 1 1/2 × 1 and gets the same number.

### Limits

The solid is a right rectangular prism and nothing else. Edge lengths are fractions; the statement gives no list of denominators. The packing cubes have edges that are unit fractions. Two formulas are named: length times width times height, and base area times height. The cluster heading names three subjects for the problems of this group: area, surface area and volume. This statement is the one about volume.

### Common mistakes

Counting each small cube as one cubic unit, or as 1/2 of a cubic unit, when a cube with edge 1/2 is 1/8 of a cubic unit. Adding the three edge lengths instead of multiplying them. Multiplying mixed numbers by multiplying the whole parts and the fraction parts separately.
