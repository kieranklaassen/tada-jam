## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child uses the properties of operations to rewrite an expression as another expression that is equivalent to it. The examples given: y + y + y becomes 3y, and with the distributive property 24x + 18y becomes 6 (4x + 3y) and 3 (2 + x) becomes 6 + 3x.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out 3 groups of (2 + x) with algebra tiles or paper pieces, regroups them as 6 unit pieces and 3 x-pieces, and writes 3 (2 + x) = 6 + 3x. The child goes the other way with 24x + 18y: looks for a number that divides both 24 and 18 and writes it in front of a bracket. The child gathers y + y + y into 3y and checks each rewrite by putting the same number into both forms.

### Limits

The distributive property is the one property named, and only in the examples; the statement does not list the other properties. The examples use whole-number coefficients, addition and one or two letters; they are examples, not a range. The cluster heading adds that the starting point is the arithmetic the child already knows, now carried over to algebraic expressions.

### Common mistakes

Multiplying only the first term in the bracket, so 3 (2 + x) becomes 6 + x. Joining unlike terms, so 6 + 3x becomes 9x. Writing y + y + y as y³.
