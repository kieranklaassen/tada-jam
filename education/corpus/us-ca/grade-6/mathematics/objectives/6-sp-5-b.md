## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sums up a numerical data set with its context in view. One way of doing so, the one named in this part, is describing what attribute is being studied, including the units it is measured in and how the measuring was done.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a label for a data set: "arm span of each student in our class, measured with a tape from fingertip to fingertip, in centimetres". The child adds the unit to the number line of a plot. Given a bare list of numbers, the child asks what was measured, with what tool and in what unit before saying anything about the numbers.

### Limits

The stem introduces its parts with "such as", so the four parts (a to d) are examples of what a summary holds, not a closed list. This part names three things to describe: the attribute, the way it was measured, and its units. The statement sets no limit on which attributes are studied. The cluster heading names the purpose of this group: describing distributions and giving summaries of them.

### Common mistakes

Leaving out the unit, or mixing two units in one data set. Naming the group that was studied (the students) instead of the attribute that was measured (their arm span). Not noticing that the way of measuring, such as rounding or asking people to report their own value, changes what the numbers mean.
