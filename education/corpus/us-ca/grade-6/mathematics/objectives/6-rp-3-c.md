## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reasons with ratios and rates to solve problems from real life and from mathematics, for example with the help of equations, double number line diagrams, tape diagrams or tables of equivalent ratios. In this part the child finds a percent of a quantity by treating the percent as a rate per 100, so that 30% of a quantity, for instance, is the quantity multiplied by 30/100. The child also solves problems in which a part and its percent are known and the whole has to be found.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shades 30 of the 100 squares on a hundred grid, then draws a double number line with 0% to 100% on one side and 0 to 80 on the other to find 30% of 80. Told that 12 marbles are 25% of a bag, the child draws a tape diagram of four equal parts, writes 12 in one part, and finds that the whole bag holds 48. The child says each time which number is the whole.

### Limits

Two tasks are named: finding a percent of a quantity, and finding the whole from a part and its percent. Finding what percent one number is of another is not named in this part, and percent increase and decrease are not mentioned. The statement sets no number range; 30% is its example. The stem gives its tools as examples of ways to reason. The cluster heading adds that understanding of ratio ideas comes together with this problem solving.

### Common mistakes

Treating the percent as the amount and answering 30 for 30% of 80. In a problem that asks for the whole, taking the percent of the part (25% of 12 is 3) instead. Believing that a percent only makes sense when the whole is exactly 100.
