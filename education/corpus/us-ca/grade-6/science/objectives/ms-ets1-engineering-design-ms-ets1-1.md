## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student states what a solution to a design problem must achieve (its criteria) and what restricts it (its constraints), exactly enough that a solution can succeed. In doing so the student considers the scientific principles that apply and the possible effects on people and on the natural environment that could narrow the choice of solutions.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Given the task of keeping a drink cold on a day trip, the student writes a list: how much the container must hold, how cold the drink must stay and for how long, what it may cost, what it may weigh, and which materials may be used. The student rewrites "works well" as something that can be tested with a thermometer and a clock. The student asks who will use the container and what happens to it afterwards.

### Limits

The record prints no clarification statement and no assessment boundary. This engineering design expectation holds for the whole grade 6 to 8 band, not for grade 6 alone.

### Common mistakes

Starting to build before saying what counts as success. Writing criteria that cannot be tested, such as "good" or "strong". Mixing up criteria and constraints, or leaving out constraints such as cost, time and effects on people.
