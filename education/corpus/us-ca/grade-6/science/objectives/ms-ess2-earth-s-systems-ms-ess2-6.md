## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student builds and uses a model that shows how the Earth's uneven heating and its rotation produce circulation patterns in the air and in the oceans, and how those patterns set regional climates. The clarification stresses that the patterns differ with latitude, with altitude and with where the land lies; for the air, bands by latitude driven by sunlight, the Coriolis effect and the prevailing winds that result; for the oceans, heat carried by the global convection cycle, whose path the Coriolis effect and the shapes of the continents limit. A model may be a map, a globe, a diagram or a digital representation.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student shines a lamp on a globe and compares how widely the light spreads near the equator and near a pole. The student draws the wind belts and the main ocean currents as arrows on a world map, then uses the map to explain why two cities at one latitude have different climates. The student turns the globe slowly while drawing a line straight down from the pole and sees that the line on the globe curves.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out the physics of how the Coriolis effect arises and acts.

### Common mistakes

Believing the equator is hotter because it is nearer to the sun. Treating weather and climate as the same thing. Thinking ocean currents come only from the wind at the surface, with no part for heating, sinking water or the shape of the coasts.
