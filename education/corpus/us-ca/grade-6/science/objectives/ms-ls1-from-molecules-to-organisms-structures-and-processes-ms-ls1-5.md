## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student builds a scientific explanation, grounded in evidence, of how both environmental and genetic factors affect the way organisms grow. The clarification's examples of local conditions are how much water, space, light and food there is; of genetic factors, large breeds of cattle and species of grass. Its examples of evidence are plants growing less in a drought and more with fertilizer, seed varieties that grow at unequal rates under unequal conditions, and fish that grow bigger in a large pond than in a small one.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student grows two varieties of bean, each in bright light and in dim light, measures the height of every plant every few days and graphs the four groups. The student writes an explanation that uses the graph to separate the effect of the variety from the effect of the light.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out how genes work, how they are regulated, and the biochemical processes involved.

### Common mistakes

Explaining growth by one kind of factor only, all surroundings or all inheritance. Changing two conditions at once, so that the effect of each cannot be told apart. Describing the results without using them as evidence for an explanation.
