## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student asks questions that clarify the evidence about what has caused global temperatures to rise over the last hundred years. The clarification's example factors are natural processes, such as volcanic activity or changes in the solar radiation that arrives, and human activities, such as farming, making cement and burning fossil fuels; its example evidence is tables, graphs and maps of temperatures worldwide and by region, of how much carbon dioxide, methane and similar gases the atmosphere holds, and of how fast the human activities proceed. The emphasis is on the large part that human activities have in the rise.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student lays a graph of global temperature since 1900 beside graphs of carbon dioxide, of solar output and of large volcanic eruptions. The student writes questions about them, such as "Which line rises together with temperature?" and "How was temperature measured a hundred years ago?" The student then sorts the questions into those the graphs can answer and those that need other evidence.

### Limits

The record prints no assessment boundary.

### Common mistakes

Offering one cold winter as evidence about the long-term trend. Confusing the thinning of the ozone layer with the warming caused by greenhouse gases. Asking questions of opinion that no evidence could answer.
