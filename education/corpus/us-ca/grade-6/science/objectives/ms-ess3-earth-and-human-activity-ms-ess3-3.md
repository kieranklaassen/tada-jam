## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student uses scientific principles to design a way of tracking one human impact on the environment and making it as small as possible. The clarification gives the design steps as examples: studying how people affect the environment, weighing which kinds of solution could work in practice, and then drawing up solutions meant to lessen the impact and judging them. Its example impacts are pollution of air, water or land; land use such as farming, urban development or removing wetlands; and water use such as building dams and levees or drawing water out of streams and aquifers.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student picks a local impact, such as rainwater running off the school car park into a drain, collects samples after rain and compares how cloudy they are. The student proposes a planted strip to slow and filter the water, builds a small version in a tray, pours the same amount of muddy water through a planted tray and a bare one, and compares what comes out. The student writes down how and how often the real strip would be checked.

### Limits

The record prints no assessment boundary. An asterisk marks this expectation as one that integrates engineering.

### Common mistakes

Proposing a solution without first measuring the impact, so that nobody can tell whether it helped. Proposing that the human activity simply stop, with no thought for what is feasible. Treating monitoring as one measurement and not as repeated checks over time.
