## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student plans an investigation into how four things relate: the energy transferred, the kind of matter, its mass, and the change in the particles' average kinetic energy, which is measured as the sample's temperature. The clarification's examples of experiments are adding a set amount of energy to unequal masses of one material; watching how the temperature of equal masses of unlike materials changes while they warm or cool in the surroundings; and melting unequal masses of ice in equal volumes of water that begin equally warm, then comparing how warm the water ends.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student writes a plan: heat 100 grams and 200 grams of water on one hot plate for the same time and read a thermometer in each, then do the same with equal masses of water and of oil. The plan names what is changed, what is measured and what is kept the same, and the student draws the empty data table before starting.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out working out the total quantity of thermal energy that moves.

### Common mistakes

Treating heat and temperature as one thing, and so expecting equal heating to raise any amount of water by the same number of degrees. Changing the mass and the material in the same test. Planning what to do without saying what will be kept the same.
