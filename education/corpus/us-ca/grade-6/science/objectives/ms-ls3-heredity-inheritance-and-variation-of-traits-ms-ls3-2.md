## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student builds and uses a model to show why asexual reproduction gives offspring whose genetic information is identical, while sexual reproduction gives offspring that vary genetically. The clarification stresses models such as diagrams, simulations and Punnett squares for describing cause and effect: how genes pass from one parent or two to the offspring, and the variation that follows.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student uses cards in two colours for the two versions of each gene. For the asexual case the student copies one parent's full set of cards; for the sexual case the student draws one card at random from each parent for every gene, repeats this for several offspring and lays the sets side by side to compare. The student fills in a Punnett square for one trait and lists the combinations it shows.

### Limits

The record prints no assessment boundary.

### Common mistakes

Thinking a child gets a trait from one parent only, or that daughters inherit from mothers and sons from fathers. Reading a Punnett square as the exact number of offspring of each kind and not as chances. Expecting brothers and sisters to be identical because they have the same parents.
