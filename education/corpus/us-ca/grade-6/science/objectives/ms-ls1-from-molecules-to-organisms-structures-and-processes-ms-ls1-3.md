## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student argues from evidence that the body is a system made of subsystems that interact and that are themselves built from groups of cells. The clarification stresses the idea that cells make up tissues and tissues make up organs, each specialised for certain functions of the body; its examples are subsystems interacting inside a system and those systems working normally.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student counts pulse and breaths for one minute at rest and again after a minute of jumping, writes both pairs of numbers down, and uses them to argue that muscles, lungs and heart work together. For a muscle, the student draws the levels in order: cell, tissue, organ, system.

### Limits

The assessment boundary: how a single body system works in isolation from the others is not assessed. Only six systems are assessed: nervous, muscular, respiratory, digestive, excretory and circulatory.

### Common mistakes

Describing each body system as working alone, one organ for one task. Leaving out a level, such as going from cells straight to organs. Stating facts about the body without a claim and the evidence for it.
