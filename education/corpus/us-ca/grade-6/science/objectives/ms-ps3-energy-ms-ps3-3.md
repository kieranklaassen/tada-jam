## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student applies scientific principles to design a device, build it and test it, where the device either keeps the transfer of thermal energy as small as possible or makes it as large as possible. The clarification's examples of such devices are a solar cooker, a Styrofoam cup and an insulated box.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student lines a box with chosen materials such as foil, wool or bubble wrap, puts in a cup of warm water with a thermometer, and reads the temperature every five minutes beside an unlined box. The student then changes one material and runs the test again.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out working out the total quantity of thermal energy that moves. An asterisk marks this expectation as one that integrates engineering.

### Common mistakes

Thinking a material such as wool makes heat, when it slows the movement of heat. Speaking of cold flowing in, when it is thermal energy that flows out. Testing a device without an unlined box to compare it with.
