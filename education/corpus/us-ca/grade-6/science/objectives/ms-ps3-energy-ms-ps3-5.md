## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student builds, uses and presents arguments that back one claim: a change in an object's kinetic energy means that energy has moved into the object or out of it. The clarification's example of empirical evidence is an account of the energy, such as an inventory, drawn up before the transfer and again after it, in terms of temperature change or of how the object moves.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student rolls a toy car down a ramp into a block and watches the car slow and the block move, then stops a spinning wheel with a hand and feels the warmth. The student draws bar charts of where the energy was before and after, and uses them to present the argument to classmates.

### Limits

The assessment boundary: energy calculations are not assessed.

### Common mistakes

Saying that energy is "used up" or is gone when an object stops. Leaving out warming by friction, or sound, as places the energy went. Using "force" and "energy" as if they were one word.
