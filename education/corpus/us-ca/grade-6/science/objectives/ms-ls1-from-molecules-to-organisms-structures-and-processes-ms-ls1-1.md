## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student carries out an investigation that yields evidence that every living thing consists of cells, whether a single cell or many cells of differing number and kind. The clarification stresses gathering evidence that living things, among them Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya, consist of cells, telling living things from non-living ones, and understanding that an organism may have one cell or many varied ones. A note marked with two asterisks says that viruses are not cells but share some features with cellular life and differ from it in others.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student prepares slides of onion skin, of a drop of pond water and of a few grains of salt, looks at each under a microscope and draws what is seen. The student sorts the drawings into "cells seen" and "no cells seen", and marks which living things showed one cell and which showed many.

### Limits

The record prints no assessment boundary. A note says that viruses are not cells, though they have some features in common with cellular life.

### Common mistakes

Thinking that cells are small things found inside a living body, and not what the body is made of. Counting anything that moves, such as fire, as living, or a seed as non-living. Mistaking air bubbles under the cover slip for cells.
