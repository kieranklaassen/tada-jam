## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student gathers data as evidence of how moving and interacting air masses change the weather. The clarification stresses that air flows from high pressure toward low pressure, so that the weather at one place (its wind, precipitation, humidity, pressure and temperature) changes over time; that sudden changes can follow when unlike air masses meet; and that weather can be forecast only within ranges of probability. Data may be handed to students, for instance as diagrams, visualizations and weather maps, or may come from laboratory experiments, for instance on condensation.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student reads a thermometer, a barometer and a wind vane at the same hour each day for two weeks, enters the readings in a table, and marks the day on which pressure fell and the wind turned. The student lays a series of daily weather maps in order and points to where a cold and a warm air mass meet. The student cools the outside of a jar of moist air and watches droplets form.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out remembering what the cloud types are called, the symbols printed on weather maps, and the station diagrams that weather stations report.

### Common mistakes

Thinking wind has nothing to do with pressure, or that air moves toward high pressure. Reading a forecast as a certain statement and not as a probability. Using one day's weather to describe a place's climate.
