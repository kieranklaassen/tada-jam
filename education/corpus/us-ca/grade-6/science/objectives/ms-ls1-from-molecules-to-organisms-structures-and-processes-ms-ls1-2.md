## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student builds and uses a model that shows what a cell does as a whole and how its parts help it do that. The clarification stresses the cell working as one system and the main role of five named parts: the cell wall, the cell membrane, the nucleus, the mitochondria and the chloroplasts.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student builds a plant cell from a box, a plastic bag inside it and objects for the parts, and fixes a card to each part that says what the part does for the cell. The student uses the model to explain to a classmate what the cell could no longer do if one part were taken out.

### Limits

The assessment boundary: how structure fits function is assessed only for the cell membrane and the cell wall. For the remaining organelles, only what each does for the cell as a whole is assessed. The biochemistry of cells and of their parts is left out.

### Common mistakes

Seeing the cell as flat, like its drawing in a book. Learning each part with a nickname, such as "powerhouse", without being able to say what the part does for the whole cell. Thinking plant cells have chloroplasts in place of mitochondria, when they have both.
