## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student works out what words and phrases mean in the text where they appear. This includes technical meanings, connotations and figurative meanings. The statement points to Language standards 4 to 6 of this grade for further expectations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a science article the student meets "cell" and says it means a unit of a living thing here, not a prison room or a phone. The student reads "a storm of protest" in a news text and explains that no weather is meant. The student compares "slim" and "scrawny" in a description and says which one the writer chose and what that suggests about the writer's attitude.

### Limits

Three kinds of meaning are named: figurative, connotative and technical. The words are those met in the text being read; no list is given. The statement refers to L.6.4, L.6.5 and L.6.6 for more. California adds to this statement, and the record does not show which words are the addition.

### Common mistakes

Students give the everyday meaning of a word that has a technical meaning in the subject ("force", "solution"). They read a figurative phrase literally. They can define a word but miss whether it sounds favourable or unfavourable.
