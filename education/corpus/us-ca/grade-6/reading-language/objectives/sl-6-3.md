## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Listening to a speaker, the student lays out the speaker's argument and its individual claims. The student separates the claims the speaker backs with evidence and reasons from those left without support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student listens to a classmate's two-minute speech in favour of a longer lunch break and writes the main claim at the top of a page. Under it the student lists each further claim and, beside it, the reason or evidence the speaker gave, or leaves a blank. Afterwards the student tells the speaker which claims had support and asks about one that had none.

### Limits

The argument is a speaker's, so the student works from listening. The statement asks the student to set out the argument and sort its claims by whether reasons and evidence are given for them. It does not say how long the speech is or who the speaker is. A related statement for a written argument is RI.6.8.

### Common mistakes

Students remember the speaker's manner and miss the structure of the argument. They take confident delivery or a personal story as evidence. They write down everything said and cannot pick out the main claim.
