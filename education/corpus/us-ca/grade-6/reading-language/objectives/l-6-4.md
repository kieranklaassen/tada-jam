## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student works out or makes clear what an unfamiliar word or phrase, or one with several meanings, means in grade 6 reading and subject matter, choosing freely among several strategies. The student uses context as a clue, for example where a word sits or what job it does in its sentence, or what the surrounding paragraph or sentence means as a whole. The student uses common Greek or Latin roots and affixes suited to the grade as clues, as in audible, auditory and audience. The student looks in print and digital reference works, such as glossaries, thesauruses and dictionaries, to find how a word is said, what exactly it means, or which part of speech it is. The student then checks a first idea of a meaning, for example against a dictionary or by trying the guessed meaning in the context.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student meets "inaudible" in a science text, covers the prefix with a finger, links the root to "audio", and says a likely meaning. The student rereads the sentences around the word to see whether that meaning fits. Then the student opens a printed or online dictionary, picks the entry that matches how the word is used in the passage, and reads its pronunciation aloud. For a word with several meanings, such as "current", the student says which meaning the passage uses and why.

### Limits

The words come from grade 6 reading and subject content; no word list is given. The roots and affixes are Greek or Latin, common, and suited to the grade; one root family is given as an example and no list follows. The reference works named are examples, and they may be print or digital. The statement names four kinds of strategy (context, word parts, reference works, checking a first idea) and leaves the choice among them to the student.

### Common mistakes

Students take the first dictionary definition listed, even when it does not fit the sentence. They guess from a part that looks familiar but is not the root, for instance reading "in-" as "not" in "inflammable". They guess from context and never check the guess.
