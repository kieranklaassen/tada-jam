## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student builds up a piece of writing and makes it stronger where the piece needs it. Adults and peers give some support and guidance, and the ways named are planning, editing, revising, rewriting, or trying another approach. A note in brackets says what should be seen when the editing is for conventions: control of Language standards 1 to 3, those of grade 6 and of the grades before it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student makes a quick outline before drafting. After a classmate reads the draft and says where they stopped understanding, the student moves a paragraph, cuts a repeated point and adds an example. The teacher marks two kinds of error, and the student finds and corrects the remaining errors of those kinds alone. When a draft is not working, the student starts again from a different opening.

### Limits

The support is "some" and comes from both peers and adults, so the student is not expected to do this alone. The steps are joined by "or" and qualified by "as needed", so not every piece goes through all of them. Editing for conventions is tied to Language standards 1 to 3, and the note's wording covers earlier grades as well as grade 6.

### Common mistakes

Students treat revising as fixing spelling and recopying neatly. They resist cutting or moving text they have already written. They accept or reject a classmate's comment without rereading the passage it refers to.
