## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child finds the features of a text that show what its author's purpose or point of view is. The examples given are which facts the author puts in or leaves out, and loaded wording.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads two newspaper reports of the same strike, one written from each side. The child circles words that express a judgement ("mob", "heroes") and lists a fact that one report includes and the other leaves out. The child then says what each writer wanted the reader to think.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. Two kinds of feature are given as examples: loaded wording, and facts put in or left out. The child identifies the features; the statement does not ask the child to judge whether the author is right. No kind of text is named.

### Common mistakes

Children state the author's view without pointing to anything in the text that shows it. They notice strong words but not what is missing. They assume that a text with facts in it has no point of view.
