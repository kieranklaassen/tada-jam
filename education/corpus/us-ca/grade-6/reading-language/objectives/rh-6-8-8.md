## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Reading a text, the child tells three kinds of statement apart: a reasoned judgment, an opinion, and a fact.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes a page about a king and colours each sentence: one colour for what can be checked ("he ruled for 40 years"), one for what someone only feels ("he was the best king"), one for a conclusion argued from evidence ("tax records suggest his wars emptied the treasury"). The child explains the third colour by naming the evidence the writer gives.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. Three categories are named and no others. The statements to sort are ones found in a text. The child is not asked to decide whether a judgment is correct.

### Common mistakes

Children call any sentence they agree with a fact. They call a reasoned judgment an opinion because it is not certain. They treat a false statement as an opinion, although a claim that can be checked is a factual claim even when it is wrong.
