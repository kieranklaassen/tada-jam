## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When the school year ends, the student reads literary nonfiction at the complexity level set for grades 6 to 8 and understands it well. Texts at the upper end of that range may be read with support where the student needs it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student reads a full memoir chapter or a long magazine essay alone, stops at marked points to say what has happened and what the writer thinks, and answers questions afterwards without rereading the whole text. With a harder text, the student reads with a teacher's notes on background and difficult vocabulary at hand, or reads it in sections with a partner.

### Limits

The target is set for the end of the year. The text type is literary nonfiction. The complexity range is the band for grades 6 to 8, and support is allowed for texts at the top of the band. The statement names no titles, lengths or reading levels.

### Common mistakes

Students read the words fluently and cannot say what the passage meant. They stop at the first hard paragraph. They read nonfiction as a list of facts and miss how the writer's points connect.
