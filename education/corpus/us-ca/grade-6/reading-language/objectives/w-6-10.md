## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Writing is a regular activity for the student. Some pieces take a long stretch of time, with room to research, reflect and revise, and others a short one: one sitting, or one or two days. The writing covers a range of tasks, purposes and audiences that belong to particular subjects.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In one week the student writes a ten-minute response to a science demonstration, finishes a paragraph for history that was begun in class the day before, and continues a three-week research report with notes, a draft and a revision. The student keeps the drafts in a folder and can show how the long piece changed.

### Limits

Two time spans are named: long ones that leave room to research, reflect and revise, and short ones, from one sitting to a couple of days. The tasks, purposes and audiences are specific to disciplines. The statement gives no number of pieces, no length, and no frequency beyond "routinely".

### Common mistakes

Students see writing as something done only in English class and only when a long piece is assigned. They treat a quick piece as unimportant and a long project as one draft written the night before. They do not adjust for the subject and write a science explanation like a story.
