## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student shows that they understand figurative language, how words relate to one another, and fine differences between word meanings. The student explains what a figure of speech, personification for example, means where it appears. The student uses the way particular words are related, for example as an item and its category, a part and its whole, or a cause and its effect, to understand each of those words better. The student tells apart the associations carried by words whose definitions are close, as with thrifty, economical, unwasteful, scrimping and stingy.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student reads "the wind whispered through the fence", says that wind cannot whisper, and then says what the writer wants the reader to hear. The student sorts five words for "careful with money" on a line from the kindest to the harshest and defends the order to a partner. Given the pair "petal : flower", the student names the relationship and uses it to work out an unknown word in a second pair of the same kind.

### Limits

Personification is the only figure of speech named, and it is an example; the statement gives no list of figures for this grade. Three word relationships are named as examples: cause and effect, part and whole, item and category. Figures of speech are read where they occur in a text, not as isolated terms. The statement asks for understanding and does not mention writing figurative language.

### Common mistakes

Students read a figure of speech literally, or can label it ("that is personification") without saying what it means in the passage. They treat words with close definitions as interchangeable and miss that one is an insult and another is praise. With word pairs they match words by topic and not by the relationship.
