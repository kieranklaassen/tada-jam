## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student compares reading a poem, play or story with hearing or watching a version of the same text, whether recorded as sound or video or performed live, and says how the two experiences are alike and different. This includes setting what the student "sees" and "hears" while reading against what they take in when the version is heard or watched.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student reads a scene from a play silently and writes down how one character's voice sounds to them and what the room looks like. The student then watches a filmed performance of the scene and notes what the actor, the lighting and the music did differently. The student tells a partner one thing the page left open that the film decided.

### Limits

The text is a story, a drama or a poem, and the other version is audio, video or a live performance of the same text. The comparison is of the two experiences, including imagined sights and sounds against perceived ones. The statement does not ask which version is better.

### Common mistakes

Students list plot differences between book and film and say nothing about the experience of each. They say which one they liked. They do not notice that what they pictured while reading was their own choice and not in the text.
