## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student takes a useful part in many kinds of shared discussion about grade 6 texts, topics and issues (led by the teacher, in a group, or with one partner, and with partners of different kinds), stating their own ideas clearly and building on what others say. The student arrives having read or studied the set material and uses that preparation openly, pointing to evidence in order to question and think over the ideas being discussed. The student follows the rules of a respectful discussion, fixes specific deadlines and goals, and settles who does what, as needed. The student asks and answers specific questions fully and in detail, with comments that add to the subject of the discussion. The student goes back over the main ideas that were voiced and, by reflecting and paraphrasing, shows that they understand more than one point of view.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Before a discussion of a novel chapter, the student rereads it and marks two passages to bring up. In a group of four, the student listens to a classmate's point, says "I want to add to what you said", and adds a detail from the page. The group agrees who keeps time, who takes notes, and what must be done by the end of the lesson. Near the end, the student restates in their own words a view they do not share and asks its owner whether the restatement is right.

### Limits

The discussions are of three kinds (with one partner, in a group, led by the teacher) and concern grade 6 texts, topics and issues. Preparation means material that was required reading or study. The words "as needed" close the line about rules, goals, deadlines and roles: they plainly cover roles, and the line leaves open whether they also cover goals and deadlines. The statement sets no group size, length or number of contributions.

### Common mistakes

Students wait for their turn to speak and do not respond to what was just said. They give an opinion with no reference to the material they prepared. They repeat a classmate's words exactly when asked to paraphrase, or paraphrase only the views they agree with.
