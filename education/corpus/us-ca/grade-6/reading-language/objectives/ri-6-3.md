## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student examines closely how a text brings in an important person, event or idea, how it illustrates it, and how it builds on it, for instance by means of anecdotes or examples.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Reading a profile of an inventor, the student marks where the person first appears and what the reader is told first. The student lists the anecdotes and examples that follow and writes next to each what it adds to what the reader knows about the person. The student then explains in a short paragraph how the writer moved from the first mention to the full account.

### Limits

The subject may be a person, an event or an idea, and it is a key one in the text. Three steps are named: bringing the subject in, illustrating it, and elaborating on it. Examples and anecdotes are given as examples of means, not as a full list. The statement asks for detail but sets no length or number.

### Common mistakes

Students retell what the text says about the person and do not look at how the writer presents it. They do not see what an anecdote is for and treat it as a side story. They describe only the introduction and stop.
