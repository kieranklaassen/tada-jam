## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student writes arguments in which claims are backed by relevant evidence and clearly stated reasons. The piece opens by stating the claim or claims and arranges the evidence and reasons in a clear order. The support draws on sources that can be trusted and shows that the student understands the text or topic. Clauses, phrases and single words make plain how reasons and claims are connected, and the style is formal from the first line and stays so. The ending, whether a sentence or a whole section, follows from the argument the piece has made.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student takes a position on whether the school day should start later and writes the claim in one sentence. The student gathers two facts from a health article and one from a school survey, notes where each came from, and puts the strongest reason first. Drafting, the student joins claim and reason with "because" and "therefore", replaces "I feel like" and "kinda" with plain formal wording, and writes a last paragraph that returns to the claim without adding a new reason.

### Limits

The writing type is argument. The lettered parts cover five things: an opening claim with organised support, credible sources, linking language, formal style, and a conclusion. The wording allows one claim or several. The statement sets no length and no number of sources, and it does not mention answering an opposing view.

### Common mistakes

Students state an opinion and repeat it in other words in place of reasons. They give evidence without saying how it supports the claim, or use a source they have not judged. They end with a new argument or simply stop.
