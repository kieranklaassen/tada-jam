## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student identifies the purpose or the point of view of the author of a text. The student explains what in the text makes that purpose or viewpoint visible.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student reads an opinion column about school start times and writes in one sentence what the writer wants readers to think or do. The student highlights words that show approval or disapproval, the facts the writer chose, and anything left out, and says how each shows the writer's position. The student reads one highlighted sentence aloud and says how a writer with the opposite view would have put it.

### Limits

Either point of view or purpose may be asked for; the statement joins them with "or". The student must also explain how the text shows it, so naming it is not enough. No text type is set beyond the strand, informational text.

### Common mistakes

Students answer "to inform" or "to persuade" for every text and give nothing from the text. They confuse the author's view with their own view of the topic. They assume that a text full of facts has no point of view.
