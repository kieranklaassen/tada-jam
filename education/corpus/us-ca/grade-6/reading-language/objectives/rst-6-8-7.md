## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child puts together two forms of the same quantitative or technical information: the form a text gives in words and a visual form of it. Tables, graphs, models, diagrams and flowcharts are the examples of visual forms.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a paragraph that gives the temperature of water at each minute of heating and finds the same readings on a line graph. The child puts a finger on the point the paragraph calls the boiling point and checks that the numbers agree. The child uses the graph to answer a question the paragraph leaves open, such as when the rise was fastest.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. The information is quantitative or technical, and the two versions hold the same information: one in words, one visual. Five visual forms are given as examples. The statement does not ask the child to make the visual.

### Common mistakes

Children read the graph or table without its axis labels and units. They do not notice that the text and the diagram describe the same thing, and treat them as two topics. They trust one version and do not notice that it disagrees with the other.
