## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student brings together information on a topic or issue that comes in words and in other media or formats, for example as numbers or as images, so as to form one connected understanding of it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Studying a river flood, the student reads a news article, reads a bar graph of rainfall by month, and looks at a map of the flooded streets. The student writes down one fact from each and then one statement that needs all three to be true. Where the graph and the article seem to disagree, the student goes back to both and checks the dates and units.

### Limits

Visual and quantitative presentation are named as examples of formats beside words. The statement asks for one coherent understanding of a single topic or issue, not a separate account of each source. It sets no number of sources. A related statement in Speaking and Listening is SL.6.2.

### Common mistakes

Students read the text and ignore the graph, or describe each source in turn without connecting them. They misread a graph's scale or units. They do not notice when a picture and the text give different information.
