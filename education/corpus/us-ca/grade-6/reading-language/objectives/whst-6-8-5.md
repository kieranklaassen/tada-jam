## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child improves a piece of writing where it needs it, with some help and guidance from adults and peers. The ways named are planning, editing, revising, rewriting, or trying another approach. The attention is on how well the piece serves its audience and purpose.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes a short plan before a history report, then exchanges drafts with a classmate. The classmate says which paragraph was confusing; the teacher asks who the report is for. The child moves a paragraph, cuts a part that does not serve the purpose, corrects spelling, and rewrites the opening for a reader who has not studied the topic.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. Some guidance and support is part of the statement: the child is not expected to do this alone, and the help comes from peers as well as adults. The ways of improving are joined by "or" and used as needed; they are not a fixed sequence. The focus named is purpose and audience.

### Common mistakes

Children treat revising as correcting spelling and punctuation and leave the content as it was. They respond to a comment by adding a sentence instead of rethinking the part. They resist starting again even when the plan does not work.
