## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student supports an analysis of a literary text by citing evidence from it, both for things the text says directly and for things the student concludes by inference.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a chapter, the student says that a character is jealous and reads aloud the two lines of dialogue that show it. The student copies a short quotation into a written response, gives the page, and adds a sentence that explains what the quotation shows. When a partner disagrees, both go back to the page and reread the passage.

### Limits

Two kinds of claim are covered: what is said outright and what is inferred. The statement sets no number of pieces of evidence and does not say whether evidence is quoted or paraphrased. The strand is literature; the same expectation for informational text is RI.6.1.

### Common mistakes

Students retell the plot when asked for evidence. They quote a long passage and do not say which words matter or what they show. They state a feeling about a character ("she is mean") from their own reaction and cannot find a line that supports it.
