## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student backs up an analysis of an informational text with evidence cited from that text. This holds both for what the text states outright and for conclusions the student infers from it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After reading an article about a drought, the student answers "Why did the farmers change crops?" and underlines the sentence that gives the reason. For a question the article does not answer directly, the student writes a conclusion and then lists two details that led to it, with the paragraph where each is found. The student says "the article states" for the first answer and "I can tell because" for the second.

### Limits

The statement covers two kinds of claim: what is said outright and what is inferred. It does not say how much evidence is needed, or whether it must be quoted word for word or may be paraphrased. The strand is informational text; the same expectation for literature is RL.6.1.

### Common mistakes

Students answer from what they already know about the topic and not from the text. They quote a sentence that mentions the topic but does not support the point. They present an inference as if the text had said it, or give the inference with no evidence.
