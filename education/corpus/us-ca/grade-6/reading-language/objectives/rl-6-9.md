## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student compares texts of different genres or forms that deal with similar topics and themes, and says how their treatments are alike and how they differ. The pairs given as examples are poems with stories, and fantasy stories with historical novels.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student reads a poem about leaving home and a short story about the same thing. The student writes what each says about leaving and then how each says it: the poem with a few images and line breaks, the story with events and dialogue. The student marks one thing the poem can do that the story cannot, and the reverse.

### Limits

The texts differ in form or genre and are alike in theme or topic. Two pairings are given as examples, not as a required list. The statement asks for more than one text and sets no upper number.

### Common mistakes

Students compare what happens in the two texts and ignore form. They say the two have "the same theme" and stop, without showing how each treats it. They describe each text in turn and never bring the two together.
