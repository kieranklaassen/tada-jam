## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

From a literary text the student works out two things: a central idea or a theme, and the way particular details carry it. The student also summarises the text, and their own judgments and opinions are kept out of the summary.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After reading a short story, the student writes the theme as a full sentence about people or life, not as one word, and marks three moments in the story that show it. The student then retells the story in a short paragraph: who is in it, what happens, how it ends. The student rereads the paragraph and removes "I liked" and "the best part".

### Limits

Either a theme or a central idea will do; the statement asks for one. The summary leaves out the student's opinions and judgments. No genre or text length is set here; RL.6.10 names stories, dramas and poems as kinds of literature read at this grade.

### Common mistakes

Students give the theme as a single word ("friendship") or as a moral command brought in from outside the story. They confuse theme with plot and retell events. In the summary they include every event at equal length or add what they thought of the ending.
