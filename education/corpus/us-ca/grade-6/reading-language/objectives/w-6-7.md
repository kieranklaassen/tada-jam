## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

To answer a question, the student does research in brief projects. The student changes the focus of the inquiry when that is appropriate, and uses several sources.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student asks "Why are monarch butterflies disappearing?" and reads an encyclopedia entry, a news article and a wildlife agency page. After finding that the question is too wide, the student narrows it to one cause in one region and looks for a source on that. The student writes a one-page answer over a few days and lists the sources used.

### Limits

The projects are short and start from a question. The sources are "several"; the number is not given. The statement does not say who chooses the question. Judging and crediting sources is the subject of W.6.8.

### Common mistakes

Students collect facts about a topic and never answer the question. They rely on the first source found. They keep a question that is too broad or cannot be answered and do not adjust it.
