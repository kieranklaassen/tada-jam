## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Writing is a regular habit, across a range of subject-specific audiences, purposes and tasks. Some pieces are short, done in one sitting or within a couple of days; others stretch over a longer period that leaves time to reflect and revise.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In one week the child writes a ten-minute explanation of a science demonstration, a paragraph answering a history question for homework, and another section of a research report begun two weeks earlier. The child returns to the report after a few days, rereads it, and changes it.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. Two time spans are named: short (a day or two, or one sitting) and extended (with time to reflect and revise). The tasks, purposes and audiences are those of the subjects. No number of pieces, length or frequency is set beyond "routinely".

### Common mistakes

Children treat every piece as a single draft to finish and hand in, including the long ones. They write with care in language class and less carefully in a science or history answer. They put off a long piece until the last day, so no time is left to revise.
