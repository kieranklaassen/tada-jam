## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child collects information that relates to the topic from several sources, both print and digital and both primary and secondary, and uses search terms well. For every source the child judges how accurate and how credible it is. When using other people's data and conclusions, the child quotes them or puts them in their own words, does not plagiarise, and cites in a standard format.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child researches a historical event with a database, a library book and a scanned letter from the time. The child tries two or three search phrases and keeps the one that brings useful results, then checks who wrote each page and whether another source agrees with it. The child copies one sentence inside quotation marks, retells another finding in their own words, and lists every source in the format the class uses.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. Sources are several, in print and digital form, primary and secondary; no number is set. Every source is to be judged for accuracy and credibility. A standard citation format is required and none is named. The standard ends with the California mark, which shows a California addition; the wording does not show which words California added.

### Common mistakes

Children change a few words of a copied passage and regard it as their own. They take the first search result as reliable without looking at who wrote it. They leave out the citation for an idea they put in their own words, thinking only direct quotations need one.
