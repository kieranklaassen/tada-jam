## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out what the main ideas or information in a source are, whether the source is primary or secondary. The child then sums the source up correctly, keeping the summary apart from their own opinions and from what they knew beforehand.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a one-page speech from the period being studied and writes in two or three sentences what the speaker said. A partner checks each sentence against the speech and crosses out anything the speech does not say. The child finds a sentence that came from their own view and removes it.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. The source may be primary or secondary. The summary holds only what the source says: the child's opinions and earlier knowledge stay out. No length is set for the summary.

### Common mistakes

Children add what they learned elsewhere about the event, so the summary says more than the source does. They add a judgement ("this was unfair") to the summary. They retell every detail in order instead of picking the main ideas.
