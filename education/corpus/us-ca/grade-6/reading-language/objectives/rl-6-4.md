## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student works out what words and phrases mean in a literary text as they are used there, including connotations and figurative meanings. The student examines how one particular choice of word affects the tone and the meaning. The statement points to Language standards 4 to 6 of this grade for further expectations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student reads a poem line where the sea "grumbles" and says what the word makes the sea seem like. The student swaps the word for "moves" or "roars", reads the line aloud each time, and says how the feeling of the line changes. In a story, the student finds a verb the narrator uses for a character, such as "crept", and explains what it suggests that "walked" would not.

### Limits

Two kinds of meaning are named: figurative and connotative. The analysis is of one specific word choice and its effect on meaning and tone. The statement refers to L.6.4, L.6.5 and L.6.6 for more. California adds to this statement, and the record does not show which words are the addition.

### Common mistakes

Students explain what a word means and stop before saying what it does to the tone. They confuse tone (the writer's attitude) with mood (what the reader feels) or with their own feeling about the story. They read a figurative phrase literally.
