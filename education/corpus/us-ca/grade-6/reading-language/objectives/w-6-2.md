## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student writes informative or explanatory texts that look into a topic and pass on concepts, ideas and information by choosing, ordering and analysing content that is relevant. The text opens with the topic or a thesis statement and orders its material with strategies such as cause and effect, comparison and contrast, classification and definition; where it helps the reader understand, the student adds formatting such as headings, graphics such as tables and charts, and multimedia. To develop the topic the student brings in facts that are relevant, concrete details, definitions, quotations, or further examples and information. Suitable transitions show how concepts and ideas are related, exact wording and the vocabulary of the subject do the informing or explaining, and the style is formal and stays formal. The ending, a sentence or a section, follows from what was explained or reported.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student writes a two-page explanation of how earthquakes happen. The student opens with one sentence that states the topic, groups notes under headings such as "causes" and "effects", and draws a labelled diagram of two plates. The student defines "fault" the first time it appears, quotes one sentence from a source, and checks each paragraph for a linking phrase such as "as a result" or "in contrast". The last paragraph restates the explanation in brief and adds no new fact.

### Limits

The writing type is informative or explanatory. Four organising strategies are named with "such as", so they are examples: definition, classification, comparison and contrast, cause and effect. Headings, charts and tables are examples of formatting and graphics, and these and multimedia are included only when they help the reader understand. The statement sets no length and no number of sources. California adds to part a, and the record does not show which words are the addition.

### Common mistakes

Students list facts in the order they found them, with no grouping and no opening statement of the topic. They copy sentences from a source in place of explaining. They add opinions ("volcanoes are awesome") or address the reader informally, and they end without a conclusion.
