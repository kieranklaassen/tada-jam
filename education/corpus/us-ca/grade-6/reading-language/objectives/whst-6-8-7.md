## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child carries out short research projects that answer a question, which may be one the child has asked. The child uses several sources, and along the way raises further focused questions, linked to the first, that allow more than one direction of inquiry.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child asks why a local river floods and looks for the answer in a library book, a map of the watershed and a city web page. While reading, the child writes down new, narrower questions (how much rain fell in the worst year? what did the levee change?) and picks one or two to follow. The child writes a short answer that uses all three sources.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. The projects are short; no length in days or pages is given. The question may come from someone else or from the child. Several sources are required; no number is set. The further questions are to be related and focused.

### Common mistakes

Children ask a question too broad to answer in a short project ("what is weather?"). They take the whole answer from the first source they find. They collect facts on the topic and never return to the question.
