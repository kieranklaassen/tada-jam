## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Reading a text, the child tells apart three things: speculation, judgments reasoned from research findings, and facts.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads an article about a newly found fossil and marks each sentence: measured or observed ("the skull is 30 centimetres long"), concluded from the research ("the tooth wear indicates it ate plants"), or guessed beyond the evidence ("it may have lived in herds"). The child underlines the words that signal a guess, such as "may", "might" and "could".

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. Three categories are named. The middle one is judgment that rests on research findings, which is narrower than judgment in general. The statements to sort are ones found in a text.

### Common mistakes

Children treat everything printed in a science text as fact. They miss hedging words such as "may" and "suggests" and report a guess as a result. They call a well-supported conclusion "only an opinion" because it is not directly observed.
