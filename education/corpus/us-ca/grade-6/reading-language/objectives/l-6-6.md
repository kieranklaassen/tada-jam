## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student learns words and phrases suited to the grade, both general academic ones and ones that belong to a particular subject, and uses them accurately. When a word or phrase matters for comprehension or for expression, the student builds up what they know about it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student meets "analyze" in an assignment and "erosion" in a science chapter, writes each in a notebook with a sentence of their own, and uses both in the next written answer. When a word in a text keeps the student from understanding it, the student stops, looks at how it is used, asks or looks it up, and notes related forms such as "analysis". In discussion the student tries the new word aloud, and a partner says whether it fits.

### Limits

The statement names two kinds of vocabulary: general academic words used across subjects, and words tied to one subject. Both are to be suited to the grade; no word list or count is given. The statement does not say how vocabulary knowledge is to be gathered.

### Common mistakes

Students recognise an academic word when reading but do not use it, or use it with the wrong grammar ("I did an analyze"). They take a subject word in its everyday sense, such as "table", "volume" or "property". They memorise a definition and cannot use the word in a sentence of their own.
