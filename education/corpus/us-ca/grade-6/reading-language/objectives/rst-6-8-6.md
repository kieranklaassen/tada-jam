## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child examines why an author, in a text, discusses an experiment, sets out a procedure, or explains something.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a passage that describes an experiment with yeast and asks what the writer wants from the reader: to repeat it, to accept a result, or to understand how yeast works. The child points to the parts of the text that show this, such as numbered steps or a sentence that states the finding.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. Three things an author does are named: explaining, describing a procedure, discussing an experiment. The statement asks about purpose only; it does not mention point of view or bias.

### Common mistakes

Children give the topic when asked for the purpose ("it is about yeast"). They answer "to inform" for every science text without saying what the reader is meant to do or understand. They assume a procedure is written only to be followed, when it may be there so that the reader can judge the result.
