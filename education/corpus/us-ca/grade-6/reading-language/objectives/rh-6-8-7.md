## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child puts together what is shown visually with the other information in texts, printed and digital. Maps, videos, photographs, graphs and charts are the examples of visual information.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a paragraph about a trade route and studies a map of it. The child traces the route with a finger, finds the towns the paragraph names, and says what the map adds: the distance and the mountains in the way. The child writes one answer that uses a fact from the map and a fact from the paragraph.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. Five kinds of visual are given as examples (chart, graph, photograph, video, map). The texts are print and digital. The child combines the two kinds of information; no number of sources is set.

### Common mistakes

Children read the paragraph and skip the map or graph, or look at the picture and skip the text. They describe each separately and do not say how one explains the other. They misread a map key or the scale of a graph and use the wrong number in the answer.
