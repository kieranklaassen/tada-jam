## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student examines how one part of a text (a section, a chapter, a paragraph or a single sentence) fits into the structure of the whole and helps develop its ideas. The student also examines how popular media use text features such as captions, headers and graphics.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student takes an article cut into its paragraphs, puts them back in order, and says what job each one does: states the problem, gives an example, answers an objection. The student removes one paragraph and says what a reader would no longer understand. With a magazine page or a web article, the student points to the headline, a caption and a chart and says what each tells the reader before the main text is read.

### Limits

The part examined can be as small as one sentence or as large as a chapter or section. Part a is about popular media and names graphics, headers and captions as examples of text features. The statement names no particular text structures. California adds to part a, and the record does not show which words are the addition.

### Common mistakes

Students summarise what a paragraph says when asked what it does in the text. They skip captions, sidebars and charts and read only the main text. They treat a heading as decoration and do not use it to predict or locate information.
