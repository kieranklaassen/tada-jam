## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student's analysis, reflection and research in writing rest on evidence taken from texts, literary ones or informational ones. For literature the student applies the grade 6 Reading standards; the example quoted is the standard on comparing texts of different genres or forms that treat similar topics and themes. For literary nonfiction the student does the same; the example quoted is the standard on following and judging a text's argument and telling its supported claims from its unsupported ones.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After reading a poem and a story on the same theme, the student writes two paragraphs comparing them and quotes one line from each for every point made. After reading an essay, the student writes a response that names the writer's main claim, quotes the evidence given for it, and points out one claim left unsupported. In both pieces the student gives the page or paragraph of each quotation.

### Limits

The texts are of two kinds: literature (part a) and literary nonfiction (part b). Each part quotes one Reading standard as an example, so the quoted standards are not the only ones that apply. The evidence has three uses: analysis, reflection and research. The statement sets no number of quotations or texts.

### Common mistakes

Students write about a text from memory and give no quotation. They insert a quotation without explaining what it shows. They summarise the text when the task asks for analysis.
