## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes arguments that centre on the content of a particular subject. The piece opens with one or more claims on an issue or topic, recognises that other or opposing claims exist and sets its own apart from them, and puts its reasons and evidence in a logical order. Sound reasoning backs each claim, together with accurate, relevant evidence and data from sources that can be trusted, in a way that shows the writer understands the text or topic. Clauses, phrases and words tie the piece together and make clear how the evidence, reasons, counterclaims and claims relate. The style is formal throughout, and a closing section or statement follows from the argument and supports it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

For science class the child writes a page arguing that a dam should, or should not, be removed from a local river. The first paragraph states the claim and names the opposite view. Each later paragraph gives one reason with a figure or finding from a named source, and the child adds linking words such as "because" and "however" between claim, reason and counterclaim. The child rereads the page, replaces casual phrases, and checks that the last paragraph follows from the reasons given.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. It belongs to the writing strand for history/social studies, science and technical subjects, so the argument is about subject content. Opposing claims are to be recognised and told apart from the writer's own; no part asks the writer to refute them. Sources are to be credible. No length, number of reasons or number of sources is set.

### Common mistakes

Children state a claim and then repeat it in other words instead of giving reasons. They give evidence without saying how it supports the claim. They leave out the opposing view, or mention it and do not separate it from their own claim.
