## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When writing, the student shows control of the standard English rules for capital letters, punctuation marks and spelling. The student sets apart a non-restrictive or parenthetical element with dashes, parentheses or commas. The student gets the spelling of words right.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student writes a sentence about a relative and adds a detail in the middle, such as "my cousin, who lives in Fresno, plays drums", then puts a comma on each side of the added detail. The student reads the sentence aloud without the added part to check that it still makes sense. The student tries the same detail inside parentheses and between dashes and says which fits the sentence best. Before handing in a draft, the student rereads it for capital letters and looks up a word whose spelling they doubt.

### Limits

The statement applies to writing only. For marking off an added element it names three marks: commas, parentheses and dashes. Spelling has no word list and no stated level; the statement only asks that it be right. The statement gives no particular rule for capital letters. A footnote explains the asterisk on part a: starting with grade 3, it marks a skill expected to keep needing work in later grades, as students' writing and speech become more demanding.

### Common mistakes

Students put a comma before the added element and forget the one after it. They put commas around information the sentence needs ("Students, who finish early, may read"), which changes the meaning. They type a hyphen where a dash is meant, and they misspell words that sound alike (their, there, they're) because a spelling tool does not flag them.
