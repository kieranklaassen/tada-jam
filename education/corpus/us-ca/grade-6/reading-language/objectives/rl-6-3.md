## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student describes how the plot of a given story or play develops through a sequence of episodes. The student also describes how the characters react or change while the plot approaches its resolution.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student writes each main episode of a story on a card and lays the cards in order from the opening problem to the ending. Under each card the student notes what the main character does or feels at that point. The student then tells a partner how the character at the last card differs from the character at the first, and which episode caused the change.

### Limits

The text is a story or a drama; poems are not named. Two things are described together: the plot as a series of episodes, and what the characters do or how they change on the way to the resolution. The statement names no plot diagram and no set number of episodes.

### Common mistakes

Students list events with "and then" and do not say how one leads to the next. They describe a character as the same from start to finish. They take the most exciting moment for the resolution.
