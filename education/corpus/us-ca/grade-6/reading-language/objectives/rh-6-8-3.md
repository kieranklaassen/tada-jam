## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Where a text describes a process from history or social studies, the child picks out the main steps of that process. The examples given are the raising or lowering of interest rates and the way a bill turns into a law.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a passage on how a proposed law passes through a legislature and writes each main step on a separate card. The child lays the cards in order and removes a card that holds only a detail. The child then retells the process from the cards without looking at the passage.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. The process is one that a text describes, and it belongs to history or social studies. Two examples are given (law-making and changes in interest rates); they are examples, not a list. The child identifies the steps; the statement does not ask the child to judge or explain the process.

### Common mistakes

Children list every sentence as a step and do not separate main steps from details. They keep the order in which the text mentions the steps when the text does not tell them in time order. They leave out a step that the text states only briefly.
