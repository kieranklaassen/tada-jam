## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child says in what way the information in a text is laid out: for example by cause and effect, as a comparison, or in sequence.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads three short passages about one city: the first tells events year by year, the second sets two rulers side by side, the third explains why a famine followed a drought. The child sorts them by how each is organised and points to the signal words ("then", "unlike", "because") that show it. The child draws a timeline for the first, a two-column chart for the second, and arrows from cause to effect for the third.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. Three ways of organising are given as examples: in sequence, by comparison, by cause. They are examples; other structures are not excluded. The child describes the structure; the statement does not ask why the author chose it.

### Common mistakes

Children say what the text is about when asked how it is organised. They call every text with dates a sequence, even when its main work is to explain causes. They read "this happened after that" as "this happened because of that".
