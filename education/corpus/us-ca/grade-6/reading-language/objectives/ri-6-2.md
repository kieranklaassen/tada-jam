## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student works out two things about a text: one central idea, and the way particular details carry it. The student also summarises the text, leaving their own opinions and judgments out of the summary.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student reads a three-page article on honeybees, writes one sentence that says what the whole article is mainly about, and highlights three details that support that point. The student then writes a summary of five or six sentences and crosses out any sentence that starts with "I think" or says whether the article was good. A partner reads the summary and checks that nothing in it comes from outside the article.

### Limits

The statement speaks of one central idea; it does not ask for every idea in a text. The summary is to be free of the student's opinions and judgments. No length is set for the text or the summary. The statement for literature, RL.6.2, speaks of a theme as well.

### Common mistakes

Students name the topic ("bees") in place of the idea ("bee colonies are shrinking and this threatens crops"). They pick an interesting detail as the central idea. Their summaries copy sentences from the text, list details without the main point, or end with their own opinion.
