## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Given two sources about one topic, one primary and one secondary, the child examines how they relate to each other.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads a soldier's diary entry and a history-book paragraph about the same battle. The child marks where the book uses something the diary says, where the book adds what the soldier could not know, and where the two disagree. The child says in a sentence or two how the later account depends on the first-hand one or differs from it.

### Limits

The standard is for the band of grades 6 to 8 and is recorded here at grade 6; it sets no separate level for grade 6. One pairing is named: a primary source with a secondary source, both about one topic. The statement does not say what kinds of relationship to look for.

### Common mistakes

Children treat the primary source as always true because the writer was there. They summarise each source in turn and do not say how the two connect. They cannot tell which source is which when the secondary source quotes the primary one.
