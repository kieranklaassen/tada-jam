## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student examines how one part of a literary text (a stanza, a scene, a chapter or a single sentence) fits into the structure of the whole work, and how it helps develop the plot, the setting or the theme.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student covers the last stanza of a poem, reads the rest, then uncovers it and says what it changes. In a novel, the student picks the chapter where the setting moves and explains why the story needs that move at that point. The student finds one sentence that hints at the ending and marks the later page where the hint is answered.

### Limits

The part may be a sentence, a chapter, a scene or a stanza, so the statement covers prose, drama and poetry. Its contribution is to one of three things: theme, setting or plot. The statement names no structural terms such as flashback or rising action.

### Common mistakes

Students retell the chapter when asked what it does for the whole. They treat stanzas and scenes as separate pieces with no link to what comes before and after. They notice what a part does for the plot and overlook setting and theme.
