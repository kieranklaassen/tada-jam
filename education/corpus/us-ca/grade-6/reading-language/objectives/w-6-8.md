## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student collects relevant information from several sources, digital ones and print ones, and judges how far each source can be trusted. The student quotes or puts into their own words other people's data and conclusions, without plagiarising, and gives basic bibliographic details for the sources.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

For each source, the student writes the author, title, date and where it was found on a note card. The student asks who wrote it and why, and sets aside a web page with no author and no date. Taking notes, the student closes the book before writing a paraphrase, puts quotation marks around any words copied exactly, and marks which card each note came from. The finished piece ends with a list of the sources.

### Limits

Sources are both print and digital, and more than one. Every source is to be judged for credibility; the statement gives no criteria. Bibliographic information is "basic", and no citation style is named. Both quoting and paraphrasing are allowed.

### Common mistakes

Students change a few words of a source sentence and call it a paraphrase. They trust a site because it looks professional or appears first in a search. They forget to record where a note came from and cannot credit it later.
