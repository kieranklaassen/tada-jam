## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student follows the argument of a text and its individual claims, and judges them. In doing so the student separates the claims that the text backs with evidence and reasons from those it leaves unsupported.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student reads a persuasive article on banning plastic bags, numbers each claim in the margin, and draws a line from each claim to the reason or evidence given for it. A claim with no line gets a question mark. The student then says whether the argument as a whole is sound, pointing to the unsupported claims.

### Limits

The statement asks for two things: following the argument and judging it. The only test of a claim that the statement spells out is whether reasons and evidence are given for it. The text is an argument within the strand of informational text. A related statement for a spoken argument is SL.6.3.

### Common mistakes

Students judge the argument by whether they agree with it. They count a repeated claim or a strongly worded opinion as evidence. They cannot tell the main claim from the reasons given for it.
