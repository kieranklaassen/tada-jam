## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out which of a person's own actions can end in someone being injured or harmed.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

For a few days a sixth grader keeps a list of things they do that carry risk: crossing while looking at a phone, riding without a helmet, balancing on a wall to impress friends, pushing in a corridor, taking a dare, playing rough when angry. For each the child writes what could happen, to whom, and what leads to it (hurry, friends watching, anger). The child picks one to change.

### Limits

Standard 2 is analyzing influences. The focus is the person's own conduct; the part others play is a neighbouring statement (6.2.1.S). No behaviour is named, and the statement does not say who is hurt.

### Common mistakes

Children think an action is safe because nothing went wrong the last time. Many judge risk by how skilled they feel and not by what could happen. Some count only dramatic actions and leave out everyday ones such as rushing or not looking.
