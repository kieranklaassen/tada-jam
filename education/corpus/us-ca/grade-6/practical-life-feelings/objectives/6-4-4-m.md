## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows that they can use the steps for resolving a conflict.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two eleven-year-olds in a real or acted quarrel, for example over a rumour or a place on a team, go through the steps: calm down, each tells their side in 'I' sentences, each repeats what the other said, both list solutions, they agree on one and check later whether it worked. The child keeps to the order when they get upset. The child acts as a go-between for two classmates.

### Limits

The statement does not say which steps or how many. It is about using them, so describing them is not enough.

### Common mistakes

Children skip the listening step and go straight to their own solution. Many use 'I' sentences to accuse: 'I feel that you are a liar'. Some regard the conflict as resolved when an adult has ruled, not when both sides agree.
