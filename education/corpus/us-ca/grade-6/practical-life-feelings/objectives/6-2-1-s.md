## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out what part they themselves play, and what part other people play, in bringing an injury about or stopping it from happening.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old reads an account of a bicycle crash and marks who did what: the rider wore no helmet and looked at a phone, a friend dared the rider to go faster, a driver opened a door without looking, a parent had checked the brakes. The child sorts each action into "made it more likely" or "made it less likely". The child says what they could have done in the rider's place and in the friend's place.

### Limits

Standard 2 is analyzing influences. The statement covers both directions (bringing an injury about and stopping it) and both the child and other people. It names no kind of injury and no setting.

### Common mistakes

Children blame one person or call it bad luck, and miss that the actions of several people add up. Many see themselves only as the one who got hurt and leave out what they did. Some do not count a dare or cheering as playing a part.
