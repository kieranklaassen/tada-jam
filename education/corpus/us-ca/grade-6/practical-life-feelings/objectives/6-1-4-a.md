## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names what people gain when the places around them are free of tobacco.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old compares two drawings of a park, one with smokers and cigarette ends and one without, and lists what is better in the second: cleaner air, no smell on clothes, no litter, less chance of fire. The child adds what changes for a baby, for a person with asthma and for someone who is trying to quit.

### Limits

Standard 1 is essential concepts: naming the gains is all that is asked. The statement does not say which places are meant, and it does not mention electronic cigarettes. The harm from other people's smoke is a neighbouring statement (6.1.5.A).

### Common mistakes

Children name only the gain for non-smokers' lungs and leave out fire, litter, smell and cost. Some think the gains apply only indoors. Some take a no-smoking rule to be against smokers as people and not against the smoke.
