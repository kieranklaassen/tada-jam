## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names places to turn to when mental, emotional or social health is troubled: both services that give help and sources whose information can be trusted.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old makes a list for their own school and town: the school counsellor, the nurse, a parent or other relative, the family doctor, a youth helpline. For a website or a leaflet, the child checks who wrote it and whether a health organisation published it. The child sorts a set of sources, including a forum post and an advertisement, into 'can trust' and 'check first'.

### Limits

The statement covers both information and services and names none. It asks for naming them; the request for help itself is practised in 6.4.1.M.

### Common mistakes

Children treat a popular video or a friend's advice as reliable because many people like it. Many know that a counsellor exists and do not know how to reach one. Some think help services are only for emergencies.
