## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells why it matters to report a weapon straight away, whether they come across one or know that someone their own age has one.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A twelve-year-old explains for two cases what to do and why speed matters. On finding a gun in a park: do not touch it, leave the spot and tell an adult or the police straight away, because the next person to find it may be a small child. On hearing that a classmate has a knife in a bag: tell a teacher the same hour, because the next day may be too late. The child says how this differs from reporting a small matter to get someone punished.

### Limits

Standard 4 is interpersonal communication, though the verb here is to explain: the child gives the reasons. Two cases are covered, a weapon found and a weapon another young person has. The statement does not say who to report to. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children feel bound not to tell on a friend and treat a weapon like any other secret. Many pick up a found weapon to bring it to an adult. Some wait until they are sure it is real, and the delay is the danger.
