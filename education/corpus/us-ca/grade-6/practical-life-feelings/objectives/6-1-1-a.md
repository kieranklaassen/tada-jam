## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child explains what comes of using tobacco, alcohol, inhalants or other drugs, both soon afterwards and over years. The explanation takes in what such use means for life among people, before the law and in money.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old sorts cards into two piles: what happens within hours of drinking, smoking or sniffing a solvent, and what develops over years. For one substance the child then adds three more cards: a friendship lost, a fine or an arrest, and the money that a pack of cigarettes a day costs in a year. The child gives the reason for each card aloud.

### Limits

Standard 1 is essential concepts, so the child explains and is not asked to act. The statement names four groups of substances and two time spans, and it adds three kinds of consequence beyond health. It sets no list of drugs and no level of medical detail.

### Common mistakes

Children name the far-off harm (lung cancer, liver damage) and leave out what happens the same day. Many think of inhalants as household products and not as drugs. The cost in money and the trouble with the law are the parts most often left out.
