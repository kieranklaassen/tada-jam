## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child gives reasons that it is fitting, and needed, to seek help when a health problem is mental, emotional or social.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old gives reasons of their own; the statement supplies none, and the ones here are the pack's examples. A mind can be hurt or ill as a body can, and a person with a broken arm goes to a doctor without shame. Problems often grow when kept secret, adults know things and people a child does not, and asking shows good sense, not weakness. The child answers a classmate who says 'just get over it'.

### Limits

The statement asks for reasons, not for the act of asking, which is practised in 6.4.1.M. It names three kinds of health problem and no particular helper.

### Common mistakes

Children think asking for help is tattling or weakness. Many believe a problem must be very serious before an adult may be told. Some think a friend's secret must be kept even when the friend is in danger.
