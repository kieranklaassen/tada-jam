## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells why it matters for a person to notice and know what they are feeling.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old explains what goes better when a person knows what they feel: they can say it instead of slamming a door, calm down before answering, ask for help, choose what to do next. The child tells of a moment when they did not notice they were nervous or angry until afterwards, and what happened. The child names the body signals that tell them a feeling is growing: a tight stomach, a hot face.

### Limits

The statement asks why awareness matters and does not ask the child to manage the emotion. Managing stress is 6.7.2.M and self-control in anger is 6.7.4.M.

### Common mistakes

Children think noticing a feeling means giving in to it. Many have few words beyond 'mad', 'sad' and 'fine'. Some see emotions as things to hide and so see no reason to notice them.
