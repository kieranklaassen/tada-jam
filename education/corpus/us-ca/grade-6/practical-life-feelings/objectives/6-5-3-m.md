## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sets the feeling of anger beside what a person does out of anger, says how the two are alike and how they differ, and talks about what follows.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old makes two columns: 'feeling angry' (hot face, fast heart, thoughts of unfairness) and 'what I do with it' (shout, hit, walk away, say 'I'm angry because'). One answer a child might give, which is the pack's example and not the statement's: the feeling is allowed and is not chosen, while the action is chosen. For three actions the child tells what happens next: to a friendship, to themselves, to the problem.

### Limits

The statement does not say whose consequences or of what kind. Showing self-control in anger is 6.7.4.M.

### Common mistakes

Children think anger itself is bad and should not be felt. Many say 'he made me do it' and treat the action as part of the feeling. Some see only punishment as a consequence and leave out hurt friendships and an unsolved problem.
