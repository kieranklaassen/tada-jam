## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When peers are teased or bullied over personal traits or over the sexual orientation others take them to have, the child objects in a fitting way.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old who hears a classmate mocked for their body, clothes or accent, or called 'gay' as an insult, says firmly and without insult, 'Stop, that's not okay', and stands next to the classmate. The child practises short sentences in role-play, checks on the classmate afterwards, and reports to an adult when it continues or is unsafe. The child chooses between speaking up at once, speaking to the teaser alone later, and getting help.

### Limits

The statement names two grounds for the teasing: traits of the person, and the sexual orientation others suppose them to have. It requires that the objection be appropriate and does not define that word. It is about the teasing of peers, not of the child.

### Common mistakes

Children object by insulting or hitting the teaser, or laugh along to stay safe. Many do not count 'that's so gay' as hurtful when no one present is the target. Some think reporting to an adult is tattling.
