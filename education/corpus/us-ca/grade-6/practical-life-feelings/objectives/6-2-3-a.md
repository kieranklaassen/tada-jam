## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out what follows when alcohol or marijuana weakens a person's judgment or affects them in other ways. Four areas are named: the person's own safety, their ties with family and friends, how they do at school, and reaching goals now and later.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A twelve-year-old follows a made-up fourteen-year-old through one evening and the weeks after it: a drink at a party, a ride accepted from a driver who has been drinking, a lie to a parent, a missed test. At each step the child says which choice a sober person would have made. The child then fills in four boxes, one for each named area, with what changed for that person.

### Limits

Standard 2 is analyzing influences. The statement names two substances only, alcohol and marijuana, and four areas of life. It sets no detail on how either substance acts on the brain.

### Common mistakes

Children see only a health risk and leave out school, family and goals. Many think a person who has been drinking can tell that their own judgment is worse. Some believe marijuana leaves judgment unchanged because it makes a person calm.
