## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells why secondhand smoke, the smoke a non-smoker breathes in from someone else's tobacco, is harmful.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

On a drawing of a room a twelve-year-old traces where the smoke from a lit cigarette goes and who breathes it: the smoker, a child on the sofa, a baby in a cot. The child says what it does to each, such as coughing, asthma attacks and ear infections in children, and heart and lung disease in adults over years. The child says why opening a window is not enough.

### Limits

Standard 1 is essential concepts: the child explains and is not asked to act. The statement sets no list of illnesses and no level of detail. What people gain from places without tobacco is a neighbouring statement (6.1.4.A).

### Common mistakes

Children think smoke is harmless once they cannot see or smell it. Many believe an open window or another room removes the harm. Some think only the smoker is harmed, because the smoker breathes in the most.
