## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells why it matters to show empathy for the ways people differ from one another; people who have a disability or a long-lasting illness are named as part of this.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old explains what it is like when others understand you and what it is like when they do not, and why that matters for a classmate who uses a wheelchair, has diabetes or asthma, or learns differently. The child listens to a classmate's or a guest's account and says back what they heard. The child gives one example of a small act that shows understanding, such as waiting without comment while someone checks their blood sugar.

### Limits

The statement names disability and chronic disease as included and does not limit the differences to those. It asks why empathy matters; practising respect and inclusion is 6.7.3.M.

### Common mistakes

Children confuse empathy with pity and speak about a classmate instead of with them. Many think treating everyone exactly the same is always the kind thing. Some assume a condition they cannot see is not real.
