## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works at settling disagreements without violence.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two eleven-year-olds act out a quarrel over a borrowed game that came back broken. Each takes a breath before speaking, says what happened and how they feel in sentences that start with "I", repeats back what the other said, and offers a solution such as paying half or lending something in return. When they cannot agree, they ask an adult or a classmate trained as a mediator to help.

### Limits

Standard 7 is practicing health-enhancing behaviors, so the child does this and does not only explain it; explaining is a separate statement (6.1.1.S). No method is named and no kind of dispute.

### Common mistakes

Children think settling a quarrel means one side winning. Many start with "you always", and the other person defends instead of listening. Some stay calm in a role-play and cannot do it when they are angry, because they skip the pause to cool down.
