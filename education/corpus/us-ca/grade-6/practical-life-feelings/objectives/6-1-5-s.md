## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child looks closely at what can go wrong when a person has a weapon. Three settings are covered: the home, the school and the community.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A twelve-year-old takes three cases and says for each what could happen. A gun kept in a drawer at home: an accident, a small child finding it, use in an argument or by someone who wants to harm themselves. A knife brought to school: expulsion, arrest, a fight in which someone is killed. A weapon carried in the street: being hurt with it, a criminal charge. The child says who else is put in danger each time.

### Limits

Standard 1 is essential concepts: the child studies the dangers and is not asked to act. Three settings are named. The statement lists no dangers and no kind of weapon. A footnote sends the reader to two places for what counts as a weapon in law: a section of the Education Code and the document's own glossary.

### Common mistakes

Children think a weapon makes its owner safer and leave out accidents and the weapon being used against the owner. Many believe a weapon hidden at home will not be found by a younger child. Some think carrying one "for protection" is allowed by school rules and the law.
