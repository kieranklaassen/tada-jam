## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows what keeping control of oneself looks like at a moment of anger.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old shows the sequence in a role-play and then in real moments: notice the signs (clenched hands, hot face), stop, breathe slowly or count, step back, and then say 'I'm angry because' in a level voice. The child tells what they do with the energy afterwards, such as a walk or bouncing a ball. The child returns later to solve the problem that caused the anger.

### Limits

The statement names no technique. It asks the child to show it, so naming techniques is not enough; the difference between the feeling and the behaviour is 6.5.3.M.

### Common mistakes

Children think self-control means not feeling angry, or hiding it. Many know the steps when calm and cannot use them when upset. Some walk away and never come back to the problem.
