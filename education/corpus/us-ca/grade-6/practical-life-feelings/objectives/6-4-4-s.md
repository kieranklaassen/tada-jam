## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child practises communicating and refusing, so as not to get involved with a gang.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a role-play an older pupil offers a twelve-year-old a place in a group and asks them to carry a package. The child says "No, I'm not doing that" in a firm voice, does not insult the other person, repeats the refusal, and leaves towards people they know. Afterwards the child tells a trusted adult about the offer.

### Limits

Standard 4 is interpersonal communication: the child does this, in a rehearsal or for real, and does not only describe it. The statement does not define a gang or say who makes the approach. Doing other things in place of getting involved with a gang is a neighbouring statement (6.7.3.S).

### Common mistakes

Children expect an approach to look like a threat, and do not recognise one that comes as friendship, gifts or protection. Many refuse in a way that mocks the other person, which raises the danger. Some agree to a "small favour" because it does not seem like joining.
