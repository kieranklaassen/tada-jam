## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child gives a short account of the emotions that come with grief and with losing someone or something.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old names what a person may feel after a loss: sadness, anger, guilt, fear, numbness, relief, and sometimes several at once. The child writes a short paragraph about a character whose pet died or whose friend moved away and tells how the feelings changed over the weeks. The child says that people grieve differently and at different speeds.

### Limits

The statement asks for a summary and names no particular feelings. It does not say which losses are meant and sets no stages or order of grief.

### Common mistakes

Children think grief is only sadness and are confused by anger or guilt. Many expect it to be over after a fixed time. Some believe a person who does not cry is not grieving.
