## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells why it matters that a person sets limits of their own: for their safety, for their privacy, and for how feelings and views are expressed.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old says where their own limits lie: who may read their messages, who may touch them and how, what they will and will not share about how they feel, and how they want others to disagree with them. The child practises saying a limit in one sentence, such as 'Please knock first' or 'I don't want to talk about that now'. The child explains what a limit protects and that other people have limits too.

### Limits

The statement asks why boundaries matter and names three areas: a person's privacy, their safety, and the way emotions and opinions are expressed. It does not say what the boundaries should be.

### Common mistakes

Children think setting a limit is rude or means not liking someone. Many state a limit once and drop it when a friend insists. Some guard their own limits and do not notice those of others.
