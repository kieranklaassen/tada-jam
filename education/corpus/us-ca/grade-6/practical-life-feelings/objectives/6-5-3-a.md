## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out what drinking alcohol or taking other drugs leads to in four ways: for health, for feelings, for life with other people and before the law.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A twelve-year-old draws a chart with four parts and fills it for a made-up fifteen-year-old who drinks at weekends: body (hangover, injury, harm to a growing brain), feelings (guilt, low mood), other people (trust lost, a team place lost), law (under-age drinking, a police record). The child marks which results come the same night and which come later, and says which one the person is least likely to have thought of.

### Limits

Standard 5 is decision making. This statement names four kinds of result. It names alcohol and then uses a general term for all other drugs; tobacco is not named separately, and the general term does not exclude it. It sets no detail of the law.

### Common mistakes

Children fill the health part and leave the other three nearly empty. Many know nothing of the law beyond "it is illegal" and cannot say what happens to a minor. Some list only extreme results, such as death or prison, and leave out the common ones.
