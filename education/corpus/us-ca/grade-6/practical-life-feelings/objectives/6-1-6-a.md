## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells how drug use can move step by step into dependence and then addiction. The child also tells what drugs do to the brain of an adolescent.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A sixth grader lays out cards in order: trying a drug, using it regularly, needing more for the same effect, feeling ill without it, not being able to stop. The child tells a short story of a made-up person moving along the cards. On an outline of a head the child marks the front part of the brain, used for planning and self-control, says that it is still growing until about the mid-twenties, and says why a drug affects a young brain more.

### Limits

Standard 1 is essential concepts: the child explains and is not asked to act. The statement speaks of stages and does not name or count them. It names no particular drug and sets no depth of brain science.

### Common mistakes

Children think addiction is a choice a person makes at one moment and can undo by wanting to. Many believe it happens only to adults or only with certain drugs. Some think a young body recovers from anything, so early use carries the least risk.
