## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells why people are more likely to be badly hurt through violence when weapons are present.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A sixth grader compares two versions of the same argument between teenagers, one with no weapon and one where someone has a knife. The child says how each could end: bruises and a broken friendship in the first, a stabbing in the second. The child says in their own words that anger lasts minutes and that a weapon makes those minutes far more dangerous, also for people standing near.

### Limits

Standard 1 is essential concepts: the child describes the link and is not asked to act. The statement names no kind of weapon and no setting. A footnote cites the Education Code.

### Common mistakes

Children think a weapon stops a fight because the other person becomes afraid and stops. Many believe only a person who means harm is dangerous with a weapon, and leave out accidents and sudden anger. Some picture weapons as they appear in films and games, where injuries heal quickly.
