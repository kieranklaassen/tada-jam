## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child urges other people to act safely. Three cases are named: a mouth guard worn in sport, a helmet worn on a bicycle, and a seat belt worn correctly in a car.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old reminds a younger brother to buckle up before the car moves and shows him that the lap belt goes low over the hips and the shoulder belt across the chest. Before a ride with friends the child puts on a helmet first, checks a friend's strap, and says "I'll wait while you get yours". At training the child tells a team-mate why they wear a mouth guard and offers to ask the coach where to get one.

### Limits

Standard 8 is health promotion: the aim is other people's conduct, not only the child's own. Seat belts, helmets and mouth guards are given as included cases, not as the full list. For seat belts the statement says proper use, which is more than wearing one.

### Common mistakes

Children nag or shame, and the other person refuses so as not to look weak. Many urge safety and do not wear the gear themselves, which removes the effect. Some think a helmet or a mouth guard is needed only in matches or on roads, not at training or on a short ride.
