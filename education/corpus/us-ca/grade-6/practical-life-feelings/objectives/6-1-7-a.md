## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names what drinking alcohol, using tobacco and taking other drugs do to a person's ability to be physically active, sport included.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old runs a short distance, then repeats it breathing only through a narrow straw, and says what running is like with a smoker's lungs. The child matches cards: tobacco with less breath and slower recovery, alcohol with slower reactions and poor balance, other drugs with poor coordination and a strained heart. The child says what each would mean for a swimmer, a footballer and a dancer.

### Limits

Standard 1 is essential concepts: naming the effects is what is asked. The statement is about bodily activity in general and gives sport as one case.

### Common mistakes

Children think harm to fitness comes only after years of use. Some believe a drink or a drug improves play by calming nerves. Many leave out balance, reaction time and recovery and name only breathing.
