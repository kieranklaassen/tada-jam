## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names laws and rules whose purpose is to keep people from getting hurt.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old collects examples from three places: school (walk in the corridor, no throwing at break), sport (shin guards, no tackling from behind), and the law (seat belts, bicycle helmets for minors, speed limits near schools, fences round pools). For each the child says which injury it is meant to stop. The child finds out who to ask or where to look when unsure whether something is a law.

### Limits

Standard 3 is accessing valid information: the child names the rules and is not asked to judge them. The statement lists none and names no setting.

### Common mistakes

Children see a rule as an adult's wish to control them and cannot say which injury it guards against. Many confuse a school or family rule with a law. Some think a rule applies only when an adult is watching.
