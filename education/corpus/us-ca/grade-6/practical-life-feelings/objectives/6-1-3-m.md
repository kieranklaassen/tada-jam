## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child talks about the ways feelings shift as a person goes through adolescence.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

An eleven-year-old talks in a small group about what is different now from a few years ago: moods that change faster, stronger feelings about friends, wanting more privacy, embarrassment in front of others. The child links these to growing up. A child might add that such shifts are normal at this age; that reassurance is the pack's example, not something the statement says. The child asks an older student or an adult what that time was like for them.

### Limits

The statement asks for discussion and lists no particular changes. It does not mention physical changes.

### Common mistakes

Children think strong or fast-changing moods mean something is wrong with them. Many believe they are the only one who feels this way. Some explain every feeling by 'hormones' and miss causes such as a quarrel or lack of sleep.
