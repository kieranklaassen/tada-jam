## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells proper use of a medicine apart from misuse, for medicines a doctor prescribes and for those bought without a prescription.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A twelve-year-old reads the label of an empty cough-syrup box and a pharmacy label with a made-up name, and says who the medicine is for, how much to take and how often. Given short cases, the child sorts them: taking the stated dose is use; taking a double dose, taking a sister's pills or taking a pill to stay awake is misuse.

### Limits

Standard 1 is essential concepts: the child tells the two apart and is not asked to give or take medicine. Both kinds of medicine are covered. The statement names no particular medicine and does not mention illegal drugs.

### Common mistakes

Children think a medicine sold without a prescription cannot do harm in any amount. Many think a prescribed medicine is safe for anyone, so sharing it with a friend who has the same complaint seems fine. Some believe that a larger dose works faster.
