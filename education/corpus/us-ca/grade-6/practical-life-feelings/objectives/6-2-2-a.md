## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out how adverts and other selling methods, among them showing admired people, shape whether people use tobacco, alcohol and other drugs.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A sixth grader looks at a printed drink advert and circles what it uses to persuade: young, happy people, a sports star or a singer, bright colours, a party, a slogan. The child says what the advert promises, what it leaves out and who it is aimed at. The child then says how seeing an admired person with a product could change what a twelve-year-old thinks about it.

### Limits

Standard 2 is analyzing influences: the child examines a message and is not asked to make one. Role models are the one technique the statement names. It names no medium, and it does not say whether the role models are in adverts or in the child's own life.

### Common mistakes

Children believe adverts work on other people and not on them. Many notice the product and miss the feeling that is sold with it. Some think a famous person in an advert uses the product and approves of it.
