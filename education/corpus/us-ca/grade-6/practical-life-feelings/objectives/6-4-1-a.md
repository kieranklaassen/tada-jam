## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child uses words well to stay out of places and moments in which people are drinking alcohol, using tobacco or taking other drugs.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a role-play a twelve-year-old is invited to a house where older pupils will be drinking. The child says in a clear voice "No thanks, I'm going to training" or "I'd rather go to the park, come with me", and says it again when pressed. In a second scene the child phones a parent and asks to be picked up.

### Limits

Standard 4 is interpersonal communication, so the child does the communicating and does not only describe it. This statement is about communicating in words and about staying out of the situation. Refusing a direct offer, with words and body language, is a neighbouring statement (6.4.2.A).

### Common mistakes

Children give a vague answer ("maybe", "I'll see") that leaves the invitation open. Many make up an excuse that fails when the other person offers a way round it. Some go along and plan to refuse once they are there.
