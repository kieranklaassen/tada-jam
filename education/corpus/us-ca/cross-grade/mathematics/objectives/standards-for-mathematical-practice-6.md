## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student tries to communicate exactly: using clear definitions when talking and when reasoning, saying what chosen symbols mean, using the equal sign consistently and correctly, labelling the axes of a graph and naming the units of measure. The student calculates accurately and efficiently and gives numerical answers as exactly as the problem calls for. The text says elementary students give each other carefully worded explanations, and that by high school students examine claims and use definitions explicitly.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child says "this side is four cubes long" and not "it's four", and calls a shape a triangle because it has three straight sides, not because it is pointed. An older child writes the unit after an area, labels both axes of a graph, rounds an amount of money to cents, and writes two separate equations where a chain with two equal signs would be false.

### Limits

This practice holds for every grade from kindergarten up and sets no grade-specific content. The text makes one remark about the elementary grades and one about high school.

### Common mistakes

Leaving the unit off an answer or giving the wrong one. Using the equal sign as "and then", as in 3 + 4 = 7 + 5 = 12. Using a loose word such as "bigger" without saying whether longer, heavier or more is meant.
