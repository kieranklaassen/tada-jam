## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student makes sense of the quantities in a problem and of how they relate. Two abilities work together: turning a situation into symbols and working with those symbols for a while without thinking about what they stand for, and stopping when needed to look back at what the symbols mean in the situation. The practice also includes forming a coherent picture of the problem, keeping track of the units, minding what a quantity means and not only how it is calculated, and using properties of operations and objects flexibly.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A younger child hears "five ducks on the pond, two swim away", writes 5 - 2 = 3 and then says "three ducks are left", not only "three". An older child, given three bags of twelve apples to share among four classes, writes the calculation, gets 9, and says that the 9 means apples for each class.

### Limits

This practice holds for every grade from kindergarten up and sets no grade-specific content. Its text gives no examples tied to an age or grade.

### Common mistakes

Calculating with the numbers and giving a bare answer, with no unit and no meaning attached. Choosing an operation from a word in the problem without picturing the situation. Giving an answer that the situation cannot have, such as four and a half buses.
