## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Across grades 3 to 5, the student states a simple design problem that comes from something people need or want. The statement of the problem says what a successful solution must do, and the limits on time, cost or materials.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A student in grades 3 to 5 notices a real nuisance, such as lunch bags falling off the classroom hooks, and writes it down as a problem to solve. The student lists what a good answer must do ("holds ten bags", "a child can reach it") and what limits the work ("only card and tape", "done by Friday", "under two dollars").

### Limits

The expectation holds for the whole band of grades 3 to 5, not for one grade. It carries no clarification statement and no assessment boundary. The problem is a simple one. The constraints named are on materials, time or cost.

### Common mistakes

Naming a solution ("build a shelf") before saying what the problem is. Writing criteria that cannot be checked, such as "it should be good". Confusing criteria (what the solution must do) with constraints (what limits the work).
