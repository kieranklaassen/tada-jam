## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Across grades 3 to 5, the student thinks up several possible solutions to a problem and compares them, judging how well each one would probably satisfy the problem's criteria and stay within its constraints.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A student in grades 3 to 5 sketches three different ways to keep a paper bridge from sagging: folding, rolling, or adding a brace. The student draws a table with the criteria and constraints across the top and marks how each idea would do. The student picks one to build and says why, pointing to the table.

### Limits

The expectation holds for the whole band of grades 3 to 5, not for one grade. It carries no clarification statement and no assessment boundary. The comparison is by how well each idea is expected to do; this expectation does not mention building or testing the ideas.

### Common mistakes

Offering small variations of one idea instead of different ideas. Choosing the favourite or the best-looking idea without checking it against the criteria. Ignoring the constraints when comparing.
