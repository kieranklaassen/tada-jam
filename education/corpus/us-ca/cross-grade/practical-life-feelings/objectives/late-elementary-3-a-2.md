## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

About people and groups, both those like the child and those unlike the child, the child can tell which statements are fact and which are opinion. The child can also name particular strong points and assets of individual people who come from a variety of groups.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten sorts sentence cards about a group into two piles: can be checked ("Many families in this town speak two languages") and someone's view ("Kids from that school are stuck-up"). The child names a specific strength of a particular person, such as a classmate who translates for her grandmother or a neighbour who repairs bicycles. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The facts and opinions are about people and groups, not about any topic at all. The strengths named are those of individuals and are to be specific. The statement lists no groups. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children treat a widely repeated opinion as a fact. Many give a stereotype as a strength ("they are all good at sport") in place of something true of one person. A statement the child agrees with is often classed as fact and one they dislike as opinion.
