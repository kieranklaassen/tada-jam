## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child reads what another person is feeling from what that person says and from their face and body, and says the name of the feeling. The child tells how other people might feel in many kinds of situation, and shows empathy for what they go through.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) looks at a classmate with a turned-down mouth and slumped shoulders and says "she looks sad". Hearing a shaky voice, they ask "are you scared?" About a picture of a child whose ice cream fell, they say how that child feels. They bring a tissue to a classmate who cries, or sit down beside them.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The cues are spoken and physical. The statement lists no emotions and no situations. The child's own emotions are in 1.B.1.

### Common mistakes

Children assume others feel what they themselves would feel, or what they feel right now. They go by the situation and ignore the face, so they call a crying child at a party happy. A smile from embarrassment and tears of joy mislead them.
