## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child practises keeping track of their own state, and puts feelings to use and shows them in ways that are both genuine and helpful. The means named is regulation strategies the child has found to work personally: the child picks them out, can say what they are, and applies them. In different social and cultural settings, the child draws on a range of ways to express themselves well.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten checks on themselves during a hard task and notices rising frustration. The child names what helps them personally ("I count my breaths", "I squeeze the clay", "I walk to the window") and does it. The child tells a friend plainly that they are upset, and chooses a different way to show the same feeling to a grandparent, a coach or a teacher. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The verb is "practice", so the statement expects practising, not mastery. It names no particular strategy and leaves the choice to what works for the child. It asks for expression that suits different social and cultural contexts and does not list those contexts. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children think regulating a feeling means hiding it. Many know a strategy by name but do not use it once the feeling is strong. Some use one way of expressing themselves everywhere, such as joking with a teacher the way they joke with friends.
