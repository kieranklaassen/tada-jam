## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child is able to get their message across firmly while staying respectful. The child advocates for what they need, and also agrees to solutions in which each side gives something up.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten says in a steady voice "I was using that; please give it back" without shouting or whispering. The child tells the group "I need to sit where I can see the board" and, when two want the same seat, agrees to swap halfway through the lesson. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement pairs advocating for one's needs with compromise; neither is put above the other. It names no setting and no kind of need. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children confuse being assertive with being aggressive, or think that being respectful means saying nothing. Many treat compromise as losing. Some give in every time and call that compromise.
