## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child can tell what a helpful member of a community is like. The child can also point out who, in their family and in the place where they learn, is able to give them support at a time they need help.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) says what a helpful person in the class does: "she picks up the pencils that fell" or "he waits for the slow ones". They trace their own hand and say for each finger one person they can go to: Mum, Grandma, the teacher, the playground supervisor, a big cousin.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The helpers are in the child's family and learning environment; the statement does not go wider. It asks the child to describe and identify, not to act.

### Common mistakes

Children name only one helper, usually a parent, and have nobody in mind at school. They name a person who is kind and cannot say what that person could do. They think needing help is for small children and do not ask.
