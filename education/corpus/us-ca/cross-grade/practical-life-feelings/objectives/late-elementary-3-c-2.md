## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can tell which limits are healthy and safe when dealing with people in their family, in the community where they learn, and beyond. The child picks out ways to build trust and to keep it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about nine says which kinds of touch, questions and secrets are fine with a parent, a teacher, a friend and a stranger, and which are not. The child lists what makes them trust someone (keeps a promise, tells the truth, gives back what was borrowed) and does one of those things for a friend that week. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement asks the child to recognise boundaries and to identify trust strategies; keeping to others' boundaries in the moment is 2.E.2 in this band. It names family, the learning community and beyond, and gives no examples of boundaries or of strategies. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children apply one rule to everyone, such as hugging all adults or sharing private things with anyone friendly. Many think trust is restored at once by saying sorry. Some believe a secret must always be kept, including one that feels unsafe.
