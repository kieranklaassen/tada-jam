## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child practises three things: looking after their own time, keeping their things in order, and collecting whatever a given job or activity calls for.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten looks at the clock, sees fifteen minutes are left, and decides to finish the drawing before colouring it. The child puts loose sheets in the right folder and sharpens pencils before starting. For a science activity the child reads the list and fetches the ruler, the cup and the notebook without being handed them. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The verb is "practice": the statement expects practising, not independent mastery. It names three things (time, materials, and what a task requires) and sets no length of task and no amount of adult support. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children underestimate how long a task takes and run out of time. Many start a task and then stop several times to fetch things. A desk or bag that looks tidy is taken for organised when papers are only pushed out of sight.
