## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In dealing with other people the child acts honestly and with integrity, and in every setting expects fair treatment from adults as well as from other children, and says so. The child is starting to understand, and to look into, what unethical actions do to the planet.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten admits a wrong move in a board game and gives back extra change handed over by mistake. When a rule is applied to one team and not the other, the child says so to the classmates or to the adult in charge. The child looks into what happens to a stream when rubbish is dumped in it, or to a forest cut down against the rules. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The words "begin to" apply to the planet: the statement expects a first understanding and exploration of what unethical actions do to it, and names no examples. Fairness is expected from both peers and adults, and in all contexts. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children take "fair" to mean "the same for everyone" or "what I wanted". Many are honest when it costs nothing and tell a lie to avoid trouble or protect a friend. Children see harm to the environment as caused by distant others and not linked to choices people near them make.
