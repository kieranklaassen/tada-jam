## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can name ways in which who a person is and what that person has lived through may make them react with different feelings from someone else. The child is curious about other people and tries to see things from their side, working from what the child knows of the person or group. Empathy and compassion are visible in how the child treats other people.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten notices that fireworks delight one classmate and frighten another who lived through a war, and says why the same event feels different to each. The child asks a new classmate about their old school before guessing how they feel. The child sits with a friend whose pet has died and offers to carry their bag. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. Perspective-taking is described as something the child strives for, using what they know of the person or group; the statement does not claim the child gets it right. The word "may" keeps the link between identity and reaction open. No identities or experiences are listed. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children assume others feel what they themselves would feel in the same situation. Some guess a person's feelings from a group label alone instead of asking the person. Children show sympathy to friends more readily than to peers they do not know or do not like.
