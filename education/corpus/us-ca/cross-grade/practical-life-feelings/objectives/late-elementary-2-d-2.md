## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When someone offers feedback meant to help, the child takes it gladly. In the other direction, the child knows several ways of giving feedback to peers and uses them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten hands a draft story to a partner, listens to the comments without arguing, and changes one thing because of them. When it is their turn, the child says one thing that works and one thing to try ("The start made me curious; I got lost when the dog appeared"), or asks a question, or writes a comment on a sticky note. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The feedback the child gives is feedback to peers; giving feedback to adults is not mentioned. The statement asks for varied strategies and names none. The feedback received is described as constructive, and nothing is said about unkind criticism. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children hear feedback on their work as a judgement of themselves and become defensive or tearful. When giving feedback many say only "it's good" or only list faults. Some confuse feedback with telling the peer how they would have done it themselves.
