## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, when another person has said what their boundaries are, for their body or for their feelings, a child understands those limits and practises keeping to them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) hears "stop, I do not like tickling" and stops at once. They ask "can I hug you?" and accept a "no". When a classmate says "I do not want to talk about my dog", they change the subject.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The boundaries are ones the other person has stated; the statement does not ask the child to guess unstated ones. It is about other people's boundaries; the child's own "no" is in 4.A.1.

### Common mistakes

Children take "stop" in a game as part of the game and carry on. They stop the one thing that was named and do something very close to it. They see a physical boundary and miss an emotional one, and keep asking about something the other child does not want to discuss.
