## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student looks into the kinds of leadership there are and sees that a given situation calls for its own leadership styles, skills and capacities. More and more often the student takes a leading role, thinks back on it, and picks out what they could do better.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), compares how a team captain, a club secretary and a classmate who quietly organises a study group each lead. They take a turn at leading something, such as chairing a meeting, running a warm-up or coordinating a group project. Afterwards they say what went well and name one thing to do differently, such as giving quieter members a turn to speak.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The word "increasingly" describes a growing share of leading, not a required number of roles. No type or style of leadership is named or preferred. Reflection on one's own leading is part of the statement.

### Common mistakes

Young people often equate leading with being in charge, being popular or being the loudest. Many use one style everywhere, for example giving orders in a group of friends as they would in a drill. After leading, they tend to blame the group for what went wrong and do not look at their own part.
