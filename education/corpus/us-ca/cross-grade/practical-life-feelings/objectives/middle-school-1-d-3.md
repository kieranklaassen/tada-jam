## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student makes a start on noticing the biases and prejudices they hold themselves. They also start to see how these could shape the way they treat other people: whom they bring in and whom they leave out.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), looks at whom they sit with at lunch, pick first for a team or message after school, and asks what the people they leave out have in common. They recall a first impression of a classmate that turned out to be wrong and say what it was based on. They say out loud one assumption they hold about a group and what it has led them to do.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The wording says the student begins to do this, so a first, partial awareness is what is described. It concerns the student's own biases and their effect on including or excluding others; it names no particular groups. It speaks of how biases might affect behaviour, not of proof that they do.

### Common mistakes

Most young people believe that bias is something other people have and that they themselves treat everyone the same. Many think bias only counts when it is deliberate and unkind, so quiet habits such as always choosing the same partners go unnoticed. Some feel accused and become defensive, which ends the reflection.
