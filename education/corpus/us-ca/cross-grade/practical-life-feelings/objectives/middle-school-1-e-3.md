## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student starts to work out what they need from the groups they are part of, and judges whether those groups give it. The student also knows in what ways they do, and do not, give the other members of their learning community what those members need.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), lists the groups they are in, such as a class, a team, a family or a group of friends, and writes beside each one what they need from it: to be listened to, help with work, a place to be quiet. They mark which needs are met and which are not. They ask a classmate what would help them in group work and compare the answer with what they actually did last time.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. Identifying one's own needs is only begun at this band. The first part is about every community the student belongs to; the second part is about the learning community only. The statement asks for awareness of whether others' needs are met; it does not say the student must meet them.

### Common mistakes

Young people often state a want ("I need everyone to agree with me") as a need. Many can say what a group owes them but have not thought about what they give the group. Some assume classmates need the same things they do and never ask.
