## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

To get a problem solved, the child puts their own view across in ways that help, and hears with an open mind how other people see it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

Two children of about ten both want the same role in a play. One says "I see it this way: I have not had a speaking part yet", then asks "how do you see it?" and repeats the answer back before replying. Together they list options and pick one both accept. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. Putting one's view across and listening both serve one purpose in the statement: solving a problem. It names no particular strategy and no kind of problem. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children plan their reply while the other person is talking and do not take in what was said. Many state their view as an accusation ("you always ..."). Some treat listening to the other view as agreeing with it and so refuse to listen.
