## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child can say what being fair to other people is, and acts fairly. The child can tell how a rule may work out better for some people than for others. With their peers the child plays and works together with respect.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) deals out the crackers so each child has the same number, takes turns on the swing, and says why that is fair. They notice that the rule "whoever gets there first chooses" always favours the fastest runner, and say so. In a game they follow the agreed rules and speak politely to the others.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement asks the child to describe how a rule can favour some people; it does not ask them to change rules. It names no rules.

### Common mistakes

Children take fair to mean exactly the same for everyone, and object when a child who needs more gets more. They call a result unfair when they lose and fair when they win. They assume a rule is fair because it is a rule.
