## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student thinks about what it takes to make a learning community one that accepts and includes people. Working with others, the student finds what keeps any member of their own learning community from belonging. The student also questions, in a healthy way, the norms that groups and systems follow.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), works with a few classmates to go through a school day and list what could keep someone out: stairs with no ramp, club fees, jokes in one language, teams always picked by the same captains. They ask a new student what was hard in the first week. They ask a club leader or teacher, politely and with a reason, why a long-standing rule exists and whether it could change.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. Finding barriers is done together with others and concerns every person in the learning community. The questioning of norms is to be done in healthy ways; the statement does not define these. It asks for reflection, identification and questioning; removing the barriers is not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Young people often assume that because they feel they belong, everyone does. Barriers that are not physical, such as cost, language or jokes only some understand, are commonly missed. Questioning a norm sometimes becomes refusing to follow it or mocking it, which is not the healthy questioning described.
