## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Drawing on brain science, the student in the Middle School band sees that what they think, what they feel and what they do are linked. The student also sees that their culture and what they have lived through may shape the way they show emotion.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), takes one event, such as a low mark on a test, and writes down the thought that followed, the emotion that came with it and what they then did. They change the thought and say how the emotion and the action would change. They compare how people in their family show anger, joy or grief with how a friend's family does.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The statement says the link is understood on the basis of brain science but names no part of the brain and no model. It says culture and experience may influence how emotion is shown, which is a possibility, not a rule. It asks for recognition, not for a change in behaviour.

### Common mistakes

Young people often believe an event causes a feeling directly and miss the thought in between. Many treat their family's way of showing emotion as the only normal one and read a quieter or louder person as cold or rude. Some take "the brain did it" as a reason they could not have acted differently.
