## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child foresees, more and more correctly, what will follow from what they do and from what other people do. The child can pick out the effect that various activities and chances have on three things: the child personally, the child's relationships, and the settings where the child learns.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten predicts before acting: "if I post that photo, she will be embarrassed", or "if he keeps teasing, the game will end". The child considers joining a second sports team: more time with friends there, less time for homework and for a best friend who is not on the team. Afterwards the child compares what they predicted with what happened. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. Accuracy is described as increasing, so the statement expects improvement, not a correct prediction every time. It covers both the child's own actions and those of others. It names three things an activity can affect: the child, relationships and learning contexts. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children foresee the immediate result and miss later ones. Many predict what they hope will happen. Children often overlook how a choice affects a friendship or the class and think only of themselves.
