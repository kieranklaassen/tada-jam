## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student asks for useful feedback and changes what they do in response to it. The student also shows that they can give feedback in a way that suits the setting, whether to family, to educators, to peers or to other people in their learning community.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), hands a draft to a classmate and asks a specific question, such as "Is my second paragraph clear?", then rewrites it using the answer. They tell a teammate what worked in a presentation and one thing to change, in private and with an example. They word a comment to a teacher or a parent differently from one to a friend, for example by asking for a moment to talk first.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. Both directions are covered: receiving feedback and acting on it, and giving it. The feedback received is described as constructive. Four receivers are named: family, educators, peers and others in the learning community. The statement does not say what makes a way of giving feedback fit a context.

### Common mistakes

Young people often hear feedback on their work as criticism of themselves and defend the work instead of changing it. Many wait for feedback and do not ask for it. When giving feedback they tend to be either vague ("it's good") or blunt, and use the same tone with an adult as with a friend.
