## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

About a conflict they are part of, the child practises four things: owning their part in it, recognising the hurt done to others, seeing what they might change in future, and finding a way to make amends and go on in a helpful way.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

After a quarrel over a game, a child of about nine says "I pushed your tower over" without adding "but you ...". The child says how the other felt ("you were proud of it and I wrecked it"), says what they will do next time ("I will ask for a turn"), and offers to help rebuild it. The two then go back to playing. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The verb is "practice": the statement expects practising these steps, with no claim that the child does them unaided. It covers the child's own part in a conflict, not judging the other person's part. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children say "sorry" quickly to end the matter and skip what they did and how it hurt. Many attach an excuse or a counter-charge to the admission. Some believe that if they did not mean harm, no harm needs repairing.
