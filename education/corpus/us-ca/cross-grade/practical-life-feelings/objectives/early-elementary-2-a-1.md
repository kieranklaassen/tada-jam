## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child practises three linked things: becoming aware of a feeling, finding the place in the body where it shows, and letting those signals guide the choice of a helpful way to let the feeling out. The child also practises ways of keeping joy going and of becoming calmer, more patient, more focused and more energetic.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) feels their fists go tight, says "I am getting angry", and picks one of a few practised options: squeeze a ball, take five slow breaths, ask for a break, say it in words. Before a hard task they jump on the spot to feel more awake, or sit with a hand on the belly and breathe slowly to settle. When something is fun they say so and tell someone about it.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement says the child practises; it does not say the child manages feelings alone or every time. It names no particular strategies.

### Common mistakes

Children notice the feeling only after they have acted on it. They can list calming strategies when calm and cannot use one when upset. They use one strategy for every feeling, though what settles anger does not give energy when tired.
