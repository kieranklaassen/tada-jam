## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Depending on what kind of help is needed, the child can pick out suitable people they trust to turn to. The child also sees ways in which they themselves can help other people.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten matches needs to people: the school nurse for a headache, an aunt for a worry about home, a teacher for trouble with a classmate, a friend for company at lunch. The child notices a classmate struggling with a locker or sitting alone and offers help or a seat. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The people are to be both suitable and trusted, and the choice depends on the specific need. The statement is about identifying people and recognising ways to help; it does not name who the people are. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children take every problem to the same person, or to a friend when an adult is needed. Many wait to be asked and do not see small ways they could help. Some think asking for help shows weakness and so name nobody.
