## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child tells about problems, both personal ones and social ones, and, with support, can say what might solve them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) says "I keep forgetting my reading bag" or "the big children take the whole football pitch". An adult asks "what could help?", and the child offers one or two ideas: put the bag by the door, ask for a rota for the pitch.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. Naming solutions is done with support; the wording leaves open whether the support also covers describing the problems. The statement asks for possible solutions; it does not ask the child to choose one or carry it out, and it does not say what counts as a social problem.

### Common mistakes

Children describe what someone did to them and not the problem behind it. The only solution they name is for an adult to fix it or for the other person to be punished. They name one solution and stop.
