## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that leading is a set of skills a person can learn. The child tries out leading in ways that matter to the child personally.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten takes a turn leading the morning meeting, organises a recess game so everyone gets a turn, or teaches a younger pupil a card trick. Afterwards the child names one leading skill they used (giving clear steps, listening, sharing out jobs) and one they want to practise. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The verb is "explore": the child tries leadership out, and no office or title is required. The ways of leading are those important to the child. No leadership skills are listed. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children believe leaders are born, or that the leader is whoever is loudest, oldest or most popular. Many equate leading with giving orders. Quiet children often conclude that leadership is not for them.
