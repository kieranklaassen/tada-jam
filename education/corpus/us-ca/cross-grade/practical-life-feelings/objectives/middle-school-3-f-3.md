## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student looks at how people act on their biases, both the ones they hold openly and the ones they are not aware of, and at how certain policies can add to injustice. The student reflects on the harm this can do to other people and to society. The student also looks into the difference between what someone meant and the effect it had.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), takes a real or written case, such as a dress code that affects some students more than others, and says who is affected and how. They sort examples into bias a person states openly and bias shown without awareness, such as calling on the same students every time. They talk through a remark that was meant as a joke and hurt someone, and separate what was meant from what it did.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The statement says some policies can contribute to injustice; it names no policy. Both explicit and implicit bias are covered. The verbs are "examine", "reflect" and "explore": the statement asks for understanding and does not ask the student to act on what they find.

### Common mistakes

Young people often hold that an act is harmless if no harm was meant. Many think of bias only as open hostility by individuals and find it hard to see how a rule that is the same for all can affect groups differently. Some conclude that implicit bias means people are not responsible for what they do.
