## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child starts to spot when a situation, or something that is asked, is not appropriate. The child understands that they can answer "no", and respects a "no" that someone else gives them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) hears "give me your snack or I will not be your friend" or "do not tell anyone", senses that it is not right, says "no" in a firm voice and goes to an adult. When a friend says "no, I do not want to play that", they stop asking and choose something else.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The child begins to identify such requests; the statement does not claim they recognise all of them. It does not define what makes a request or a situation inappropriate, and it sets no level of detail.

### Common mistakes

Children believe they must do what any adult or older child asks. They think saying "no" is rude or will end a friendship. They accept a "no" from an adult and keep pressing another child who says it.
