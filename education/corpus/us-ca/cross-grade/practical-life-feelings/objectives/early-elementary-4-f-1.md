## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child can say sorry in a way that works: they see what took place and take responsibility for what they themselves did.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) who knocked over a classmate's tower goes to them, looks at them, and says "I ran past and knocked your tower down. I am sorry." They do not add "but you were in the way". They may offer to help build it again.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement gives two parts of an effective apology: seeing what took place and taking responsibility for one's own part. It does not mention putting things right afterwards or being forgiven.

### Common mistakes

Children say a quick "sorry" without saying for what, in order to end the matter. They add an excuse or blame ("sorry, but he started it"). They apologise for being caught or for the other child's crying, not for what they did.
