## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child knows what they want and what they need and can tell the two apart. The child can say what they are strong at; the statement gives talents, things they know and skills as examples.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) sorts picture cards (water, a toy, sleep, sweets, a coat on a cold day) into "need" and "want" and explains one hard card. They finish "I am good at ..." and "I know a lot about ..." and show it: they tie a shoe, count in another language, or name ten dinosaurs.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. Skills, knowledge and talents are given as examples of strengths, not as a full list. The statement does not say which wants and needs are meant.

### Common mistakes

A strong want feels like a need: "I need that toy." Children name as a strength what they like doing, whether or not they do it well, or name what they own. Some can name no strength because they compare themselves with an older child.
