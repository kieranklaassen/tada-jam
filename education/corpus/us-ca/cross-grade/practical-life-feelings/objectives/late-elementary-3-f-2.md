## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In a group of peers who differ from one another, the child can work together with the others. The child can pick out which basic rights are theirs, and which rights other people hold. The child is starting to understand that racism, stereotypes, prejudice and bias can influence what people do and decide, and can give examples of the damage or disruption these can cause to groups in society.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten works on a shared poster with classmates of different backgrounds and abilities, taking turns and dividing the tasks. The child states a right they have, such as being safe at school or being heard, and says that the classmate beside them has the same one. From a history lesson or a news story the child gives an example of a group that was kept out of a school, a job or a neighbourhood because of a stereotype. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The words "begin to" apply to understanding bias, prejudice, stereotypes and racism: the statement expects a first understanding. The child's own rights are described as basic, and no rights are listed. The child gives examples of harm to groups; the statement does not ask the child to remedy it. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children think prejudice is only open name-calling and miss its part in everyday choices such as who gets picked. Many treat a stereotype that sounds positive as harmless. Children often speak of rights as what they are owed and forget that the same rights belong to others.
