## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child knows ways of making a decision and uses them often. By thinking critically, the child works out which option turns out best for everyone it affects.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten deciding how the group will divide a project stops, lists the options, and for each one says who gains and who loses. The child writes reasons for and against in two columns, or asks "what would happen if everyone did this?" The child then picks the option that is good for the whole group, not only for themselves. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement says the strategies are practised regularly and names none of them. The measure of a good choice is the outcome for all involved, not for the child alone. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children decide on impulse and give reasons afterwards. Many consider only what happens to themselves, or only what happens right away. Some believe every decision has one right answer that an adult already knows.
