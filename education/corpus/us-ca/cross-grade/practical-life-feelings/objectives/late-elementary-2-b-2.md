## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

For handling stress, the child has easy practices that they know and actually use. Where it lies in the child's own power, the child takes part in activities that are good for mind and body.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about nine, tense before a spelling test, breathes out slowly five times or stretches their shoulders. The child chooses to run at recess, drink water, or draw quietly after a hard day. The child can say which of these they decide for themselves and which an adult decides. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The stress practices are described as simple, and none is named. The health activities are limited to ways within the child's own control; the statement does not say which ways those are. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children do not recognise stress until it shows as tears or anger, so they use a practice too late. Many think screen time is the only way to calm down. Some believe health is entirely up to adults and overlook the choices that are theirs.
