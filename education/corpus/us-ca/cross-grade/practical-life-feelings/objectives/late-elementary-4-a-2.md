## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child is getting better at telling when something asked of them is not appropriate and at refusing it. The child shows several ways of dealing with harmful pressure from other people.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten is asked by a friend to copy homework, to keep a secret about someone being hurt, or to leave a classmate out, and recognises that the request is not all right. The child says "no, I don't want to", suggests something else to do, walks away, or goes to an adult. The child practises these replies aloud in role-play with a partner. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement speaks of progress, not of always succeeding. It asks for more than one strategy and names none. It does not say who makes the requests or what makes one inappropriate. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children think a request from a friend or an older child cannot be wrong. Many know what they would say in role-play and still go along when the real group is watching. Some see only two choices, obeying or telling an adult, and overlook leaving or changing the subject.
