## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child is willing to find out about other people's cultures. The child is proud of the culture that is theirs, and passes information on to other people.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) listens to a classmate tell about a festival, tastes a food that is new to them, and asks a question about it. They bring something from home, such as a song, a word, a dish or a photo of a celebration, and tell the class what it is and when the family uses it.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement asks for willingness and pride; it sets no body of knowledge about any culture. It names no cultures. It does not say what the information the child shares is about, or how it is shared; that it is about the child's own culture is a likely reading, not a stated one.

### Common mistakes

Children call unfamiliar food, clothes or names "weird" and laugh. Some hide what their family does in order to be like the others. Many do not know that their own everyday habits are a culture, and think only other people have one.
