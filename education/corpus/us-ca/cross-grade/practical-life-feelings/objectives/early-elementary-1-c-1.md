## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child can say which people, which places and which ideas matter to them. The child can also tell about the traditions their own family keeps.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) brings a photo or drawing of someone or somewhere that matters to them and tells the class who or what it is. They tell what their family does on a birthday, a holiday or a Friday evening: the food, the song, who comes.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement lists no kinds of tradition and no number of things to name. It asks the child to describe their own family's traditions; learning about other people's cultures is 4.D.1.

### Common mistakes

Naming an important idea is the hard part: children of this age name people and places easily, and name things they own in place of ideas. Many do not know that what their family does is a tradition, because they assume every family does the same.
