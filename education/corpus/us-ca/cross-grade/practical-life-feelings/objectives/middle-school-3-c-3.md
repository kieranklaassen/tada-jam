## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Together with others, the student in the Middle School band helps to build every part of a learning environment that is caring, fair and centred on relationships; the agreements the group shares and its norms for taking part are named as parts of this. The student also asks whether the curriculum includes everyone.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), sits with the class at the start of term and proposes, debates and votes on agreements such as how to disagree and how turns to speak are shared. They say so when an agreement is not working and suggest a new wording. They look through the term's reading list and ask the teacher whose stories and viewpoints are missing.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The statement speaks of every aspect of the environment and names two: shared agreements and norms for engagement. The building is done jointly; the statement does not say with whom. Questioning the curriculum is asked for; changing it is not mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Young people often expect the teacher to make the rules and see their own part as following them. Agreements written at the start of a year tend to be forgotten because nobody returns to them. Some take questioning the curriculum to mean complaining that a topic is boring, not asking who is represented in it.
