## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student learns ways of communicating without violence and practises them. Drawing on their own strengths, the student communicates better, says what they think in a direct and tactful way, and asks openly for what they need and want. The student negotiates with respect.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), states an observation, a feeling, a need and a request in turn: "When the group met without me, I felt left out; I need to be told; please message me next time." They tell a teacher they disagree with a mark, give a reason and ask for a second look, in a calm voice. In a negotiation over chores or roles they make an offer, listen to the counter-offer and do not insult or threaten.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. Nonviolent communication strategies are to be learned and practised; none is described. The student's own strengths serve three listed purposes: better communication, opinions that are direct and tactful, and advocacy for what they want and need. The statement does not say with whom the student negotiates.

### Common mistakes

Young people often confuse being direct with being harsh, or being tactful with saying nothing. "I feel" sentences are commonly used to carry an accusation ("I feel that you are selfish"). Many state what they do not want and never make a clear request.
