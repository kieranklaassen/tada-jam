## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child picks out how they can help at school, at home and elsewhere, and why being a helper there matters. The child starts to explore the idea of citizenship: what a good citizen is, or what taking part in a democracy means.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) names a job they do or could do, such as feeding the cat, carrying the register to the office or picking up litter in the park, and says who is helped by it. In class they vote by a show of hands on which story to read, and accept the result when their choice loses.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The child begins to explore citizenship; no knowledge of government is set. The statement names home and school and leaves the other settings open. Helping in practice is 1.E.1; here the child picks out ways and the reason.

### Common mistakes

Children give "because I am told to" or "to get a reward" as the reason for helping. They take voting to mean that their choice wins, and call the result unfair when it does not. The word "citizen" means nothing to most children of this age unless it is tied to something they do.
