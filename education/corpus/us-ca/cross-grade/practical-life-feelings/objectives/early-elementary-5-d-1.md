## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child knows the reason there are safety rules. The child can tell which actions and choices at home and in the place where they learn put the body in danger, and can show how to behave safely.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) walks with scissors pointing down, climbs the slide by the ladder, and waits behind the line at the door. They look at a picture of a kitchen or classroom and point to what is unsafe: a chair used as a ladder, a pan handle sticking out, water on the floor. Asked why the rule "no running by the pool" exists, they say what could happen.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. Only the sentence about describing is limited: the unsafe behaviours and choices it covers are physical ones, at home and in the learning environment. The sentence about the reason for safety rules and the sentence about showing safe behaviour name no setting and no kind of safety. The statement lists no rules.

### Common mistakes

Children think a rule exists because an adult says so, and that it does not apply when no adult is there. They judge danger by whether they were hurt last time. They can state the rule and still break it when excited.
