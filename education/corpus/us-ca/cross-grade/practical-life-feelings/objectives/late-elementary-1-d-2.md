## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can think back over what they themselves have lived through of belonging and of being left out, both the times the child was shut out and the times the child shut someone else out.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about nine recalls a time they were invited into a game and a time they were not, and says how each felt. The child also tells of a time they told someone "you can't play" or stayed silent while a group left a classmate out. The child writes or draws both sides and says what they wish had happened. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement asks for reflection on the child's own experiences, in both roles; it does not itself ask the child to act to include others (acceptance and inclusion are in 3.E.2 of this band). It names no setting. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children remember being left out far more readily than leaving someone out. Many do not count quiet exclusion (not saving a seat, whispering, ignoring) as excluding. Some justify their own excluding with a reason ("she was being annoying") and so do not see it as the same act.
