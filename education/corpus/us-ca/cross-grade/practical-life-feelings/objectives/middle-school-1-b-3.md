## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student looks at what lies behind an emotion and thinks about where it came from. The student knows that an emotion and a feeling are not the same thing, and practises giving a precise name to the state they are in.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), traces their anger after an argument back to what started it, for example being left out of a plan or being tired. They pick a word from a wide vocabulary, such as "embarrassed", "jealous" or "disappointed", in place of "bad" or "mad". They say what happened in the body first (a hot face, a tight chest) and what they then understood it to be.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The statement says emotions and feelings differ but does not define either word or say what the difference is. It asks the student to work at precise naming, which describes effort, not mastery. It sets no list or number of feeling words.

### Common mistakes

Young people often name the event that started a feeling ("she ignored me") as if it were the feeling. Many use a few broad words, such as "fine", "annoyed" or "stressed", for very different states. Anger is frequently named when the first feeling was hurt, shame or fear.
