## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student picks out the social groups that are part of who they are, among them gender, ability, language, ethnicity and race, and looks into what each one means for them. The student takes first steps with the idea of intersectionality. The student also builds a steady, healthy confidence in themselves that rests on what they are good at.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), lists the groups they belong to, for example the languages spoken at home, their family's background and their gender, and says which of these matter most to them and why. They describe a situation in which two of these parts of themselves mattered together. They name three things they do well and give an occasion that shows each one.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The five kinds of identity are introduced as included, so the list is not closed. Intersectionality is only begun at this band: the statement asks for a first look at the idea, not command of it. Self-confidence is tied to the student's strengths; the statement names no other basis for it. The source prints the compound "self- confidence", near the end of the statement, with a space after the hyphen.

### Common mistakes

Young people of this age often treat identity as one label and leave out the parts that are not visible, such as language or ability. Some build confidence on comparison with classmates instead of on their own strengths, so it drops when someone else does better. Many describe how others see them when asked how they see themselves.
