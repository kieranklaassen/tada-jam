## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This is the third of the eight overarching health standards, which hold for all students in every grade. Every student is to show that they can get to health services, products and information, and can examine them. The title adds that the information is to be valid.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) names the adults they can ask about health, such as a parent, a teacher, a school nurse or a doctor, and shows how they would ask for help when hurt. An older child (about 9 to 12) reads the label on a food packet or a sunscreen bottle, compares two sources on the same question, and says which one they trust and why.

### Limits

This overarching standard is State Board-adopted, holds for every grade and sets no grade-specific content; each grade's own health standards give it. It names no particular sources, products or services.

### Common mistakes

Young children treat anything an adult, a screen or a package says as true. Older children judge a source by how it looks or how sure it sounds, and do not ask who made it or why.
