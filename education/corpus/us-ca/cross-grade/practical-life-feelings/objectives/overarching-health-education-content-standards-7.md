## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This is the seventh of the eight overarching health standards, which hold for all students in every grade. It is about doing: every student is to show that they can carry out habits and actions that make harm less likely and that support health.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) washes hands with soap before eating, puts on a helmet and buckles it, covers a cough with the elbow, and holds an adult's hand at the road. An older child (about 9 to 12) packs water and a hat for a hot day, warms up before sport, puts on a seat belt without being told, and leaves a place that feels unsafe.

### Limits

This overarching standard is State Board-adopted, holds for every grade and sets no grade-specific content; each grade's own health standards give it. It names no particular behaviours or risks. It asks for doing, where standard 1 asks for understanding.

### Common mistakes

Children can say the rule and still not do it when nobody reminds them. They do the action only in part: hands under the water for a second, helmet on with the strap open. They judge a risk by whether something went wrong last time.
