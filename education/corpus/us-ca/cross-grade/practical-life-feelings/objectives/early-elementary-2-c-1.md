## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child picks out ways of keeping hope and of recovering after something hard, ways already tried or ones still open to them, and practises those ways.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) tells about a time something went wrong, such as a tower that fell or a lost game, and what helped then: a hug, a rest, trying again, asking for help. They use one of those the next time and say afterwards whether it helped.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement names no particular strategies and no kind of challenge. A strategy may be one the child used before or one they could use.

### Common mistakes

Children think recovering means not being upset at all, and hide the upset. They give up after the first failure and say "I can never do it". They wait for an adult to fix the problem and do not see their own action as a strategy.
