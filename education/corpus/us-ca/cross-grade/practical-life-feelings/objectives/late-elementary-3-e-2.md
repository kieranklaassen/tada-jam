## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child accepts and includes people who are unlike the child, and shows it in what they do. In a group whose members differ from one another, the child values what each member brings.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about nine invites a classmate who speaks little English into the four-square game and shows the rules by playing. In a group project the child asks each member for an idea and uses the drawing of one, the facts of another and the careful handwriting of a third. The child thanks a group member by name for what they added. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement asks the child to demonstrate acceptance and inclusion, so it is about what the child does, not only what the child thinks. It does not say in what ways people differ. Reflecting on one's own experiences of being included or left out is a separate statement (1.D.2). This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children include a different peer only when an adult asks. Many tolerate a classmate in the group but give them no real task. Children often value only contributions like their own (the fastest answer, the loudest idea).
