## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student thinks about the effect that social identities have on how people see one another and deal with one another, people from diverse groups among them. Ability, language, gender, class, race and culture are given as examples of such identities.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), describes a time someone made a guess about them, or they about someone else, from an accent, clothes or a wheelchair, and what happened next in the conversation. They read two accounts of the same event by people of different backgrounds and point to where the accounts differ. They note who speaks to whom in the lunch hall and say what part language or background seems to have.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The six kinds of identity in brackets are given as examples. The statement asks for reflection on people in general, and does not ask the student to change anyone's behaviour. It does not say whether the effect is good or bad.

### Common mistakes

Young people often think identity affects how others act but not how they themselves see people. Many notice visible differences such as race or gender and overlook class and language. Some turn the reflection into a fixed statement about a group ("they always ...").
