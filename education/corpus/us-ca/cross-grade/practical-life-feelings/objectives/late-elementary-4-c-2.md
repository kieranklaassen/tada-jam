## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can pick out what marks a friend, and the kinds and degrees of friendship. The child works out why they are thankful for the people around them and, without waiting to be prompted, shows that thanks in a variety of ways.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about nine names what a friend does (keeps a promise, shares, tells the truth kindly) and places people they know in circles: a close friend, a team-mate, a child they say hello to. The child writes a thank-you note to a grandparent, tells the bus driver "thank you for waiting", and helps a friend who helped them. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement asks the child to identify what marks a friend and the kinds and degrees of friendship, and lists none. Gratitude is to be shown proactively and in more than one way. The people are those in the child's life, not only friends. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children expect every friend to be a best friend and take a looser friendship for rejection. Many think a friend is anyone who gives them things or always agrees. Thanks is often said only when an adult reminds the child, and only in words.
