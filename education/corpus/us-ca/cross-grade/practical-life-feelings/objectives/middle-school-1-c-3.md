## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student looks into what values are: where a person's values come from, what they show about that person's identity and life so far, and how people act on them. The student also makes a start on looking at the ways a person's own views and values can be questioned or tested.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), names a few things they hold to be important, such as honesty or loyalty to friends, and says who or what taught them each one. They describe a choice they made last week and which value it shows. They talk through a time when a friend, a book or a new situation made them doubt something they had always believed.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The statement names three things to look into about values: how they form, what they reflect and how they are used. Looking at challenges to one's views is only started at this band. The statement names no particular values and does not say which are right.

### Common mistakes

Young people often confuse a value with a preference or a rule ("I value pizza", "no phones at dinner"). Many assume everyone shares their family's values and are surprised when a classmate does not. Some hear any question about a value as a personal attack and stop listening.
