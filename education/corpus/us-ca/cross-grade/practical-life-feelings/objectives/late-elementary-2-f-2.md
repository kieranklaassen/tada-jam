## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child picks out goals that matter to them: some near and some further off, some the child's own and some shared with a group. The child shows ways of reaching those goals that suit them personally.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten writes down a goal for this week (finish the chapter book) and one for the term (swim a full length), and with the class agrees on a shared one (keep the reading corner tidy). The child breaks a goal into steps, ticks them off on a chart, and says which method helps them most, such as doing the hardest step first. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement asks for both shorter and longer goals and for both personal and group goals, and sets no time span for either. The goals are ones meaningful to the child; the statement does not say who sets them. No strategy is named. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children set a wish with no steps ("be good at maths") and cannot tell when it is reached. Many choose a goal to please an adult and lose interest. Long-term goals are often abandoned after the first setback because the child has no plan for one.
