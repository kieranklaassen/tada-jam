## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands why being curious matters. When the child meets people from varied groups, or is in a situation that is new to them, the child practises keeping an open mind.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about nine joins a new club and, before deciding whether they like it, asks two questions and tries the first activity. On meeting children from another school the child asks what games they play in place of assuming. The child catches a quick judgement ("this will be boring") and says "I don't know yet." The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The verb for open-mindedness is "practice", so the statement expects practising, not a settled habit. It names two occasions: meeting diverse groups of people, and new situations. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children decide from a first impression and then look only for signs that confirm it. Many take being open-minded to mean having to like or agree with everything. Unfamiliar situations often bring worry, which children show as refusal ("I don't want to go").
