## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The ways other cultures differ are something the child takes joy in and honours. The child picks out ways of showing respect to cultures other than their own, and shows them in practice.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten learns to greet a classmate in the classmate's home language and pronounces the classmate's name correctly. The child tries a food at a class meal and asks how it is made before giving an opinion, and listens to a classmate explain a holiday their family keeps. The child asks "may I ask about this?" before touching clothing or objects that matter to someone. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement speaks of other cultures; the child's own culture is not its subject. It asks the child both to identify and to demonstrate ways of showing respect, and names none. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children call an unfamiliar food, dress or custom "weird". Many reduce a culture to food and festivals, or expect one classmate to speak for a whole culture. Some copy an accent or costume as a joke and do not see why it is disrespectful.
