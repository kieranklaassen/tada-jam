## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This is the fourth of the eight overarching health standards, which hold for all students in every grade. Every student is to show skill in communicating with other people, used for the sake of better health.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) tells a trusted adult "my tummy hurts" or "I feel scared", asks for help, and says "no" or "stop" in a clear voice. An older child (about 9 to 12) refuses an unsafe offer from a friend without ending the friendship, listens to a classmate's side in a disagreement, and asks an adult for help on a friend's behalf.

### Limits

This overarching standard is State Board-adopted, holds for every grade and sets no grade-specific content; each grade's own health standards give it. It does not say which communication skills are meant, such as asking, refusing, listening or settling a conflict.

### Common mistakes

A young child knows something is wrong and waits for an adult to notice, without saying it. Children say "no" while smiling or looking away, so the other child does not take it as a refusal. Older children think a refusal has to be rude.
