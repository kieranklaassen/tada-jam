## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Together with peers and with adults, the student in the Middle School band searches for an answer that each of the two sides can accept and that deals with the concern lying behind each side's position.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), asks the other person in a dispute, for example over shared space in a group project or over screen time at home, what they are worried about, and states their own worry. They write down both concerns and suggest several options before choosing. They agree on one that both can accept, such as a later bedtime at weekends in exchange for homework done first, and repeat it to check.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The solution must be acceptable to both sides and must deal with the concerns beneath the stated positions. The student works on this with both peers and adults; the statement speaks of two sides and does not say who they are. The statement names no method of negotiation or mediation.

### Common mistakes

Young people often argue over stated positions ("I want the laptop") and never learn why the other person wants what they want. Many see a compromise in which each side gets half as the only fair outcome, even when it meets neither concern. With adults, some either agree at once or refuse any change.
