## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child can say how a "stop, think, act" strategy works and is starting to use one.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) about to grab a toy back holds still, takes a breath and asks themselves "what could I do?", then acts: asks for the toy, or fetches an adult. At a calm time they explain the three steps with their body: a hand held up for stop, a finger on the head for think, a step forward for act.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The child begins to practise; reliable use when upset is not claimed. The statement does not spell out what each of the three steps contains.

### Common mistakes

Children can recite the three words and still act first when upset. They stop and then do what they were going to do, without thinking of another option. They treat "stop" as a punishment and not as their own step.
