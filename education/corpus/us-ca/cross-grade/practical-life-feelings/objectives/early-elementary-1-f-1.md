## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child thinks back over what happened and sees that it brought on a particular feeling. The child recognises what feelings are for, and can say in simple terms how a feeling changes what they decide and what they do.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) finishes sentences such as "I felt angry when ..." and "I felt proud because ..." about their own day. They say what the feeling led them to do: "I was scared, so I stayed near the teacher." With an adult they say what a feeling is for: fear tells you to be careful.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The ways the child names are simple ones. The statement speaks of certain emotions and lists none, and it does not say what the purpose of any emotion is.

### Common mistakes

Children name the wrong cause: the last thing that happened, or another person ("he made me angry"), and miss being tired or hungry. They think of feelings as good or bad, and see no use in the unpleasant ones. They do not see the feeling as a step between the event and what they did.
