## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child can tell accepting someone apart from excluding them, and knows the good or the hurt that each does to other people.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) moves over on the bench so another child can sit down, or says "you can play too". After a story in which one child is left out, they say how that child felt and what the others could have done. They tell an adult about a time they were left out and a time they were let in.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement asks for understanding; it does not say the child must act on it. Acting to make others feel welcome comes under 3.E.1.

### Common mistakes

Children think leaving someone out is wrong only when it is done with unkind words, and do not count "we already have enough players". They see being left out as hurtful when it happens to them and not when they do it. Some think including a person means having to be best friends.
