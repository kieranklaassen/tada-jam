## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Faced with a personal problem or a social one, the child comes up with several solutions that could really work. The child can also tell when a problem cannot be solved without help.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten who keeps forgetting their gym kit lists three remedies: pack the bag the night before, put a note on the door, keep spare shoes at school. For a quarrel between two friends the child thinks of several things to try and says which could actually be done. When someone is being hurt, or the problem is too big, the child says "I need an adult for this." The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The solutions are to be several and feasible. Both personal and social problems are named. The statement asks the child to recognise when help is needed; choosing whom to ask is 3.D.2 in this band. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children stop at the first idea. Many offer solutions that depend on someone else changing ("he should just stop") or that cannot be done. Children either ask for help at once without trying, or carry on alone with a problem that needs an adult.
