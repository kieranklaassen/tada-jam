## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Faced with a problem, whether their own or a social one, the student in the Middle School band takes the view that things can be made better and works from that view. The student is able to lead a conversation in which peers solve a problem together.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), writes down a problem, such as low marks in a subject or a recurring quarrel in a friend group, as something to improve, and names a first change to try. They bring three or four classmates together, state the problem, ask each for their view and ideas, and keep the talk on solutions. They close by asking who will do what and check on it later.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. Both personal and social problems are covered. The statement says the student can facilitate, that is, guide the conversation, among peers; it does not mention adults. It names no problem-solving method.

### Common mistakes

Young people often spend a problem-solving talk on who is to blame. A student asked to facilitate tends to push their own solution or do all the talking, instead of asking the others for their ideas. Many treat a problem as fixed ("that's how it is") and do not look for a part they can change.
