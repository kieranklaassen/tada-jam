## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child puts what they need into words, and does so in ways that help. The child is starting to form a feeling of having a purpose of their own, and to feel that their life matters and has meaning.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about nine tells a group partner "I need a quieter spot to finish this" in place of snapping or going silent. The child asks a teacher for more time and says why. Asked what they care about doing, the child talks about something that feels important to them, such as looking after a younger sibling, drawing comics or helping at an animal shelter. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The words "begin to" apply to the sense of purpose: the statement expects a start, not a settled life goal. The wording can be read two ways as to whether "begin to" also governs the feeling that one's life matters and has meaning, or whether that feeling is expected outright; the Summary takes the first reading, and the statement allows both. It does not say what counts as a constructive way, and it names no kind of need. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children show a need through behaviour (sulking, grabbing, quitting) before they say it. Many state a need as blame ("you never let me ..."). Asked about purpose, children often answer with a job title they have heard and not with what matters to them now.
