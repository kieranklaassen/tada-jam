## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student looks into their part as a citizen and resident who cares, and into their social responsibility to take part in democracy so that everyone is better off. The student also thinks about what elected representatives and civic leaders are there to do.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), votes in a class or school election, speaks at a student council meeting or signs and delivers a petition about a school matter. They find out who represents their area on the city council and what that person decides. They take part in something for the common good, such as a clean-up or a public meeting, and say what it changed for other people.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The verbs are "explore" and "reflect"; the statement names no civic act the student must carry out. It names both citizen and resident. It gives the purpose of taking part as collective well-being and names no office, party or level of government.

### Common mistakes

Young people often think civic life begins at voting age and that they have no part before then. Many know the name of a national leader and nothing about local representatives who decide on their school or street. Some see participation only as protest or only as voting.
