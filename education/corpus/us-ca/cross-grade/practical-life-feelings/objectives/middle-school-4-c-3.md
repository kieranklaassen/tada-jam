## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student looks at how friendships and other relationships change over time and at why a varied circle of peers matters. The student can tell the signs of a healthy relationship from those of an unhealthy one. The student also thinks about what makes communication between people in a relationship work, and about what technology may do to communication and to relationships.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), draws a map of their friendships now and two years ago and talks about who has become closer or more distant and why. They sort behaviours into healthy and unhealthy: listening, respecting a "no" and sharing time, against checking a friend's phone, threatening to end the friendship, or mocking. They compare how a disagreement went by text message with how a similar one went face to face.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The statement covers friendships and other relationships without naming which. It does not list the characteristics of healthy or unhealthy relationships. Technology's effect is called potential and is not said to be good or bad.

### Common mistakes

Young people often take the end or fading of a friendship as a personal failure, when change at this age is common. Many read control or jealousy as a sign of caring. Tone is frequently misread in text messages, and some say by message what they would not say in person.
