## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child picks out what makes someone a good friend. The child shows other people that they are thankful.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) says what a good friend does: shares, waits, listens, says sorry, helps when others are unkind. They say "thank you for helping me" to a classmate, draw a thank-you card for the cook or the bus driver, and hand it over themselves.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement does not list the qualities of a good friend. It asks the child to show gratitude and does not say to whom or how.

### Common mistakes

Children define a friend as someone who does what they want or gives them things. They say "you are not my friend" after one disagreement. They say "thank you" only when prompted and do not say what it is for.
