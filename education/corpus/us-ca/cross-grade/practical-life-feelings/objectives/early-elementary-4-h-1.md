## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child knows that fair treatment is every person's right. The child can tell reporting apart from tattling, and knows the times when an adult has to be told about something.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) sees a classmate being pushed off the climbing frame again and again and goes to the teacher: that is reporting, because someone is being hurt. They see a classmate using the wrong colour pencil and do not go to the teacher about it. They say "everyone gets a turn" when one child is skipped.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement does not say where the line between tattling and reporting lies, and lists no cases that must be reported.

### Common mistakes

Children report everything, including small rule-breaking that hurts nobody, to get another child into trouble. After being told not to tattle, they stop telling adults about things that are dangerous or hurtful. They think fair treatment is owed to friends and not to children they dislike.
