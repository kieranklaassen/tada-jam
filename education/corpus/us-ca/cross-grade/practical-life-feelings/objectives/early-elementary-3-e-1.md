## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child can say which communities or groups they belong to and what qualities those have. The child shows that they are proud to belong, and does things that help other people feel welcome.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) says "I am in the Robin class, in my family, in the Saturday football team" and one thing about each: "our class sings a lot". They wear the team shirt or show the class flag with pleasure. When a new child arrives they say hello, show where things are, and ask them to join the game.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement names no kinds of group and no number of them. The wording leaves open whether the qualities are the groups' or the child's own; the summary reads them as the groups'.

### Common mistakes

Children do not think of family or class as a group they belong to, and name only clubs. Pride in their own group turns into "our group is better" and into keeping others out. They feel friendly toward a newcomer and take no action.
