## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student comes to feel that they have power of their own. They can pick out the resources they have in themselves, in the people around them and in their culture, and can use these in a healthy way.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), writes down what they can draw on: a skill such as drawing or speaking two languages, people such as a grandparent or a coach, and traditions, stories or foods from their culture. They pick one of these to deal with a real problem, for example asking an older cousin for advice or translating for a new classmate. They name one thing in their week that they changed through their own action.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. Three kinds of asset are named: personal, social and cultural; the statement gives no examples of any of them. It requires that the use be healthy and does not define that word. It sets no measure for a sense of personal power.

### Common mistakes

Young people often count only school marks or sports results as strengths and leave out family, language and culture. Some confuse personal power with power over others, such as telling classmates what to do. Many can list their assets but do not think of using them when a problem comes up.
