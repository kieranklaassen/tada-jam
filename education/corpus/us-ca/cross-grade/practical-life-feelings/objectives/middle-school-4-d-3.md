## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student looks into the practices and world views of different cultures, and starts to build the skills needed for communicating and for dealing with others across cultures. The student sees that cultural humility rests on reflecting on oneself and judging oneself critically, and that this is a commitment for life.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), interviews a classmate or neighbour about a celebration, a food custom or a belief, asks open questions and listens to the whole answer. They work on a shared task with someone whose first language or customs differ from theirs and check that they have understood before going on. After an error, such as mispronouncing a name or assuming a custom, they ask, correct themselves and say what they will do next time.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. Skills for communicating across cultures are only begun at this band. The third part asks the student to recognise why continued self-reflection matters; it does not say the student has reached cultural humility. No cultures or practices are named.

### Common mistakes

Young people often treat one person as speaking for a whole culture. Many think that learning facts about a culture, such as its festivals and foods, is the same as understanding it. Some believe that cultural understanding can be finished, which is the opposite of the lifelong reflection the statement names.
