## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

What the common good is, and how it adds to the well-being of people as a whole, is something the child comes to understand over time. The child looks into what being a citizen in a democracy gives a person and what it asks of them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten names things that are there for everyone (the library, the playground, clean water) and says what happens to all if nobody looks after them. The class votes on a class rule or on how to spend a small budget, and the child casts a vote, accepts the result, and does the job assigned to them. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The verbs are "develop" and "explore": the statement expects a growing understanding, not finished knowledge of government. It names no institutions, no form of voting and no particular rights or duties. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children equate the common good with what the majority wants, or with what they personally want. Many know citizenship brings rights and overlook the duties. Some think a vote is unfair whenever their side loses.
