## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student tells apart three kinds of safety (of the body, of feelings and of the mind) and respects each, for other people as well as for themselves; the student knows that who a person is affects how safe they are. The student follows the safety standards of the place where they learn and, more and more, judges risks on their own.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), sorts situations by the kind of safety involved: a wet floor in the lab, a classmate mocked for crying, a student laughed at for a wrong answer. They wear goggles in science, follow the workshop rules and stop a friend from running with scissors or sharing someone's secret. Before a bike ride or a dare they think through what could go wrong and decide for themselves.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. Three kinds of safety are named: physical, emotional and intellectual, each for oneself and for others. Following the learning environment's safety standards is expected outright; judging risk independently is described as increasing. The statement does not explain how identity affects safety.

### Common mistakes

Young people often count only physical danger as a safety matter and treat mocking an idea or a feeling as harmless. Many assume a place that feels safe to them feels safe to everyone. At this age risk is commonly judged lower when friends are watching.
