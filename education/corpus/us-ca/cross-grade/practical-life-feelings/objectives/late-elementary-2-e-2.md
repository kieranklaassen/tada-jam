## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child picks up on signals from other people and holds back urges, so that others' limits stay respected. Those limits may concern the body or feelings, and may have been stated outright or only hinted at.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about nine sees a classmate lean away or go quiet and stops tickling, teasing or asking about a subject that upsets them. The child wants to pick up a friend's new pencil case and asks first. When a friend says "stop" or only looks uncomfortable, the child steps back. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement concerns other people's boundaries, not the child's own (recognising boundaries in general is 3.C.2 in this band). It covers both physical and emotional boundaries, and both stated and unstated ones. It names no particular cues. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children treat "she didn't say no" as permission and miss unspoken signals such as turning away or a forced laugh. Many keep a joke going after the other person has stopped laughing. Physical boundaries are learned sooner than emotional ones, such as not pressing someone to talk about something painful.
