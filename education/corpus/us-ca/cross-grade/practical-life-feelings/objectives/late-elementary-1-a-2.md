## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Parts of who a child is, both personally and as a member of groups, are not fixed: the child understands that some of them shift as time passes, and that the child themselves, other people, and what happens to the child all help form them. The child shows that they are proud and sure of who they are, and that pride does not depend on feeling better than anyone else.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten lays out photos or drawings of themselves at five and now, and says what has stayed the same (a name, a family language) and what has changed (a favourite activity, a group of friends). The child names who or what brought a change about: a move, a coach, a choice of their own. The child tells a classmate something they are proud of about themselves without comparing it to the classmate. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. It says that some parts of identity change, not all, and names three things that form them: the child, other people, and experiences. It does not list which parts of identity are meant. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age often treat traits as fixed ("I am just bad at drawing") and miss that practice or new experiences change them. Many express pride by ranking themselves above a peer ("I am better than you at this"). Some describe who they are only through likes and possessions and leave out family, language, culture and the groups they belong to.
