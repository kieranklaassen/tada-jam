## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student explains what stress does to the choices they make and the things they do, and tries out more than one way of handling it. The student picks activities that are good for their body and mind, does them, and treats them as their own responsibility.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), recalls a stressful week and says what changed: snapping at a sibling, skipping homework, staying up late. They try several ways of coping, such as slow breathing, a walk, talking to someone or breaking a task into steps, and say which helped. They choose something for their own health, such as a sport, regular sleep or time away from a screen, and continue it without being reminded.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The statement asks for more than one stress strategy but names none and sets no number. Both mental and physical health are named. The activities are the student's own choice; the statement does not list any.

### Common mistakes

Young people often do not connect their own irritability, forgetfulness or avoidance to stress. Many rely on one strategy, often distraction with a phone, and count that as coping. Some do a healthy activity only when an adult arranges it, which is not the ownership the statement describes.
