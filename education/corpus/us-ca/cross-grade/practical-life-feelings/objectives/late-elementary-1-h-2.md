## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child perceives more accurately and grows surer of their own thinking and of opinions that rest on good reasons. At the same time the child stays willing to hear ideas that are both new and different, and to try them out.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten gives an opinion in a class discussion and supports it with a reason or something they observed. When a classmate proposes another way to build the bridge model, the child listens, asks a question, and tries that way before judging it. The child checks a first impression ("she is ignoring me") against what else could explain it. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement speaks of opinions that are well-founded, so confidence is tied to having reasons. It describes growth (more accuracy, more confidence) and sets no end point. Openness covers both listening to ideas that are new and different and trying them. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children treat changing their mind as losing. Some hold an opinion firmly with no reason behind it; others give up their view as soon as a friend disagrees. Children of this age often misread a neutral look or a short answer as hostile.
