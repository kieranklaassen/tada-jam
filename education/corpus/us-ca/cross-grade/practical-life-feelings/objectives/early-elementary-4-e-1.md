## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child picks out a problem and talks it through, and, together with other people, comes up with ways to solve it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) and a classmate both want the red truck. Each says what the problem is ("we both want it"), they listen to each other, and they think of two or three things to try: take turns with a sand timer, play with it together, find a second truck. They pick one and try it.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. Coming up with solutions is done with others; the wording leaves open whether picking the problem out and talking it through are done with others too. The statement names no kind of problem and does not say the solutions must be carried out.

### Common mistakes

Children state the problem as the other child's fault ("she took it") and not as something both have. They offer one solution, the one in which they get what they want. They go to solutions at once, before agreeing what the problem is.
