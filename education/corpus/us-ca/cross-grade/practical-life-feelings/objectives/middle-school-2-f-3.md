## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student sets small step-by-step goals, both their own and ones shared with a group, that lead toward something bigger further off, and works at them. For a shared goal the student works out which role is theirs and commits to it. The student starts to look at how their own goals and a group's goals can be kept in balance.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), breaks a long project, such as a science fair entry or learning a piece of music, into steps for this week and next, and ticks them off. In a group task they agree who researches, who builds and who presents, and each does their part by the date set. They talk about what to do when football practice and the group's meeting fall at the same hour.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The statement covers both personal and shared goals and sets no time span for the longer-term achievement. Balancing one's own goals with a group's is only begun at this band. The statement names no planning method. The source prints a comma after "longer-term" in the first sentence.

### Common mistakes

Young people often set a distant goal ("get fit", "get good grades") with no next step. In group work some accept a role and then wait for others to do the work, or one person takes every role. Many see a clash between their own goal and the group's as something one side must lose.
