## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child can describe hardship of several kinds. From other people's stories of getting through hard times, recovering, and keeping hope when things were difficult, the child can say what there is to learn; people of today and figures from history are given as examples of whose stories these may be.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten reads or hears about a person who faced something hard, such as an athlete who was injured, a child who moved to a new country, or a historical figure who was treated unjustly. The child says what kind of difficulty it was (illness, loss, unfair treatment, a failure) and what the person did to keep going. The child then states one thing from that story that they could use in their own life. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement asks the child to describe hardship and to draw lessons from others' stories; it does not ask the child to recount hardship of their own. Current and historical characters are given as an example of sources, not as a required list. No kinds of adversity are named. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children conclude that resilient people did not feel afraid or sad. Many retell the events of the story and leave out what can be learned from it. Some count only dramatic events as adversity and miss everyday ones such as a long illness in the family or struggling to read.
