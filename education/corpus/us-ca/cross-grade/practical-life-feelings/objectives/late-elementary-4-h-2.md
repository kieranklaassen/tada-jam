## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child recognises that dignity and justice are rights belonging to every person. The child shows ways of standing up for someone who is being wronged; the examples given are getting a person who is the target of bullying out of the situation, and drawing attention elsewhere.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten sees a classmate being mocked at the lunch table, walks over and says "come and sit with us" or "the teacher is looking for you", and leaves with them. Another time the child changes the subject loudly or starts a game to interrupt what is happening, then tells an adult. The child practises these actions in role-play. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. Distraction and getting the target away are given as examples of upstander strategies, not as the full list. Bullying is named only inside those examples; the statement's term for the strategies themselves is a general one. The right to dignity and justice is stated for all people. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children think the only choices are to fight the bully or to stay out of it. Many believe that watching silently or laughing along is neutral. Some fear that telling an adult is "snitching" and so do nothing.
