## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This is the fifth of the eight overarching health standards, which hold for all students in every grade. It is about choosing: every student is to show that they can apply the skills of making a decision so that health improves.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) chooses between two real options, such as water or a sweet drink, and says why. An older child (about 9 to 12) pauses before a choice, names two or three options, says what could follow from each, picks one, and afterwards says how it went.

### Limits

This overarching standard is State Board-adopted, holds for every grade and sets no grade-specific content; each grade's own health standards give it. It names no steps of a decision and no situations.

### Common mistakes

Young children choose what they want right now and give the reason afterwards. Children see only two options (do it or do not) and miss a third. They think about what happens next and not about what happens later.
