## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Middle School band the student is able to set up a routine and keep to it. When something gets in the way or distracts them, they adjust without help, and they use the ways of persevering that suit them personally.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young person in the Middle School band, about 11 to 14 years old (the document itself names no ages), writes their own after-school order of homework, practice and free time, and follows it for a week. When a sibling is noisy or a phone buzzes, they move to another room or put the phone away without being told. On a hard problem they use a method they know helps them, such as a short break, starting with an easier part or setting a ten-minute timer.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. The statement says the student can do these things, and that adapting is done independently. The strategies for persevering are the ones that work for that student; none is named. It does not say how long a routine lasts or what it covers.

### Common mistakes

Young people often make a routine that is too full to keep and drop it after the first missed day. Many underestimate how much a phone nearby distracts them. Some copy a friend's study method that does not suit them and conclude that they cannot persevere.
