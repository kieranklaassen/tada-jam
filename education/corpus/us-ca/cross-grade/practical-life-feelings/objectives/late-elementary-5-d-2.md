## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands safety of two kinds, of the body and of feelings, and makes safe choices where they learn. The child gives active thought to how safe other people are, in both senses. The child looks into how identity and safety can be connected.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten carries scissors point down, wipes up a spill, and does not pass on a classmate's embarrassing mistake. Before a rough game the child checks that everyone wants to play that way, and before a joke asks themselves whether it would hurt someone present. In a class talk the child considers why a pupil who uses a wheelchair, or one who is new to the language, might feel less safe in the playground. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. Safe choices are tied to the learning environment. The link between safety and identity is something the child explores; the statement does not say what the link is or which identities are meant. Both physical and emotional safety apply to the child and to others. The document prints the Early Elementary code, 5.D.1, in this cell, so the code used here is the one the cell's place in the table gives. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children think of safety only as avoiding injury and leave out teasing, exclusion and humiliation. Many assume that what feels safe to them feels safe to everyone. "It was only a joke" is taken as proof that no harm was done.
