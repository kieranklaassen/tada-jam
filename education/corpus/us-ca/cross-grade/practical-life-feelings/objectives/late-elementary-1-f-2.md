## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sees feelings as something that tells them things, which they may decide to put to use. The child understands that no feeling counts as a bad one, and that a person can shift how they feel on purpose.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child of about ten notices nervousness before a presentation, reads it as a sign that the presentation matters to them, and uses the energy to practise once more. The child says "I am jealous" without calling themselves bad for it, and asks what the jealousy shows they want. To change a low mood the child chooses to go outside, listen to a song or talk to a friend, and checks afterwards whether the feeling has changed. The document names no ages for this band; the child described here is in the later primary years, about 8 to 11.

### Limits

This statement belongs to the Late Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The statement is about how the child understands emotions; the regulation strategies themselves are in 2.A.2 and 2.B.2 of this band. It says a feeling state can be changed on purpose and does not say how, how fast, or that it always should be. This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard.

### Common mistakes

Children sort feelings into good and bad and hide anger, envy or fear because they think having them is wrong. Many mix up a feeling with what they did while feeling it ("anger is bad" when hitting was the problem). Some believe a mood only changes when its outside cause goes away.
