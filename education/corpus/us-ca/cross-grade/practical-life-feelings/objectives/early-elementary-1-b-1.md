## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child notices the simple feelings they have, puts a name to them and makes sense of them. The child knows that two or more feelings can be present together, and notices what feelings can do to their body.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) points to a face card or says the word for how they feel now: happy, sad, angry, scared. They say "I am excited and a bit nervous" on the morning of a school trip. They put a hand on the place in the body where they feel it: a tight tummy, hot cheeks, a fast heartbeat.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The emotions are basic ones, and they are the child's own; other people's feelings come under 3.B.1. The statement gives no list of emotions.

### Common mistakes

Children use one word, often "mad" or "sad", for several different feelings. They believe a person has only one feeling at a time, so mixed feelings confuse them. They report a body sign ("my tummy hurts") and do not link it to the feeling.
