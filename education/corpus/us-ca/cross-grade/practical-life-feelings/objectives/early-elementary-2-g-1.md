## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In the Early Elementary band, a child starts to practise checking on what they themselves are thinking and doing, and asking for help, so as to stay focused and motivated. The child is aware of their own body while listening actively, and can tell when they are paying attention and focused.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child in the early primary years (about 5 to 8; the document itself names no ages) stops in the middle of a task, asks themselves "am I still doing my work?", and goes back to it or raises a hand for help. While someone speaks they check their own body: eyes toward the speaker, hands still, feet on the floor. Asked "were you listening just now?", they answer honestly yes or no.

### Limits

This is voluntary guidance, not an adopted standard. It belongs to the Early Elementary band, which the document ties to no grades. The child begins to practise self-monitoring; the statement does not claim they do it unaided or all the time. It does not say what active listening looks like in the body or how long attention lasts.

### Common mistakes

Children believe they are paying attention because they are sitting still, while thinking about something else. They notice that their attention has gone only when an adult points it out. They do not ask for help because they see asking as failing.
