## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child gets ready for, and takes a useful part in, many kinds of conversation and joint work with a variety of partners. In them the child adds to what others have said and states their own ideas clearly and convincingly.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) sits in a circle, waits for a turn, listens to a classmate, and adds a sentence about the same subject. An older child (about 9 to 12) reads the chapter before a group discussion, brings two notes, links a comment to the previous speaker's ("I agree with Maya, and also ..."), and gives a reason that changes a partner's mind.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. It covers preparing as well as taking part, and both conversations and collaborations. Partners are to be diverse. No group size or topic is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children talk over others or speak about something unrelated to what was just said. Older children wait for their turn to speak and do not respond to what others said. They come to a discussion without having read or prepared.
