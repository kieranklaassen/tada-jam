## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes arguments. In them, claims made while analysing texts or topics of substance are backed by sound reasoning and by enough relevant evidence.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) draws, tells and writes a sentence that names a book and says "I like this book because ...". An older child (about 9 to 12) writes a page arguing that the town should keep its library open late: a claim, three reasons, a fact or example for each, and a closing sentence that follows from them.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. The topics or texts are to be substantive. The reasoning is to be valid and the evidence both relevant and sufficient. No length and no number of reasons is set. The adopted document notes, in a footnote on the heading over anchors 1 to 3, that the three kinds of writing these anchors name are wide categories with many sub-kinds inside each, and it sends the reader to its Appendix A, where the main kinds are defined.

### Common mistakes

Young children state a liking with no reason. Older children repeat the claim in different words in place of reasons. They give evidence that is true but does not relate to the claim.
