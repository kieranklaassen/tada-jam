## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Where a piece of writing needs it, the child makes it better and stronger. The ways named are planning, editing, revising, rewriting, or taking a different approach.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) answers a classmate's question about a story ("what colour was the dog?") by adding a detail to the writing. An older child (about 9 to 12) plans a report with a short outline, writes a draft, reads it aloud, moves one paragraph, cuts a repeated sentence, corrects the spelling, and rewrites the opening that did not work.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Five ways are named, joined by "or" and used as needed. The anchor does not mention support from adults or peers.

### Common mistakes

Young children regard a piece as finished once it is written down. Older children correct spelling and call it revising. They are unwilling to delete or start again when a draft does not work.
