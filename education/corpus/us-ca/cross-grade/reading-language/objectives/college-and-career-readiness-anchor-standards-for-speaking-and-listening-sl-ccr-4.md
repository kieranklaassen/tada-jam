## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When the child presents findings, information and the evidence behind them, listeners are able to follow the reasoning from step to step. The way the talk is organised, developed and styled fits the audience, the purpose and the task.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) describes a familiar place or a thing brought from home, in sentences the class can follow, with details in a sensible order. An older child (about 9 to 12) gives a three-minute talk on a science project: the question first, then the method, the result and the evidence for the conclusion, in words chosen for classmates who did not do the project.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. What is presented is information, findings and supporting evidence. The test is whether listeners can follow the reasoning. The talk is to suit task, purpose and audience. No length or setting is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children tell details in the order they remember them and leave out what the listener needs to know. Older children read a script word for word without looking up. They give facts with no order, so listeners cannot tell how one point leads to the next.
