## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child changes how they speak to suit many different settings and tasks of communication. Where formal English is called for or fitting, the child shows control of it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) speaks loudly enough for the group and answers a visitor's question in a complete sentence. An older child (about 9 to 12) explains a game to a friend in casual speech, then presents the same rules to the class in full, formal sentences without slang, and says why the two differ.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Formal English is required only when indicated or appropriate, not always. No setting or task is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children answer in single words or speak too quietly to be heard. Older children use the same casual speech in a presentation as with friends. Some think formal speech means long words, and become less clear.
