## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes narratives about events or experiences, which may be imagined or real. The telling uses effective technique, details chosen with care, and a sequence of events that is well built.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) draws, tells and writes about a trip to the park: what happened first, next and last. An older child (about 9 to 12) writes a story about getting lost in a market, opens with the moment of noticing, uses dialogue and a detail of sound or smell, and ends with how the problem was resolved.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. The events may be real or imagined. Three means are named: technique, details, sequence of events. No technique is named and no length is set. The adopted document notes, in a footnote on the heading over anchors 1 to 3, that the three kinds of writing these anchors name are wide categories with many sub-kinds inside each, and it sends the reader to its Appendix A, where the main kinds are defined.

### Common mistakes

Young children list events joined by "and then" with no ending. Older children tell what happened without showing how anyone felt or spoke. They start a story with care and end it abruptly ("then I woke up").
