## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child learns, and uses correctly, a range of words and phrases, both general academic ones and ones that belong to a subject. The range is to be wide enough for listening, speaking, writing and reading at the level that readiness for college and a career requires. When an unfamiliar term matters for understanding or for expressing something, the child finds out about it on their own.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) learns "compare" and "habitat" from lessons and read-alouds and uses them when talking about a class project. An older child (about 9 to 12) keeps a notebook of terms from science and history, uses "evidence" and "erosion" correctly in a written answer, and, on meeting an unknown term that a passage depends on, looks it up without waiting for the teacher.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Two kinds of vocabulary are named: general academic and domain-specific. The level named is college and career readiness, not the level of any one grade. No word list or number of words is given.

### Common mistakes

Young children repeat a new word without knowing what it means. Older children recognise an academic word when reading but avoid it in their own writing, or use it in the wrong sense. They skip an unknown term instead of finding out what it means.
