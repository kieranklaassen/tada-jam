## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In speech and in writing alike, the child follows the accepted rules of grammar and usage of standard English, and shows control of them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) tells what happened at the weekend in whole sentences and says "we went", not "we goed"; writing a sentence about pets, the child makes the verb match ("the dogs run"). An older child (about 9 to 12) rereads a paragraph aloud, hears that it moves from past to present tense, and changes the verbs so that they agree.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. It covers grammar and usage, in both writing and speaking. It names no particular rule.

### Common mistakes

Young children apply a regular pattern to irregular words ("mouses", "runned"). Older children write sentence fragments or run several sentences together, and change tense partway through a piece. A child of any age may use a home or regional form without knowing that standard English differs.
