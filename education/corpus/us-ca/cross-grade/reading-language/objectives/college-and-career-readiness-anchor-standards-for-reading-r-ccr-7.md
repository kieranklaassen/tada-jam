## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child brings together, and judges, content that comes in different media and formats. These include content given in pictures or other visual form and in numbers, alongside content given in words.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) looks at the picture and the sentence on a page and says what the picture shows that the words do not. An older child (about 9 to 12) reads an article on rainfall, studies its bar chart and a short video, combines the three in one answer, and says which gives the clearest evidence for the article's claim.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Visual and quantitative presentation are named alongside words. Both combining and judging are asked. No medium is named. The adopted document prints a footnote on this anchor that refers the reader to the cluster of Writing anchors 7 to 9 and the cluster of Speaking and Listening anchors 1 to 3, where further standards deal with collecting, judging and using information from sources in print and in digital form.

### Common mistakes

Young children look at the pictures and ignore the words, or the reverse. Older children skip charts and tables, or read them without the labels and scale. They repeat what each source says and do not judge it or join the sources.
