## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child examines the people, ideas and events in a text: in what way, and for what reasons, they change and affect one another from its start to its end.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) says who is in a story, where it happens, and what the bear did after the rabbit took the honey. An older child (about 9 to 12) explains that a character becomes braver after the storm scene and says how that change causes the ending; in a history text the child explains how one invention led to a change in a town.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Three things are followed through a text: individuals, events and ideas. It asks both how and why. No kind of text is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children name characters and events but do not link one event to the next. Older children describe a character at one point and miss that the character changes. They say what happened and leave out why.
