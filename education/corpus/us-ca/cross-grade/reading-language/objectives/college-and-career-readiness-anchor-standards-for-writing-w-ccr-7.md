## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child carries out research projects, some short and some longer-running, each built on a focused question. The work shows that the child understands the subject being looked into.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) joins a class project on snails, looks at several books with the class, and tells one thing learned. An older child (about 9 to 12) spends two weeks on the question "why did our town grow where it did?", reads three sources, takes notes under sub-questions, and writes an answer that explains the reasons.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Both short and more sustained projects are named; no length is set. The questions are to be focused. No number of sources is given.

### Common mistakes

Young children collect facts they like and do not connect them to the question. Older children choose a question too broad to answer. They copy what sources say and cannot explain the subject in their own words.
