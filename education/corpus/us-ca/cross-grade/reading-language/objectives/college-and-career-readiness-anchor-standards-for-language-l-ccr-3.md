## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child puts what they know about language to three uses. One is understanding that language works differently from one setting to another. A second is choosing well for style or meaning. The third is understanding more of what they hear or read.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) asks the head teacher for something in a different way from how they ask a friend at play, and notices when a storybook character speaks very politely. An older child (about 9 to 12) rewrites "the dog went across the yard" with a more exact verb, reads both versions aloud, and says which suits a tense scene; listening to a speech, the child notices that short sentences mark the important points.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. It names three uses of knowledge about language and no particular knowledge to apply. Listening and reading are both named for the third use.

### Common mistakes

Young children speak the same way everywhere and do not yet notice that settings differ. Older children think a longer or rarer word is always the better choice. They read for what happens and do not notice how the wording produces the effect.
