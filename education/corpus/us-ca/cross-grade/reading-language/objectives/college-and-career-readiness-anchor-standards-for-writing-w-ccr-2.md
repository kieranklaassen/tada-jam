## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes texts that inform or explain. Their purpose is to look into complex information and ideas and pass them on accurately and clearly, and the means is choosing, organising and analysing the content well.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) draws, tells and writes two sentences that name a topic, such as frogs, and give facts about it. An older child (about 9 to 12) writes a report on how a volcano erupts: selects the facts that matter, groups them under headings, defines the terms, and explains each stage in order.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. The purpose is to examine and convey ideas and information. Three means are named: selecting, organising and analysing content. No length is set. The adopted document notes, in a footnote on the heading over anchors 1 to 3, that the three kinds of writing these anchors name are wide categories with many sub-kinds inside each, and it sends the reader to its Appendix A, where the main kinds are defined.

### Common mistakes

Young children write one fact and then write about something unrelated. Older children copy facts from a source in the order they found them, with no grouping. They add their opinion where an explanation is asked for.
