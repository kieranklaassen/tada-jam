## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Careful reading has two aims here: finding out what a text states outright, and drawing reasonable inferences from that. When the child speaks or writes about conclusions reached from a text, the child points to particular evidence in it as support.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) listens to a story, answers "who found the kitten?" by turning to the page that shows it, and says the girl is sad "because she is crying in the picture". An older child (about 9 to 12) reads that a character packs a bag at night and leaves a note, infers that the character is running away, and reads out the two sentences that support the inference.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. It covers both what is stated and what is inferred, and asks for evidence in writing and in speaking. It names no kind of text.

### Common mistakes

Young children answer from their own experience instead of from the story. Older children treat an inference as a guess and give no evidence, or state an inference that the text contradicts. They quote a line that is near the answer but does not support it.
