## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child examines the way several texts, at least two, deal with topics or themes that are alike. The aim is either to compare how the authors go about it or to build knowledge.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) hears two stories about a lost toy and says one thing that happens in both and one thing that differs. An older child (about 9 to 12) reads a novel chapter and a factual article about the same flood, notes what each adds to their knowledge, and describes how the storyteller and the reporter treat the event differently.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. At least two texts are needed. Two aims are named, joined by "or": building knowledge, comparing the authors' approaches. No kind of text is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children describe one text and forget the other. Older children list differences in plot or facts and say nothing about how the authors approach the subject. They treat two texts on one topic as saying the same thing and miss where they disagree.
