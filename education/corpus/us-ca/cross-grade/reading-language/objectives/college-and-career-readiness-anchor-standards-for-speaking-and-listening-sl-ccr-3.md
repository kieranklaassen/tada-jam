## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Listening to a speaker, the child judges four things: the rhetoric and the evidence the speaker uses, the reasoning, and the point of view.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) asks a visiting firefighter a question about something that was not clear. An older child (about 9 to 12) listens to a classmate's speech for a longer break, writes down the claim, the reasons and the evidence, notes which reason had no support, and says which phrases were chosen to cause strong feeling.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Four things about a speaker are judged: point of view, reasoning, evidence, rhetoric. No kind of speech or setting is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children accept what a confident adult says without question. Older children judge a speaker by voice and manner and not by the reasons given. They cannot tell a point backed by evidence from one that is only repeated loudly.
