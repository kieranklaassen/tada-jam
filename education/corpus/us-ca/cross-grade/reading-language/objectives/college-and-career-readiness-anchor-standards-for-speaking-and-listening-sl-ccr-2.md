## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child brings together, and judges, information that arrives in different media and formats. These include information that is spoken aloud, given in numbers, or shown in visual form.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) listens to a book read aloud or watches a short film and answers questions about what it said and showed. An older child (about 9 to 12) listens to a talk with slides, combines what the speaker said with what the graph on a slide shows, and says whether the two agree.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Three ways of presenting are named: visual, quantitative and oral. Both combining and judging are asked. No medium is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children remember the pictures and forget what was said. Older children accept a number in a chart or a statement in a video without asking where it comes from. They report each source separately and do not join them.
