## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child judges in what way a text's style and content are formed by its purpose or by the point of view behind it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) names the author and the illustrator of a book and says who is telling the story; reading aloud, the child gives the wolf a different voice from the pig. An older child (about 9 to 12) reads two reports of one football match, each by a supporter of a different team, lists what each includes and leaves out and how each sounds, and says how the writer's side explains the differences.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. It links two causes (point of view, purpose) with two effects (content, style). No kind of text is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children do not separate the storyteller from the author. Older children confuse the author's point of view with a character's. They state the author's purpose in one word ("to persuade") and do not show how it changes what is in the text.
