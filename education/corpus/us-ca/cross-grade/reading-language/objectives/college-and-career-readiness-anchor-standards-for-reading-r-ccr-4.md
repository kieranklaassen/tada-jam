## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child explains what words and phrases mean in the place where a text uses them, which includes figurative meanings, connotations and technical meanings. The child also examines how a particular choice of words affects the tone or the meaning.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) asks what "gigantic" means in a story and points to the words that show a character is angry, such as "stomped" and "shouted". An older child (about 9 to 12) explains what "a blanket of snow" means, says how "slammed" differs from "closed" in a sentence, and describes how the writer's word choices make a passage feel threatening.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Three kinds of meaning are named: technical, connotative, figurative. It also asks about the effect of word choice on meaning or tone. No kind of text is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children skip unknown words without asking. Older children read figurative language literally, or give a word's dictionary meaning and miss the feeling it suggests. They say a text "sounds sad" and cannot point to the words that make it so.
