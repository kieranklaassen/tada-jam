## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Writing is a regular habit, for many audiences, purposes and tasks. Some pieces are short, done in one sitting or within a couple of days; others run over a longer period with time to revise, reflect and research.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) writes or draws something most days: a label, a list, a sentence in a journal. An older child (about 9 to 12) writes a quick response to a reading in one lesson, a letter over two days, and a research report over three weeks with time set aside to reread and revise it.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Two time spans are named: shorter (a day or two, or one sitting) and extended (with time to research, reflect and revise). No number of pieces, length or frequency is set beyond "routinely".

### Common mistakes

Young children write only when told to and stop after one line. Older children treat every piece as one draft to hand in, including long ones. They leave a long piece until the last day, so there is no time to revise.
