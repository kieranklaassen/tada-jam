## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out what a text's themes or main ideas are and examines how the text builds them up. The child also sums up the most important ideas and details that support them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) retells a familiar story with its beginning, middle and end, and says what the fox learned. An older child (about 9 to 12) says that a novel's theme is that honesty has a cost, follows it through three events, and writes a summary of four sentences that leaves out the small details.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. It asks for three things: finding the central ideas or themes, following how they develop, and summarising. No kind of text and no summary length is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children retell one favourite part instead of the whole story. Older children give the topic ("friendship") when asked for the theme, which is a statement about the topic. Their summaries list events in order and miss the main idea.
