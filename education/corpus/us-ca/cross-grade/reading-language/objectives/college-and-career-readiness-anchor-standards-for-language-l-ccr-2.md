## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When writing, the child spells, punctuates and uses capital letters according to the accepted rules of standard English, and shows control of them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) writes a sentence about a friend, starts it with a capital letter, gives the friend's name a capital, ends with a full stop, and spells short words by their sounds. An older child (about 9 to 12) writes dialogue with quotation marks and commas in place, checks an uncertain spelling in a dictionary, and puts capitals on the words of a title.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. It covers three things, all in writing: capital letters, punctuation and spelling. It names no particular rule and gives no word list.

### Common mistakes

Young children put capital letters in the middle of words, leave out end punctuation, and spell every word as it sounds. Older children confuse words that sound the same ("their", "there"), and put commas where they pause when speaking instead of where the sentence needs them.
