## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows that they understand three things: how words relate to one another, the small differences between the meanings of words, and language that is figurative.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) sorts picture cards into foods and clothes, pairs opposites such as hot and cold, and acts out the difference between "walk", "march" and "tiptoe". An older child (about 9 to 12) explains what "time is a thief" means and why it is not literally true, places "cool", "cold" and "freezing" in order of strength, and says why "thrifty" sounds kinder than "stingy".

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Three areas are named: figurative language, relationships between words, and nuances of meaning. No figure of speech and no kind of word relationship is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children take a figurative phrase literally ("it is raining cats and dogs"). Older children treat near-synonyms as interchangeable and miss the difference in strength or feeling. They can label a simile but cannot say what it means in the sentence.
