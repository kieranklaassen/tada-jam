## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sets out the argument a text makes and its particular claims, and judges them. The judgement covers whether the reasoning is valid and whether the evidence is relevant and there is enough of it.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) says which reasons an author gives for a point, such as two reasons why bees matter. An older child (about 9 to 12) reads an editorial for a longer school day, lists its claim and each reason, marks a reason that does not connect to the claim, and says that one survey of ten pupils is not enough evidence.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. Three tests are named: valid reasoning, relevant evidence, sufficient evidence. No kind of text is named.

### Common mistakes

Young children accept a statement because it is in a book. Older children judge an argument by whether they agree with its conclusion. They count the pieces of evidence and do not ask whether each one relates to the claim.
