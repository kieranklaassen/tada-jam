## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Working alone and with skill, the child reads complex texts, informational as well as literary, and understands them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A young child (about 5 to 7) reads a short picture book alone and tells what happened and what it was about. An older child (about 9 to 12) reads a chapter of a novel and a science article without help, and afterwards explains each in their own words and answers questions that need more than one part of the text.

### Limits

This is an anchor standard that holds from kindergarten to grade 12: it sets no grade-specific content, and each grade's own standards give the detail. It names two kinds of text, literary and informational, and requires that they be complex. It gives no measure of complexity. Reading is independent; no support is mentioned.

### Common mistakes

Young children read the words aloud correctly without following the meaning. Older children choose only easy or familiar texts and avoid harder ones. They do not notice the point at which they stopped understanding.
