## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child picks out the evidence and the reasons that a speaker or a media source gives in support of specific points.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child listens to a classmate's talk arguing for a longer lunch break, or watches a short news clip. On paper the child writes the speaker's main point at the top and lists underneath each reason and each fact or example the speaker gave for it. The child tells a partner which point came with evidence and which was only stated.

### Limits

The source is a speaker or a media source. The child identifies reasons and evidence for particular points; the statement does not ask the child to judge them. The stored wording is the export's as corrected from the adopted document at one word. California adds to this standard: the statement ends with the mark of a California addition, which does not show how much of the statement was added.

### Common mistakes

Children write down what the speaker's opinion is and miss the reasons given for it. They count a repeated or louder statement as evidence. They remember the last thing said and lose the earlier reasons because they took no notes.
