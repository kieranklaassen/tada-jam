## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

To decode words, the child knows the phonics and word-analysis skills of the grade and applies them. Faced with a word of several syllables that they have not met, inside a text or standing alone, the child reads it correctly by drawing together everything they know about how letters match sounds, how words divide into syllables, and how words are built from parts such as affixes and roots.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child meets 'uncomfortable' in a chapter book, covers the ending, finds 'comfort', adds 'un' and 'able', and reads the whole word aloud. Given a card that holds only the word 'transportation', the child marks the syllables with a pencil, says each one, and blends them. The child then reads the sentence again with the word in place.

### Limits

The skills are those of the grade level; the statement does not list them. The words are unfamiliar and have more than one syllable, and the child reads them both inside a text and on their own. Roots and affixes are given as an example of word structure. The statement asks for accurate reading of the word and does not mention explaining its meaning.

### Common mistakes

Children read the first syllable or two and guess the rest from a word they already know. They stress the wrong syllable, so a word they know by ear goes unrecognised. They miss a prefix or suffix and read only the base word.
