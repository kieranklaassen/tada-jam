## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child makes sense of information that comes in visual, spoken or numerical form, and explains how it helps a reader understand the text it belongs to. The examples are interactive parts of web pages, animations, time lines, diagrams, graphs and charts.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads an article on rainfall with a bar graph beside it. The child reads the tallest and shortest bars aloud, says which month is wettest, and then finds the sentence in the article that the graph makes clearer. With a labelled diagram of a volcano, the child traces the path of the lava with a finger and tells what the diagram shows that the words do not.

### Limits

The information is visual, oral or quantitative, and it appears with a text. The six formats named are examples. The statement asks the child to interpret such information and connect it to the text; it does not mention making a chart or graph.

### Common mistakes

Children look at the picture or graph and skip its title, labels and scale. They describe the graphic alone and do not connect it to the text. They read a single bar or point and miss the overall pattern.
