## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes opinion pieces about texts or topics and backs a point of view with information and reasons. The piece opens by clearly introducing the text or topic and stating an opinion, and it is organised so that related ideas sit together in a way that serves the writer's purpose. The reasons rest on details and facts. Words and phrases such as 'in addition', 'in order to' and 'for instance' connect the opinion to its reasons. The piece ends with a closing statement or a closing section connected to the opinion it gave.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child writes a page arguing that the class should keep a pet. The first paragraph names the question and says what the child thinks. Each following paragraph gives one reason with a fact or detail, such as what a guinea pig costs to feed, and begins with a linking phrase like 'for instance' or 'in addition'. The last lines come back to the opinion.

### Limits

The piece is about a topic or a text. Each reason needs facts and details behind it; the statement does not say how many reasons. The three linking phrases are examples. The statement does not mention answering an opposing view.

### Common mistakes

Children state an opinion and then repeat it in different words where reasons should be. They give reasons with no fact or detail behind them ('because it is fun'). They end with 'The End' or 'That is my opinion' and no real closing.
