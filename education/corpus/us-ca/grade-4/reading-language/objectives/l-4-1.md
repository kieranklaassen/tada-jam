## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child follows the grammar and usage rules of standard English in both speech and writing. That covers interrogative and relative pronouns (that, which, who, whom, whose) and the relative adverbs why, when and where; the progressive tenses of a verb, which the child builds and uses (the examples are I will be walking, I am walking and I was walking); and modal auxiliaries such as must, may and can for expressing different conditions. Adjectives go in the customary order inside a sentence (in the example, a small red bag is the accepted order and a red small bag is not), and the child builds prepositional phrases and puts them into sentences. Sentences are whole: the child spots a fragment or a run-on that does not belong and repairs it, and picks the right word from sets that are often mixed up (their and there; two, too and to). Handwriting is in cursive or joined italics, flowing and readable.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child writes a paragraph about a walk to school and then tells it aloud to a partner. The child joins two short sentences with 'who' or 'which', changes 'I walk' into 'I was walking', and decides whether 'may' or 'must' fits a class rule. While rereading the draft the child finds a sentence with no verb, two sentences run together without a full stop, and a 'there' that should be 'their', and corrects each one. The final copy is written by hand in joined letters.

### Limits

The standard covers both writing and speaking. The pronouns and adverbs of part a are listed by name. The progressive forms, the modal verbs, the adjective order and the confused words are given as examples, not as complete lists. A footnote explains the asterisk on parts f and g: starting in grade 3, an asterisk marks skills that will probably still need work in later grades, as children's writing and speaking grow more advanced. California adds to this standard: parts a and h carry the mark of a California addition. The mark stands at the end of a line and does not show how much of the line was added. Part h allows either cursive or joined italics and names no letter size or speed.

### Common mistakes

Children of this age often write 'who' where 'whom' or 'whose' is needed, and say 'which' for a person. They join two full sentences with only a comma between them. They mix up words that sound the same, such as 'their' and 'there' or 'too' and 'to'.
