## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child describes how the information, ideas, concepts or events in a whole text, or in one part of it, are organised overall. The examples of such structures are problem and solution, cause and effect, comparison, and time order.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads two short passages about wolves: one tells the history of wolves in a park year by year, the other sets wolves beside coyotes. The child says which passage is in time order and which compares, and circles the signal words ('in 1995', 'unlike', 'both') that show it. The child fills in a blank timeline for the first and a two-column chart for the second.

### Limits

The four structures are given as examples, not as a complete list. The child describes the structure of one text or one part of a text. The statement does not ask for a comparison of structures across texts.

### Common mistakes

Children describe what the text says and not how it is organised. They call every text 'time order' because they read it from start to finish. They miss that one section of a text can be organised differently from the rest.
