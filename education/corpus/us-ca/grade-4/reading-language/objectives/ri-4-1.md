## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

When the child explains what a text states outright, and when the child infers something the text leaves unstated, they point to particular examples and details in that text.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads an article about honeybees and is asked what bees do in winter. The child answers and puts a finger on the sentence that says so, or reads it aloud. Asked why the beekeeper leaves some honey in the hive, which the article never states, the child gives a reason and names the two details that led to it.

### Limits

This record belongs to the informational text strand; the literature strand of this grade has a standard with the same wording. The statement covers both what is stated outright and what is inferred. It does not say how many details or examples to give, and it does not ask for exact quotation.

### Common mistakes

Children answer from what they already know about the topic and not from the text. They give an inference with no detail behind it. They point to a detail that is about the topic but does not support the answer.
