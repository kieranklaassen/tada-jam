## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes narratives about experiences or events, real or made up, with a clear order of events, descriptive detail and effective technique. The opening orients the reader: it sets up a situation and brings in a narrator, characters or both, and the events then come in an order that unfolds naturally. Description and dialogue build up the events and experiences or show how characters respond to what happens. Varied transition words and phrases manage the order of events, and sensory details and concrete words and phrases make the events and experiences exact. The ending is a result of the events or experiences told.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child writes a story about getting lost at a market. The first lines say who is telling it, where they are and what goes wrong. The child writes what the stallholder says in quotation marks, describes the smell of frying onions and the noise of the crowd, and moves the story along with 'a moment later' and 'by the time I looked up'. The last paragraph tells how the child was found, which comes from what happened before.

### Limits

The narrative may be about real or imagined events. The statement names no particular transition words and no length. It asks for dialogue and description, for sensory details, and for an ending that comes from the events told.

### Common mistakes

Children start in the middle with no setting or characters introduced. They join every event with 'and then'. They stop suddenly, or end with 'it was all a dream'.
