## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Given a firsthand account and a secondhand account of one event or topic, the child finds likenesses and differences between them, and describes how the two differ in what they concentrate on and in the information they give.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads a diary entry by a girl who lived through an earthquake and a textbook paragraph about the same earthquake. The child marks in one colour what only the diary tells (what she heard, how afraid she was) and in another what only the textbook tells (the strength, the number of houses damaged). The child tells a partner who wrote each one and whether that person was there.

### Limits

The statement asks for one firsthand and one secondhand account, both about one event or topic. It names two differences to describe: focus and information. It does not ask the child to judge which account is more reliable.

### Common mistakes

Children think the firsthand account is always the true one and the secondhand one is wrong. They compare the events and not how the two accounts differ. They take a secondhand account that quotes a witness for a firsthand one because it has 'I' in it.
