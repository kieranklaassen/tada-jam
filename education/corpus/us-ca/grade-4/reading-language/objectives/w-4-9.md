## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child takes evidence from informational or literary texts to back up their research, reflection and analysis. In doing so the child applies the grade 4 reading standards for literature, for example by describing an event, setting or character of a play or story thoroughly from particular details in the text. The child likewise applies the grade 4 reading standards for informational texts, for example by explaining how the author of a text backs up specific points with evidence and reasons.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child writes a paragraph answering 'What kind of person is the captain?' and includes two things the captain says and one thing he does, each with a page number. For an article on recycling, the child writes which reasons the author gives for a point and copies one piece of evidence inside quotation marks. The child rereads the answer and checks that every claim has something from the text beside it.

### Limits

The evidence comes from literary or informational texts. The two reading standards quoted in parts a and b are examples; the parts refer to the grade 4 reading standards for literature and for informational texts as a whole. The statement does not say how much evidence to give or how long the writing is.

### Common mistakes

Children write what they think about the text and give no evidence from it. They copy a long passage and do not say what it shows. They pick a quotation that is about the topic but does not support their point.
