## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Reading stories, myths and traditional literature that come from different cultures, the child finds likenesses and differences in how they handle similar topics and themes (good set against evil is the example) and similar patterns of events (the quest is the example).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads a Greek myth and a West African folktale in which a young person leaves home to find something. On two halves of a page the child lists what each hero seeks, who helps, and how it ends, then draws lines between the matching steps. The child tells a partner one thing both tales say about bravery and one thing they treat differently.

### Limits

The texts come from different cultures and are of three kinds: traditional literature, myths and stories. The statement names two things to compare: themes and topics, and patterns of events, with one example of each. It does not say how many texts or which cultures.

### Common mistakes

Children compare surface details (both have a river) and miss the shared pattern or theme. They list what happens in each tale separately and never compare them point by point. They assume the tale they know is the original and the other is a copy.
