## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child learns words and phrases suited to the grade, both general academic ones and ones that belong to a subject, and uses them correctly. Among them are words that name an exact action, feeling or state of being (stammered, whined and quizzed are the examples) and words that a given topic depends on (for a discussion of protecting animals, the examples are endangered, conservation and wildlife).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child retelling a scene says that the boy 'stammered' his answer, where a year earlier the child would have said 'said'. In a talk about sea turtles the child says 'habitat', 'endangered' and 'conservation' and can explain each one. The child keeps a list of new words met in reading and puts two of them into the next piece of writing.

### Limits

The words are those suited to the grade; the statement gives no list. The six words it names are examples, and animal preservation is an example topic. The statement asks for accurate use, not only recognition.

### Common mistakes

Children put a new word into a sentence where it does not fit, because they know roughly what it means but not how it is used. They recognise a subject word in reading and go back to a vague everyday word when speaking or writing. They treat near-synonyms such as 'whined' and 'complained' as the same.
