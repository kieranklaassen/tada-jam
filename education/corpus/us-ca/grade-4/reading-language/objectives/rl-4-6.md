## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child finds likenesses and differences in the narrator's point of view across different stories, and this includes telling first-person narration apart from third-person narration.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads the opening page of two stories about a first day at school, one told by the new pupil as 'I' and one told by a narrator as 'she'. The child circles the pronouns that show who is telling each story. The child tells a partner what the 'I' story lets a reader know that the 'she' story does not, and rewrites three sentences of one story in the other point of view.

### Limits

The statement names first-person and third-person narration and compares across different stories. It does not mention second person, and it does not divide third person into kinds.

### Common mistakes

Children think a story is in the first person whenever 'I' appears, including inside a character's spoken words. They confuse the narrator with the author. They say who tells the story and stop without saying how that changes what a reader learns.
