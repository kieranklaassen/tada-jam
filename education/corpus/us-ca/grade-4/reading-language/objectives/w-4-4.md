## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child's writing, which includes texts of several paragraphs, is clear and coherent, and the way it is developed and organised suits the audience, the purpose and the task. A note in the statement says that standards 1 to 3 of this strand set out what is expected at this grade for each type of writing.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child writes a letter to the head teacher asking for a new bench, and a set of instructions for a younger class on how to plant beans. The letter has a greeting, three paragraphs and a polite close; the instructions have numbered steps and short sentences. Before writing each one, the child says who will read it and what it is for.

### Limits

The statement includes texts of more than one paragraph but sets no number of paragraphs or words. It refers to writing standards 1 to 3 of this grade for what each type of writing requires. California adds to this standard: the statement ends with the mark of a California addition, which does not show how much of the statement was added.

### Common mistakes

Children write one long block with no paragraphs. They write for themselves and leave out what a reader who was not there needs to know. They keep the same form and tone for every task, for example a story opening on a report.
