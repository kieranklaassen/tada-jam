## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes texts that inform or explain: they look into a topic and present its ideas and information clearly. The text introduces its topic clearly and puts related information together in paragraphs and sections, with formatting such as headings, illustrations and multimedia where these help a reader understand. The topic is built up with quotations, concrete details, definitions, facts, or other examples and information that relate to it. Inside each category of information, linking words and phrases such as 'because', 'also', 'for example' and 'another' connect the ideas, and exact language and subject-specific vocabulary do the informing or explaining. The text ends with a closing statement or section that connects to what it explained or reported.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child writes a two-page report on how bees make honey. The report has a title, an opening paragraph that names the topic, and sections with headings such as 'Collecting nectar' and 'Inside the hive'. Each section holds facts, one definition (of 'nectar') and a labelled drawing, and its sentences are joined with 'also', 'because' and 'for example'. The child swaps 'bee food' for 'pollen' where that is the right term, and writes a closing paragraph.

### Limits

Headings are an example of formatting, and formatting, illustrations and multimedia are included when they help understanding, not always. The kinds of content in part b are alternatives joined by 'or'. The linking words named are examples. The statement gives no length; another writing standard of this grade mentions texts of several paragraphs.

### Common mistakes

Children list facts in the order they found them, with no grouping. They copy sentences from a source where they should explain in their own words. They mix in opinion ('bees are the best insects') and stop without a closing.
