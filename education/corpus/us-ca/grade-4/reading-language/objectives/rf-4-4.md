## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reads accurately and fluently enough for the reading to support understanding. The child has a purpose when reading on-level text, and understands it. Read aloud, on-level poetry and prose are accurate, suitably paced and expressive over repeated readings. The child checks their recognition of words and their understanding against the context, puts right what is wrong, and rereads where that is needed.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads a page of a chapter book silently to find out why a character left home, then tells a partner the answer. The child reads one poem aloud three times across a week; by the third reading the words are correct, the pace matches the lines, and the voice rises and falls with the meaning. On reading 'the horse galloped into the bran' the child stops, sees that it makes no sense, looks again, and reads 'barn'.

### Limits

The statement calls the texts on-level and does not define the term: it does not say whose level or which level is meant. Oral reading covers both prose and poetry and is described over repeated readings, not a first attempt. The statement gives no number of words per minute.

### Common mistakes

Children read fast and treat speed as the goal, ignoring punctuation and meaning. They say a wrong word that makes no sense and keep going. They read aloud in a flat voice, one word at a time, pausing in the wrong places.
