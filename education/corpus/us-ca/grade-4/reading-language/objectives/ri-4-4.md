## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

In a text on a grade 4 topic or subject, the child works out what its general academic words and its subject-specific words or phrases mean. A cross-reference sends the reader to Language standards 4 to 6 of the same grade for further expectations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reading a chapter on the water cycle meets 'evaporation' and 'process'. The child looks at the diagram and the nearby sentences to say what each word means, then checks the glossary. The child explains 'evaporation' to a partner with a wet towel drying on a line as the case.

### Limits

The words come from a text on a topic or subject of grade 4; the statement gives no word list. The statement itself refers to Language standards 4 to 6 of this grade for more on how meanings are worked out. California adds to this standard: the statement ends with the mark of a California addition, which does not show how much of the statement was added.

### Common mistakes

Children give the everyday meaning of a word that has a special meaning in the subject, such as 'table' or 'volume'. They can pronounce a subject word and cannot say what it means. They pass over a general academic word such as 'process' or 'factor' because it does not look like a topic word.
