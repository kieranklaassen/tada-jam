## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells apart occasions that need formal English, with presenting ideas as the example, from occasions where informal talk is fitting, with a discussion in a small group as the example. The child speaks in formal English when the task and the situation require it. A cross-reference points to Language standards 1 and 3 of the same grade for the specific expectations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child presenting a science project to visiting parents says 'I am going to explain how our filter works' and speaks in full sentences. Ten minutes later, planning the next step with two classmates, the same child says 'OK, so who's doing the poster?'. Asked afterwards, the child can say why they spoke differently each time.

### Limits

The two settings named, a small-group discussion and presenting ideas, are examples. The statement asks for formal English when the task and situation make it appropriate, not all the time. For what formal English includes, the statement refers to Language standards 1 and 3 of this grade.

### Common mistakes

Children speak in slang or sentence fragments during a presentation without noticing. They think formal English means long words and become hard to follow. They are unsure which setting they are in, for example when a teacher joins a small group.
