## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out what words and phrases mean in the way a text uses them. This includes words and phrases that allude to important figures of mythology, with 'Herculean' as the example. A cross-reference sends the reader to Language standards 4 to 6 of the same grade for further expectations.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads that a character faced 'a Herculean task', remembers or looks up the story of Hercules and his labours, and says that the task must be very hard and need great strength. The child rereads the sentence with that meaning in place. Meeting 'the wind whispered', the child says in plain words what the wind is doing.

### Limits

The meaning is the one the word or phrase has in the text at hand. Words that allude to figures from mythology are included, with one example; the statement does not list which myths or figures a child should know. The statement itself refers to Language standards 4 to 6 of this grade. California adds to this standard: the statement ends with the mark of a California addition, which does not show how much of the statement was added.

### Common mistakes

Children do not know the myth, so the allusion carries no meaning for them and they pass over it. They take a figurative phrase literally. They give a meaning the word can have elsewhere that does not fit this sentence.
