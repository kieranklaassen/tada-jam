## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child puts into their own words parts of a text they hear read aloud, or information that reaches them through varied media and formats, including spoken, numerical and visual ones.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child listens to a teacher read two paragraphs about tides and then tells a partner, without the book, what those paragraphs said. After watching a short video with a chart of high and low tide times, the child says in two or three sentences what the chart showed. The child speaks in their own words and keeps the facts the same.

### Limits

The child paraphrases portions, not a whole text. The source is a text read aloud to the child, or information given in visual, quantitative or oral form. The statement sets no length.

### Common mistakes

Children repeat the exact words they heard and call it a paraphrase. They change the meaning or add their own opinion while rewording. They restate the first thing they heard and leave out the rest, or get a number from a chart wrong when restating it.
