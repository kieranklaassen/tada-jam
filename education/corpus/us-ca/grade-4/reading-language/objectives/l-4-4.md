## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out or makes clear what an unfamiliar word or phrase means, or one that has several meanings, in grade 4 reading and subject matter, and moves freely between strategies to do it. The parts name three. One is context as a pointer to what a word or phrase means; a definition, a restatement or an example found in the text are given as examples of such context. Another is word parts: common Greek and Latin roots and affixes suited to the grade point to what a word means (autograph, photograph and telegraph are the examples). The third is reference materials in print and digital form, such as glossaries, thesauruses and dictionaries, which the child consults to learn how a key word or phrase is pronounced and to find or make clear its exact meaning, and to find other word choices in all subject areas.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reading a science article meets 'erosion', rereads the next sentence, and finds that the article explains the word there. Meeting 'thermometer', the child covers 'meter', remembers 'therm' from 'thermal', and says what the whole word probably means. For a word that is still unclear, the child turns to the glossary in the back of the book or an online dictionary, says the pronunciation aloud, and picks the meaning that fits the sentence. When writing, the child opens a thesaurus to replace a word already written three times.

### Limits

The words and phrases come from grade 4 reading and subject matter; the statement gives no word list. Roots and affixes are limited to common Greek and Latin ones suited to the grade, and the three words ending in -graph are examples. The three kinds of context clue named in part a are also examples, not a complete list. Reference materials are named in both print and digital form. California adds to this standard: part c carries the mark of a California addition. The mark stands at the end of the line and does not show how much of the line was added.

### Common mistakes

Children take the first meaning listed in a dictionary even when it does not fit the sentence. They guess a meaning from a word part that only looks like a root they know. They pass over an unknown word and read on without noticing that the nearby sentences explain it.
