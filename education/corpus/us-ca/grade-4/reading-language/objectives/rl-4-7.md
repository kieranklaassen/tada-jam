## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child links the written text of a play or story with a presentation of it that is seen or heard, and picks out the places where each version follows particular descriptions and directions in the written text.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads a scene of a play script and then watches a recording of the scene or hears a cast read it aloud. With the script open, the child puts a mark beside each stage direction and description that the performance followed, and a different mark where the performance did something else. The child tells a partner one place where an actor's voice matched what the script asks for.

### Limits

The texts are stories and dramas. The presentation may be visual or oral. The child locates where each version follows the text's descriptions and directions; the statement does not ask the child to judge which version is better.

### Common mistakes

Children say which version they liked more and do not point to the text. They remember the film or performance and credit its details to the written story. They pass over stage directions when reading and so cannot match them to what they saw.
