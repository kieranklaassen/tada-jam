## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out what a text is mainly about, explains how its important details back that main idea up, and gives a summary of the text.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child reads a three-page article on how deserts form and says in one sentence what it is mostly about. The child underlines three details and tells a partner how each one backs that sentence. The child then writes a summary of four or five sentences without copying from the article.

### Limits

The statement speaks of the main idea of one text and of the key details that support it. It does not say how long the text or the summary is. The matching standard of the literature strand at this grade asks for a theme where this one asks for a main idea.

### Common mistakes

Children name the topic ('deserts') in place of the main idea (what the text says about deserts). They pick an interesting detail and call it the main idea. Their summaries retell every detail in order or add their own opinion.
