## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

Speaking in an organised way, the child gives a report on a text or topic, tells a story, or recounts something that happened to them, and supports the main ideas or themes with suitable facts and with descriptive details that are to the point. The child speaks clearly and at a pace listeners can follow. The child also plans and gives a narrative presentation: it sets out a clear context, tells of ideas, observations or things remembered, and shows clearly why the experience or event is one to remember.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child plans a three-minute talk about the day their family moved house, with notes on four cards: where and when, what happened, one detail they can still see and hear, and why they still remember it. The child practises with a partner, slows down where the partner could not follow, and then gives the talk to the class. On another day the child reports on a book about bridges with three facts in a planned order.

### Limits

The standard covers three kinds of speaking: a report on a text or topic, a story, and an account of an experience. It names no length of talk. California adds to this standard: part a, the planned narrative presentation, carries the mark of a California addition. The mark stands at the end of the line and does not show how much of the line was added.

### Common mistakes

Children tell events in the order they remember them and not in the order they happened. They speak too fast or too quietly when nervous. They list what happened and leave out why it mattered to them, or give details that have nothing to do with the main idea.
