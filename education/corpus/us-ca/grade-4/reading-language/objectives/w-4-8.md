## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child draws on what they remember from their own experience, or collects information from digital and print sources, keeping to what is relevant. The child takes notes, puts the information in their own words, sorts it into categories, and gives a list of the sources.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child researching volcanoes reads a library book and a website. On note cards the child writes short notes in their own words, one fact per card, and writes the source on the back. The child sorts the cards into piles such as 'kinds of volcano' and 'what comes out', and at the end writes a list naming the book and the website.

### Limits

Information may come from the child's own experience or from print and digital sources, and it has to be relevant. The statement asks for a list of sources and names no citation format. California adds to this standard: the statement ends with the mark of a California addition, which does not show how much of the statement was added.

### Common mistakes

Children copy sentences word for word where they should take notes. They write down everything, including facts that have nothing to do with the question. They forget to record where a fact came from and cannot list the source later.
