## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child compares ways of staying out of places and moments in which people use tobacco, alcohol or other drugs, and judges how well each one would work.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child gets three ideas for one scene, such as a party at a neighbour's house where teenagers drink: stay near a trusted adult, leave and go home, or not go at all. For each idea the child says what would probably happen next and whether a nine-year-old could really do it. The child then picks the idea that would work best and says why.

### Limits

Standard 5 is decision making: the child judges strategies and is not asked here to carry them out. The statement is about keeping away from the situation; what to say once inside it belongs to 4.4.1.A and 4.4.2.A. No list of strategies is given.

### Common mistakes

Children often pick the idea that sounds bravest and not the one they could really do. Many judge a strategy only by whether it keeps their friends pleased. Some forget that a child often cannot leave a place without an adult's help.
