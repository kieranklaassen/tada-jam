## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names where trustworthy facts on drugs, tobacco and alcohol can be found.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child lists people and places to ask: a parent, the school nurse, a doctor, a pharmacist, a health textbook, a government health website. For each one the child says why it can be trusted: training, nothing to sell, facts that can be checked. The child sets aside an advert and a rumour from an older pupil and says why.

### Limits

Standard 3 is accessing valid information: here the child only names the sources. The statement does not ask the child to look anything up or to judge a piece of information, and it lists no sources.

### Common mistakes

Children often trust whoever sounds most sure, such as an older child. Many treat everything found online as equally reliable. Some think a famous person is a good source.
