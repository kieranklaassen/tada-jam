## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names things a person can do to handle a moment in which drugs, tobacco or alcohol are involved.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child hears a short scene, such as an older cousin smoking in the car, and lists what the child could do: move away, say how it feels, phone a parent, tell a teacher the next day. The child writes each idea on a slip of paper and keeps the ones that are safe and possible for a nine-year-old.

### Limits

Standard 1 is essential concepts: the child names ways of coping, and putting them to use is a separate item under standard 7 (4.7.1.A). The statement does not say which situations are meant or how many ways the child must name.

### Common mistakes

Children often name only one answer, usually "say no", which does not fit a moment in which an adult is the one using. Some think coping means making the other person stop. Some keep the situation secret because they fear getting a relative into trouble.
