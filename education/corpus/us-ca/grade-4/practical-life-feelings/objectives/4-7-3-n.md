## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names how a person can start good eating habits and make them last. The habits meant are ones that match today's guidelines, grounded in research, on a balanced diet.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child compares their own usual day of eating with a current food guide picture, such as a plate divided into food groups, and names one habit to start: a vegetable at dinner, or breakfast every day. The child says what would help the habit last: a regular mealtime, the food being in the house, a reminder on the fridge.

### Limits

The statement refers to guidelines that are current and based on research, and it neither names one nor gives its contents. It asks for ways both to start habits and to keep them. It stands under standard 7, yet its verb asks the child to name ways, not to carry them out.

### Common mistakes

Children often think a balanced diet means the same amount of every food. Many think a habit is formed after doing something once or twice. Some sort foods into "good" and "bad" and do not consider the whole day's eating.
