## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names the main nutrients, says what each one is, and tells what work it does in the body.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child matches three sets of cards: the name of a nutrient (carbohydrate, protein, fat, vitamins, minerals, water), a one-line meaning, and a job, such as building muscle for protein or giving energy for carbohydrate. The child picks up a real food, such as an egg or a slice of bread, and says which nutrient it mainly gives.

### Limits

The statement does not list which nutrients count as the main ones. It asks for a name, a meaning and a job for each. Amounts are not part of this item: servings are 4.1.2.N and reading a label is 4.3.2.N.

### Common mistakes

Children often confuse a nutrient with a food ("milk is a nutrient"). Many think fat is only harmful and has no job in the body. Some believe one food gives one nutrient only.
