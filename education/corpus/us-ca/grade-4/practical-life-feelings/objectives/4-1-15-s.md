## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child says how to respond when a person has taken in poison, with paint or cleaning supplies kept in the house as the examples. The response is a phone call: to the local emergency number, to a poison control center, or to 9-1-1.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child rehearses on an unplugged phone: presses the three digits, says what the person swallowed or breathed in, and reads the product name from the container. The child states the first steps aloud: get an adult if one is near, make the call, keep the container, stay with the person. The child finds where the poison control number is posted in the house.

### Limits

The response the statement gives is a phone call, to one of three kinds of number. Its two product types are examples only. It says nothing about first aid. Reading product labels is a separate grade 4 statement.

### Common mistakes

Children often want to give water, milk or food, or to make the person vomit, before calling. Many think only something swallowed counts and leave out fumes and splashes on skin or in eyes. Some wait to see whether the person looks sick before telling anyone.
