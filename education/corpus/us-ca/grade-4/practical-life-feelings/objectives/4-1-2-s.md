## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child picks out ways of acting that can start a quarrel or a fight between people.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child listens to a short playground story and stops it at the moment trouble starts: grabbing the ball, name-calling, leaving someone out, cutting in line. The child says what the other person probably felt at that moment. The child sorts a set of described actions into those likely to start an argument and those not.

### Limits

The statement asks only for picking out the actions. It gives no examples and no list. Settling a quarrel is practiced under a separate grade 4 statement.

### Common mistakes

Children tend to see what the other person did and overlook their own part, such as teasing first. Many count only hitting or shouting and miss quiet actions like excluding, eye-rolling or spreading a rumor. Some treat every disagreement as a fight, though people can disagree calmly.
