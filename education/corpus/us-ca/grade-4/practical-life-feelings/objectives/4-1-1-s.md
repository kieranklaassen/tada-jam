## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child describes things that endanger people's safety. Among them are the dangers tied to going online, staying in the house with no adult, hazardous objects, water and fire.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A nine-year-old walks through a kitchen, a bathroom or a yard and points at what could burn, cut or drown someone, saying why. The child talks through an afternoon with no grown-up in the house: who is at the door, what stays switched off. The child names what can go wrong online, such as a stranger asking for an address.

### Limits

The statement names five kinds of danger, and its word "including" leaves room for more. It asks for describing only; ways to lower the chance of getting hurt belong to a separate grade 4 statement. No number of hazards is set.

### Common mistakes

Children often name only dramatic dangers, such as a house fire, and miss ordinary ones, such as a wet floor or a pan handle sticking out. Many think of online danger only as a broken device or a virus and leave out people who ask for personal details. Some believe shallow water is always safe.
