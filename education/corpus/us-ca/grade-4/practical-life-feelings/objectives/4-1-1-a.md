## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells how tobacco, alcohol and other drugs hurt a person, both soon after use and over many years. Inhalants are named as one of those drugs.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A child sorts cards that each name one kind of damage into two piles: what happens the same day and what develops over years. The child says aloud that smoke makes a person cough at once and damages the lungs after years of smoking. The child points to a spray can or a glue tube and says that breathing its fumes on purpose is drug use too.

### Limits

The statement names inhalants beside alcohol and tobacco and leaves "other drugs" open. It asks about harm only, on two time scales, and does not list which effects a child must know. Standard 1 is essential concepts: the child describes and is not yet asked to practise a skill.

### Common mistakes

Children often think a harm that does not show at once is not there. Many do not count household products such as glue or spray paint as drugs. Some believe only adults who use a lot are harmed.
