## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child works out the part feelings play in what people do, both when they act safely and when they act with violence.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads two short scenes that start with the same feeling, such as anger at losing, and traces what each person did next: one walked away and breathed, one pushed. The child names the feeling behind an action: fear behind running from a dog, excitement behind a risky jump. The child marks on a body outline where they feel anger and says what they can do at that point.

### Limits

The statement asks for analysis of the link between feelings and actions and names no particular feelings. It covers actions of two kinds, safe ones as well as violent ones.

### Common mistakes

Children often think a feeling forces the action ("he made me mad, so I hit him") and do not see the choice between the two. Many label anger itself as bad, though only the action can be unsafe. Few notice that happy excitement also leads to risky acts.
