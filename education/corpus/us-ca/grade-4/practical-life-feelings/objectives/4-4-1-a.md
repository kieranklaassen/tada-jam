## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child shows how to say no when someone urges them to try tobacco, alcohol or other drugs.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

In a role-play with a partner, a child is offered a pretend cigarette and answers "No, I don't want to" in a firm voice, standing straight and looking at the partner. The child repeats the refusal when asked again, gives a reason or suggests something else to do, and walks away if the partner continues.

### Limits

Standard 4 is interpersonal communication: the child shows the skill and does not only describe it. The statement is about pressure to try a substance and does not say who applies the pressure. It gives no number or list of refusal techniques.

### Common mistakes

Children often say no in a quiet or joking voice, and the other person asks again. Many think one refusal ends the matter and have nothing ready for the second or third request. Some fear that refusing a friend ends the friendship and give a false excuse in place of a refusal.
