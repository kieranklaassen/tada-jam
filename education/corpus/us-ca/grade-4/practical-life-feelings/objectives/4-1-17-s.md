## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child names things a person can do so that sight and hearing are not harmed.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child turns the volume on headphones down until a person speaking nearby can still be heard. The child puts on sunglasses and a hat in bright sun, safety goggles for a science task, and ear plugs near loud machines. The child rests the eyes after a stretch of reading by looking at something far away.

### Limits

The statement names no particular ways, no sound level and no time limit. It asks for naming them only.

### Common mistakes

Children often think sound harms the ears only when it hurts, and do not know that long listening at a moderately loud level also does harm. Many believe looking at the sun briefly, or through sunglasses, is safe. Some think damaged hearing recovers the way a cut heals.
