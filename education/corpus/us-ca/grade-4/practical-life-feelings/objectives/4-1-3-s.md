## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child tells apart the forms that harassment and bullying take and says what each one looks like.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears short scenes and names the form in each: hitting or shoving, name-calling, leaving a classmate out on purpose, spreading rumors, unkind messages on a phone. The child explains what makes an act bullying: it is done on purpose, it repeats, and one side has more power. The child gives an example of each form in their own words.

### Limits

The statement does not list the forms or say how many there are, and it defines neither term. It asks for describing. What bullying does to people and how to report it are separate grade 4 statements.

### Common mistakes

Children often count only physical acts and leave out exclusion, rumors and online messages. Many call any single unkind act, or a quarrel between equals, bullying. Few nine-year-olds know the word harassment; they need it explained as unwanted behavior that keeps bothering or frightening someone.
