## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student gathers information and puts it together to describe two things: that the energy and fuels people use come from natural resources, and that using them affects the environment. The clarification's examples of renewable energy resources are sunlight, wind and water held behind dams; it names fossil fuels and fissile materials as the non-renewable ones. Its examples of effects are air pollution from burning fossil fuels and habitat lost to surface mining or to dams.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student reads two short articles and a chart about where the local electricity comes from, takes notes on cards, and sorts the cards into "resource", "how it is used" and "effect on the environment". The student joins the cards with arrows on a poster and explains one chain aloud.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. The clarification gives the renewable resources and the environmental effects as examples, and states the non-renewable resources as fossil fuels and fissile materials.

### Common mistakes

Thinking electricity is itself a natural resource. Believing renewable sources have no effect on the environment; the clarification's own examples include habitat lost to dams. Confusing "renewable" with "recyclable".
