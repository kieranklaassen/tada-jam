## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student develops a model of waves and uses it to describe wave patterns by their wavelength and amplitude, and to describe that a wave can make an object move. The clarification's examples of models are analogies, diagrams, and physical models in which wire shows the amplitude and wavelength.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student shakes one end of a long rope or spring, slowly then quickly, gently then widely, and watches the crests get closer together or taller. The student floats a cork in a tray, makes ripples, and watches the cork bob up and down. The student bends a wire into a wave shape and labels its height and the distance from crest to crest.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out four things: electromagnetic waves, interference, waves that do not repeat regularly, and models of wavelength and amplitude that use numbers. The kinds of model in the clarification are examples.

### Common mistakes

Thinking the wave carries the water or the rope along with it; the cork bobs in place. Measuring amplitude from the bottom of a trough to the top of a crest instead of from the rest position. Confusing wavelength with the height of the wave.
