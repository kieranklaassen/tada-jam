## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student thinks of several ways of sending information by means of patterns, and compares them. The clarification's examples are Morse code for sending text, a grid of ones and zeros that stand for white and black and so describe a picture, and drums that send a coded message as sound.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student and a partner invent a code of long and short taps, send a three-letter word across the room, and check what arrived. The student colours squares on grid paper and reads the grid out row by row as "one" and "zero", and the partner redraws the picture from what was read out. They compare the two methods for speed and for errors.

### Limits

The expectation states no assessment boundary. It is marked as integrating engineering practices. The three ways of coding in the clarification are examples. More than one solution is asked for, with a comparison.

### Common mistakes

Agreeing on no shared key, so the receiver cannot decode the pattern. Leaving no gap between symbols, so letters run together. Producing one solution and not comparing it with another.
