## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student uses evidence to build an explanation that links how fast an object is moving with the energy that object has.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student rolls a ball down a ramp from a low start and from a high start, so that it reaches the bottom slower or faster, and lets it hit a block or a lump of clay. The student compares how far the block slides or how deep the dent is. The student uses those results to explain that the faster ball had more energy.

### Limits

The assessment boundary leaves out measuring changes in speed with numbers, and leaves out any exact or numerical definition of energy. California adds a clarification statement to this expectation, which the department's grade 4 document prints and marks as California's own; the export this record was read from has no clarification at all. Its examples of evidence for the link between speed and energy are the results of collisions, among them how an object changes shape when it hits something.

### Common mistakes

Treating speed and energy as the same thing. Thinking only living things or fuels have energy, and a rolling ball has none. Giving the claim with no observation to support it.
