## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student observes, measures, or both, to get evidence of what weathering does or of how fast erosion happens when the agent is wind, water, ice or plant growth. The clarification's examples of things to vary in a test include how steep the slope is that water runs down, how much water flows, how much plant cover there is, how fast the wind blows, the relative rate at which material is deposited, and repeated heating and cooling or freezing and thawing.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student fills two trays with damp soil, grows grass in one, tilts both, pours the same amount of water over each, and measures the soil washed into a tub at the lower end. The student changes one thing, such as the tilt, and repeats. The student writes down each amount and compares.

### Limits

The assessment boundary keeps what is assessed to one form of weathering or erosion. The variables listed in the clarification are examples. The expectation allows observations, measurements, or both.

### Common mistakes

Using "weathering" and "erosion" as the same word, when one breaks rock down and the other carries material away. Changing two things between trials, such as the slope and the amount of water. Thinking such changes happen only in large sudden events and never slowly.
