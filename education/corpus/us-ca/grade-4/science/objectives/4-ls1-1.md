## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The student builds an argument that animals and plants have structures, inside and outside, whose functions support their survival, growth, behaviour and reproduction. The clarification's examples of structures are skin, brain, lung, stomach, heart, coloured petals, roots, stems and thorns.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The student takes apart a flower and a bean seedling, lays out the petals, stem and roots, and writes beside each what it does for the plant. The student feels their own pulse and breathing after running and links the heart and lungs to what the body needs. The student states a claim and backs it with two of these observations.

### Limits

The assessment boundary keeps to structures large enough to be seen without a microscope, within plant and animal systems. The structures named in the clarification are examples. California adds a closing sentence to the clarification, which the department's grade 4 document prints and marks as California's own and which the export this record was read from lacks. That sentence is about function: within the system a structure belongs to, the structure has jobs of its own.

### Common mistakes

Naming a structure without saying what it does. Describing a function as something the organism wanted ("the rose grew thorns so that it would not be eaten"). Forgetting that plants have internal structures too.
