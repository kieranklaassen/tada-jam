## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child writes fractions in tenths or in hundredths as decimals. Examples given: writing 0.62 in fraction form as 62/100, calling a length 0.62 metres, and marking where 0.62 is on a number line.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shades 62 squares of a hundred grid, writes 62/100 and 0.62 beside it, and reads both aloud as "sixty-two hundredths". The child measures a ribbon with a metre stick marked in centimetres, records 62 cm as 0.62 m, and puts a marker for it on a number line from 0 to 1 that is divided into tenths.

### Limits

Tenths and hundredths only, so a decimal has at most two digits after the point; the statement does not mention thousandths. It does not say whether decimals greater than 1 are included; its example is less than 1. A footnote in the adopted document permits just 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6, then 8, 10 or 12, or else 100 as the denominator of a grade 4 fraction, and it stands on the whole domain, so it covers this statement.

### Common mistakes

Putting the digit in the wrong place: 0.6 for 6/100, or 0.05 for 5/10. Reading 0.62 as "point sixty-two" and treating the digits after the point as a whole number, with no link to hundredths. Thinking that 0.5 and 0.50 are different amounts.
