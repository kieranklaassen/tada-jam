## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child draws the basic elements of geometry: points, rays, line segments and lines; angles of three kinds, acute, right and obtuse; and lines that are parallel or perpendicular to one another. The child also finds each of these inside two-dimensional figures.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

With a ruler the child draws a segment with two endpoints, a ray with one endpoint and one arrow, and a line with an arrow at both ends. The child holds the corner of a card against an angle to tell whether it is right, smaller than right (acute) or larger than right (obtuse). On a drawing of a trapezoid or of a capital E, the child marks the parallel sides in one colour and the right angles in another.

### Limits

Three kinds of angle are named: right, acute and obtuse; the statement does not mention straight or reflex angles. Figures are flat. The statement names no drawing tool and asks for no measuring in degrees; measuring angles is the neighbouring statement 4.MD.6.

### Common mistakes

Recognising right angles and perpendicular lines only when one line is horizontal and the other vertical. Calling two lines parallel because they do not meet inside the drawing, or denying it because they differ in length. Treating a line, a segment and a ray as one thing, with the line ending where the ink ends.
