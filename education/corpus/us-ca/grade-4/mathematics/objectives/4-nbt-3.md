## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child rounds a whole number that has several digits to whichever place is asked for, and does so by reasoning about place value.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child marks 3,476 on a number line between 3,000 and 4,000, finds the halfway point 3,500, and says that the number is nearer to 3,000. The child then rounds the same number to the nearest hundred and to the nearest ten, each time naming the two round numbers it lies between.

### Limits

Any place can be the place rounded to. A footnote to the base-ten domain (recorded with 4.NBT.1) keeps grade 4 work to whole numbers up to and including 1,000,000. Whole numbers only; the statement does not mention rounding decimals. It does not spell out what to do with a number that is exactly halfway.

### Common mistakes

Rounding in a chain from the right (3,446 to 3,450 to 3,500 to 4,000) instead of looking once at the digit to the right of the target place. Changing the target digit but leaving the digits after it as they were, or dropping them so that the number gets shorter (3,476 becomes 35). Rounding down by lowering the target digit by one (3,420 becomes 3,300).
