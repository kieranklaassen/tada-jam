## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands a fraction a/b whose numerator is more than 1 as unit fractions 1/b added together. In this part, the child solves word problems that call for adding or subtracting fractions with like denominators, where the fractions are parts of one same whole. One way named is to show the problem with equations and with visual fraction models.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads "Ana ate 2/8 of a pizza and Leo ate 3/8 of that pizza; how much was eaten?", draws a circle or a bar cut into eighths, shades each share, writes 2/8 + 3/8 = 5/8, and answers in words with the unit: "five eighths of the pizza".

### Limits

Like denominators only, and all fractions of one same whole. A footnote to the fractions domain (recorded with 4.NF.1) allows only these denominators in grade 4: 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, then 8, 10 and 12, and also 100. Sevenths, ninths and elevenths are not used. Fractions with unlike denominators are outside this statement.

### Common mistakes

Adding the denominators as well as the numerators. Not noticing that two fractions in a story are of different wholes (a fourth of a small pizza and a fourth of a large one). Choosing to add or to subtract from a single key word and not from what happens in the story.
