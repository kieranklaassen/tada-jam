## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child solves word problems of more than one step that are set in whole numbers and have whole-number answers, with the four operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division); in some of them the child has to decide what a remainder means. The child writes such a problem as equations in which a letter holds the place of the amount to be found. The child judges whether an answer is sensible by calculating in the head and by estimating, rounding included.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads "34 children travel in cars that seat 4", writes 34 ÷ 4 = c, gets 8 remainder 2, and decides from the story that 9 cars are needed. In a problem with two steps the child says what each step finds before calculating it. Before accepting an answer, the child rounds the numbers, calculates roughly in the head, and checks that the rough result is close.

### Limits

The numbers in the problems and the answers are whole numbers, in the statement and in the cluster heading alike; fractions and decimals are not part of this statement. The statement gives no number range. The neighbouring base-ten statements of this grade multiply at most four digits by one digit or two digits by two digits, and divide at most four digits by one digit. The unknown is written as a letter.

### Common mistakes

Stopping after the first step and giving that result as the answer. Writing the remainder into the answer without asking what it means in the story ("8 remainder 2 cars"). Chaining steps with equals signs that are not true, such as 6 × 4 = 24 + 5 = 29.
