## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that angle measures add: if an angle is split into parts that do not overlap, the parts' measures add up to the measure of the whole angle. The child adds and subtracts to find angles that a diagram does not give, in practical problems and in purely mathematical ones, for example by writing an equation in which a symbol holds the place of the missing measure.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts a 30-degree paper wedge next to a 60-degree wedge with the points together and checks against the corner of a sheet of paper that the two make a right angle. Given a right angle split by a ray, with one part marked 25 degrees, the child writes 25 + x = 90 and finds 65.

### Limits

The parts of the angle do not overlap. Only addition and subtraction are used. The angles are shown on a diagram; the neighbouring statement 4.MD.6 measures in whole-number degrees. The statement names no facts about the angles of particular shapes.

### Common mistakes

Adding the given measures when the unknown is a part and subtraction is needed (25 + 90). Estimating the unknown angle by eye from the drawing and not calculating it. Not using what the diagram shows without a number: that a square corner is 90 degrees and a straight line is 180 degrees.
