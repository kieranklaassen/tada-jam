## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sees an angle as the shape made by two rays that start from one shared endpoint, and understands the ideas behind measuring angles. In this part: when the turn of an angle is made up of n one-degree angles, its measure is n degrees.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays ten-degree paper wedges side by side inside a right angle, with no gaps and no overlap, counts 9 of them, and says "nine tens, so 90 degrees". With a transparent circle marked in single degrees the child counts the one-degree steps across a narrow angle and says, for example, "30 one-degree turns fill it, so it is 30 degrees".

### Limits

The measure is stated as a count, n, of one-degree angles; the neighbouring statement 4.MD.6 measures in whole-number degrees. The statement says nothing about parts of a degree. No largest angle is stated.

### Common mistakes

Counting the marks on a scale and not the spaces between them, which gives a measure that is one too large. Treating degrees as a length along the arc, like centimetres, and not as a count of unit turns. Thinking that the measure depends on how long the rays are drawn.
