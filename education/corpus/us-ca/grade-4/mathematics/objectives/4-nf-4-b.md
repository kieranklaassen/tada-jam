## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child builds on what they already know about multiplication to work out a whole number times a fraction. In this part, the child treats a multiple of a/b as so many copies of 1/b, and multiplies on that basis. For example, a visual fraction model shows that three lots of 2/5 are six lots of 1/5, which is 6/5; the general rule is that n × (a/b) equals (n × a)/b.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child makes four groups of two 1/3 tiles, pushes the groups together, counts eight thirds, and writes 4 × 2/3 = 8/3. The child then trades thirds for whole tiles to see how large the product is: 2 wholes and 2 thirds.

### Limits

A footnote to the fractions domain (recorded with 4.NF.1) allows only these denominators in grade 4: 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, then 8, 10 and 12, and also 100. Sevenths, ninths and elevenths are not used. The multiplication is a whole number times a fraction. The product can be more than 1. The statement does not ask for the product to be rewritten as a mixed number.

### Common mistakes

Multiplying both the numerator and the denominator by the whole number (4 × 2/3 = 8/12). Adding the whole number to the numerator (4 × 2/3 = 6/3). Multiplying the denominator only (4 × 2/3 = 2/12).
