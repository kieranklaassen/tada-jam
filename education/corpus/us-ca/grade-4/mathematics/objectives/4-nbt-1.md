## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands that, in a whole number of several digits, a digit is worth ten times as much as the same digit would be worth one place further right. As an example, place value and division together show that 700 divided by 70 is 10.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child bundles ten unit cubes into a rod, ten rods into a flat and ten flats into a large cube, and says each time "ten of these make one of the next". On a place value chart the child slides a digit card 7 from the tens column to the hundreds column and says that 70 has become 700, ten times as much.

### Limits

A footnote to this domain keeps grade 4 work to whole numbers up to and including 1,000,000, so 1,000,000 is the largest number. The statement speaks of whole numbers only; it does not mention places to the right of the ones. The relation it states is between two neighbouring places (ten times).

### Common mistakes

Reading each digit as if it stood alone, so that the 7 in 700 and the 7 in 70 seem to be worth the same. Using "add a zero" as a rule without seeing that every digit has moved one place. Saying that a place is worth ten more, not ten times as much, as the place on its right.
