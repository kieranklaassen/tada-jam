## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

For any whole number from 1 to 100, the child finds every pair of factors, and understands that the number is a multiple of each factor it has. For numbers in that same range, the child decides whether one is a multiple of a stated one-digit number, and whether it is prime or composite.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes 24 square tiles and builds every rectangle that uses all of them (1 by 24, 2 by 12, 3 by 8, 4 by 6), then writes the side lengths as factor pairs. With 13 tiles only a single row works, and the child calls 13 prime. The child skip-counts by 6 to check whether 54 is a multiple of 6.

### Limits

Every number is a whole number from 1 to 100. The cluster heading asks for a first acquaintance with factors and multiples, not for mastery. The test for a multiple is against one-digit numbers only. The statement does not mention prime factorisation, greatest common factors or least common multiples.

### Common mistakes

Stopping before every pair is found, or leaving out the pair made of 1 and the number itself. Calling 1 a prime number, or taking every odd number for a prime (9, 15, 21) and denying that 2 is one. Swapping the words factor and multiple.
