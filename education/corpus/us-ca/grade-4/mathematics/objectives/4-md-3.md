## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child uses the formulas for the area and for the perimeter of a rectangle to solve problems, practical ones and purely mathematical ones alike. For example, when the length of a rectangular room and the area of its floor are known, the child finds the width by treating the area formula as a multiplication with one factor missing.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures the sides of a tabletop or of a rectangle taped on the floor, multiplies length by width for the area, and adds the four sides for the perimeter. Given a rectangle made of 24 square tiles with one side of 6, the child writes 6 × w = 24 and finds the other side. The child lays string around the edge for the perimeter and covers the inside with tiles for the area, and names which is which.

### Limits

Rectangles only; no other shape is named, in the statement or in the cluster heading, which speaks of measurement problems in general. The statement sets no number range; the neighbouring statements of this grade on multiplying and dividing give the size of the calculations (at most four digits by one digit, or two digits by two digits).

### Common mistakes

Mixing up the two measures: adding the sides when the area is asked for, or multiplying when the perimeter is asked for. Adding only the two sides that carry a label when finding a perimeter. Giving an area in plain length units and not in square units.
