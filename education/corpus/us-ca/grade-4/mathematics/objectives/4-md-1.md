## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child knows how the units inside one system of measurement compare in size; the units listed include kilometres, metres and centimetres, kilograms and grams, pounds and ounces, litres and millilitres, and hours, minutes and seconds. Staying inside one system, the child rewrites a measurement given in a larger unit as so many of a smaller unit, and sets out matching measurements in a table of two columns. For example, a foot is 12 times the length of an inch, so a snake 4 feet long is 48 inches long, and a table for feet and inches pairs 1 with 12, 2 with 24, 3 with 36, and so on.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays twelve 1-inch tiles along a 1-foot ruler and a hundred centimetre cubes along a metre stick, and says how many of the small unit make the large one. The child pours a litre of water into a jug marked in millilitres and reads 1,000. The child fills in a table of two columns, feet and inches, row by row.

### Limits

The change of unit goes one way, from a larger unit to a smaller one, and the cluster heading names that same direction; the statement does not ask for a smaller unit rewritten in a larger one. Everything stays inside one system: nothing is converted between metric and customary units. The units are introduced with the word "including", and the example uses feet and inches, which are not in the list. No number range is set.

### Common mistakes

Dividing where multiplying is needed, from the idea that a smaller unit goes with a smaller number. Using 10 or 100 for every pair of units (100 minutes in an hour, 10 ounces in a pound). Mixing up the metric prefixes, so that kilo and centi are both taken to mean a hundred.
