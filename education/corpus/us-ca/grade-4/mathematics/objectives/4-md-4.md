## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child makes a line plot that shows a set of measurements taken to fractions of a unit: halves, fourths and eighths. The child then answers questions that need fractions added or subtracted, taking the numbers from line plots. For example, the child reads from the plot of an insect collection how much longer the longest insect is than the shortest, and says what that difference means.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child measures a set of pencils or leaves to the nearest eighth of an inch, draws a number line marked in eighths, and puts one X above the line for each measurement. The child reads the longest and the shortest value from the plot and subtracts one from the other, and says how many objects share a length.

### Limits

The fractions of a unit are 1/2, 1/4 and 1/8 only. The calculations are additions and subtractions of fractions. The values on one plot can have different denominators, while the neighbouring fraction statements of this grade add and subtract with like denominators; on a line marked in eighths every value can be read in eighths.

### Common mistakes

Spacing the marks on the number line unevenly, or writing only the values that occur and leaving out the empty ones, so that distances on the plot mislead. Counting the X marks when a length is asked for (how many is confused with how long). Not seeing that 1/2, 2/4 and 4/8 are one point on the line.
