## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child solves word problems in which one amount is a number of times another, by multiplying or dividing. One way named is to draw the situation and write an equation in which a symbol holds the place of the number not yet known. The child tells a multiplicative comparison ("times as many") apart from an additive comparison ("how many more").

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child reads "A red ribbon is 3 times as long as a blue ribbon, and the red ribbon is 24 cm long", draws one bar for blue and three equal bars for red, writes 3 × □ = 24, and divides to find 8 cm. Given a second problem that says "6 cm longer than", the child says that this one needs adding or subtracting and not multiplying.

### Limits

The statement sets no number range; the cluster heading names whole numbers, so the amounts in these problems are whole numbers. The footnote that accompanies it is only a pointer to a table in the glossary of the standards; it states no limit. The statement does not say how many steps a problem has; problems of several steps are named in the neighbouring statement 4.OA.3.

### Common mistakes

Multiplying whenever the word "times" appears, even when the larger amount is given and the smaller one is asked for, which needs division. Treating "3 times as many" as "3 more". Drawing bars of unequal size, so that the picture does not match the equation.
