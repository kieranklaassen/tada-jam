## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sorts two-dimensional figures by whether or not they have parallel lines, perpendicular lines, or angles of a stated size. The child knows right triangles as a class of their own and picks them out. California adds that the shapes are to include special triangles (for example scalene, isosceles and equilateral) and special quadrilaterals (for example square, rectangle, rhombus, trapezoid and parallelogram).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child sorts a pile of cut-out shapes into hoops labelled by a property: "has a pair of parallel sides", "has a right angle", "has no perpendicular sides". The child tests corners with the corner of a card and checks parallel sides by measuring the gap between them at two places. The child pulls out every triangle with a square corner, however it is turned, and names the group right triangles.

### Limits

Figures are flat. The sorting properties are parallel lines, perpendicular lines and angles of a stated size, each as present or absent; the cluster heading likewise sorts shapes by what their lines and angles are like. The triangle and quadrilateral types in the California addition are given as examples of what to include. Among classes of triangle by angle, only right triangles are named as a category.

### Common mistakes

Not recognising a shape once it is turned: a square standing on a corner is called a diamond, and a right triangle whose right angle is not at the bottom is missed. Treating the classes as separate, so that a square is not accepted as a rectangle or as a rhombus. Calling two sides parallel by eye without checking.
