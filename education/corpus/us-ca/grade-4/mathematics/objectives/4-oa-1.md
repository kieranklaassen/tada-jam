## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reads a multiplication equation as a comparison of two amounts: for instance, 5 × 7 = 35 says that 35 is seven times as much as 5 and also five times as much as 7. The child also turns a comparison of this kind that is given in words into a multiplication equation.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays a strip of 4 cubes beside a strip three times as long, says "12 is three times as many as 4", and writes 12 = 3 × 4. On hearing "Mia has four times as many stickers as Ben, and Ben has 6", the child writes 4 × 6 = 24 and says who has more.

### Limits

The statement sets no number range; its example uses one-digit factors, as an example only. The cluster heading names whole numbers and the four operations as the ground for the problems of this group. It covers reading an equation as a comparison and writing an equation from a comparison in words. Solving word problems of this kind is the neighbouring statement 4.OA.2.

### Common mistakes

Hearing "times as many" as "more than" and adding where multiplying is meant (7 and 5 more, not 5 sevens). Reading the equals sign as "the answer comes next", so that an equation with the product on the left, such as 12 = 3 × 4, looks wrong or backwards. Mixing up which of the two amounts is the larger one.
