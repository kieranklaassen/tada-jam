## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child builds on what they already know about multiplication to work out a whole number times a fraction. In this part, the child sees that a fraction a/b is a multiple of the unit fraction 1/b: for example, a visual fraction model shows five fourths as five copies of one fourth, and the child writes this as 5 × (1/4) = 5/4.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays out 1/3 tiles one at a time, counts "one third, two thirds, three thirds, four thirds", and notices that four thirds is more than one whole. The child writes the count as a multiplication, 4 × 1/3, in the way that four 5s are written 4 × 5.

### Limits

A footnote to the fractions domain (recorded with 4.NF.1) allows only these denominators in grade 4: 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, then 8, 10 and 12, and also 100. Sevenths, ninths and elevenths are not used. This part is only about multiples of a unit fraction. The multiplier is a whole number; a fraction times a fraction is not in the statement. The result can be more than 1, as it is in the statement's own example.

### Common mistakes

Multiplying the denominator, or both numerator and denominator, by the whole number (4 × 1/3 = 4/12 or 1/12), a confusion with making equivalent fractions. Expecting the answer of a multiplication to be a whole number. Rejecting a fraction greater than 1, such as 4/3, as impossible.
