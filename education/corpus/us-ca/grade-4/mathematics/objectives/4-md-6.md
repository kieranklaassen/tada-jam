## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

With a protractor, the child measures angles and gives each size as a whole number of degrees. The child also sketches an angle when told what its measure has to be.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child puts the centre mark of the protractor on the vertex, lines the zero line up with one ray, and reads where the other ray crosses the scale. To draw an angle of 70 degrees the child draws one ray, makes a dot at 70 on the scale, and joins the dot to the vertex with a ruler. Before reading, the child says whether the angle is smaller or larger than a right angle, and uses that to pick the scale.

### Limits

Whole-number degrees only. The protractor is the tool named for measuring. The statement sets no largest size; it does not say whether angles above 180 degrees are included.

### Common mistakes

Reading the wrong one of the two scales (110 for an angle of 70 degrees). Putting the edge of the protractor on the vertex where the centre mark belongs, or not lining the zero line up with a ray. Drawing the second ray to the wrong side of the first.
