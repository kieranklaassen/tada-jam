## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands a fraction a/b whose numerator is more than 1 as unit fractions 1/b added together. In this part, the child splits one fraction into a sum of fractions that keep its denominator, does this in more than one way, writes an equation for every way, and gives a reason, for example with a visual fraction model. The examples given are three eighths split into three single eighths or into one eighth and two eighths, and the mixed number two and one eighth split into 1 + 1 + 1/8, which is eight eighths twice and one eighth more.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child takes five 1/6 tiles and groups them in different ways (1 and 4, 2 and 3, 1 and 1 and 3), writing an equation for each grouping, such as 5/6 = 2/6 + 3/6. For a mixed number the child trades each whole tile for six sixths and writes the total in sixths.

### Limits

A footnote to the fractions domain (recorded with 4.NF.1) allows only these denominators in grade 4: 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, then 8, 10 and 12, and also 100. Sevenths, ninths and elevenths are not used. Every fraction in the sum keeps the denominator of the fraction that is split. More than one way is asked for, each written as an equation. Mixed numbers are included: one of the statement's own examples is a mixed number.

### Common mistakes

Changing the denominator while splitting (5/6 = 2/3 + 3/3). Giving only the split into unit fractions and seeing no other. Doubting an equation that has the single fraction on the left and the sum on the right, because the equals sign is read as "the answer comes next".
