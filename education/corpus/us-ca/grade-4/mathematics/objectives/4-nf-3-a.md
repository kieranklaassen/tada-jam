## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands a fraction a/b whose numerator is more than 1 as unit fractions 1/b added together. In this part, the child understands adding fractions as putting together parts of one whole, and subtracting fractions as taking such parts apart.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child lays three 1/8 tiles end to end and says "three eighths is one eighth and one eighth and one eighth". The child joins two eighths and three eighths cut from wholes of one size and names the result five eighths, then takes two eighths away again and names what is left.

### Limits

A footnote to the fractions domain (recorded with 4.NF.1) allows only these denominators in grade 4: 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, then 8, 10 and 12, and also 100. Sevenths, ninths and elevenths are not used. The parts joined or separated belong to one same whole. The neighbouring parts of this standard work with like denominators only. The numerator is any number above 1; the statement does not say that the fraction has to be less than one whole.

### Common mistakes

Adding the numerators and also the denominators (2/8 + 3/8 = 5/16). Seeing 3/8 as two separate whole numbers, 3 and 8, and not as three pieces that are each one eighth. Joining parts that come from wholes of different sizes.
