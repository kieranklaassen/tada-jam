## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child multiplies in two cases: a whole number of at most four digits times a one-digit number, and a two-digit number times a two-digit number. The methods rest on place value and on the properties of operations. The child shows and explains each calculation with area models, rectangular arrays or equations, or with more than one of these.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

For 34 × 26 the child draws a rectangle, cuts it into four parts (30 × 20, 30 × 6, 4 × 20, 4 × 6), writes each partial product inside its part, and adds the four. For 4 × 2,315 the child multiplies the thousands, hundreds, tens and ones one after the other and says where each partial product comes from.

### Limits

Two cases only: up to four digits by one digit, and two digits by two digits. So the largest factors are 9,999 × 9 and 99 × 99; a three-digit number times a two-digit number is outside the statement. A footnote in the adopted document makes 1,000,000 the largest whole number expected in grade 4, and it stands on the whole domain, so it covers this statement. The statement asks for strategies that can be explained from place value; it does not name the standard algorithm for multiplication.

### Common mistakes

In a two-digit by two-digit product, multiplying only tens by tens and ones by ones (34 × 26 as 600 + 24) and missing the other two partial products. Treating a tens digit as ones (3 × 2 = 6 where 30 × 20 = 600 is meant), so that a partial product has the wrong size. Forgetting a regrouped digit, or adding it before multiplying.
