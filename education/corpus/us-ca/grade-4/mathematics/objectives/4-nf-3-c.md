## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child understands a fraction a/b whose numerator is more than 1 as unit fractions 1/b added together. In this part, the child adds and subtracts mixed numbers whose denominators are alike. Ways named as examples are turning every mixed number into an equivalent fraction, and drawing on the properties of operations and on how addition and subtraction are related.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

For 2 3/5 + 1 4/5 the child lays out whole tiles and fifth tiles, joins the wholes (3) and the fifths (7 fifths), trades five fifths for one whole, and names 4 2/5. For 3 1/4 − 1 3/4 the child trades one whole for four fourths before taking away, or rewrites both numbers in fourths (13/4 − 7/4 = 6/4).

### Limits

Like denominators only. A footnote to the fractions domain (recorded with 4.NF.1) allows only these denominators in grade 4: 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, then 8, 10 and 12, and also 100. Sevenths, ninths and elevenths are not used. A footnote to the neighbouring statement 4.NF.5 says that adding and subtracting fractions whose denominators differ is, in general, not required in grade 4. The statement does not ask for answers in lowest terms.

### Common mistakes

In a subtraction, taking the smaller fraction part from the larger one whichever number it belongs to (3 1/4 − 1 3/4 = 2 2/4). When trading a whole, taking it as ten parts, as with whole numbers, and not as the number of parts the denominator names. Leaving a fraction part of more than one whole untraded (3 7/5).
