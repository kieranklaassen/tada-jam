## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child builds a pattern of numbers or of shapes from a rule that is given, and then points out things about the pattern that the rule did not state. For example, starting at 1 and adding 3 each time gives numbers that seem to switch between odd and even, and the child says in everyday terms why that switching will go on.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child writes 1, 4, 7, 10, 13 by adding 3 each time, circles the even numbers, and says what they see. With sticks the child builds a growing row of squares from a rule such as "one more square at each step" and counts the sticks at every step. The child gives a reason in their own words, such as "adding an odd number changes odd to even and even to odd".

### Limits

The rule is handed to the child; the statement does not ask the child to work out a rule from a finished pattern. It sets no number range. The explanation is informal: no proof and no formula is asked for.

### Common mistakes

Getting one term wrong, after which every later term is wrong too. Repeating the rule ("it goes up by 3") when asked for something the rule did not say. Deciding that a feature always holds after looking at only two or three terms.
