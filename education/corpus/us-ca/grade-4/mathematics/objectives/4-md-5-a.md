## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child sees an angle as the shape made by two rays that start from one shared endpoint, and understands the ideas behind measuring angles. In this part: to measure an angle, picture a circle centred on that endpoint; the size of the angle is the fraction of the circle's arc that lies between the two places where the rays cross it. A turn of 1/360 of the full circle is named a one-degree angle, and it serves as the unit for measuring angles.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child opens two card strips joined at one end by a fastener, from closed to a full turn, and watches the opening grow while the strips stay as long as they were. The child puts the joint on the centre of a paper circle folded into equal wedges and says what fraction of the circle the opening covers. The child turns their own body through a quarter turn, a half turn and a full turn.

### Limits

An angle here is two rays from one point, and its size is a fraction of a full circle. A one-degree angle is 1/360 of the circle, so the full circle holds 360 of them. This part is about what the measure of an angle means; measuring with a protractor is the neighbouring statement 4.MD.6.

### Common mistakes

Judging the size of an angle by how long its sides are drawn, or by how large the arc mark is, and not by the amount of turn. Thinking that a larger circle around the vertex gives a larger angle. Believing that an angle changes when the drawing is turned.
