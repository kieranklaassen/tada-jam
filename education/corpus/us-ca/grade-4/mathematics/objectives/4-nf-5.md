## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child rewrites a fraction in tenths as an equivalent fraction in hundredths, and uses that step to add one fraction in tenths to one in hundredths. For example, 3/10 becomes 30/100, so 4/100 added to 3/10 makes 34/100.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child shades 3 full columns of a hundred grid for three tenths and counts that 30 small squares are shaded. The child then shades 4 more single squares and reads the total as 34 hundredths. With coins, the child trades 3 dimes for 30 pennies before joining them with 4 pennies.

### Limits

Only the denominators 10 and 100 appear, and the sum is of two fractions, one with each denominator. The cluster heading places this statement with the writing of fractions as decimals. A footnote says that children who can make equivalent fractions are able to work out ways of adding fractions with different denominators, but that adding and subtracting such fractions in general is not required in grade 4. The statement names addition; it does not mention subtracting tenths and hundredths. A footnote in the adopted document, a different one from that reported above, permits just 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6, then 8, 10 or 12, or else 100 as the denominator of a grade 4 fraction, and it stands on the whole domain, so it covers this statement.

### Common mistakes

Adding numerator to numerator and denominator to denominator (3/10 + 4/100 = 7/110). Adding the numerators without renaming the tenths (3/10 + 4/100 = 7/100). Changing only the denominator when renaming (3/10 written as 3/100).
