## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

The child reads and writes whole numbers of several digits in three forms: in digits, in words, and in expanded form. The child compares two such numbers by what the digit in each place is worth, and writes down the outcome with one of the symbols <, = and >.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

The child hears "forty thousand three hundred six", writes 40,306, and then writes 40,000 + 300 + 6. Given two number cards such as 58,204 and 58,190, the child lines them up place under place, looks from the left for the first place where the digits differ, and puts the correct symbol card between them.

### Limits

A footnote to the base-ten domain (recorded with 4.NBT.1) keeps grade 4 work to whole numbers up to and including 1,000,000. Whole numbers only. Two numbers are compared at a time, and the result is written with <, = or >.

### Common mistakes

Leaving out or adding zeros when writing a number from its name (4,306 or 400,306 for forty thousand three hundred six). Judging which number is larger from the first digit or from the largest digit, without checking how many digits each number has. Pointing the symbols < and > the wrong way.
