## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child letting one thing, action or idea stand for another. By 23 to 36 months, children make one object stand for a different object when they play pretend. Now and then they pretend with nothing in their hands, imagining the object instead of using a real one.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old holds a block to an ear as a phone, or pushes a shoe along the floor as a car while making an engine sound. The child sips from an empty hand as if holding a cup, or offers an adult a pinch of nothing and says "cookie".

### Limits

Object-for-object pretending is stated plainly; pretending with no object at all happens only sometimes. The indicator names no objects and does not mention pictures, letters or other written symbols. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Needing the stand-in to look like the real thing, so a block works as a phone but a ball does not. Forgetting the pretend meaning partway and going back to using the object in its usual way. Being upset when another person gives the same object a different pretend meaning.
