## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child getting involved in an activity and continuing with it when it becomes hard or fails. By 23 to 36 months, children spend longer working at a task in order to get good at it. When a difficulty upsets them, they find a few different ways to handle the upset, guided by a caregiver.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old tries again and again to fit the lid on a box, turning it each time until it closes. When the tower falls for the third time the child cries, goes to an adult, takes a slow breath with the adult or hugs a soft toy, and then goes back to the blocks or asks for help with the bottom row.

### Limits

Coping with distress is stated with caregiver guidance; coping alone is not stated. "Several ways" is not given a number and no ways are named. The longer time on a task is not given in minutes, and no tasks are named. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Repeating the action that did not work, with more force. Stopping or throwing the object at the first failure when no adult is near. Refusing both help and a pause, so the upset grows.
