## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child responding to caregivers and to other people and joining in with them. By 23 to 36 months, children interact with people they know and people they do not know in order to play, to work out a problem, or to tell about an idea or something that happened to them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old hands a ball to a visiting adult to start rolling it to and fro, or shows a new neighbour a scraped knee and says "I fall". The child pulls a familiar adult by the hand to a stuck drawer and says "help, stuck".

### Limits

Familiar and unfamiliar people are both named. The indicator lists three purposes joined by "or": play, solving problems, and telling about experiences or ideas. It does not say how readily a child approaches an unfamiliar person, and it mentions no caregiver support. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Speaking to a stranger as if that person already knows the child's family, places and words. Hiding or going silent with unfamiliar people although able to talk at home. Expecting any adult to respond as the child's own caregiver does.
