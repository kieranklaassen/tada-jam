## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child being able to change focus, ways of thinking and ways of acting. By 23 to 36 months, children move their attention from one thing to another and change how they behave to suit the situation. Their pretend play also becomes more complex.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old leaves the sand when called, walks to the table and speaks quietly indoors after shouting outside. In pretend play the child feeds a doll, wipes its face and puts it to bed, in that order, instead of doing one pretend action alone.

### Limits

The indicator does not name the situations or say how quickly a child changes from one to the next. "More complex" pretend play is not defined; no number of steps or roles is given. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Carrying the earlier activity's way of behaving into the new one, such as running indoors after outdoor play. Protesting or standing still when a routine changes without warning. Insisting that an object can only be used in the first way it was shown.
