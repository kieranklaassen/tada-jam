## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child getting to know particular children well by being with them again and again. By 23 to 36 months, children prefer the company of the same few children in their group. With these children their play is more complex than with others; long pretend play in which each child has a role, and movement games, are the examples.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old looks for the same two children on arriving, sits beside them at snack and asks where one is when that child is away. With them the child plays "you be the baby, I be the mummy" for a long time, or a chasing game with a start and a stop that all of them know.

### Limits

The indicator says a small number of children without giving a figure. Pretend play with roles and games with movement are examples of more complex play. It gives no length of time for the preference. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Telling other children they cannot join the play ("not you"). Being unable to settle into play on a day when the preferred child is absent. Wanting the preferred child to play only the role the child assigns.
