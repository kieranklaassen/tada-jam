## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child keeping something in mind for a short while, which the text calls short-term memory, so that it can be used to carry out a plan or reach a goal. By 23 to 36 months, children show that they can hold simple ideas or rules in mind, supported by a caregiver.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old is told "blocks go in the blue bin" and carries three blocks across the room to the blue bin, with the adult pointing once on the way. The child remembers "we wash hands before snack" and goes to the sink when the adult says it is snack time.

### Limits

Simple rules or concepts only, and with caregiver support. The indicator gives no number of rules or steps and no length of time for remembering them. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Remembering the first part of an instruction and losing the second. Forgetting the rule as soon as something else takes the child's attention. Following the rule in the place where it was learned but not elsewhere.
