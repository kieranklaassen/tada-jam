## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child growing close to particular caregivers who look after the child dependably. By 23 to 36 months, children who are away exploring make contact again now and then with the people they are attached to; the examples are a facial expression, eye contact, or a moment of joint attention in talk about plans, activities or feelings they share. If upset, they may try to get physically near those caregivers.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old climbing at the far side of the playground looks back, meets the parent's eyes, smiles and goes on, or calls "look, I up high!". After a fall, the child runs back and presses against the parent's legs or asks to be picked up.

### Limits

Reconnecting is occasional, and the ways of doing it (eye contact, facial expressions, talk about feelings, plans or activities they have in common) are examples. Seeking closeness when upset is something the child may do; it is not stated as always happening. The foundation is about certain caregivers, not every adult. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Going too far to see or hear the caregiver and then becoming frightened. Refusing comfort from anyone but the one preferred adult. Not going back to play after returning to the caregiver, when the caregiver looks worried or is busy.
