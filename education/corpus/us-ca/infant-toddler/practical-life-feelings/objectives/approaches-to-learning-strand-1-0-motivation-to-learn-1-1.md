## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child investigating the surroundings in order to find out about things, people and happenings. By 23 to 36 months, children go about exploring and finding things out in several ways, of which watching carefully and asking questions are the two named. They also look more closely at what is around them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old crouches to watch an ant carry a crumb, pokes the soil beside it and asks "what that?" or "where going?". The child opens drawers and lids to see what is inside, turns a new object over, shakes it and holds it up to an adult.

### Limits

Observing and asking questions are named as examples; the indicator sets no number of ways to explore and no list of things to explore. No length of time for paying attention is given. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Asking "why?" or "what that?" again and again without waiting for the answer. Exploring every object with one habitual action, such as banging or throwing, when the object needs a different one. Stopping at the first interesting feature and missing the rest.
