## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child's growing idea of being a separate person who also has things in common with the people he or she is connected to. By 23 to 36 months, children can tell what they feel, what they need and what interests them. At times they speak of themselves and other people as belonging to a group or groups, by mentioning a social category (gender, ethnicity and race are the examples) or a cultural practice.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old says "I sad", "I hungry" or "I like trains". The child says "I a girl, Mia a girl too" or "we eat with chopsticks at my house", or points to a picture of a family and says "like my family".

### Limits

Speaking of group membership happens only sometimes. Race, ethnicity and gender are given as examples of social categories; cultural practices are named without examples. The indicator does not state that the child uses any category correctly or completely. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Deciding who belongs to a group from one visible feature, such as hair length or clothing. Assuming every family does things the way the child's own family does. Using a feeling word that does not match, such as "sad" for tired.
