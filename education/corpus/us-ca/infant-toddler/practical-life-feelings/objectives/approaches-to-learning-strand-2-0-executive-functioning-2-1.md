## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child turning attention to an activity or to another person and holding it there. By 23 to 36 months, children can, to some degree and guided by a caregiver, handle distractions and control where their attention goes. In surroundings that are orderly and predictable, they attend with more care and can attend to two or more things at once.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old threads large beads while another child bangs a drum nearby; when the adult says "eyes on your string", the child looks back at the beads and continues. At a tidy table in a familiar routine, the child stirs batter and also listens to the adult say what comes next.

### Limits

Managing distractions is stated as "some ability" and with caregiver guidance. Attending to more than one thing is stated for a setting that is orderly and predictable; a noisy or unfamiliar setting is not covered. No length of time is given. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Leaving the task when something new appears and not returning to it. Not hearing what is said while the hands are busy. Being unable to choose where to look when many things move or make noise together.
