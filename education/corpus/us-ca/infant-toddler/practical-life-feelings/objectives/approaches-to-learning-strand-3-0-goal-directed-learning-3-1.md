## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child trying more than one approach to solve a problem or to have a need met. By 23 to 36 months, children try to solve problems in various ways; the two named are requesting help when it is needed and reusing an approach they learned before, both where they have met the situation already and where it is new to them.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old cannot reach a ball under the sofa, tries an arm, then fetches a long spoon to pull it out, as was done once before with a stick. When a jar lid will not turn, the child carries the jar to an adult and says "open".

### Limits

Asking for help and reusing a known strategy are examples; no number of strategies is set. Familiar and new situations are both named. The indicator does not name any kind of problem and does not state that the child succeeds. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Repeating one approach many times without changing it. Asking for help before trying at all, or never asking. Using a remembered approach where it cannot work, such as pulling a door that has to be pushed.
