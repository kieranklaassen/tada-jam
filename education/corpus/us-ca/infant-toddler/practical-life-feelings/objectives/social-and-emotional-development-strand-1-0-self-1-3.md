## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child showing many different feelings with the face, the body, gestures, sounds or words. By 23 to 36 months, children show self-conscious emotions, which the text also calls secondary; guilt, shame, embarrassment and pride are the examples. They show they are aware of what they feel by describing it to other people in gestures or words, or by playing it out in make-believe.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old holds up a drawing with a wide smile and says "I did it", or hides behind an adult's leg when people laugh. After knocking over another child's tower the child looks down and goes quiet. The child says "I mad" and stamps a foot, or makes a doll cry and says "baby sad".

### Limits

Guilt, shame, pride and embarrassment are examples of self-conscious emotions, not a full list. Feelings can be described in words or in gestures, or acted in pretend play; exact feeling words are not stated as needed. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Using one feeling word, often "sad" or "mad", for every unpleasant feeling. Showing a feeling with the whole body, by dropping to the floor or hiding, without any word for it. Smiling or laughing when embarrassed, which adults can misread as not caring.
