## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child's growing grasp of what other people mean to do, how they react, what they communicate and how they act. By 23 to 36 months, children at times talk about what they themselves and other people need, feel and intend, and they join other people in longer exchanges and in joint activities, such as conversations or activities that have a goal. Linked to their grasp of what others need, they also take an interest in the way people are treated and in the way things are shared out and used, turn-taking being the example.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old says "Sam wants the ball" or "she crying, she hurt", and says "I going to build a house" before starting. The child watches snack being handed out and says "he has two, me one", or tells an adult "my turn next" at the slide.

### Limits

Talking about needs, feelings and intentions happens only sometimes. Conversations and goal-oriented activities are examples of the longer interactions; taking turns is an example of how resources are used. The indicator says the child shows interest in how others are treated and how things are shared out; it does not state that the child shares or takes turns reliably. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Assuming another person wants what the child wants. Judging fairness only from the child's own share, so equal shares feel unfair when the child wanted more. Taking an accident, such as being bumped, as done on purpose.
