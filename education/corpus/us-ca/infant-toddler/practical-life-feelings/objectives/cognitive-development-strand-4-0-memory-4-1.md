## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child keeping what happened before in memory and bringing it back later. By 23 to 36 months, children know ahead of time which steps come next in a routine or activity they know well. They remember features of their surroundings and of the people there, and at times they tell about, or play out, things that happened a short while ago.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old fetches a towel when the bath water is turned off, before anyone asks. The child goes straight to the shelf where the crayons are kept and notices that a teacher has new glasses. Back from the zoo, the child says "elephant splash" or stomps around the room like the elephant.

### Limits

The routines and activities are familiar ones. The past events are recent ones, and telling about them or acting them out happens only sometimes. The indicator gives no number of steps and no length of time remembered. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Expecting the usual next step of a routine on a day when it is different, and being upset by the change. Mixing two past events into one when telling about them. Saying "yesterday" for any past time.
