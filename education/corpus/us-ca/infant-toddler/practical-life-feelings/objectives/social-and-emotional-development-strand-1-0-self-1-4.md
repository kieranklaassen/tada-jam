## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child handling feelings and reactions, telling others what he or she feels, and behaving as people expect, both when a caregiver helps and when one does not. By 23 to 36 months, children foresee that they will need comforting and try to get ready for a change from one activity or place to another. At times they control their feelings and actions with hardly any help from a caregiver, or none. They comfort themselves in many ways that vary with the situation, and they tell others which particular feeling they have.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old fetches a blanket and a soft toy before the parent leaves at drop-off, or asks "one more, then go?" before leaving the playground. When upset the child sucks a thumb, sits in a quiet corner or hums, and says "I scared" or "I miss mummy".

### Limits

Managing without much help happens only sometimes; the foundation covers acting both with a caregiver's help and without it. "Many" self-comforting behaviours is not given a number, and none are named. Preparing for transitions is described as something the child tries. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Coping with one small upset and then losing control at the next when tired or hungry. Using a comfort habit that does not fit the place, such as running off to find a blanket during a walk. Saying the feeling word only after the outburst, not before it.
