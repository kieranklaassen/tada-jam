## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child's growing grasp of numbers and amounts. By 23 to 36 months, children use number words for an amount, or in answer when someone asks them how many there are. They also go through stretches of the counting sequence, possibly with errors; the example given is a count that leaves out three.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old holds up two crackers and says "two", or answers "how many?" with a number word, which is often not the right one. Walking up steps, the child chants "one, two, three, five", leaving a number out or starting again from one.

### Limits

The indicator names no number range and does not say how far the count list goes. It asks for number words used about amounts and for parts of the count list, and says the child may make mistakes in it. It does not state that the number word used has to match the amount, and it does not mention counting objects one by one or written numerals. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Leaving a number out or saying the numbers in the wrong order. Answering "how many?" with a favourite number word, often "two", whatever the amount. Saying the count list while pointing at objects without matching one word to one object.
