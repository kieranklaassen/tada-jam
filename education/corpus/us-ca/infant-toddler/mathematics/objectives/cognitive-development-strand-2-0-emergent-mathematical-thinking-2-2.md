## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child's growing grasp of the ways objects fit into places and travel through them. By 23 to 36 months, children foresee how an object will fit and how it will move, and do not need to test each possibility in turn. They also show that they understand words for size (little, small and big are the examples), for where something is (under, on, in) or for which way it goes (down, up).

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old looks at a shape sorter and turns the triangle block to the triangle hole before pushing, instead of trying each hole. The child picks the big cup when asked for the big one, puts the bear under the blanket when told to, and lifts a car to the top of a ramp to watch it roll down.

### Limits

The size, place and direction words are given as examples, not as a list to cover. The indicator asks that the child understands these words; it does not ask the child to say them. It names no shapes, no number of pieces and no kind of puzzle. Predicting is described as not needing to try everything, which does not rule out some trying. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Forcing a piece into a hole that is close in shape instead of turning it or choosing another hole. Mixing up "on" and "under", or "in" and "on", when the object could go either way. Taking "big" to mean tall, so a wide, low object is treated as the small one.
