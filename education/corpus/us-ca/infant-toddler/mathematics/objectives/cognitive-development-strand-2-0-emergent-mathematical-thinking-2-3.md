## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child's growing ability to see how objects or people are alike and unlike, and to group objects by their features. By 23 to 36 months, children put objects into at least two groups according to how they are alike or different in a single attribute, with function, shape, size and colour given as examples. At times they give the groups a name, and the name may be stretched too far, as when every fruit is called "banana".

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old puts the red blocks in one pile and the blue blocks in another, or the spoons in one basket and the cups in another. The child may point at a pile and name it, calling all the toy animals "dog".

### Limits

One attribute at a time; the indicator does not mention sorting by two attributes together. Two or more groups, with no upper number stated. Colour, size, shape and function are examples, not a required list. Naming the groups happens only sometimes, and the indicator says the names may be too wide. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Starting to sort by one attribute and switching to another partway, for example from colour to shape. Using one familiar name for a whole group, such as "car" for every vehicle. Making a row or a picture out of the objects instead of groups.
