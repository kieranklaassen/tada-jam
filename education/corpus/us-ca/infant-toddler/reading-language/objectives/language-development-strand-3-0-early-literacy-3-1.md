## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child learning how to take part in books and other reading-related activities, in any language the child is learning, the home language among them. By 23 to 36 months, children take part in rhymes, songs, books or told stories: they listen or watch, make remarks and ask brief questions. They show they know the basics of handling a book, page-turning being the example, and they are beginning to grasp that print stands for words.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old brings a picture book, sits beside an adult, turns the pages (sometimes several at once) and points at a picture saying "what that?". During a song the child watches the singer, joins in on a word or an action, and says "again". The child points at the print on a page or on a cereal box and asks what it says.

### Limits

Turning pages is given as an example of knowing how to use a book; holding it the right way up and going from front to back are not stated. The understanding that print stands for words is called emerging, and the indicator does not mention recognising letters, naming them or reading any word. The child's questions are short. This holds in whichever language or languages the child is learning; no one language is named. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Treating the pictures as the only part that tells the story and the print as decoration. Turning many pages at once, or starting at the back. Taking any mark, including a drawing or a scribble, for writing.
