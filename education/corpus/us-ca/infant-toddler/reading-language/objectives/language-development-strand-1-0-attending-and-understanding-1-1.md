## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child's growing ability to notice the signals people give when they communicate and to learn language from being with other people; it applies in whatever language the child is learning, the home language included. By 23 to 36 months, children watch signing or listen to speech as a way of finding out about people, things, actions and ideas. They understand more and more of the words, phrases and sentences addressed to them or to someone else, including when what is talked about is not there. A note says that a blind child may follow signals that do not depend on looking, with touch and sound as examples.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old stops playing and watches an adult's face and hands while the adult talks or signs about the dog outside. Asked "where is your coat?" when the coat is in another room, the child goes to fetch it. The child hears two adults mention the park and runs to the door. A child who is blind goes still and turns toward the adult's voice, or feels the cup the adult puts in their hand while naming it, instead of watching.

### Limits

The indicator is about attending and understanding, not about speaking or signing. It sets no number of words and no sentence length. Understanding talk about things that are not present is described as increasing, not complete. A note widens how the attending may be done: a blind child may go by signals other than visual ones, with touch and sound named as examples, so looking is not the only way of attending that counts. Speech and signing both count, in whichever language or languages the child is learning; no one language is named. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Understanding a word only in the setting where it was learned, so "bath" means the bathtub at home and nothing else. Acting on the last word or two of a long sentence and missing the rest. Not following what is said when the speaker faces away or the signing is out of view.
