## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child understanding more and more words, whether spoken, signed or both, and more of the things people say, in any language the child is learning, including the one used at home. By 23 to 36 months, children know a large number of words on many subjects and can learn a new word from meeting it just once or twice. They understand what other people say when those people comment, ask, request or tell a story.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old hears "that is a whisk" once while helping in the kitchen and hands over the whisk the next day when asked. The child brings shoes when asked to, answers "where is the ball?" by pointing, and laughs at the funny moment in a short told story.

### Limits

"Many words" and the range of topics are not given numbers or lists. Learning a new word after one or two meetings is stated as understanding the word, not saying it. The indicator names comments, questions, requests and stories, and sets no length for any of them. Speech and signing both count, in whichever language or languages the child is learning; no one language is named. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Attaching a new word to the wrong thing when several new things are in view. Taking a new word too narrowly (only this one whisk) or too widely (every kitchen tool). Following a familiar request from the routine and the adult's gestures more than from the words, so the same request in a new place is not understood.
