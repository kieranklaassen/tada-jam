## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child taking turns in communication with another person, in whichever language the child is learning, including the language of the home. By 23 to 36 months, children hold conversations with turns on each side, in which their replies to the other person keep, broadly, to the subject being talked about. A note says that AAC devices may be used by children who do not yet express themselves in speech or signs, and gives communication boards, programs on a tablet and card systems as examples.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old is asked "what did you eat?" and answers "noodles"; the adult says "were they hot?" and the child says "hot, blow". The exchange goes on for a few turns about lunch before the child starts talking about a truck outside. A child who does not yet speak or sign takes the same turns by pointing to pictures on a communication board or pressing symbols on a tablet.

### Limits

The indicator says the child's replies are on the same topic "in general", so an exact answer every time is not stated. It gives no number of turns and does not say who starts the conversation. A note widens the means of taking part beyond speech and signing: a child not yet expressing themselves in either may use an AAC device (AAC stands for augmentative and alternative communication). Tablet programs, card systems and communication boards are given as examples of such devices, not as a complete list. This holds in whichever language or languages the child is learning; no one language is named. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Answering a question with something else the child is thinking about instead of the topic. Repeating the last words of the question instead of answering. Leaving no pause for the other person, or saying nothing when the turn comes.
