## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child making sounds, gestures and spoken or signed words and joining them together, in whatever language the child is learning, the home language included. By 23 to 36 months, children use words on many subjects, and someone who knows them and shares their spoken or signed language can understand them. They put two or three words together as a sentence, and they change the form of certain words, for instance to put a verb in the past, though with many errors. A note says that AAC devices may be used by children who do not yet express themselves in speech or signs, and gives communication boards, programs on a tablet and card systems as examples.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old says or signs "more juice", "daddy go work" or "my shoe off" to a parent, who understands; a visitor may not. The child talks about food, animals, vehicles and people in the family. Telling what happened, the child says "I falled" or "we goed park". A child who does not yet speak or sign hands over a picture card, points to pictures on a communication board or presses symbols on a tablet to tell the same things.

### Limits

Sentences of two or three words are the length the indicator states. Being understood is tied to a familiar person who shares the child's language; being understood by strangers is not stated. Changing the form of words is described as happening with some words and with many errors; the past tense is an example. A note widens the means beyond speech and signing: a child not yet expressing themselves in either may use an AAC device (AAC stands for augmentative and alternative communication). Tablet programs, card systems and communication boards are given as examples of such devices, not as a complete list. The note does not say how the sentence length of two or three words applies to a child who uses a device. Speech and signing both count, in whichever language or languages the child is learning; no one language is named. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Putting a regular ending on an irregular word ("goed", "foots"). Leaving out small words such as "is", "the" and "to". Using one word for a wide range of things, or a shortened form of a word that only family members recognise.
