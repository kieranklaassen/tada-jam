## Summary

_The pack's summary, not the official text._

This foundation is about a child making sense of stories and books, in whatever language the child is learning, the home language included. By 23 to 36 months, children show that they grasp, at a basic level, what a book or story is mainly about, when it is one they have met again and again.

## Design notes

_The pack's inference, not the official text._

### In a child's hands

A two-year-old picks up a well-known book and says "bear sleep" or "the bus one". Asked what the book is about, the child points to the main character on the cover or acts out the main event, such as rocking a doll to sleep after a bedtime story.

### Limits

Only a story or book the child has met many times; a new story is not covered. The understanding is basic and concerns the main idea or subject, not details, the order of events or a character's reasons. The indicator does not say how the child shows it, so words, signs, pointing and acting out all fit. This holds in whichever language or languages the child is learning; no one language is named. It describes what children typically show at this age, not a requirement on a child.

### Common mistakes

Naming one favourite picture or page as what the whole book is about. Mixing up two familiar books that have the same kind of animal or character. Reciting remembered lines without connecting them to what happens.
