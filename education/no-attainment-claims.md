---
title: The pack informs a game's design, and no game claims, shows or records that a child has reached a standard, a grade or a level
applies_when:
  - describing what a learning game teaches to parents, in ART.md or in a PR
  - designing a game that teaches counting and deciding what it shows about progress
  - adding a grown-up corner, a report or a level label to a game with a learning goal
  - reviewing a game that claims to follow a curriculum
tags: [learning-games, claims, attainment, scores, grown-ups]
---

A record says what an official framework expects, offers or works towards. It does not measure a child, and a game does not assess one. The pack is used while designing and building: no game reads it at run time and a child never sees it.

Do:

- A description says the game is "designed from" or "built on" the records it names, with their standing and check state.
- A record may shape the design: the range in its Limits, the order of difficulty, the errors the game makes visible.
- What may be said about evidence for a game's representations and mechanics is in the game-design pack rule `fade-to-school-symbols.md`.

Do not:

- No statement that a child has reached, mastered, passed or is working at a standard, a grade, a fase or a reference level. Not on the kid side, not in a grown-up corner, not in `ART.md` and not in a PR.
- Finishing a game or a level of it is not taken as meeting the record it was designed from.
- No score, percentage, tick list of standards or report per record. The jam bans scores and counters (`AGENTS.md`, Tada R15), and a grown-up corner never shows the child a score, log or verdict (`AGENTS.md`, Δ4).
- No grade, groep or level label is shown to the child or stored about the child.
- No end-of-primary goal or reference level is used to describe a younger child. Those records say what a school works towards by the end of groep 8.
- No game is described as raising attainment or as proven to teach a standard.

Reason: the records are statements about schooling, and several are addressed to the school and not to the child. Nothing in a game checks a child against them.
