  - planning the levels or the order of difficulty of a learning game
  - reviewing a game that claims to follow a curriculum

A record's `## Design notes` have three parts: `### In a child's hands`, `### Limits` and `### Common mistakes`. All three are the pack's inference, not the official text. `### Limits` holds the limits the framework itself states: the top of a range, the units, the steps, and what belongs to a neighbouring statement. `### In a child's hands` holds the pack's illustrations of the statement in use, and the numbers and objects in them were chosen by the pack.

Do:

- The range, the units, the letter set and the content boundary of a game are taken from `### Limits` of each record the game names.
- Where Limits says the statement names no range, no unit or no scale, the plan says the statement leaves it open. The value the game uses is then the game's own design choice and is labelled as that.
- Where Limits points to a neighbouring statement, that record is looked up before its limit is used.
- A game may go beyond a limit as its own choice. The plan then says which part is beyond the record.
- Where the design notes read `_pending_`, the record gives no limits yet, and the plan says so.

Do not:

- No number, unit or object from `### In a child's hands` is given as what the standard requires.
- No limit is read into a statement that Limits says the statement does not name.
- No limit is taken from a record of the other jurisdiction, or from a record for another level, without saying so.
- No scene from `### In a child's hands` is taken as what the game must show. The notes describe a child, and what a game shows on the kid side is governed by the jam's wordless rule (`AGENTS.md`).

Reason: the illustrations are written to show one concrete case, not to bound the statement. A number copied from one becomes a false claim about the standard.
