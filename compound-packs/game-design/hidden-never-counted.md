---
title: Things a child can find are hidden in the world and are never counted, listed or shown as missing
applies_when:
  - adding secrets, collectibles or things to find to a game
  - adding a shelf, album, gallery or collection screen
  - reviewing a game's plan for engagement mechanics
tags: [secrets, discovery, collection, counters, engagement-mechanics]
---

`AGENTS.md` allows hidden finds when nothing counts them or dangles them (Δ1). This rule adds what a good secret is and how to tell a find from a lure.

Do:

- A secret answers curiosity: a particular place, object or combination gives a particular result (hat on the cat, and the cat struts). It is never hinted at.
- A secret works every time, so a child can find it again and show someone.
- A found or made thing stays in the world as a thing, where the child put it. The shell is on the shelf because the child put it there.
- If some things in a scene respond to a poke, everything that looks as if it should respond does.

Do not:

- No tally, no "3 of 12", no list of targets, no album or shelf with empty outlines, no meter that fills as things are found.
- No find is announced with a fanfare or added to a list.
- No secret is released by the date or by amount of play.

The test: is there a number, a meter or an empty outline on screen that says there is more to get?

Exception: none.

Evidence: the makers of Hidden Folks cut its timed, counted areas because they threatened the relaxed feel, and found that making some things respond raised the expectation that everything does [V]. Trophies and stickers shown at decision points are classed as lures, found in 45% of the apps most used by children aged 3 to 5 [V, Radesky et al. 2022]. Applying that to an album with empty outlines is inference [I]. Detail: `research/depth-and-replay.md`, summary rule 10, section 1 (sources of depth 4 and 7) and section 5.
