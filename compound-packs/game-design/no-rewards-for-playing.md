---
title: Nothing is given, promised or shown to a child as payment or approval for playing
applies_when:
  - reviewing a game's plan for engagement mechanics
  - adding a celebration, a prize or praise to a game
  - adding feedback for a correct answer or a finished task
  - turning a lab demo that has scores, coins, tips or unlocks into a real game
tags: [rewards, praise, engagement-mechanics, motivation, celebration]
---

`AGENTS.md` already bans scores, XP, streaks and counters (Tada R15). This rule says what else counts as a reward, what does not, and how to tell.

A reward is anything the game hands over or shows because the child did the activity, and that is not the activity's own result.

Counted as rewards:

- Coins, stars, gems, stickers, trophies, badges and tips.
- Prizes, outfits or decorations earned by amount of play.
- Praise words, cheering voices, fanfares, confetti, flashes and floating words.
- A bar or meter that fills for taking part.

Not rewards:

- The result itself, in the world: the customer eats the pizza and reacts, the boat floats, the door opens.
- A character's delight or disgust at the thing the child made, inside the fiction.
- New work to do, reached by understanding and not by amount of play.

The test: is this information about what happened, or a prize for having done it?

Exception: none on the kid side.

Evidence: preschoolers promised a certificate for drawing later drew less in free play than children given none or a surprise one [V, Lepper 1973]. Expected rewards lower later interest, more so in children (d -0.28 to -0.40) [V, Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999]. The evidence is firm against promised rewards and weak against warmth: praise looks close to neutral for young children, and points raise scores on a drilled skill in the short term (`research/open-questions.md`).
