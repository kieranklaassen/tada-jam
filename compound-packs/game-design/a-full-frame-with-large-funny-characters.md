---
title: The first frame is a place filled from edge to edge, with large characters who are already funny, and the pieces the child works with stay the plainest things in it
applies_when:
  - art-directing the first frame of a game, or taking a still to show the owner
  - a game's look is called bare, minimal or unfinished while its style is right
  - making a pass for a fuller, funnier frame on a game that is already built
  - adding a setting, scenery or bystanders to a learning game
tags: [look, first-frame, setting, characters, comedy, art-direction]
---

Each jam game has its own look (`docs/art-direction.md`). This rule is about how much of that look is in the frame, and how funny it is, before a finger lands. A frame that is mostly empty page, mat, cloth or wall reads as unfinished, however right the style.

Do:

- The game is set in a place, in its own style, that fills the frame: what is behind, what is underneath, what stands and hangs around. What stands still is painted once and stamped, so it costs a few draws.
- Characters are large in the frame and have faces that do something: eyes that follow the finger, brows, a mouth with more than two shapes, a body that leans, sags and bounces. Each result the child causes gets a reaction that is a small joke, and the wrong use gets the best one.
- Something is alive before a touch that has nothing to do with the task: a bystander who watches, a pet under the table, a window with weather.
- On a first visit the one who wants something is on stage, large, from the first frame.
- Whatever in the setting looks touchable answers a touch as itself, and changes nothing in the game.
- Before a still is shown, three questions are answered in writing: what share of the frame is empty, what in it is funny, what in it is alive.

Do not:

- The pieces the child counts, fits, cuts or sorts gain nothing in such a pass: no faces, no sway, no pattern, and the plain surface under them stays (`working-objects-stay-plain.md`). The setting is lower in contrast than what can be touched and never competes with the one want of the scene.
- A fuller frame does not change the mechanic, the error, the order of challenges or what is saved, and it does not add a reward: no confetti, no cheering (`no-rewards-for-playing.md`).
- A look is not swapped for another because it was called bare. The style stays; what is in the frame changes.
- A fuller frame is not finished while it drops frames: the draw-call budget and the quality tiers hold.
- Nothing new is drawn that reads as a letter, a numeral or a sign: a star of crossed fronds, shut eyes as level bars, a dot with a tail.

Exception: where the bare surface is the thing the child works on (tar to draw on, a tray to rake), that surface stays bare and the place goes round it.

Evidence: at the toy checkpoint of the learning-games wave the owner said no to four of thirteen looks; of the set he said "some look too minimal", and of one, "Too minimal make more funny" (`docs/build/runs/look.md`). Readers of other games of that wave measured first frames at about three fifths to five sixths empty, with characters at about a tenth of the frame's width. The games were then filled out in the same style within the budget: one game went from 104 draws in its fullest frame to 63 while its page was painted from edge to edge. That the fuller frames meet his bar is inference until he has answered on them [I]. The split between a plain piece and a rich surrounding is itself inference (`working-objects-stay-plain.md`).
