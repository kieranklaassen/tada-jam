---
title: A voice never carries an instruction, and spoken words are content that belongs to an object and sounds when the object is touched
applies_when:
  - adding speech, narration or recorded words to a game
  - deciding how a game explains what to do
  - adding number words, feeling words or letter sounds to a learning game
  - making a game work in more than one language
tags: [voice, speech, instructions, language, sound, learning-games]
---

`AGENTS.md` forbids voice instructions and allows short spoken words (Δ3). This rule adds why, and where spoken words belong.

Do:

- A spoken word is attached to a thing and is heard at the moment the child touches or moves that thing: the number word on each object as it is handed out, the feeling word with the creature that shows the feeling, the first sound of a word while its object is held.
- Touching the thing again says it again. Any touch can cut it short.
- Characters speak in invented babble with clear intonation: a question, delight, a grumble.
- Every cue that matters can be seen as well as heard.
- Spoken content comes from the content pack chosen by `ctx.language`, with a silent fallback. Sound games share one design across languages, with a word list for each.

Do not:

- No "tap the bag", no "try again", no sentence that tells the child what to do.
- No spoken praise or verdict.
- No narration that must finish before the child can act.

Exception: none on the kid side.

Open: whether a letter itself may be shown saying its sound is the owner's decision (`research/open-questions.md`, item 3).

Evidence: young children do not attend to audio prompts without a visual model [S, Hiniker et al. 2015]. Children under about five often cannot follow prompts of any kind in an app without an adult [V, a later study that summarizes Hiniker]. Children grow impatient with audio they cannot interrupt [V, Sesame Workshop]. Detail: `research/learning-games-that-work.md`, summary rule 10 and section 8; `research/depth-and-replay.md`, section 4.
