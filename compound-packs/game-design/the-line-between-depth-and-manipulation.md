---
title: Nothing in a game depends on when or how long a child plays, refers to the child leaving, staying or returning, or is won by luck or by repeating something the child does not enjoy
applies_when:
  - reviewing a game's plan for engagement mechanics
  - designing what makes children come back to a game
  - turning an arcade lab demo into a real game
  - deciding how to measure whether a game is working
tags: [engagement-mechanics, manipulative-design, retention, timers, chance, metrics]
---

`AGENTS.md` lists the banned engagement mechanics. This rule gives three questions for a case that list does not name. A yes to any of them rules the feature out.

1. Does anything get better or worse because of when or how long the child plays, and not because of what they do?
2. Does anything on screen refer to the child leaving, staying or coming back?
3. Is there anything the child wants that comes only by luck, or by repeating something they do not enjoy?

Ruled out, with the similar thing that is fine:

- Daily gifts, "come back tomorrow". Fine: the world looks like the season.
- A character who pleads or sulks when the child stops. Fine: a character with feelings about what happens in the scene.
- A countdown; something that burns or expires. Fine: a process the child started that takes its natural time and stays done.
- Rare drops; a wanted result left to chance. Fine: secrets that work every time, and chance in ordinary detail (which of three yawns).
- Harder or faster rounds that arrive unasked. Fine: a harder thing the child chose. A next cycle chosen from how the last one went, never the current one, is not a round that arrives unasked (`ordered-challenges-high-success.md`).
- Content locked by amount of play. Fine: depth reached by understanding.

A game is judged by whether a child asks for it again the next week, how varied the things they make are, and whether they stop without distress. It is not judged by session length or daily return, which lead design toward these techniques.

Rewards, counted finds, the world on return, automatic next rounds and what a character may feel have their own rules in this pack.

Evidence: four in five of the 133 apps most used by children aged 3 to 5 had at least one such design [V, Radesky et al. 2022]. The pairs and the questions are a synthesis [I]. Detail: `research/depth-and-replay.md`, section 5.
