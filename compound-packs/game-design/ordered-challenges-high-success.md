---
title: Challenges come in a designed order that adds one thing at a time, most attempts succeed, and harder work is something the child chooses
applies_when:
  - planning the difficulty or progression of a learning game
  - adding levels, stages or adaptive difficulty to a game
  - deciding what a child does in week three with the same mechanic
tags: [learning-games, difficulty, progression, adaptivity, choice]
---

Do:

- The order is designed: one new object or idea at a time, then combinations of what is known.
- Most attempts succeed.
- The amount of practice changes with the child even when the order does not. Children differ very widely in how much they need.
- Only the next problem is adjusted, never the current one. What changes is the size of the quantities, the number of wrong options, how much is already built, and whether a support is shown.
- A harder option looks harder in the world (a taller mountain, a recipe with more parts), and the child picks it.
- The same mechanic deepens by reversing the question, removing a support, adding a limit, and opening a free place to build with the same pieces.

Do not:

- No level numbers, difficulty labels, map of locked stages, or any sign that the game has made things easier.
- Difficulty never comes from speed and never rises unasked inside a task.
- Age sets a starting point only (`AGENTS.md`, Tada R8).
- Free choice of level is not the only structure.

Exception: for ages 9 to 12 real difficulty is wanted (`ages-9-to-12.md`). The order still adds one thing at a time.

Evidence: across 23 evaluated math apps, a designed order of difficulty together with feedback was a necessary condition for the most effective ones [V, Outhwaite et al. 2023]. Among 70,000 players, children played longer the easier the game was, and the settings that kept them longest gave the slowest learning [S, Lomas et al. 2013], which is why the order matters and ease alone is not the aim. The adjustment details are inference [I]. Detail: `research/learning-games-that-work.md`, summary rules 7 and 13, and section 6.
