---
title: A game is lively because the child causes things and because it is funny, never because something presses the child, and the wrong use of anything works and is funny
applies_when:
  - a game feels boring, too slow or too quiet
  - designing a new game for the jam
  - turning a calm lab demo into a real game
  - deciding what happens when a child uses a tool or object the wrong way
tags: [liveliness, comedy, humor, misuse, pressure, pacing]
---

Do:

- Something is already going on when the scene opens: a character is in the middle of wanting something.
- Every object has a right use and a wrong use. The wrong use always works and is at least as funny: the boot on the head, soap in the soup, the hose on the baker.
- Things topple, spill, bounce and roll off the table, and can be picked up again at no cost.
- Things get bigger within a cycle because the child made them so, and the child can stop at any point.
- The child sets the tempo. The scene settles when the child stops.

Do not:

- Energy does not come from a clock, a countdown, rounds that speed up, something that burns or expires, or a character who hurries the child.
- A wrong use never gets a buzzer, a refusal, or no response.
- The scene does not cut, flash or race by itself while the child watches.
- The character a joke is played on is bewildered and never hurt.

Exception: a real hazard shown for a safety skill gets a clear consequence that is not frightening, not a joke.

Evidence: at two, children's humor is misusing objects, strange sayings and invented words [V, Hoicka et al. 2022, 671 children]. House House on the goose: everything "feels like 'success' when your goal is just to bother people" [V]. The owner marked five of six lively job demos "build", and all ten calm building and open-play demos he rated "no" (`lab/arcade/RATINGS.json`).
