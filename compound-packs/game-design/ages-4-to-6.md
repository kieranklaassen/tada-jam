---
title: A game for ages 4 to 6 is pretend play with characters who react, uses tap, drag and tracing, and is funny through tricks, wrong names and a victim who overreacts and is never hurt
applies_when:
  - designing a game for children aged 4 to 6
  - planning a learning game for a given age when the band covers 4, 5 or 6
  - choosing the humor, characters or gestures for preschool and kindergarten children
tags: [ages-4-6, pretend-play, role-play, humor, gestures, preschool]
---

Do:

- The game supplies a place, props and characters who react. The child supplies the plot. By week three the child is playing out characters and intentions (a terrible haircut on purpose), not learning the tools.
- Tap and drag-and-drop are reliable. Tracing and drawing are loved, and a traced line survives a lifted finger.
- Sorting, ordering and simple deduction are within reach from about four.
- Funny: everything from ages 2 to 4, plus wrong names, nonsense words, tricks, mild naughtiness, and slapstick where someone overreacts.
- Characters have routines and notice only what is in front of them, so mischief done behind their back works.
- Quantities go to 10 and then 20: dot patterns seen at a glance, a straight path of equal steps, lengths that fit. Reading starts as sound play.

Do not:

- No reading is required, and no double tap.
- No memorized riddles or wordplay. Those come at about six to eight.
- The victim of a joke is never hurt, and the child's own character is never really bad.
- No written list of tasks and no text clue, even where the game being studied has one.

Exception: none.

Open: whether a single letter that says its sound may appear in this band, and how letters are first shown, are the owner's decisions (`research/open-questions.md`, items 3 and 4). Until then the jam's wordless rule stands: no letter or written word at any age, and no numeral or symbol in a game whose age band starts below 6 (`fade-to-school-symbols.md`).

Evidence: tap and drag-and-drop get faster and more accurate between ages 3 and 6 [S, Vatavu et al. 2015]. Hoicka et al. on humor [V]. House House on the goose, who "is never really that bad", and on villagers with limited vision [V]. Riddles at six to eight are from a summary of McGhee [S].
