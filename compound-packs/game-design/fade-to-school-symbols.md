---
title: Play alone gives intuition, so a learning game fades from objects to the school's symbols, has a bridge to the school form, and is never described as raising attainment
applies_when:
  - planning a learning game for a given age
  - adding numerals, notation or written symbols to a game
  - describing what a learning game teaches to parents or in a pull request
  - deciding how a game connects to what a child does at school
tags: [learning-games, symbols, notation, transfer, claims, grown-ups]
---

Do:

- The order is object, then picture of the object, then symbol, inside the same play.
- A game whose age band starts at 6 or above may show numerals and mathematics symbols on the kid side, each laid on or beside the quantity it stands for. The symbols are the digits, the signs for plus, minus, times, divide, equals, less than and greater than, the fraction bar, the decimal mark and the percent sign.
- The band in the game's manifest decides this, never the child's age while playing.
- A strand for older children ends in the form the child will meet at school, in each country's convention (a decimal comma in the Netherlands, a decimal point in California).
- A short note for the grown-up, kept off the kid side, says what to talk about.
- A description of a game may say it is built on representations and mechanics that have evidence behind them.

Do not:

- A game whose age band starts below 6 shows no word, letter, numeral or symbol on the kid side, optional or not. The optional numeral from five that the jam's cue table once allowed is withdrawn.
- No letter and no written word on the kid side at any age. Unit abbreviations, a letter standing for a number, ordinals and currency names are words.
- No symbol before the child has handled the quantity.
- Mastering the game is not taken as mastering the school skill.
- No game is described as proven to teach, or as raising attainment or test scores.

Exception: a game whose age band starts below 6 has no symbol stage. Its order ends at the picture.

Open: whether a symbol may stand alone for ages 9 to 12, so that play depends on reading it, and whether the grown-up corner may hold a note, are the owner's decisions (`research/open-questions.md`, item 5). Until then no symbol stands alone.

In the jam: where a game's symbols are drawn, and what the wordless check enforces and cannot see, are in `docs/solutions/conventions/wordless-clarity-for-the-declared-age-band.md`.

Evidence: games taught about twice as much when paired with other teaching (d 0.41 against 0.20) [V, Wouters et al. 2013], though another meta-analysis found no difference [V, Clark et al. 2016]. Players who solved 140 equations in a game did not improve on paper equations [V, Long and Aleven 2014]; a larger trial later found gains [V, Decker-Woodrow et al. 2023]. A large independent trial of a learning app should expect about 0.07 SD [V, Gilbert's summary of Kim et al. 2021].
