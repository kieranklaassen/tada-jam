---
title: Every touch is answered on touch-down, in the same frame, with sound and motion, and the answer is bigger than the touch
applies_when:
  - adding feedback, sound or animation to a child's action
  - a game feels too slow or too quiet
  - designing the main interaction of a game
  - turning a calm lab demo into a real game
tags: [feedback, sound, motion, touch, response]
---

The jam's quality bar already asks for motion and sound on every touch, with weight, squash and follow-through (`docs/art-direction.md`). This rule adds when the answer starts, how large it is, and what the sound needs.

Do:

- The answer starts when the finger lands, not when it lifts. Young children press harder and again until they see a response.
- One touch sets off a chain: the cap pops, water arcs, the dog bolts, a puddle spreads.
- Reactions run alongside input. No animation blocks the next touch.
- Sound is dense and varied, not just louder: several variants of each sound, pitch that follows size or speed, music layers that rise with the action and fall to near silence when the child stops.
- Big moments are separated by rests.

Do not:

- A game is not made calm by shrinking its response. What is removed is pressure. The size of the response is a separate setting.
- The answer shows weight and consequence, not approval (`no-rewards-for-playing.md`).

Open: whether camera shake and a brief pause on heavy impacts may be used is the owner's decision (`research/open-questions.md`, item 1). Until then the response grows through chains, sound, and the squash and anticipation the quality bar already requires.

Evidence: Sesame Workshop's tablet guidelines say to register a touch when it lands and to give each action a sound [V]. The owner rated calm demos "No" as "too slow or too quiet"; that the cause is the size of the response is inference [I]. Detail: `research/depth-and-replay.md`, summary rule 8 and section 6.
