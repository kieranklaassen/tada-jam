---
title: The world keeps what the child made and is found exactly as it was left, with no decay and no remark about the time away
applies_when:
  - deciding what a game saves and what a child sees when they come back
  - adding a home, garden, shelf or village that fills up over time
  - adding pets, plants or anything that needs care
  - reviewing a game's plan for engagement mechanics
tags: [persistence, saved-state, returning, world, care, engagement-mechanics]
---

`AGENTS.md` already requires a lossless exit and no punishment for leaving, and allows a world to mirror the season (Δ1, Δ2). This rule adds what the world remembers and what a child sees first on return.

Do:

- The world is small, bounded and the child's own. It shows the last few things the child made or placed, where they put them.
- On return everything is as it was left. A creature that wandered off while the child watched comes back with one tap.
- The world may look like the real season or time of day.
- What fills up over time is a record of what the child did (a garden, a shelf). It is not a currency, is never spent, and never ranks.

Do not:

- Nothing wilts, gets dirty, goes hungry, is tidied away or is lost while the child is away.
- The first thing on return is never a chore, a loss, or a remark about the absence in any form: a sad pet, weeds, a character that missed the child.

The test: is the first thing the child sees on return a chore, a loss, or a remark about being away?

Exception: none. A world that remembers is the same part appointment games are made of. The only difference is whether the remembered state can go bad.

Evidence: Gingold reads Miyamoto's "miniature garden" as a small, complete world that feels safe to experiment with [V]. Eguchi describes both halves of Animal Crossing: a town a family shares, which is the model, and upkeep that "pretty much requires that you come", which is left out [V].
