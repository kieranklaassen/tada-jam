---
title: The objects a child works with where the learning happens stay plain, and the charm goes around them, never on them
applies_when:
  - art-directing the pieces, counters or tiles of a learning game
  - adding decoration, animation or side activities to a learning game
  - choosing a visual style for a game that teaches a school skill
tags: [learning-games, visual-design, manipulatives, decoration, art-direction]
---

Every jam game has its own look and is alive at idle. In a learning game that life belongs to the characters, the setting and the result. The pieces the child counts, fits, cuts or sorts are plain.

Do:

- A working piece shows the one property that matters (its length, its number of units) and nothing that distracts from it.
- Working pieces sit on a plain surface of a contrasting hue.
- Animation and sound that match the content are welcome: the word acted out, the sum playing out.
- Characters, the setting and the result scene hold the look and the comedy.

Do not:

- Working pieces are not made to look like everyday things, and have no faces, patterns or idle motion that are not part of the idea. The jam's glow on what can be touched still applies.
- No tappable extras, side games or decorations inside the working area.
- Realism is not added for its own sake.

Exception: a piece may be a character when its body is the idea, as when a creature's height is its number. Then the body is the plain property and nothing else about it varies.

Evidence: schematic games taught more than realistic ones (g 0.48 against -0.01), and more visual and story dressing went with less learning [V, Clark et al. 2016]. In story apps, animation that matches the story helps, and tappable extras and side games hurt [V, Takacs et al. 2015]. Plain manipulatives transfer better than perceptually rich ones [S, Carbonneau et al. 2013]. No study tested a plain piece inside a richly drawn scene, so the split between piece and surroundings is inference [I].
