---
title: The representation of a school idea is chosen before the game is designed, and its physical structure matches the idea
applies_when:
  - choosing how a math or reading skill appears on screen
  - planning a learning game for a given age
  - choosing the objects, board or model for a number or science game
tags: [learning-games, representation, manipulatives, number-line, models]
---

A representation teaches when the child can see in the objects why something worked. It is picked first, from those with evidence and those children meet at school, and the game is built around it.

Do:

- The idea is visible in the object. A number is a length that fits a gap or does not; it is not a label on a balloon.
- The shape matches the idea. Number order is a straight path of equal steps, counted on from where the piece stands.
- An estimate is followed by the true answer shown beside it.
- Ratio starts with continuous amounts (pouring, mixing), not countable pieces. The size of a fraction is shown on a line or strip, not only as cut areas.
- A science model is true wherever it claims to be science.
- The choice is looked up by skill and age in `research/learning-games-that-work.md`: the tables in section 4, and the table at the end (school skill, representation, mechanic, example game, age band).

Do not:

- No representation chosen for charm, or because the game idea came first.
- No circular or winding track for number order.
- No change shown as science that is not real.

Exception: where those tables mark a line [I] or cite no trial (place-value blocks, the area model for multiplication, the balance for equations), the representation is school practice without a trial behind it, and the plan says so.

Evidence: about an hour on a straight number board improved preschoolers' sense of number size, and the same game on a circular board did not [V, Siegler and Ramani 2009]. Placing fractions on a line and then seeing the true position gave large gains in under 15 minutes; the same estimates without that feedback gave none [V, Fazio et al. 2016]. Detail: `research/learning-games-that-work.md`, summary rule 2 and section 4.
