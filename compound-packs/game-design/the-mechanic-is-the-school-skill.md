---
title: In a learning game, what the child's finger does at the moment of decision is the school skill itself, expressed in the game's own objects
applies_when:
  - planning a learning game for a given age
  - choosing the mechanic for a math, reading or science skill
  - turning a school standard into a game idea
  - reviewing whether a learning game teaches what it says it teaches
tags: [learning-games, mechanic, school-skill, quiz, attention]
---

Whatever the child must look at and think about in order to succeed is what the child learns. So the skill is the play itself. It is not a question the child has to answer in order to get back to playing.

Four questions, answered in the plan before building:

- Swap: could the content be replaced with another subject without changing how the game plays? If yes, the content is attached and not built in.
- Attention: at the moment of decision, what must the child look at and think about? If the answer is where to aim or when to tap, the game teaches aiming or timing.
- Fun: is the skill used in the most enjoyable moment of play, or does play stop for it?
- Guess: can the child succeed by tapping at random or by trying every option?

Do not:

- No question that pauses the game, and no "pick the right one among several" with a theme added.
- No extra demands (aiming, timing, finding the way) that take attention from the idea.
- In a game about feelings, no "which face is sad?". The child reads the feeling from how a creature behaves in order to do something kind that works.

Exception: none. The evidence is thin, and this is a design position, not an established finding.

Evidence: in the one direct experiment, children who divided numbers as the fighting move scored 58% on a delayed test, against 41% with the division moved into quizzes, and chose the built-in version about seven to one [V, Habgood and Ainsworth 2011; 58 and 16 children, one school]. A small study with adults went the other way [V, Nidd and Muldner], and a meta-analysis found no advantage [V, Clark et al. 2016].
