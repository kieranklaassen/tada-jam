title: For children under ten a character shows a new idea once, without words, before the child tries it, and for ages 9 to 12 the child may try first and is then shown a neat way to compare
applies_when:
  - introducing a new idea, tool or rule in a learning game
  - deciding how much a game shows a child and how much it leaves the child to find
  - designing a science or experiment game
  - adding puzzles to a game for children aged 9 to 12

The jam's guidance ladder covers what happens when a child is idle. This rule covers the moment a new idea arrives.

Do:

- Under ten: a character in the scene does the new thing once, inside the fiction, with no words. Then the child does it. Any touch ends the showing.
- Under seven: an attempt that fails is small, local and fixed in a moment.
- Ages 9 to 12: the child may be given a problem to try their own way first. A character then shows a neat way, and the child can compare the two. The second step is required.
- In a game about fair tests: a muddled comparison is shown giving a misleading answer, a character runs one clean comparison, then the child takes over.
- What is shown is a move in the world. It is never a spoken or written instruction, and never the answer to the problem in front of the child.

Do not:

- A young child is not left alone in an open space to find the idea.
- A demonstration does not play again unasked once the child is acting.

Exception: free play with no target idea needs no showing.

Evidence: discovery without help loses to explicit teaching (d -0.38), and discovery with feedback and examples wins (d 0.30) [S, Alfieri et al. 2011]. After being shown, 77% of third and fourth graders learned to change one thing at a time, against 23% left to discover it [V, Klahr and Nigam 2004]. Trying before being taught helps older students, but in grades 2 to 5, which reach age 11, the effect favored teaching first [V, Sinha and Kapur 2021]. So trying first is unproven for nine- and ten-year-olds, and the line at ten is a judgment [I]. Detail: `research/learning-games-that-work.md`, summary rule 8, section 4 (science) and section 5.
