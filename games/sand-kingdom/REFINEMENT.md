### How the game meets the quality bar

- **Alive at idle.** The sea laps and its foam travels in, the glitter shifts, the clouds drift, the grass sways, and each of the six sea folk has its own idle moves at its own tempo (`folk.test.ts`). By day each shows its one want at its place: the crab walks a ring with nothing in it, the starfish stretches up on its rock and plops, the eel threads its rock, the gulls shuffle for room on their log.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** Every cell of the grid has its own change and its own sound, held one by one in `grid.test.ts` (26 tests); the bucket, the sun, each of the folk, the sea, the rocks, the sky and the gulls' log answer as the sheet says. `sync.test.ts` plays three whole sessions and holds that each sound falls in the frame of its motion and that nothing sounds for a thing that was taken away.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** The bucket hops, swings its handle, lifts off its tower in an arc and lands with a squash; a shell squashes when pressed and skitters when shot out; driftwood rocks like a see-saw and floats with a lag; the sand rim rises with the dent; a stack of three comes down in one long slump.
- **Kid-clear silhouettes and tappables.** Round folk on faceted rocks, a sea-green bucket and pastel shells against orange sand. The touch is taken from the drawn shape; each thing a finger must find answers over at least 100 logical pixels at 1024 by 768 and at 1180 by 820, none lies in the bottom strip on a first visit, nothing answers under the home control or in the top right corner, and the folk at rest stand clear of the things (`frame.test.ts`, `layout.test.ts`, `overlay.test.ts`, `hand.test.ts`). A finger shapes the sand to the bottom edge of the frame on every surface (`reach.test.ts`).
- **Wordless clarity for the band, youngest age 3.** No word, letter, numeral or sign on the kid side and no `symbols.ts`; one activity, one tool that works at a tap, and the consequence of every choice shown at the place by the water and the folk. The wordless check passes.
- **Wordless idle guidance.** After a rest a glow lies on open sand clear of the things, and later a ghost hand with one finger out draws one stroke there and digs nothing; a working finger, a scene and a travelling sun all keep the ladder down (`guide.test.ts`, `guidance.test.ts`).

### What the next builder should know

- **Play a long frame as short steps.** The attended clock hands over frames of up to a tenth of a second. A spring stepped that far at once flies off for good, and the thing it carries is gone from the picture with its shadow left behind. The model here splits a frame into steps of a fiftieth of a second. A test that plays every poke at a tenth of a second a frame would have caught it on day one.
- **The cold playtest can be stepped a tenth of a second a frame** where drawing is slow: a minute of play is then six hundred frames, and it finds the bug above for free.
- **The audit takes its objects from `userData.jamObject`.** A character built of several meshes needs the name on its group, or its own parts are counted against each other.
- **Moments start through the game's own start-from hook** (`beach=` in the address, read in `beaches.ts`), never from a saved slot.
- **A height field in the vertex shader** is cheap and looks right, and the audit cannot see it; overlap with the sand is the model tests' work.
- **Hold the shapes to the meshes.** The game keeps things apart with plain shapes (rounds on the sand, the height of a top), and three audit runs each found a place where a mesh stood outside its shape: a rock at a big rock's foot, a pebble, a starfish spread flat, a bucket handle wider than its patch. `solid.test.ts` builds the two views and holds every mesh inside its shape, and would have found each on day one.
- **An allowance is for a contact made on purpose, at the depth it has by design.** A cap set a little above what a run printed excuses the next pass-through. Where a reason said "brushes" or "slides over" and the depth was a quarter of the piece, the motion was wrong: the fix was a lift, a step round, or a rest on top, in the model, with a test that holds the clearance (`clear.test.ts`).
- **Pin the seed in the audit's address** (`seed=` beside the tier): a finding can then be played again, before and after its fix.
- **The folk share one random stream.** A change in how one of them draws from it shifts all the others, and tests pinned to a seed then fail for no reason of their own. Make every change of behaviour first, then find the seeds again, then take each fix out once more on the final code: a test that leans on chance can stop holding a fix after a later change.
- **Nine sentences of the sheet were no longer the whole truth after the last close**: pairs 18 to 26 said so and are in the sheet now (the status block lists them). The older note follows.
- **Three sentences of the sheet were no longer the whole truth**: pairs 15 to 17 said so and are in the sheet now. In the folk's table: the eel goes under driftwood and comes out the other side only where the water beyond is long enough for it, and otherwise comes back out the way it went in; the starfish on the tallest tower lies with two arms parted round the bucket where the bucket stands by, and leaves alone a tower the bucket stands right against; the gulls take no wall within reach of the starfish's arms on its tower. The look's part on motion, below the sheet, already says each of these.
- **Step a model check as the audit steps.** The audit's clock draws a frame every 16 ms and takes a sample every 33, runs on for three frames after each load, and plays its first moments one after another on one page. A check that takes one step of 0.033 s for each sample, on a fresh page for each moment, sees another game: it missed two crossings the audit found.
- **One mesh for one allowance.** A rule written for the eel in its rock, on a mesh that held every rock, also covered the eel in the small rocks beside its own. Give the piece an allowance is for a mesh of its own.
