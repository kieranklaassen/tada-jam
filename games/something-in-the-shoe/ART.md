# Design sheet

Written before any game code, and checked by someone who did not write it before the game is built on the toy. The look follows the sheet at the end of this file.

## The band and its age rule

The manifest band is 3 to 6. Three governs: the cue table's row for 3 to 4 applies with its "Avoid" column (no text, numeral or icon, no spoken instruction, no verdict, one next act offered at a time). Everything essential is a tap; a drag is an extra way and counts when partly done. Targets are about 100 units or more and never closer than 95 units centre to centre. `ctx.childAge` changes nothing: three-year-olds and six-year-olds get the same room, so no age given is the same as any age.

## The toy

Tap a thing in the basket: it squashes and sounds in the same frame, leaps in an arc over the room and drops into the giant boot with its own thump, and the boot bulges. A bare foot hangs over the boot with its toes wiggling. The foot goes in, everything stops for a moment while the owner's eyes grow, and then the owner goes off. The thing comes out, hops back into the basket, and the boot is empty again. Dropping a thing into a deep boot, and seeing what it does to a foot, is a pleasure with nothing to achieve. Tickling the bare toes makes the owner giggle.

## The object-by-action grid, and what is new on day 15

Seven things: mandarin, carrot, biscuit man, frog, mouse, porridge, bell. Three are the proper treats of the evening and four are not; all of them work and none is better. Alone in the boot:

| Thing | What the foot finds | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mandarin | juice fountains out of the top and falls into the owner's open mouth, which closes on an orange grin | squelch, pour, slurp |
| carrot | its tip pokes out through the toe of the boot; a kick sends it up and into the mouth | pop, whirl, crunch |
| biscuit | three crunching stamps, a cloud of crumbs, crumbs falling on a licking tongue | crunch, patter |
| frog | the boot hops off with the owner in it, along the room and back, and the frog ends on the owner's head | boing, croak |
| mouse | a lump runs up the leg and over the head while the owner squirms and giggles | scurry, squeak |
| porridge | the boot swells, porridge shoots out of the toe and lands on the owner's face, which licks itself clean | gurgle, splat |
| bell | every step tinkles, and the owner cannot stop dancing | bell |

Two things in one boot do something together, one result for each of eleven kinds of pair: frog and mouse chase each other round the owner's head; frog and porridge hop splattering; frog and bell leap three times as high and ring; a frog that meets a treat eats it and comes out fat; a mouse eats its way through the porridge and out of the toe, and the owner's toes follow it through the hole; a mouse steals a treat over the owner's head until the mouth catches it; a mouse wears the bell; porridge fires the bell up out of sight until it comes down on the owner's head; porridge fires a treat into the owner's mouth; the bell bounces a treat on the foot; two treats come out stuck on the toes. A third thing makes the first one jump back out.

Wrong uses work: a thing dropped on the owner sits on the head, is looked at cross-eyed, and is sneezed off. A thing dropped on the floor bounces home. Every owner has a different jump, so each row above looks different on each of five owners.

Day 15: what two things do together on each owner, whose favourite is what, and filling five shoes at night so that five people go off in the order the child laid.

## The representation

No school idea. The room is the evening shoes are set out and treats are found in them, with the child as the one who fills the shoe. No saint and no helper of any kind appears.

## The four mechanic questions

- **Swap.** No school content, so there is nothing to swap.
- **Attention.** At the moment of choosing the child thinks about what this thing will do to that foot.
- **Fun.** The choice is the fun: what goes in is what goes off.
- **Guess.** Any tap works; there is no success to guess at.

## The error as a consequence

There is no wrong attempt. A thing that misses the boot bounces back to the basket.

## The designed order, and what is stored

No ladder. The room opens one thing at a time: first one boot and one foot; after the first foot has gone in, the next owner looks round the edge at the left; the sun in the window is there from the start and goes down when it is touched. The next owner is always the next in a fixed order of five, so no position lays anyone out.

Stored: the version, the owner, what is in each of the five shoes, day or night, whether the whole round has been shown, and whether a foot has gone in yet. A thing in the hand is stored in the basket. A round's outcome, the shoe empty again, is stored when the foot starts down.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

Each one's want is the same and always visible: the bare foot over the shoe, looking at it. Tastes never change and show as a longer, rounder bliss at the end: the grandmother (mandarin, jumps straight up and comes down slowly under her skirt), the bear (porridge, heavy, spins), the goose (bell, many small flapping hops), the elephant (mouse, rises like a balloon and lands so that the basket jumps), the white horse (carrot, rears). The victim is bewildered and delighted and never hurt. A taste changes nothing the child can do.

## The scenes

- **The whole round, shown once.** On a first visit the frog hops into the boot by itself, the foot goes in, the owner hops off and back, and the frog goes home. About six seconds. Its mark is stored when it starts. Any touch ends it with everything in place.
- **A foot goes in.** Caused by the child's thing in the boot, two and a half seconds after the last thing landed and no finger is down, or at once on a touch of the foot or the boot. The foot sinks and trembles meanwhile. Four to five seconds, and a thing touched meanwhile waits its turn and leaps in afterwards.
- **Night and morning.** Touch the sun: it goes down, the owners are five sleeping heads in one long bed, and five shoes stand in a row. Things go into the shoe they are dropped in, or on a tap into the next empty one; a touched shoe tips out. Touch the moon: the sun comes up, five feet go in one after the other along the row, everyone goes off with what they found. An empty shoe lets out a puff of dust and a moth. The shoes' emptying is stored when the sun starts up. A touch after the last foot is in ends it. Then the room is the one boot again with the next owner over it, waiting.

A finished round stays as long as the child likes. Nothing starts by itself but the foot, which is the answer to the thing the child put in.

## The records

No learning goal, so no records and no claim.
