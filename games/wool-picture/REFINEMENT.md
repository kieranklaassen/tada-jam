- **For the template:** the lessons a second run is given could come as a test file to fill. `lessons.test.ts`, `found.test.ts`, `viewRead.test.ts` and `docs.test.ts` here are named by lesson, and most of `docs.test.ts` (no text call, no test that writes, no machine path in a document, each proposed pair found once) would hold for any game as it is.
- **For the template:** `guidance.ts` shows two presses for a tap from a band that starts at 4, and the comment in `config.ts` asks the builder to list what a second tap does. This game found two harms only on its sentence-by-sentence reading. A test that taps twice wherever the hint is a tap would find them sooner.

### For the owner to decide

The nearest things in the game to praise, blame, a count, a prompt and a punishment come first, in that order.

- Nearest to praise: when laid wool wakes as a thing, all four figures turn to look at it, and for ten of the fifteen kinds the one figure that is drawn to such a thing is heard once in its plain voice as it looks (the sheep at a hill or a bush; the goose at a pond, a stream, wind or a cloud; the shepherd at a fire or a trunk; the pig at earth or corn). Nobody hops or sounds glad. Set on a place it is drawn to, a figure does its act and is heard doing it (the sheep tears and munches, the goose splashes), with no voice of its own. The one glad sound left is the giggle of a figure the comb tickles, which is about the comb. A child may still take the voice at a waking, or a figure that grazes on the hill they laid, for "well done".
- Nearest to blame: what a figure does in a place that does not suit it. The shepherd under a cloud sits down wet with a sniff and a drip off his nose; the sheep under a sun shuts its eyes and turns its back to the sun; the goose hisses at a fire. No figure has a cross voice, a pleased one or a mood of disliking, nothing is undone, and nobody looks at the child.
- Nearest to a count: three pegs that fill one by one, and a tuft that visibly thins as it is spent. A sneeze comes three times and a shake two or three, and then stops. Nothing is numbered, and nothing happens when the wall is full except the last point of this five.
- Nearest to a prompt: the idle ladder. A glow after 3 seconds and a ghost hand after 5, at most four times with longer and longer gaps, then nothing. The hand shows laying wool, or a figure carried onto a living thing. Once in a visit it points at the comb, at the first showing after wool lies on the felt, and once in a visit, as the fourth and last showing only (84 idle seconds in, with a thing alive and a figure in the picture), it takes the loop to a peg, which is the game pointing at its own ending. Once in a visit the comb wriggles on the table by itself, a moment after the first thing wakes. The hand shows a bowl before it ever shows a figure.
- Nearest to a punishment: when a fourth picture is hung, the goose flies up, unhooks the oldest and tucks it behind the easel, and that picture is gone for good. It is seen and heard, and it is the one thing in the game a child cannot get back. Whether a picture may go at all, whether three is the number, and whether the goose is the one to take it are the owner's call.
- A picture is hung only by dragging the loop to a peg and letting go there (within 30 units of the peg, or past it); a tap or a shorter drag lets the loop swing back. A hung picture cannot be taken down to go on with. Is 30 units right for a small hand, and should a hung picture be able to come back to the easel?
- With the comb chosen, a touch on bare felt lays wool from the bowl last chosen and the comb goes back to its place; only a touch on wool combs. The comb's dust and standing nap on bare felt are now seen only when the comb is drawn on from wool or carried from the table.
- A palm that lies still on the felt for a second gives way to the next finger. So does a finger that has laid wool and then waits a second in one place.
- Which cloud rains: any cloud with wool more than one level thick, so one tap of a handful rains and a cloud combed to its last level does not. The other way is that every cloud rains, thinly when it is thin.
- A first visit opens on a bare sheet, as the sheet says. Should a wisp already lie there on the first visit?
- The look: wool veils on a dark sheet in a dusk-purple room. Is it his, and is the sheet too dark?
- Each living thing carries a few stitched lines (rays round a sun, grass tips on a hill, ripples on a stream) so that kinds of one dye can be told apart in a still. The first run's guide said nothing on the sheet is drawn as the thing it becomes. Are the lines right, or too much drawing?
- A figure does a thing two or three times and then goes quiet (a sneeze, a shake, a bump, the hat blown off, the roll downhill). The other way is for it to go on for as long as it stands there, with its sound each time.
- How much the laid wool moves: from 12 px (earth) to 70 px (wind) at 1180 by 820.
- The four figures at these sizes and in these characters. The shepherd is a person; the others are animals.
- The sounds, once somebody has heard them.

### The cold playtest proxy

This is a script, not a child. It was written by the builder, who knows the game, to do what a child of four who has been told nothing might do, in that order, and it read the surface back as pixels. It cannot be puzzled, bored or delighted. It ran on the dev page, since no production build was allowed in this run.

The first thirty seconds:

1. It looks for 1.9 seconds. The tuft on the green bowl sways, the figures in the dish move, the moth moves a little. Nothing on the felt.
2. At 4.7 seconds the tuft on the green bowl glows. At 7.4 seconds the ghost hand has laid a green line low on the felt.
3. It puts a finger on the felt. Wool is under the finger 120 ms later, at the first read. It scribbles for five seconds; the middle of the felt is green, and a moment after the finger lifts the wool is moving by itself.
4. It pokes the sheep, the pig and the moth. Each answers within a quarter of a second; the moth leaves its corner.
5. It taps the yellow bowl and the yellow tuft stands up. It taps high on the felt and yellow lies there.
6. It carries the pig onto the felt and lets go. The pig stands where it was let go, and its place in the dish is empty. That is 22 seconds.

Then to a hung picture: it waits. The ladder now points at the loop (a glow from 3 seconds, the hand from 5); the script read the loop's box at 3.7 and at 6.3 seconds and it had changed between the two. It drags the loop to the first peg; the frame swings up, the room darkens, the picture hangs on the first peg, and the pig is back in the dish. It waits; the felt's back glows (the glow is in still `4-small-found-as-left`). It taps the felt and a bare sheet is there in daylight with the green tuft up. From the first look to the fresh felt: 50 seconds.

### What the next builder should know

- Stamp the wool by cell, level and way, never by a random draw at the time of the stroke: then a save of the cells alone brings back the same strands.
- A shape drawn twice to seem to run doubles the draw count on a full sheet. Cap it by tier from the start.
- Put every line a kind carries into one path for each dye and stroke it once: fifteen kinds of marks cost five draws.
- Answer when the finger lands, but change a setting only when the touch ends as a tap. A finger that lands on a thing on its way elsewhere should not change it.
- A thing that sits on what the child made will cover it. Give it a perch beside the work.
- Read the surface as pixels from the page when stills are short: a mean colour of a box, and how much it changed since the last read, shows that a touch was answered without a picture.
