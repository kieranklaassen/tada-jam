## For the pull request

Written as the game is built and kept at the end of this file: the pull request is made from it.

### How the game meets the quality bar

- Alive at idle: the fox, the five who wait, the goose, each obstacle and each neighbour move with nothing touched, each in its own way (`wayside.test.ts`: "moves every neighbour by itself", "are each large, and already alive at rest").
- Motion and sound on every touch: a press runs the fox on the frame it lands, with a voice; a lift stops it dead, with a voice; a press on a load takes it on that frame; a press on anyone who watches pokes it. Every cell of the grid has a motion and a voice of its own, on one clock (`game.test.ts`, `voices.test.ts`).
- Weight, squash and follow-through: plaques are stiff, so weight is in springs: the fox gives as a whole on a start, a stop and a landing, its tail hangs from a spring, the goose's neck follows her, and loose beads fly from every stop and tumble.
- Kid-clear silhouettes and tappables: every character is outlined in the dark bead and the setting is not; the five loads stand alone on the shelf against the sky, clear of the home control and of everyone who waits. Every load and each of the five is 48 pixels or more each way as drawn at 1180 by 820, 1024 by 768 and 820 by 1180, and a tap goes to what is drawn under it (`game.test.ts`).
- Wordless clarity for ages 6 to 10: no word, letter, numeral or sign is drawn, and there is no `symbols.ts`. One want at a time: a load at the shelf, the run on the trail, the next lap after the hand-over. Shapes that could be misread are listed under "Reader".
- Wordless idle guidance: after three seconds a pool of light behind each load (or under the fox); after five the ghost hand taps a load, or on the trail presses and stays down while the fox jogs on the spot, and on later stops slides, flicks up and flicks down in turn; when the load lies where only a roll gets it back, it shows the roll at once. The hand moves at a pace the game takes: its own path, fed to the steering as a finger's, is a lane, a jump and a roll (`game.test.ts`). It taps once.
- Frame rate: not measured, and none is claimed. Counted with no renderer (`budget.test.ts`): ten draws at every tier; at the most 910 triangles at tier 0, 820 at tier 1, 742 at tier 2 and 670 at tier 3. Seen in the stills by the renderer's own count: 7 to 11 draws and 670 to 830 triangles. No shadow maps, no post pass, pixel ratio capped at 2, four tiers, the grown-up frame-rate overlay from the template.
- Procedural assets only: every plaque, picture and sound is made in code. No web address is in the folder (`meta.test.ts`).
- Nothing passes through anything: `clear.test.ts`, and the audit script at `enforce: true` with no allowance.
- Found as left: put away at any instant, the game is what a load of its save shows, to the last plaque, and nothing plays on load (`game.test.ts`).
- No engagement mechanics: no score, timer, count, streak or reward. Speed is the child's: the fox runs at one speed while the finger is down and stops dead when it lifts, for as long as the child likes. Any finger runs it and any other finger steers it, so a thumb left resting on the glass is no fault.

### What was measured, and on what

Nothing was timed. The stills, the cold playtest and the audit (176 of its 237 moments; the other 61 were written in passes 5 and 6 and have never been run) were made on a machine with no graphics card, on a paused clock stepped by a script, with `Math.random` seeded. The frame budget is counted from the model. A frame rate on a tablet is still to be measured by the lead with the shared probe.

The game has no learning goal and makes no learning claim.

### What the next builder should know

- On a machine with no graphics card a frame of this game takes a second or more to draw. Stills are taken with the paused clock stepped 100 ms at a time; the game runs in fixed ticks, so the stills come out the same as at 16 ms.
- The dev server reloads the page when a source file of the game changes. Change nothing while a run of stills is being taken.
- A fox seen from behind hides what is right in front of it. Whatever is funny has to happen beside the fox, above it, or after it has been thrown clear. From the high eye, what is ahead of the fox is drawn above it, which is where a load over its head is.
- Anything placed towards the child from the fox is off the bottom of the frame out on the trail. The ghost hand was lost that way until pass 4.
- A spring's `kick` takes how far it should swing, not a speed.
- What happens as the fox passes a thing is behind it, under the bottom of the frame, about a third of a second later: the goose on her back with her feet up is in the frame for some twenty frames after the bang. A still of it needs the fox stopped short of her, and the last still of pass 6 missed her for that reason.
