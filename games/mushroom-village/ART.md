# Design sheet

Written before any game code, and checked by someone who did not write it before the game is built on the toy. The headings stay in this order. The look follows the sheet at the end of this file.

What each heading asks for is in the section "The design sheet" of `docs/solutions/conventions/building-a-jam-game.md`.

## The band and its age rule

The manifest band is 3 to 6, and its youngest age, three, governs the design.

- **The cue-table row.** The row for a youngest age of 3 to 4 in the wordless-clarity convention. Its "Avoid" column binds the game: no text, numeral or pictorial icon that has to be decoded, no spoken instruction, no verdict, never several activities live at once, and no tool on screen before it means something. So the door cart is not in the glade until a mushroom stands there, and the ladder offers one next act at a time.
- **The pack's rule for the range.** At three the rule for ages 2 to 4 holds (pack: game-design, ages-2-to-4.md): one loved action offered again and again, every touch answered, no dead end, no double tap, targets about 100 units across and well apart, nothing essential in the bottom strip. From four the rule for ages 4 to 6 adds what the older child does with it (pack: game-design, ages-4-to-6.md): the game supplies a place, props and characters who react, and the child supplies the plot.
- **What works with a tap, and what asks for more.** The core touch is a press that is held, and a press that is not held still works: a tap on the moss gives a small mushroom. A tap on a door on the cart sends it to the nearest mushroom that has none, a tap on the sun sends it down or up, and the sleepy gnome needs no path, so a child who only taps can grow a village, give it doors, house a gnome and make it night. Drawing a path is a drag: it survives a lifted finger (a finger that comes back within a third of a second, near where it left, goes on with the same stroke, and at any later time a finger that lands on the last pebble laid and moves off it, whichever way, draws the same path further, except that a path of one pebble, rested on and then pulled up, lifts), every pebble laid stays laid, and it counts however short it is.
- **The symbol rule.** The band starts below 6, so the kid side shows no word, letter, numeral or symbol, and the game has no `symbols.ts`. The rule follows the manifest band, never the child's age while playing.
- **What `ctx.childAge` sets.** Only what a first visit opens on. For a child of 3 or younger the glade opens with one squat red mushroom already standing in the sun, so the first thing a finger meets is a thing that answers. For a child of 4 or older the glade opens with bare moss. Age locks and hides nothing: every kind, the cart, the paths and the night are there for every child from the first touch that calls for them, and a saved village wins over the age.
- **What no age gives.** `null` opens a first visit as for the youngest child, with the one squat red mushroom.

## The toy

**The action.** Press a finger into the moss and hold it there. A mushroom pushes up under the finger and swells for exactly as long as the finger presses.

**In an empty scene.** A glade of moss with a stream through it, and nothing asks for anything.

- When the finger lands, in that frame, the moss dimples under it and a button of a mushroom pops out with the first creak.
- While the finger stays, the mushroom swells in steps, about six a second. Each step is one notch taller and wider, lands with a small squash, and creaks one step higher than the last, so a hold sounds like a rising creak and the mushroom is felt growing under the finger. After twelve steps it is full and stops by itself with a soft thump; a finger that goes on holding makes it tremble and squeak but no bigger.
- When the finger lifts, the mushroom springs up a little, wobbles and settles, with the kind's own last sound: the red one pops out one more spot with a plip, the blue bell lets a dewdrop fall with a plink, the puffball lets out a small puff.
- A short press leaves a squat mushroom and a long one a tall mushroom. What it is comes from the ground under the finger, and the three grounds are painted so that they can be told apart: a red cap with white spots in the sun, a blue bell on the wet bank of the stream, a white puffball in the shade under the big tree.
- A finger pressed on a mushroom that already stands makes it go on swelling from the size it has. A tap on it is a poke, and each kind answers in its own way: the red one wobbles like jelly with a boing, the bell swings and rings, and the puffball breathes in and sneezes a cloud of spores.
- A finger that takes a mushroom by its cap and pulls upward stretches it like gum, and a puffball goes flat instead. A quick smear upward only stretches it, and it snaps back. Held up for longer than a tap, the cap comes loose and shows it, leaning over with a creak; let go once it has leaned for a moment, it comes out with a pop and dives back into the earth, cap first; let go sooner, or brought back down instead, it sits tight again. That is the calm way to tidy up, and it costs nothing: the moss gives the same mushroom again.
- A finger in the water grows no mushroom: the water bulges and rings spread with a bloop.
- A finger that drifts a little while it holds is still holding: within about a finger's width of where it landed the swelling goes on. A finger that runs off across the moss has stopped holding, and the mushroom stays as tall as it was. In the game, a finger that runs off before the third step was drawing a path: the button slips back into the earth with a gulp and the pebbles start where the finger landed.
- A mushroom's foot stands in the band of moss between the far trees and the gnomes' log. A finger a little above or below the band grows its mushroom at the band's edge, and the band's upper edge dips under the sun so that the sun's face is never covered, and under the home control so that no mushroom stands up under it. The sun stands to one side of the top centre, so neither it nor the way it is pulled down lies under the jam's round home control (48 px across at the top centre, 10 px from the top), and nothing else a finger must reach lies there. What the game sets down covers no target: the cart stands at the right edge outside the band, the snail's three pebbles lie where no mushroom's foot stands, a hat left on a doorstep lies beside the door and never on it, and a tap on any of them is never taken for what is behind it. An overlap test holds this at 1180 by 820 and at the narrowest surface the game's tests name.
- Where the moss has no room left for one more, the earth still answers: a cap peeks out, turns once and slips back with a low gulp. A mushroom has no face and no move of its own at rest: it moves only when a finger, a door, a pebble or a gnome touches it.

**Why repeating it is a pleasure with no goal.** The answer starts when the finger lands and lasts as long as the finger does, so the child feels that the pressing itself is the growing (pack: game-design, touch-answers-bigger-than-the-touch.md). How long to hold is the child's own call every time, and each hold leaves a different mushroom that stays where it was grown. Random tapping covers the glade in buttons and pokes, each with its sound, and nothing a finger can do is refused (pack: game-design, toy-first.md). Four large gnomes at the edge and a snail watch every mushroom come up and react to it, each in its own manner, so even the toy has an audience.

## The object-by-action grid, and what is new on day 15

Six things that stand in the glade by five things a finger can do to them. Every cell looks and sounds different. Bare moss is not a row: what it does under a finger is the toy above, and a finger drawn across it lays the pebble path; where no more pebbles can be laid, a pebble hops up under the finger with a clack and drops back into the moss with a thup, so the finger is still answered.

| Thing | Pressed and held | Tapped | Pulled up | A door dropped on it | A path drawn onto it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Red spotted mushroom (in the sun) | Swells on with the creak rising, and a white spot pops out on the cap at each step with a plip | Wobbles like jelly, cap one way and stem the other, with a boing that is lower the taller it stands | Stretches like gum with a rubbery squeal, comes out with a pop, and its spots fly off and patter down like hail before it dives back | The door thuds in, and a crooked chimney pokes out of the cap with a pok and coughs one smoke ring | Pebbles clack up to it, and the last one settles under the door as a step with a clunk; with no door yet, the pebbles pile against the stem and the mushroom hiccups |
| Blue bell (on the stream bank) | Grows longer downward with the creak, a dewdrop swelling on its rim that drops off with a plink | Swings on its bent stem and rings one clear ding, lower the bigger it is | The bent stem straightens with a twang, the bell jangles all the way out and sinks back with a glug | The door clicks in with a chime, and a knotted rope unrolls down the stem with a rattle | Each pebble that lands nearer makes it ring a little louder, ting by ting, and the last one sets it swinging |
| Puffball (in the shade) | Blows up with a wheeze like a balloon, sides bulging, and lets off a small puff when the finger lifts | Breathes in, holds it, and sneezes a cloud of spores out of its top with a tshoo; the bigger the ball, the bigger the sneeze | Goes flat as it is pulled, with one long parp, and slips out of the fingers back into the moss with a slurp | The door squeaks in like a cork, the ball lets out a sigh and sags, then plumps up again with a pomp | The pebbles sink into its soft foot with muffled thumps, and each one squeezes a small ring of spores out of it with a pff |
| The stream | The water bulges up into a wobbling dome with a frog sitting on it, who croaks lower the longer the finger stays and plops back when it lifts | One ring spreads with a bloop, higher upstream and lower downstream | A column of water is drawn up like toffee and falls back with a splat | The door floats off downstream as a raft, turning, with a gurgle, and is back on the cart with a clack | The pebbles that reach the water turn into flat stepping stones, each landing with a deep plonk and a ring |
| A pebble path | The pebble under the finger sinks and the ones beside it rise like a see-saw, with a creak down and a creak up | The pebble hops with a clack, and the hop runs along the path both ways like a hammer along chime bars, every pebble its own note | A finger that has rested on the pebble for longer than a tap lifts the path by it like a string of beads, rattling; lifted high enough the path comes loose and shows it, every pebble askew, with a clatter; let go a moment after that, its pebbles roll away to the edge, and let go sooner or lower, or brought back down, it drops back into place. A finger that leaves a pebble without resting on it, or carries the lift on far above it or off to one side, is drawing a path from that pebble. The last pebble laid is the exception: a finger that moves off it draws the path further, rested or not, unless it is the path's only pebble | The door stands up on the path as a gate to nowhere, creaks open and shut once, falls flat with a clap and goes back to the cart | Where two paths meet, the pebbles shuffle round into a small ring with a run of clicks |
| A gnome who waits | His hat is pressed down over his eyes with a muffled grumble that rises, and springs back with a twang when the finger lifts | His own poke: the tall one's hat bends over and boings back, the baker goes off in a cloud of flour with a poff, the sleepy one snorts awake, the sneezy one's nose honks | A touch that starts on his hat is the hat's, however far the finger goes, and lays no pebble; a path from him starts on his body. The hat comes off with a cork's pop, he covers his bare head with both hands and squeaks, and the hat drops back on with a plop | He holds the door up, knocks on it twice, tok tok, looks behind it and hands it back to the cart | He tests the first pebble with one toe, tap tap, then walks the path to its end, stops there scratching his head with a rasp, and walks back to the log |

Beside the grid: the sun, tapped, spins its rays once with a shimmer and goes down behind the hill, or comes up again when it is night; held, it puffs out its cheeks and hums; pulled down, it brings the night as the tap does ("The scenes"). The snail, tapped, pulls in its eyes with a squelch and lets them out one after the other.

**The wrong use of each thing works and is funny.**

- A mushroom grown where it suits nobody is still a mushroom, and a gnome sent to it moves in and shows what is wrong with it in his own way ("The error as a consequence").
- A door on the stream is a raft, a door on a path is a gate to nowhere, and a door on a gnome is knocked on.
- A path drawn to nothing is walked to its end by a gnome who stops there, scratches his head and walks back. A path drawn in a loop is walked round and round until the gnome is dizzy, sits down with a bump, gets up and walks back. Neither is saved: on load he is on the log or at home.
- A finger held in the water raises a frog.
- The one a joke is played on is bewildered and never hurt.

**On day 15** the child plans who lives where and who must pass whose door. The child knows each gnome's taste and builds for it on purpose, or against it on purpose: a tall house on the bank for nobody, a squat one for the tall gnome to bump his hat on, a path that takes the sneezy gnome past the puffball. The child holds a mushroom to the height it is meant to have, which is a skill of the finger that shows in the world. None of that needs new content: it is three kinds by twelve sizes, doors, paths that join or do not, four fixed tastes, and day and night (pack: game-design, depth-from-combinations.md; pack: game-design, liveliness-from-causing-and-comedy.md).

## The four mechanic questions

The game has no learning goal, so the four questions have no school skill to be asked of; its verb, pressing for as long as a thing should grow, is play for its own sake.

## The error as a consequence

Nothing in the glade is wrong, and nothing gives a verdict. What can go against a gnome's taste shows as what that gnome does about it, at the place where it goes against him, every time.

- **A house that is too low.** The tall gnome bumps his hat on the door frame with a bonk, backs off, tries again, and in the end crawls in and leaves the hat on the doorstep, where it stays. In a middling house he stoops and the tip of his hat sticks out of the cap.
- **A house away from the stream.** The baker walks from his door to the water with his bucket and back, slopping, whenever he is at home. Where no path leads to the water he stands at the end of his path and looks at the stream.
- **A house with a path to it.** The sleepy gnome is woken by everyone who passes his door, and pulls his nightcap down over his ears with a groan.
- **Spores.** The sneezy gnome sneezes his hat off when a puffball sneezes near him, when he walks past one, and when he lives in one.
- **A path that ends.** A gnome walks only pebbles, with three exceptions: the sleepy one on his way to a house no path leads to; a gnome the child sends by a tap; and a gnome on his way back to the log, when his house or his door has gone or another was sent in his place. The last two take the pebbles that lead their way and cross the rest of the moss in their own way, a sound at every step, wading or hopping the stream. Where the pebbles of a path he was given to walk stop, he stops, scratches his head and walks back.
- **Where and why.** Each of these happens at the door, the path's end or the puffball that causes it, so the child sees what to change.
- **The state stays.** Nobody moves out by himself and nothing is taken away. The child changes one thing (holds the mushroom taller, pulls the path up, draws a path to the water) and the gnome's manner changes with it at once.
- **As interesting as a fit.** A bumped hat and a sneezed-off hat are worth causing on purpose, and a child may.
- **A fit is a consequence too.** A gnome in a house to his taste goes straight in, and his window shows him at what he likes: the tall one standing upright, the baker kneading, the sleepy one asleep, the sneezy one with his nose in the sun.
- **Never about the child.** A gnome's feelings are about his house and his path. There is no buzzer, cross, sad face turned to the child, praise or cheer.

## The designed order, and what is stored

**The order.** The game is open pretend play with no learning goal, and its designed order has one place, `glade`, which is the only id in `LADDER` in `config.ts`. No cycle is judged, so the position never moves.

**What a first visit starts with, by age.** For a child of 3 or younger, and for no age, one squat red mushroom stands in the sun. For a child of 4 or older the moss is bare. In both the four gnomes wait on their log and the snail is in its corner. A saved village wins over the age.

**What play changes.** Only the village: the mushrooms, their doors, the paths, who lives where, and whether it is day or night. New things come in when the thing they belong to is there, never by amount of play or by the date: the door cart is pulled in by the snail when the first mushroom stands, and the first pebbles are shown when the first door is in ("The scenes").

**A harder thing the child picks.** Nothing is harder by the game's choice. A village for all four gnomes at once, each to his taste, is more to plan than a house for one, and the child sees that in the four who wait.

**What is stored.** Small plain JSON under one version number, read field by field.

- `v`: the version.
- `position`: always `glade`.
- `finished`: always false. Open play has no ending.
- `mushrooms`: at most ten, each with `id` (a small whole number, one more than the highest in the village when the mushroom is grown, so ids rise in the order of building; the id of a mushroom that was pulled up may come again, which harms nothing, since its gnome went back to the log), `x` and `y` (where its foot stands, in whole stage units), `size` (1 to 12, the steps it was held for) and `door` (whether it has one). Its kind is not stored: it is read from the ground at `x` and `y`, which never changes.
- `paths`: the pebble paths, each a list of points on a grid of 20 stage units, at most 240 points in all. Stepping stones are not stored: a point that lies in the water is one.
- `homes`: for each of the four gnomes, the `id` of the mushroom he lives in, or none while he waits on the log.
- `hatOff`: whether the tall gnome's hat lies on his doorstep.
- `night`: whether the sun is down.
- `shown`: the first showings that have started: `cart` and `pebbles`. `pebbles` is also written when the child lays a first pebble, so a child who has drawn a path is never shown the pebbles, even after every path is pulled up.

A mushroom under a finger is saved at each step at the size it has reached, so a put-away under the finger leaves it as tall as it was held. A mushroom being pulled is saved where it stands until it is out. A door in the hand is saved where it came from: on the cart, or still in the mushroom it was taken from with its gnome at home, until the finger lets it go. Pebbles are saved as they are laid. A gnome on his way is saved at home ("The scenes"). Spores, rings on the water, the frog, a hat in the air and smoke are short-lived and gone on load. The largest legal state is far below half of the 64 KB cap, and a test says so.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

Four gnomes wait on a log at the bottom edge, large, each holding his bundle open towards the child so that what he wants can be seen. Their eyes follow the finger. A fifth character, the snail, wants nothing and is in nobody's way.

| Gnome | What shows his want | Likes, always | Dislikes, always | His manner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The tall one | A hat twice as tall as he is, and he keeps looking up to see what is taller than it | A tall house: twelve steps is best, nine and over will do | Anything squat: he bumps his hat | Slow and swaying like a reed, the hat's tip always a beat behind him |
| The baker | An empty bucket held out towards the stream, flour on his apron | A house on the bank of the stream, of any height | Being far from water: he fetches it by bucket | Quick and round, bouncing on his heels |
| The sleepy one | A pillow under his arm and a nightcap, eyes half shut, turned to the quietest corner | A house that no path leads to; he walks there over the moss in his sleep | A path to his door: passers-by wake him | Sinks slowly and jerks awake |
| The sneezy one | A large red nose held up to the sun and a handkerchief | A red house in the sun | Puffballs and their spores, near him or under him | Sniffs and twitches, always about to sneeze |

**Who moves in.** A gnome moves in when a mushroom has a door, nobody lives in it, and he can reach it: by pebbles from the log, or over the moss for the sleepy one when no path leads to it. Of the gnomes who still wait, the one whose taste the house fits goes. When it fits nobody, the one nearest the path's start goes and shows what is wrong with it. A tap on a gnome who waits, while another gnome is on his way to a house or the snail is still showing the first pebbles for him, sends that one instead, whatever his taste and whether or not pebbles lead all the way, so the child decides who lives where; at any other time the tap is his poke. A door is touched on the door and on the doorstep under it, never on the cap above it; on a house too small for both (a bell or a puffball of the three smallest sizes; a red house's door is in its stem), the cap takes the tap and the pull up, and the door is reached by its doorstep and comes off sideways or downwards. A tap on a door brings its gnome out to walk his path; a house pulled up or a door taken off sends its gnome back to the log with his bundle, unhurt.

**In the toy.** The gnomes wait and watch. Each reacts to a mushroom coming up in his own way: the tall one rises on his toes with it and sags when it stays squat, the baker leans towards anything by the stream and swings his bucket, the sleepy one is startled by a pop and nods off again, and the sneezy one sneezes when a puffball does.

## The scenes

**The cart comes** (a first showing). Cause: a mushroom stands, no door has ever been offered, and the child lifts a finger from any touch; so the mushroom a first visit opens with brings no cart until the child has touched something. Put away before the showing starts, it is still owed and starts at the child's first touch; nothing starts on load. Beats: the snail pulls a small cart of doors in from the right edge to its place, stops with a clack, and looks at the mushroom. About 4 seconds. Saved when the showing starts: `cart` in `shown`. Filled in from the state: the snail looks at whichever mushroom was grown. A touch puts the cart at its place at once.

**The first pebbles** (a first showing). Cause: the child puts the first door in and `pebbles` is not in `shown`. Put away before the showing starts, it is still owed and starts at the child's first touch; nothing starts on load. Beats: three pebbles lie beside the snail on its stone; it flicks them one after the other in a high arc over the pond to the log, where they land as a crooked trail, clack, clack, clack, and then it looks at that door. The snail never leaves its stone. About 3 seconds. Saved when the showing starts: `pebbles` in `shown`, and the three points in `paths`. Filled in from the state: the three pebbles start in the gap of the log between the sneezy gnome and the baker, and the trail bends towards the side of the glade the fitted door is on. A touch lays the three pebbles at once.

**Moving in.** Cause: a house with a door and nobody in it becomes reachable, or the child sends a gnome who waits. Beats: he shoulders his bundle, walks the pebbles at his own pace (over stepping stones with a plonk each), arrives, does what his taste makes of this house ("The error as a consequence"), goes in, and the window shows him. 5 to 10 seconds. Saved with the act that makes it certain, in the same save as the door that goes in, the pebble that joins the path or the tap on the gnome: his entry in `homes`, and `hatOff` when the tall gnome will leave his hat outside. Put away before he sets off, he is found at home and nothing plays on load. Filled in from the state: which gnome, the kind and size of the house, the route, and whose doors he passes on it. A touch puts him inside at once, with the hat on the step if it belongs there.

**A walk.** Cause: a tap on a lived-in door, or a path newly joined to it. Beats: the gnome comes out, walks his path to its other end, reacts to each door and puffball he passes and to whatever is at the end (a neighbour's door is knocked on, the water is looked at, nothing is scratched at), and walks home. 6 to 10 seconds. Saves nothing: it is a view of the saved village and ends where it began. A touch puts him back indoors.

**Night.** Cause: the sun is pulled down behind the hill, or tapped. Beats: the sky is painted over in deep blue from the top down, the windows light one by one in the order the houses were built, each kind with its own glow and note (a crackle of orange in the red, a hum of pale blue in the bell, the whole puffball lit like a paper lantern with a hush), chimneys smoke, the snail's lamp comes on, and the sleepy gnome snores. About 6 seconds, and then it stays night for as long as the child likes, with every touch working as by day. Saved when the scene starts: `night`. Filled in from the state: every house and who is in it. A touch finishes the lighting at once. At night the top of the sun stays in sight above the hill, asleep, so there is always something to tap or pull up. Morning is the same in reverse, in about 2 seconds, when the sun is pulled up or tapped; saved when it starts: `night` cleared. The village stands as it was built.

**How a cycle ends and how the next one starts.** There is no cycle to end: this is open pretend play, and nothing starts unless the child starts it, with one kind of exception: what a villager does by himself while the child watches. A baker who lives away from the stream goes for water now and then, a sneezy gnome in a puffball sneezes, and at night the sleepy one snores and the chimneys smoke. Each of these counts from nothing on load, stands still under a finger and during a scene, keeps nothing, and moves nothing the child cannot get back with one touch. The calm way to tidy up is the pull: a mushroom pulled up by its cap goes back into the earth, a path lifted high enough rolls away, and a gnome whose house has gone walks back to the log with his bundle. On load nothing replays: the village is as it was left, by day or by night, with the gnomes who wait on their log and those who live somewhere at home. Nobody remarks on the child having been away (pack: game-design, endings-and-short-scenes.md; pack: game-design, the-world-keeps-and-waits.md).

## The look

Written after the style spike. The look is **poster paint on sugar paper**, the first of the two reserved for this game, and it held on clarity and on the frame budget, so the second was not tried.

**What it is.** Thick opaque poster paint laid with a fat brush on purple-grey sugar paper. Nothing has an outline. Every shape is one wobbly filled blob with the ridges the bristles left in it, a bead of thicker paint along its edge that is light on one side and dark on the other, and here and there a drip that ran before the paint dried. Where two wet colours met, one has run into the other in soft tongues. The paper shows wherever the brush did not go: between the strokes of the sky, and in dry streaks where a stroke ran out of paint.

**Palette.** The paper is a muted purple grey that no paint uses, so it always reads as paper. The sky is cream laid in long horizontal passes. The sunny moss is a yellow green, the shade under the big tree a dark olive green with fallen leaves and ferns on it and no glint anywhere, so that it is never taken for water, the wet bank a sea green and the water a strong blue: the three grounds a mushroom takes its kind from are three plainly different greens, and the kinds answer them in colour (red in the yellow green, blue on the sea green, white in the dark green). A bell stands only well inside the bank as it is painted. The gnomes carry the strongest colours in the frame, one each: red, pale blue, orange with purple, white with ochre. Black is used only for pupils. Night is the same painting under one deep blue, laid over it from the top down along a hard brush edge: every piece has a night twin, the same paint multiplied by that blue, and so has every colour that is filled or drawn live (the frog and the water it rises on, a door in the hand or in the air, a gate, a raft, a hat in the air, the whites of every eye). What is lit keeps its day colours: an orange window in a red house, a pale blue one in a bell, the whole puffball like a paper lantern, the snail's lamp, the sun's top above the hill, and a few stars of unequal size in the open sky.

**Materials and light.** There is one material, paint, and no lighting model. Depth comes from overlap and from size, and a thing stands on the ground by a flat darker patch of the ground's own colour under it. The ridges run the way the brush went: across the sky and the moss, down a stem, round a cap. A highlight is a stroke of lighter paint, never a gradient. No blur, no glow other than the pale halo painted round the sun and, at night, the wash of light round a lit window and the snail's lamp; no transparency except in the guidance (the glow on a place, the ghost hand, and its ghost mushroom, which is one pale shape in the ground's own colour and never lies over a real mushroom) and in specks that fade.

**The frame.** The place fills the frame from edge to edge: the big tree and its canopy on the left and over the top, far trees along the skyline, the sun with a face, the stream from the skyline to its pond, reeds, ferns, daisies, the log along the bottom and the snail on its stone. The four gnomes are the largest things in it, about two fifths of its height, and the tall one with his hat three fifths. The mushrooms the child grows are the plainest things in it: a stem and a cap in two or three colours. A house is the same mushroom with its parts painted on: an arched wooden door, a round window, a crooked chimney on a red one, one knotted rope hanging under a bell's door for its gnome to climb. Whoever lives there shows in the window as a face with his hat, large enough to tell who it is from across the glade, and carries himself there as his taste makes him: upright, kneading, asleep, nose in the sun, or stooped, thirsty, woken, sneezing. A gnome on a path is the same painted gnome at about a third of his size on the log. The pebbles are grey dabs of unequal size that sit a little off their grid, flatter and wetter where they lie in the water. The door cart is a small blue cart with three doors of unequal height, at the right edge above the snail. The tall gnome's hat on a doorstep lies on its side with the dark of its open end towards the door.

**Motion rules.**

- Paint does not morph. A thing moves as a whole painted piece: it slides, turns about a joint, squashes and stretches about its foot. A face changes by swapping or moving small painted parts (pupils, lids, brows, a mouth), which are the only shapes drawn live.
- Everything that is touched squashes first and overshoots after, on a spring. A mushroom lands each step of its swelling with a small squash.
- Each character has its own move at rest, and no two share one: the tall gnome sways like a reed with the tip of his hat a beat behind, the baker bounces on his heels with his bucket swinging, the sleepy one's head sinks and jerks back up, the sneezy one sniffs in bursts with his nose swelling. The snail's eyes sway one after the other, the sun's rays turn, glints run down the stream.
- All eyes follow the finger, and a mushroom that is swelling: a gnome's on the log and out on the moss alike, from where his face is.
- Sight and sound share one clock: each answer and each scene is written once as timed beats that set the pose and the notes together.
- Every gnome walks at his own pace and in his own way: the tall one slowly with his hat lagging, the baker quickly and bouncing, the sleepy one slowest and wading through whatever is in his way, the sneezy one in bursts. A sleepy face has drooping lids, a slack mouth and a nod, and never a lowered brow.

**How it is drawn, and the tiers.** The whole place is painted once into one backdrop at the surface's own size and stamped once a frame. Every mushroom (three kinds by twelve sizes), every part of a gnome, the snail, the sun, the frog and the hand is painted once into a sprite at twice the stage scale and stamped. A house is one sprite for each kind, size and state (with its door, half built, lit), painted when it is first needed, and a night twin is made the same way. All pebbles are three fills a frame however many lie. A frame is those stamps plus a few live fills and lines, counted by the view and held under 80 by `view.test.ts`. The four tiers in `config.ts` keep the look and change only the pixel ratio, how many glints run on the stream and how many specks are in the air at once; no tier removes a painted thing, a character or a move.

**Small marks, kept uneven.** Three painted marks could be taken for a sign if they were drawn evenly, so their numbers live in `marks.ts` and a test holds them (`marks.test.ts`). A star is one plump dab of cream, tilted, and no two stars share a size or a tilt; none lies level, and none is built from two strokes. What hangs under a bell house's door is one rope and nothing across it: it hangs askew in a slack curve, has one to four knots of unequal size at uneven steps, one knot only on the four shortest houses, where two would sit close together like two dabs on a line, and frays into two short strands at its end; two posts with rungs across are never painted. The end of the log shows its grain as four short pieces of rings round a heart that lies off the middle of the end: each piece is a short arc of its own width that lies on the end with both its ends, a piece and the next one out lie on different sides of the heart, the steps between them are uneven, and together they leave most of the way round open, so none closes and they do not fan out from a corner.

**Small things that move.** The red cap's one more spot is a white dab that pops out on the cap as the plip sounds and sinks into the paint. The first pebbles lie as three grey dabs on the bank in front of the snail; each flies in a high arc over the pond, turning, and lands at the log with a clack. A gnome whose house has gone stands at where the door was, small as on a path, walks back to the log at his own pace, jumps up onto it and is himself again; nobody is ever moved by a cut. A cap that is about to swell stretches once before the first step, so the eye sees the swelling start. A cap that is pulled up and held comes loose before it comes out: it leans over with a creak, and sits tight again when it is brought back down. A bell's rope is let down as its door goes in: it grows from the door to the moss with the roll at its end getting smaller, and a knot shows as the rope reaches it. Where a path has reached a red mushroom its last pebble lies under the door as one flat step, and with no door yet three pebbles of unequal size lean against the stem. A gnome who crosses moss with no pebbles on it does it in his own way, with a sound at every step: the tall one in long strides, the baker bouncing, the sleepy one shuffling, the sneezy one in quick steps; through the stream the tall and the sleepy one wade, with a ring at each step, and the baker and the sneezy one hop it and splash down. A gnome on his way back to the log stands still under a finger and squashes like himself. The same holds for a gnome on his way back to the log: he takes the pebbles that lead from his feet towards his place, and wades or hops the rest. The snail's lamp is a lantern on a crook that stands on the stone behind the snail, below the cart and clear of it. A red mushroom and a bell that are pulled stretch taller and thinner; a puffball goes flat instead, wider and lower the farther it is pulled, hanging from the finger off the moss, with one long falling parp for the whole pull. A path that is lifted until it comes loose shows it: every pebble rises a little more and lies askew, with a clatter, and it sits again with a click when it is brought back down. A gnome's hat is touched in its own painted shape, the brim with it: a pointed hat narrows to a finger's width at its tip, the baker's hat is as wide as its side puffs, and the sleepy one's nightcap has its fallen end and its tassel. Each gnome steps off the log at a place of his own beside himself, where the moss shows, and a path drawn from him starts there. While a lifted stroke waits for the finger, the gnome it concerns shifts his weight towards where the finger left, with a small questioning sound. The baker's bucket shows blue water up to its rim while he carries some.
