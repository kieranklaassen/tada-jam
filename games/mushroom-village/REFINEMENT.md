### For the owner to decide

- The look: poster paint on sugar paper, by day and by night. Is it full enough once all four gnomes have moved in and the log is bare?
- Villagers who act by themselves while the child watches (a baker far from water fetches some every 22 seconds, a sneeze in a puffball, a snore and chimney smoke at night): keep them, or should nothing at all happen without a touch?
- A tap on a waiting gnome sends him only while another gnome is on his way; at any other time it is his poke. Should a tap be able to move a gnome who has already settled?
- A press that does not move is a tap for up to 0.45 seconds; after that a mushroom swells. Only a hand can say whether that is right for three.
- A gnome is touched in two shapes: his hat, in its own painted shape from its tip to the lower edge of its brim, and his body below it. A touch that starts on the hat is the hat's however far the finger goes; a path from him starts on his body, and its first pebble lies beside him. Is the brim's lower edge where a child's finger expects the line to be?
- A mushroom comes out only when it is held up past the tap's time and its cap has been seen to lean for a moment (0.15 s); a quick smear upward only stretches it. Is that wait short enough for the calm tidy-up and long enough for a three-year-old's smear?
- On a bell or a puffball of the three smallest sizes, the cap takes the tap and the pull, and the door is its doorstep on the moss below the foot; such a door comes off sideways or downwards only. On every other house the painted door is the door, and a drag straight up off it takes the door and never the house.
- A pull begins a finger's width (46 units) above where the finger landed, so that a finger that drifts while it holds goes on swelling; the cap then catches the finger up. Is that slack right for a child who means to pull?
- A path is lifted only by a finger that has rested on a pebble for longer than a tap; it comes loose 56 units up, with a clatter and every pebble askew, and rolls away when it is let go 0.15 s after that. A quick drag up from a pebble draws a path instead. Is that deliberate enough, and still easy enough for the calm tidy-up?
- Under the home control no mushroom stands higher than 390 units down the stage, which keeps the tallest one out from under it on a phone on its side; a finger above that grows its mushroom at that edge.
- A gnome the child sends takes the pebbles there are and crosses the rest of the moss in his own way; the tall and the sleepy one wade the stream, the baker and the sneezy one hop it. A gnome on his way back to the log does the same, which the sheet does not yet say (pair 19).

- The glade holds ten mushrooms and 240 pebbles. Are those the right limits?
- A finger has a third of a second to run off before a hold becomes a mushroom instead of a path. Only a hand can judge it.
- A first visit by a child of three or younger, or of no known age, opens with one squat red mushroom; older children start on bare moss.

### How the game meets the quality bar

No frame rate has been measured on any engine, build or device, and no physical iPad.

- Alive at idle: each gnome's own move on the log and his own way of showing in a window, blinks, the snail, the sun, the stream's glints; at night smoke, a snore, the lamp (`motion.ts`, `view.ts`, `village.ts`).

A sound never comes without a move: each answer and scene is one list of beats that sets both.
- Weight, squash and follow-through: springs on every touched thing; a swelling step lands with a squash; a door thuds in and the house gives; the tall hat lags its gnome.
- Kid-clear silhouettes and tappables: no outline, strong flat colours; what the youngest must find first is about a hundred stage units across (a mushroom's touch shape is never under 100 by 92, a gnome 116 wide, the cart 120 by 96, the sun 112, the snail 124); a door's touch shape is at least 58 each way and a pebble's 56 across, which is 48 px on a 1024 by 768 tablet, and so are a hat on a doorstep, a gnome's hat and a gnome out on the moss; a thing is touched where it is drawn and the front one wins (`overlap.test.ts`, `grid.test.ts`). ### What the next builder should know

- A hold that grows something needs three ends told apart: the child lifted, the finger ran off, the touch was taken away. Only the first is the child's doing.
- Where the kind of a thing comes from the ground, the idle hand's places and the first-visit piece need a test that they lie on ground of their own kind.
- A still taken on a slow machine is taken at whatever tier the governor has dropped to; pin the tier in the address.
- Keep what is kept and what is seen apart from the first scene on: the outcome goes into the kept village when a scene starts, and one function puts everything seen where the kept village says. Rest, load and a touch that ends a scene then all use it, and "parked equals loaded" is a short test.
- A delayed answer that belongs to one thing and plays on another (a gnome who sneezes back at a puffball) has to be hushed with the thing it belongs to, or it outlives a pull.
- What the idle hand shows has to be replayed through the touch code, point by point, in a test: a hand that shows a move the touch code reads as something else teaches the wrong thing.
- Keep "the child is busy" apart from "something is moving": what a villager does by himself must not reset the idle ladder, or the hand never comes while the village is alive.
- One tap window for everything, in one constant, tested at its edges; a pull or a carry is decided by movement only.
- A mark that must not read as a sign is easier to hold as numbers in a pure module than to judge in paint.
- A wash of see-through paint reaches further than its radius; a glow painted onto a sprite needs room inside the sprite or it shows a square edge at night.
- Give each gesture one start that a child can see, a shape of its own, and never tell two gestures apart by how far or how steeply the finger went: a three-year-old cannot aim a distance or a slope. Here a hat is the hat's and a path starts on the body; a door is the door and its doorstep, and the cap is the cap.
- An act that removes something should show itself before it happens and be undone by lifting early: a cap that leans and creaks before it comes out costs a short wait and saves a house from a smear.
- Put what the Mount does with a gesture into functions a test can call with the real gesture recogniser; the one finding of reading 2 that sat in the Mount (a lift that ended a stroke) could not be held by any test until then.
- A touch shape that is one box round a slanted, drooping or lobed thing leaves paint outside it and bare ground inside it. Give such a thing its own shape as numbers, and test the points of its paint that lie farthest out.
- Hold the home control clear by where things may stand, not by how large they are drawn, and let the test run at the smallest surface the game is fitted to: a size that is left out of a test is a claim the file should not make.
- A fix that no test fails without is not finished. Taking each one out in turn found three such in this pass, and one line that did nothing.
- A night that is painted by twins and by live colours needs a test that draws the same frame by day and by night into a context that writes the paints down, and finds none of the day's in the night's.
