## For the pull request

Written as the game is built and kept at the end of this file: the pull request is made from it.

### How the game meets the quality bar

- **Alive at idle.** Each friend breathes, blinks, sways or chews at its own rhythm and follows the finger with its eyes; the cat washes, the bird hops, the line swings in the wind, the water ripples. `motion.test.ts` holds that no two characters share an idle, a walk or an answer.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** Every touch that lands on a thing, a friend, a place or the bare linen is answered in the frame it lands; `yard.test.ts` ("everything answers") checks each one for a movement and a voice. No one has heard the voices.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** Garments fly in an arc, squash on landing and settle; wet ones sag and drip; friends squash when set down, and bellies, beards, crests and tails follow late.
- **Kid-clear silhouettes and tappables.** Only what can be touched carries the thick chain-stitch outline. `layout.test.ts` holds every target at 48 px or more on the fitted surfaces it lists, clear of the home control, the grown-up corner and the strip where wrists rest.
- **Wordless clarity for ages 3 to 6.** Nothing is written or counted on the kid side; the wordless check passes. No two stitches of mud or of the scenery's flecks cross, so none reads as a sign; `figures.test.ts` holds that for every cell at every step of mud. Each state has one want, and `cycle.test.ts` walks the whole cycle by following only that want.
- **Wordless idle guidance.** The ladder is the template's: a gold ring on the next thing after a wait, then a stitched hand that shows the rub or the tap. The cold playtest found one unclear ring and it is fixed.
- **Frame rate.** Not measured. The budget is held by `frameBudget.test.ts`: at most 78 draws a frame against about 80, one full-surface composite, no blur, shadow or blend, pixel ratio capped at 2, four tiers that lower the pixel ratio and then the loose threads. The frame rate, with engine, throttle, pixel ratio and build, is the lead's to add, and no iPad has been measured.
- **Procedural or committed assets only.** Everything is drawn in code and every sound is synthesized; the source egress check passes. The built-asset scan was not run by the builder.
- **Found as left.** `cycle.test.ts` puts the game away at every ninth frame of a whole cycle, in the middle of a drag and in the middle of each scene, and opens it as it was; each scene saves its outcome when it starts and never replays. The wind is not saved: an opened yard waits for a gust of its own before anything unpegged slides off the line.
- **Nothing passes through anything.** `overlap.test.ts` plays seeded hands over six seeds and holds, at every frame, that no two things share a place they should not; the five meant overlaps are named at its head.
- **No engagement mechanics.** Nothing is scored, counted, timed or praised; a friend's answer is to the garment as it is. The position only decides how many friends step in.

### The learning claim

None. Wash Day has no learning goal and names no record.

### Defaults taken for the owner

- A first visit starts with one friend below age 4 or with no age, two from 4, three from 5; any saved position wins, and each step is reached by play.
- The puddle makes mud without end, so a clean garment can be muddied again at will.
- Each kind of friend has two colourways that alternate by cycle; the three are always a pig, a heron and a goat.
- The grown-up frame-rate overlay opens by a hold of a second in the top right corner and then three taps there, as the template has it; no run of quick taps opens it.

### What the next builder should know

- A canvas game can prove its draw count: draw through a counting fake canvas in a test and compare with the view's own tally.
- Bubbles and other loose things that take a touch must not rise over the middle of the thing they come from, or they swallow the rub.
- A test that checks an invariant at every frame should throw from a plain check and not build an assertion each time; here that took the run from two minutes to four seconds.
- A want that names a place needs a ring that takes in something a child can see there (the pegs, the bar), or it reads as pointing at the scenery behind.
