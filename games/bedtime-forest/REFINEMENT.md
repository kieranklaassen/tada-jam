# Bedtime Forest: thirty refinement passes

Each pass: an idle screenshot (or a recorded scene) → an honest critique → one focused fix → re-measure. Performance comes first: every pass re-runs the jam's shared perf probe.

**How each pass was measured.** The production build (`npm run build && npx vite preview`) at 1180×820, DPR 2, touch. The idle shot is taken in WebKit with the top tier pinned (`?tier=3`) on a fresh save, after 128 rendered frames. Game time advances at most 50 ms per frame, so on this GPU-less VM a frame count is the honest clock: 128 frames is 6.4 s of idle, when the ring and the ghost hand are both in frame. The CPU number is the shared probe's `cpuP95Ms` in Chromium at 6× CPU throttle (update plus render submit per frame, 12 taps over 6 s, target under 8 ms). Draw calls are `renderer.info` at the top tier, in the idle shot (the ghost hand adds one while it shows).

Shots are in the Project store under `media/jam-10-games/bedtime-forest/` (`iter-01.png`, `iter-10.png`, `iter-20.png`, `iter-30.png`, `hero.png`, `walkthrough.mp4`).

## Still weak

- **No physical iPad was measured.** Every number comes from a GPU-less cloud VM: Chromium through SwiftShader and WebKit through software GL. There, WebKit's governor settles on the lowest tier at 23 to 25 fps (Pebble Table, which has no tiers, runs at 15 to 16 fps in the same session), but with the top tier pinned WebKit manages only 8.3 to 9.3 fps, since DPR 2 plus the paper pass is fill-bound in software. Whether a real iPad holds the top tier at 60 fps is unknown.
- **A very slow CPU still drops frames.** At 20× Chromium throttle, cpuP95 is 18.9 ms even at the lowest tier, over the 8 ms target.
- **The playtest is a proxy.** The three cold playtests were a script plus a reviewer reading frames, not a child. A real four-year-old will find moments these missed.
- **Sound was only measured offline.** Loudness was rendered through the real graph in an offline context and never heard on a device speaker. An animal's two tricks share its one voice.
- **Motion is close in places.** The rabbit's thump and the bear's belly drum are the closest pair of tricks (0.30, just over the test's 0.25 floor), and the songbird's hover is the smallest trick at play distance. Every play of a trick is identical (no per-play amplitude or speed variation), and there are no rare idle delights beyond yawns, glances, tilts, and sniffs.
- **Backs to the child.** While everyone gazes home, the animals whose homes are at the back of the clearing turn their backs, so the ghost hand's bear demo is seen mostly from behind, and the owl's back view is plain brown.
- **The shiver is subtle.** A small animal in a big, draughty home (the songbird in the cave) shivers and hops out, but at play distance the shiver is easy to miss; the hop out carries the message.
