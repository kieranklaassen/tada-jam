## For the pull request

Written as the game is built and kept at the end of this file: the pull request is made from it.

### How the game meets the quality bar

- **Alive at idle.** Every guest breathes at its own tempo and, awake and left alone, does one of three fidgets nobody else has; the tuba and the singer keep their beat at night and their marks travel through the walls; sleepers snore, each in its own voice; snow drifts, warmth rises, the lump behind the coach's blind shifts. All of it runs on the attended clock and stops when the game is unattended or hidden.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** Every point of the page answers when the finger lands, except the grown-up's corner: a test presses a grid of points and fails on a silent one. Every cell of the object-by-action grid has an outcome of its own in the rules, a sound of its own in `voices.ts` (a test fails on two sounds alike) and something of its own to see (`acts.ts`, `inkMoments.ts`, tests in `play.test.ts`). Turning the wheel sets off a chain: each placed thing that has something to do at the new hour answers in turn. Nobody on the machine that wrote the sounds has heard them.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** A guest squashes at once and springs back past its height by its own weight; a carried guest or thing swings behind the finger; a guest set down thuds; a displaced guest walks out by one door and in by another, and a thing a scene moves hops the same way; a stove put to a wall slides to its place. Numbers in `motion.ts` and `acts.ts`, with tests.
- **Kid-clear.** Rooms of about 200 logical pixels, one or two guests a room, each its own silhouette, interiors light and the roof, foundations and night sky dense. What can be touched carries the one spot colour. A model test (`overlap.test.ts`) holds that nothing is drawn through anything, with each meant overlap named and capped.
- **Wordless clarity for the declared age.** No word and no letter is drawn. The band starts at 9: the only numerals are the steps of the two dials, drawn through `symbols.ts` beside the flames and icicles they count, and play never depends on reading one. `npm run wordless:check` passes.
- **Wordless guidance.** One want in every scene: guests in the lobby stare at the door they ask for, each with a row of pen dots from its eyes to that door; a guest given another room glances back at it, and on its own page the room it asked for stays ringed in dots; a cross guest turns to the wall its trouble comes through; a judged house leaves the coach's door open. The idle ladder glows on what can be touched and then shows one verb a showing (a waiting guest lifted and put back, a cross guest touched, the wheel, a thing lifted from the cupboard, the coach), never where anything goes: a test holds that copying the hand changes nothing in the house. For this age help is fetched: touching a guest shows what reaches it and where from.
- **60 fps on a mid-range iPad.** Not measured on the building machine, which draws in software; the lead measured the merged game on a real graphics card and holds the numbers, engine, throttle and build. What this branch adds since then has not been measured. Built for it and counted by `frameBudget.test.ts`: canvas 2D, the house in cached layers, each figure a cached sprite, the pixel ratio capped at 2, one whole-surface stamp a frame at rest and two during a sweep, at most 69 sprites and figures in a frame. No physical iPad has been measured.
- **Procedural or committed assets only.** Everything is drawn and sounded at run time from numbers and a seed. No file, font or address is loaded. `node scripts/egress-check.ts`, `npm run egress:built` and `npm run education:built` pass.
- **Its own art direction.** Pen-and-ink crosshatch: black pen on cream paper, tone by hatching only, one vermilion spot colour; touched, a guest turns the page to pencil with its own room in ink, in a hand that is that guest's own. The art guide is in `ART.md` under "The look".
- **Found as left.** The house, the hour and whose page it is are saved through `ctx.storage` as they change; a guest or a thing in the hand is saved where it came from; each scene's outcome is in the save when the scene starts (the porter's mark when he comes in), so a put-away in the middle of any scene loses nothing and nothing replays. The next coach-load waits for the child's touch and nothing starts by itself. Tests hold each of these.
- **No engagement mechanics.** No score, count, timer, reward or praise. The count of set-downs is kept only to judge a cycle and is shown nowhere; the place in the designed order is shown nowhere; the only thing a finished house does is leave the coach waiting, its door open and its engine running.

### The learning claim

As the sheet has it, with every check state read from the lookup on 2026-10-03 (to be read again on the day of the pull request):

Monster Hotel is designed from two content standards adopted by the California State Board of Education (`us-ca 4.4.2.S` for grade 4 and `us-ca 6.4.4.M` for grade 6), neither of which the game carries out: the first asks for practice with other people and the second asks the child to show the use of the steps for resolving a conflict, and the game has only invented guests and keeps no record of what a child shows; from three of California's Transformative Social and Emotional Learning competencies, which are voluntary guidance tied to no grade (`us-ca 3.B.2`, in part, and `us-ca 5.F.2`, Late Elementary, and `us-ca 4.E.3`, in part, Middle School); from five fase goals of the Dutch curriculum institute, which are guidance on what a school can offer and not law (`nl ojw/ja/1/07/fase2`, `nl ojw/ja/1/06/fase2` in part, `nl ojw/ja/3/08/fase2` in part, `nl ojw/ja/1/06/fase3` and `nl ojw/ja/3/08/fase3` in part); and from two items of the Dutch draft core goals for 2027, a draft not in force (`nl 20 A d`, in part, and `nl 20 B e`). Every one of the twelve records is about other people or about talking with them, and in that place the game offers only invented guests, monsters in a pretend hotel: the child deals with no person and talks with nobody in the game. Of the two standards the game takes only what a settlement is, a way that works, and that steps are used and not described. Of `us-ca 3.B.2` it takes trying to see a thing from another's side, and not the naming of what makes people take it differently; of `us-ca 4.E.3` the answer both sides can accept that meets the worry behind each demand, and not the working on it with peers and adults; of `nl ojw/ja/1/06/fase2` that a conflict comes from wants that pull against each other, shown and not discussed; of `nl ojw/ja/3/08/fase2` how a conflict arose and which solutions are possible, shown and not put into words, and of `nl ojw/ja/3/08/fase3` the solutions that suit every party, and of neither the child's own surroundings; and of `nl 20 A d` dealing with conflicts, in pretend, and not dialogue or treating people as equals. All twelve records were `confirmed` when read on 2026-10-03. The game says nothing about what a child has reached, practised or can do, and keeps no record of it.

### What the next builder should know

- **Write the rules before the content, and let a solver pick the content.** The thirty casts were chosen by running every candidate through a small exhaustive solver and reading three numbers: how many ways of giving out the rooms there are, how many settle the house bare, and how many settle it with the things placed. Hand-picked casts would have had houses that no arrangement settles and "neat ways" with an idle thing in them. The solver stays in the game folder for the tests and is never imported by anything the Mount reaches.
- **A sheet sentence about how hard something is must be a number a test holds.** The first draft of "Guess" said most arrangements settle at the first places. Measured, it was between one in eight and all. The sentence was corrected and a test now fails if a cast drifts.
- **A hand-drawn spike scene should be a state the rules produce.** `page.test.ts` builds the spike's house from the rules and compares the result with the scene the renderer was given. The first comparison showed the rules had a cross guest turning to the less obvious of its two troubles; the rule was changed (what others make comes first, loudest first), not the picture.
- **Keep a spare bed.** A "harder option the child can choose" that cannot be housed is a trap. Every cast keeps beds for one more than its guests, and a test holds that the house can be settled with the extra guest in.
- **Answer on the press, decide on the lift.** The sheet says the page turns when the finger lands, and that the same guest touched again gives the plain page back. Turning the page off on the press would fight a carry that starts on the same press. So the page turns on at the press and off at the lift of a tap, and a press that becomes a carry keeps the page.
- **Whatever the page transforms, the finger must transform back, from one module.** The large room and the upside-down page are two numbers in `inkPlaces.ts`, used by the page to draw and by the toy to find what a finger landed on. The tests press a guest where it is drawn in each view.
- **Do not enlarge anything under a moving finger.** The first build drew the room a carried guest was held over large. At a wall the enlarged room changed which room the finger was over, and the large room flickered between two rooms. Under a carrying finger the house now lies as it is.
- **One touch, one voice.** See the template note on `audio.ts`.
- **A hint that is a verb cannot be a solution.** The first idle hint carried a waiting guest to the door it asked for. In the simplest cast that settles the house. The sheet check caught it; the hint is now "lift it a little and put it back", and a test holds that copying the hand changes nothing.
- **A beat that is landed is not a beat that played.** `Scene.finish()` plays every unfinished beat at progress 1. A beat that sounds, sweeps or saves must know it is being landed by a touch, or the touch that ends a scene sets off everything the scene had left.
- **Walks are part of "nothing passes through anything".** The first changeover walked ten figures in straight lines through walls and floors to the coach. A walk is now a list of legs between doors, and a guest is seen only on a leg.
- **Take stills of saved states, not only of play.** A script that writes a saved state into the shell's slot before load reaches any place of the designed order in one step; playing there by script takes minutes and breaks whenever a layout number moves.
- **A subagent can draw the spike while the rules are written,** if it is given a view-model type to draw from and the two never write the same file. Commit by path while it works.
