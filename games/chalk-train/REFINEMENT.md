### How the game meets the quality bar

- **Alive at idle:** the engine breathes a smoke puff every second and a half, blinks, and every few seconds does one of six things of its own, never the same twice running. A rider that waits, rides or is at home has its own idle piece and looks toward its home or the way the train goes. All chalk figures are redrawn like a flip book. All of it stops while unattended or hidden. Tests hold the engine's six and the no-repeat, and each rider's pieces.
- **Motion and sound on every touch:** a landing finger is answered in the same call by what lies under it (tar, water, weed, engine, a line, a rider, a home), with dust or a ripple and a sound, and the engine's eyes snap to it. A test holds that every landing asks for a sound. The 30 cells of the grid have each their own sight and sound, held by a test, and were read against the sheet cell by cell in the closing run, and what was short was built.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through:** the engine squashes and hops on springs (landing, corner, bump, brake, sneeze), leans to peer over an open end, loses a stride at a corner, and its wagons bunch and spring apart; each rider has its own weight in every piece; dust and smoke follow everything.
- **Every character moves like itself:** four riders with fixed tastes, each with its own tempo, weight and manner in every piece, its own voice, and one funniest part that is moved by itself. No two share a piece; a test holds that.
- **Kid-clear:** one train of three large figures in pastel chalk on mid-grey tar; each rider one silhouette in its own chalk and each home plainly that rider's. The train is never a heap and is always in view.
- **Wordless clarity for the declared age:** no word, letter, numeral or symbol; the wordless check passes. Everything works with a tap; a line counts when partly done; one finger at a time; no mark is wrong, and what a mark did not do shows as a consequence in the world.
- **Wordless guidance, one want per scene:** the rider aboard looks at its home, and a waiting rider at the train. Left alone, a glow comes up on the bare tar on the way to where the train is wanted, then a ghost hand draws a line to it from the engine; with nobody about it taps. It backs off as the template's ladder does and vanishes on any touch. A first showing plays once for a child who has not touched. - **60 fps on a mid-range iPad:** not measured. Canvas 2D, one full-surface copy a frame, no post pass, no shadow, pixel ratio capped at 2, adaptive tiers and the grown-up overlay from the template. ### What the next builder should know

- Measure overlap by what is drawn. The first overlap test here used a small round for each thing, in the wrong place for a home, and passed while a still showed the engine standing on a home. A box per thing, measured from its picture, found it at once; and the model then stops the train by the same boxes.
- A rule that stops a ride short of every figure on its way reads well and plays badly: in a seeded test four cycles in five no longer finished. Stop only where the line ends at a figure, and ride in front of the rest.
- What is seen lags what is saved. A rider can be aboard or home in the world while it still stands at its stop on the screen, so a place is free in the world and taken on the screen. The model is told the places where someone is still seen, and lays nobody out there.
- A thing at rest that may not swing through another gets stuck where the ride left it. Let it pass, and hold only where it comes to rest.
- When stops and homes can be neighbours, a rider can climb in and get out in the same instant. Ask for a little ride first.
- The ghost hand must show the move the scene wants. A tap was shown where a line was wanted, and the copy of it moved the train a hand's width.
- A mark's points land on whole tar units, so a new line can pass exactly through a point of an older one. A crossing test with strict inequalities misses that case; count a point that lies on the other path as belonging to one side.
- A corner drawn at 80 degrees reads as under 70 once the mark is evened to a step, because the sharpest point falls between two points. Test shapes need corners well past the threshold, as a real zigzag has.
- A rule that counts something inside a `map` must count in a local variable: reading the array being mapped gives the count from before the pass, and two riders boarded a full train. A seeded test of hundreds of random marks found it; the hand-written cases did not.
- Wagons that follow the engine's exact tracks fold onto it on a zigzag and the train becomes a heap. Coupled as trailers they cannot, and it took one small class.
- A drawing made of pattern strokes costs its time when the canvas is next copied, not when it is drawn, so a timer round the drawing calls shows nothing. Time the frame.
- An ink that already has the grain in it (a pattern of the colour with the grain knocked out) makes a live line and a kept mark look the same with no second pass.
- A chalk figure stands above its rail, so marks along the top edge take it out of the picture. Decide where chalk may go from the size of the figure.
- A helper that plays a cycle for a test (go to whoever is first in play) can trap itself where a child would not: it found a rider crowded out of the train by one fetched early, which became a rule (a wagon is kept for the layout in play).
- Places where things can stand run out fast. Eight places could not hold three riders with their stops and homes; twelve can, and a layout still needs a fallback.
