### What the next builder should know

- **Solve the circuit, do not script it.** A circuit of pads, traces, leads and parts is a small linear system: nodal analysis with each cell as a push in parallel with its own small resistance needs no extra unknowns, and Gaussian elimination over forty nodes is nothing. Every cell of the grid then falls out as a test (`solve.test.ts`) and no cell needs a special case.
- **Give every pad a tiny leak to ground** so that a pad nothing reaches does not make the system singular. It shows as a difference in the ninth decimal, so compare currents to six.
- **Let a job prove itself.** `jobs.test.ts` lays out 120 jobs at every position and checks that each arrives broken and that one plain mend of each break, made only with the finger's acts and the tray, makes it run. The layout itself refuses a second break that undoes the first (a flat cell beside one that pushes the wrong way lights a lamp dimly).
- **A waiting customer needs its own idea.** With the next customer laid out a cycle ahead, and one job in three holding an earlier kind of break, anything that depends on "the idea of this job" must be stored with the job. Read from the stored position it is wrong twice over.
- **Whatever the sheet says stays must have a field.** The first check found three things the sheet said stay and the save had no place for: a part lying loose, the old hand's mended board, and which idea a job was for. Read the sheet for the word "stays" before writing the save.
- **A claim about guessing is a rule.** "No single lead or swap makes it run" is only true if the layout refuses the pairs one act would mend, and a test tries every act.
- **The hand-back takes the stall's tool away.** A mend that only works through the test lamp must not count, so the owner's try runs with the test lamp off the board.
- **On a 2D canvas a shadow ignores the transform.** Scale blur and offset by hand, and cast each part's shadow once from a plain footprint, or every inner shape darkens the one under it.
- **Let the toy be a controller with no drawing in it.** `toy.ts` takes stage points and leaves a list of voices and marks. Whole touches are then played in tests, hundreds at random, and "every press is answered in that call" becomes an assertion.
- **Keep the toy's state in the game's own save.** The toy's circuit sits in the stall's `job`, so found-as-left came from the reader the game will use, and nothing has to be thrown away at the game stage.
- **A lift that finishes the act must end the drag.** See the template note on `input.ts`: without it the second of two quick touches a hand's width apart is lost in silence.
- **Draw the hit target where the finger thinks it is.** A boot placed by a share of the lead's length sat on the pad for a short lead. It now stands a fixed distance back along the lead, and the view and the hit test read the same function.
- **Look at the lit moment before anything else.** The first fix set was all about things covering what had just happened: the moth on the lamp, the lead on the flag.
- **Read the sheet for what touches what.** The second check found that the sheet joined two leads end to end and held a buzzer by its leads, while the save knew only pads. If the sheet lets a clip bite it, the model needs a node for it.
- **Give every kind of join a node and let one solver do the rest.** A pad, each end of a loose part and each clip of a lead are all nodes; a clip takes the node of what it bites. Joined leads and a part hanging by its leads then needed no rule of their own.
- **When numbers name things, one function takes things out.** Leads bite leads by number, so `tidy` in `circuit.ts` is the only place a lead or a loose part leaves: it lets clips go, turns a lead end for end, and closes the numbering.
- **Keep the carried part in the saved circuit.** A part in the hand is left out of the circuit that is solved and drawn, and stays where it came from in the one that is saved. "Nothing is saved in the air" then needs no code at the moment of saving.
- **Name each act in the grid's own terms.** The controller says what the finger did (`Did` in `benchSound.ts`) and one function turns that, with the board before and after, into voices. Every cell of the grid then has one place it is answered, and the tests read like the sheet.
- **Count the frame, do not time it.** A recording 2D context that only counts fills, strokes and stamps holds "one full-surface stamp a frame" in a test, on any runner (`budget.test.ts`).
- **Let the overlap test design the layout.** It moved the sign, the bench odds and the test lamp, each a few units into a neighbour, and it is why parts have a room and why each board keeps its own loose parts.
- **Seed the slot from the game's own modules for stills.** On the dev server a page can import the game's modules, build any stall, write it to the slot and reload. Every state of the game is then one still away, with no debug code in the game.
