### Template notes

- `fix-it-stall.tsx` (the Mount): changed in one place. `draw` calls the spike with the canvas's 2D context, the size, the pixel ratio and the attended clock's seconds, and writes the count of sprites and figures into `drawn.drawCalls`. Everything else is as copied.
- `audio.ts`, `input.ts`, `guidance.ts`, `scene.ts` and the four frozen files: as copied, untouched. `overlay.ts` and its test were as copied until run 7, which changed the gesture (below).
- **For the template:** a shadow's blur and offset on a 2D canvas ignore the transform, so a painter that scales its scene has to scale them by hand (`lifted` in `paint.ts`). A canvas game's first helper will hit this.
- `config.ts`: `LADDER` holds the ten position ids and `FIRST_VISIT` its two rows. Nothing else changed.
- `state.ts`: untouched, and wrapped by `save.ts` as its header says to: `deserializeStall` calls `deserialize` for the three template fields and reads the same raw record again for its own.
- **For the template:** `finishCycle` returns the whole object it was given with two fields changed, typed as `GameState`. A wrapper has to spread it back over its own state (`{ ...stall, ...judged }` in `cycle.ts`) to keep its type. A generic signature, `<S extends GameState>(state: S, …): S`, would save every game that wraps the state the same line.
- **For the template:** `FIRST_VISIT` as generated puts its second row at the band's oldest age. A band of four ages wanted it one age earlier, which the file allows; the comment could say the row's age is the game's choice.

- `fix-it-stall.tsx` (the Mount), at the toy stage, as the pilot notes say: `draw` has a case with no state (the bare mat); the toy is built when the slot has been read and draws once there; the toy queues its voices and the Mount plays them in the gesture's handler and again after the step; what `ladder.update` returns is kept and handed to `draw`; `seed` is read from the address. One finger works at a time, as copied.
- **For the template:** `input.ts` keeps a drag alive for 300 ms after a lift, and a new touch within 140 px in that time is read as the same drag coming back. Where the lift itself finished the act (a clip that bites where the finger lifts), the next touch nearby is swallowed with no answer. The lamp's two legs are 112 apart, so the second lead of a loop vanished. The Mount now calls `touch.clear()` when a lift has put the thing down. A game needs a way to say "this drag is over" on `dragLift`; a return value from the handler, or a method named for it, would do.
- **For the template:** `audio.ts` holds only the newest voice of a touch that could not sound yet. A first touch that sets off three voices is heard as one. That is right for a first tap; a game with chains may want the last two or three.
- `config.ts`: `BACKDROP` is the mat's grey.

- At the game stage: `scene.ts` is used as copied, with the scene's `finish()` called first thing in every press, inside the controller. `guidance.ts` as copied: what `ladder.update` returns is kept, and the ladder is touched while a scene plays. `symbols.ts` and `symbols.test.ts` are the template's, copied at the base branch's commit named in the game run page, unchanged.

A note in `scene.ts` that either way is fine would save the next builder the doubt.
- **For the template:** nothing in it stamps a band of the still layer back over moving figures. Any game whose characters stand behind something (a counter, a wall, a table edge) needs that one call; it is in `benchView.ts` and costs a thin strip, not a full-surface composite.

### For the owner to decide

- **The plus and minus signs on a cell.** A real cell has them printed on its ends, and both are in the jam's list of signs. They would mark a pole, not a quantity, so the sheet does not use them: the two ends of a cell are told apart by shape and colour. May they be drawn on the cell as on a real one?
- **The numeral on an order ticket.** The sheet lays a numeral, 2 or 3, beside the drawn group of parts a customer asks for, from the position `ticket` on. The drawing carries the order without it. It is a quantity asked for, which the default on readings allows; if the owner would rather have no numeral in this game, the ticket works without.
- **A lamp that blows.** Three cells on one lamp blow it: the lamp becomes a gap and the tray gives another. It is true, large and free, and the state otherwise stays. If a part that breaks reads as a lost piece, the lamp can dazzle without blowing.
- **The look, again.** He said no to the game as it was brought in. The look pass is the answer, in the same style; what to try first is in the status block.
- **The old hand picks up what the child lays down beside her.** She looks at it and puts it back exactly where it lay, the circuit never changes, and she lets go the moment the finger comes near. If a part leaving its place for three seconds reads as something taken away, the rule is in one place (`REACH` in `cast.ts`: with no places in it she never does it).
- **The clockwork mouse.** A toy on the bare mat that answers a tap and has nothing to do with any mend. It is there so that the mat is never an empty page at rest, and it is gone the moment a board comes down. If it draws the first tap away from the gadget the customer holds out, it can stay in its matchbox until the first cycle has ended.
- **How a part is turned round.** A part dragged a finger's width and let go over its own place is turned round; dragged further it is carried. It is one gesture for one finger, and a child may turn a part without meaning to. If he would rather have turning as its own, slower act, the rule is in one place (`setDown` in `bench.ts`).
- **Sending a customer away.** A tap on the customer who waits, while the gadget on the mat does not run, sends its owner off with it as it is, as the sheet has it. One stray tap ends a cycle badly and moves the position down. If that is too easy to do by accident, the sheet's rule changes first. A second tap in the same place within a second of the first, as the ghost hand shows two, does nothing but have the customer answer (`SETTLES` in `bench.ts`): that guards the hand's own double tap, on the gadget held out and on the one who waits, and not a stray tap later.

## For the pull request

### How the game meets the quality bar

- **Alive at idle.** The old hand breathes, blinks and does one of nine small things of her own, and meddles with what lies within her reach; each customer breathes at its own rate, does one of three small things of its own and follows the hand with its eyes; somebody goes by down the lane, a pigeon struts on a doorstep, a clockwork mouse goes round the bare mat, the soldering iron smokes; a live loop runs its beads, a lit lamp's glow breathes, a blade turns and stirs the leads. All on the attended clock, all stopped when the game rests. Nothing asks, flashes or beckons.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** Every press is answered in its handler. Tests play 500 random touches on the bench and 2500 in the overlap play, and each press queues a voice in that call. Every object of the grid has a voice for each of the five actions, and each wrong use its own. No one has listened to them.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** Leads swing on an under-damped spring and settle; a dropped clip falls to where it lies; a flicked part hops with its shadow left behind; a carried part is drawn larger with its shadow further off; each customer's channels follow on its own spring, the tortoise slow and heavy, the magpie quick. A part set down squashes and raises a ring of dust, a board comes down with a settle, a short leaves soot, a blown lamp its glass, a finger its print, and at a pop every coat, crest and fringe on screen stands on end and comes down in its own time.
- **Kid-clear.** One board in the middle of a grey mat, green on grey, copper and silver on green; parts plain, with no faces. Everything round it (the stall, the lane, the old hand's clutter) is paler and greyer than anything the finger works with. A break in plain sight is a patch of bare board with a black crack. A pad on a gadget answers within 34 of its middle at the scene's own size, 68 across; a little less on the sign and never under 48.
- **Wordless clarity for the declared age.** No letter and no word. One numeral, on an order ticket, beside the drawn parts it counts, drawn through `symbols.ts`; the ticket reads without it and nothing in play depends on it. `npm run -s wordless:check` passes.
- **Wordless guidance.** One want in every scene and one move that is never a mend: a tap on the gadget held out; the test lamp's clip to the cell; the gadget by its lid to its owner; a tap on the customer who waits. A test does each shown move and checks that it does what it shows and mends nothing.
- **60 fps on a mid-range iPad.** Not measured by the builder, and no physical iPad was measured. The lead measured the game as it was before the look pass on a graphics card (60 frames a second at six times CPU throttle); the frame as it is now has not been measured. This machine draws in software, so no frame rate is given; the game's own work a frame is in the pass log. Counted in a test instead: a frame that only draws solves nothing; a touch solves a bounded number of times; a frame of the busiest board, and of the stall at rest with everything flicked at once, draws at most 80 figures and 700 paint calls with exactly one full-surface stamp.
- **Procedural or committed assets only.** Everything is drawn or synthesized at run time. `node scripts/egress-check.ts`, `npm run egress:built` and `npm run education:built` pass.
- **Its own art direction.** Electronics bench, the first reserved look; `ART.md` under "The look", "The game" and "The look pass", and the registry row at its end.
- **Nothing passes through anything.** A canvas game: its own model tests (`overlap.test.ts`). Every part has a room on the board that the model keeps clear and the painters draw to; a loose part lies only on bare mat, one to a place; a clip is exactly on what it bites; the fixed things of the stall stand clear of each other with either board down.
- **Found as left.** The stall is saved as it changes, at once at a scene's start and at a cycle's end, and read back defensively field by field. A put-away with a lead or a part in the hand saves the lead with one clip loose and the part where it came from, and the game going to rest does the same on screen: the touch is forgotten, not ended as a lift, so nothing bites, is seated or is handed back that the child did not do (`letGo` in `bench.ts`). On load no scene replays: a finished cycle shows its owner as the reaction left them, the gadget running, the old hand's board as she left it, and the next customer waiting.
- **No engagement mechanics.** No score, count, streak, timer or praise. The position is hidden and moves only between cycles. A reaction is about the gadget and never about the child.

### What the next builder should know

- **Solve the circuit, do not script it.** A circuit of pads, traces, leads and parts is a small linear system: nodal analysis with each cell as a push in parallel with its own small resistance needs no extra unknowns, and elimination row by row over forty nodes is nothing. Every cell of the grid then falls out as a test (`solve.test.ts`) and no cell needs a special case.
- **Give every pad a tiny leak to ground** so that a pad nothing reaches does not make the system singular. It shows as a difference in the ninth decimal, so compare currents to six.
- **Let a job prove itself.** `jobs.test.ts` lays out 120 jobs at every position and checks that each arrives broken and that one plain mend of each break, made only with the finger's acts and the tray, makes it run. The layout itself refuses a second break that undoes the first (a flat cell beside one that pushes the wrong way lights a lamp dimly).
- **A waiting customer needs its own idea.** With the next customer laid out a cycle ahead, and one job in three holding an earlier kind of break, anything that depends on "the idea of this job" must be stored with the job. Read from the stored position it is wrong twice over.
- **Whatever the sheet says stays must have a field.** The first check found three things the sheet said stay and the save had no place for: a part lying loose, the old hand's mended board, and which idea a job was for. Read the sheet for the word "stays" before writing the save.
- **A claim about guessing is a rule.** "No single lead or swap makes it run" is only true if the layout refuses the pairs one act would mend, and a test tries every act.
- **The hand-back takes the stall's tool away.** A mend that only works through the test lamp must not count, so the owner's try runs with the test lamp off the board.
- **On a 2D canvas a shadow ignores the transform.** Scale blur and offset by hand, and cast each part's shadow once from a plain footprint, or every inner shape darkens the one under it.
- **Let the toy be a controller with no drawing in it.** The toy's controller (now `bench.ts`) takes stage points and leaves a list of voices and marks. Whole touches are then played in tests, hundreds at random, and "every press is answered in that call" becomes an assertion.
- **Keep the toy's state in the game's own save.** The toy's circuit sits in the stall's `job`, so found-as-left came from the reader the game will use, and nothing has to be thrown away at the game stage.
- **A lift that finishes the act must end the drag.** See the template note on `input.ts`: without it the second of two quick touches a hand's width apart is lost in silence.
- **Draw the hit target where the finger thinks it is.** A boot placed by a share of the lead's length sat on the pad for a short lead. It now stands a fixed distance back along the lead, and the view and the hit test read the same function.
- **Look at the lit moment before anything else.** The first fix set was all about things covering what had just happened: the moth on the lamp, the lead on the flag.
- **Read the sheet for what touches what.** The second check found that the sheet joined two leads end to end and held a buzzer by its leads, while the save knew only pads. If the sheet lets a clip bite it, the model needs a node for it.
- **Give every kind of join a node and let one solver do the rest.** A pad, each end of a loose part and each clip of a lead are all nodes; a clip takes the node of what it bites. Joined leads and a part hanging by its leads then needed no rule of their own.
- **When numbers name things, one function takes things out.** Leads bite leads by number, so `tidy` in `circuit.ts` is the only place a lead or a loose part leaves: it lets clips go, turns a lead end for end, and closes the numbering.
- **Keep the carried part in the saved circuit.** A part in the hand is left out of the circuit that is solved and drawn, and stays where it came from in the one that is saved. "Nothing is saved in the air" then needs no code at the moment of saving.
- **Name each act in the grid's own terms.** The controller says what the finger did (`Did` in `benchSound.ts`) and one function turns that, with the board before and after, into voices. Every cell of the grid then has one place it is answered, and the tests read like the sheet.
- **Count the frame, do not time it.** A recording 2D context that only counts fills, strokes and stamps holds "one full-surface stamp a frame" in a test, on any runner (`budget.test.ts`).
- **Let the overlap test design the layout.** It moved the sign, the bench odds and the test lamp, each a few units into a neighbour, and it is why parts have a room and why each board keeps its own loose parts.
- **A place costs nothing a frame if it stands still.** The whole stall, the lane and the clutter went into the one still layer; the frame's cost went up only by what moves.
- **Characters behind something need the band as tall as the largest of them.** Scaling the customers up left a body showing under the counter until the stamped band was made as tall as the biggest.
- **Give every face one place to look.** A customer's eyes take a point on the stage and nothing else; the cast decides the point (the finger, where something just happened, the board). Six painters then needed one line each.
- **Whoever has something on top needs room for it.** A crest, a pair of feelers and a neck each go up; the awning comes down. Each small customer stands lower by its own amount (`STAND`), and the endings were looked at again at the new size.
- **Take contact sheets.** Six customers at one moment, or one character every 0.7 s for 20 s, pasted into one image by the same page that took them: one look finds what thirty stills would.
- **Reload before seeding the slot.** The page being left writes what it still holds on the way out, and a slot seeded just before that is overwritten.
- **Seed the slot from the game's own modules for stills.** On the dev server a page can import the game's modules, build any stall, write it to the slot and reload. Every state of the game is then one still away, with no debug code in the game.
