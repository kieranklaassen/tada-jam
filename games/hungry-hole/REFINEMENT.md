### For the owner to decide

- **The look and the toy.** Papier-mâché carnival, and a hole that eats what it is slid under and burps it back as a stack. The lead took both as the default.
- **How wide the hole gets.** Each thing widens the mouth by 15% of its own width, so a hole that has eaten the whole of the fullest street is 4.4 units wide on a street 5.2 deep. It is funny and nothing is left to bump into by then, but the owner may prefer a mouth that tops out sooner.
- **How large the things are.** A pie is about 80 px across at 1180 by 820. To keep every thing inside its own foot, the tuba is drawn smaller than a tuba beside a drummer would be.
- **The near crowd.** A row of backs of big heads fills the bottom edge, as the audience the child sits among. It could face the child instead.
- **What "covers" means for a set-down stack.** Here a stack covers a thing when it hides that thing's middle from the eye. A stricter reading (any part hidden) leaves a tall stack no spot at all on most streets.
- **Things that step aside.** A thing standing where a stack will hang at a burp, or where a tall stack has to come down on a full street, steps out of its way. The lead ruled to keep it. A wide thing on a full street can have several units to go, which it covers in a quarter of a second on the ground, or in seven tenths of a second in one leap over what stands in its way. And a tap on the hole where its stack would have no room is now a hiccup with the thing staying down, which the owner may want told from the hiccup of an empty hole.
- **The tower taller than the frame.** The eye now lifts and draws back for it, as the lead ruled. With a whole street on the breath a pie is a little over half as wide on screen as at rest, and the roofs behind the street come into view.
- **How far the mayor leans.** He stays his side of the kerb, so his dive after his hat is smaller than in the toy the owner saw.
- **The whistle is a tally.** When a stack is set down the gate keeper whistles one note for each thing in it, up to twelve, as the sheet has it. Nothing is shown, compared or kept, but it can be heard as a count of what the child made, and the owner may want it softened to one rising whistle that is longer for a taller stack.
- **What a put-away does.** Whatever was on its way when the game is put away arrives unseen: the hole reaches the end of its glide and takes what it was sent for, a hanging stack comes down, a float that was about to go out has gone. The child comes back to the street a load would show. The other reading, a game that stands still while it is away and carries on from the same frame, would differ from a load.

### What the next builder should know

- The stage is built with no renderer and no canvas (the paper texture is a `DataTexture` made from numbers), so `view/carnival.test.ts` builds it under vitest's node environment and counts the frame budget instead of timing it.
- Colours are handled as display values in the shaders, which write them straight out; there is no colour-space conversion to account for.
- A thing is in the belly from the moment it tips, and a let-down stack is on the street from the moment it drops: `Play.world()` is always at rest, whatever is on screen.
- Everything that moves on the street by itself (a step aside, a stack's hop and walk) takes a turn in `Play.turns` and looks at its way in `setOff` as it sets off. A new move belongs there too, or the frame-by-frame tests in `play.test.ts` will find it in something.
- `Play.found` is the one place a street is read at rest: the constructor and `rest` both end in it, so a load and a put-away cannot drift apart. Anything new that eases, springs or counts time is reset there.
- A sound that starts after its cue is a note with a `delay`; the Mount keeps it in `Later` on the game's clock (`heard` in `later.ts` is the Mount's whole note path). A sound that belongs to a moment of the model (a landing, the lip letting go, the gate's leaves meeting, a thing coming up) is a cue pushed in that frame, never a delay; `landings.test.ts` holds every landing to it, and a new thing that comes down belongs in that test. A note that should stop with something carries a `cause`, and the model pushes an `over` cue when that thing ends.
