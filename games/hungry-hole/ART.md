# Design sheet

Written before any game code, and checked by someone who did not write it before the game is built on the toy. The headings stay in this order. The look follows the sheet at the end of this file.

What each heading asks for is in the section "The design sheet" of `docs/solutions/conventions/building-a-jam-game.md`.

## The band and its age rule

Band: 4 to 8, as in `manifest.ts`. The youngest age, 4, governs every choice below.

- **Age rule.** (pack: game-design, ages-4-to-6.md) The game supplies a place, props and bystanders who react, and the child supplies the plot. It is played with a tap and a drag, and nothing needs reading or a double tap. It is funny through objects used wrongly, tricks and a bystander who overreacts and is never hurt. Everything essential also works with a tap alone: a tap on a thing calls the hole to it, and a tap on the hole burps.
- **Cue table.** The row for a band that starts at 3 to 4 governs. One next act is offered at a time, a glow marks what can be touched now, and the ghost hand shows one move. Its "Avoid" column binds: no text, numeral or icon to decode, no spoken instruction, no verdict, never several activities live at once, and no tool on screen before it means something.
- **Symbol rule.** The band starts below 6, so the kid side shows no word, letter, numeral or symbol, optional or not. The game has no `symbols.ts`.
- **What `ctx.childAge` sets.** Only how full the street of a first visit is: 5 and younger starts at `few`, 6 and older at `some`. `null` starts at `few`. A saved position wins over the age, and the age never hides or locks anything.

## The toy

**Sliding a hole under a thing until it tips in.** A round hole lies in a carnival street. The finger lands anywhere on the street and in that same frame the hole's rim stretches toward it and the hole starts to glide there with a low whoosh, a little behind the finger. As its rim comes under a thing, the thing leans over the edge and wobbles there in its own way with its own rattle. Once the middle of the thing is over the mouth, and the mouth is wider than the thing, it tilts, slides and drops in, turning as it falls and getting darker, with a bloop that is higher for a small thing and lower for a big one. The rim bulges as it swallows, a ring of torn paper scraps hops up round it, and the hole is left a little wider, because of what is now in it. The last thing eaten can be seen dimly in the mouth.

A thing wider than the mouth cannot go in. The mouth stops at its foot, in full view beside it, and tugs: the lip stretches under the foot in quick pulls and stands up taller, with a creak that climbs, and the thing teeters above it. After a moment, or as soon as the hole is led away from the foot, the mouth lets go with a puff, heard in the frame the lip lets go, and the thing settles, leaning. It teeters again for as long as the hole moves at its foot, and at every tap on it.

**A tap on the hole burps.** The finger lands on the hole and the rim draws in with a short breath. On the lift the hole burps: the last thing eaten shoots up out of the mouth and rides there on the hole's breath, bobbing above a fan of fluttering paper streamers, and the hole is a little narrower again. Another tap burps the next thing up underneath the first, and they stick. The stack hides the mouth it rides over, so that tap lands on a thing of the stack: the thing answers as itself and alone, where it hangs, in the frame of the touch, and the touch is the hole's as well, whose rim draws in and which burps on the lift. A thing of a stack the hole has left calls the hole back under it. A thing that stands where the stack will hang takes one quick step aside first, and is saved where it steps to: where several must step they go one after the other, on the ground where the way is clear and over a low thing where it is not, and the thing coming up waits down in the mouth, with the hole staying where it is, until they have: the tap is answered in its frame by the rim's twitch and a low growl, and the pop, the thing's own sound, the sound of what it comes up under, the scraps and the bystanders come in the frame the thing comes up. Where the stack would have no room to hang, because a thing has no spot to step to or because a thing that is hopping down or walking has its way there, the tap is a hiccup and the thing stays down; it comes up at the next tap that has room. When the hole moves out from under them, or a thing on the street begins to tip into the mouth beneath them, the stack drops onto the street and stands there as one thing, and its thump, the gate keeper's whistle and his rise, and the scraps round its foot come in the frame it lands, however long it hung first; the thing tips in once the stack is out of its way. What a riding stack was playing stops in the frame it drops: no note of it starts after that. It drops onto a street that stands still: while another thing is still hopping down, walking or stepping aside, the stack hangs where it rode and drops when that thing has come to rest, and a tap on the hole that has left it is a hiccup meanwhile. A stack goes back in whole, bottom first, when the mouth is wider than its bottom thing; when it is not, the stack teeters like any thing too wide. A tap on an empty hole is a dry hiccup with a puff of scraps.

**A tap on a thing** makes it answer as itself (the dog squeaks, the tuba honks), in a stack the thing the finger is on, where it is drawn at its height (riding on the breath, hanging, hopping down, leaping over another, hopping or bouncing in its own walk, a balloon on top where it floats), in motion as well as in sound even while it hangs, hops or walks, when it moves what is its own to move and keeps its way, and calls the hole, which glides over and, if the thing fits, eats it. So the simplest use always works: tap things and they go in, tap the hole and they come out.

Why it is a pleasure with no goal (pack: game-design, toy-first.md): it is dropping things into a hole and getting them back, which children do unprompted with drains, boxes and letter slots. Every thing has its own wobble on the rim and its own way of going in, the pitch of the bloop tells its size, and the hole visibly swells with what it was fed. The answer starts when the finger lands and is bigger than the touch: the hole lurches, the thing wobbles, drops and bloops, the rim bulges, scraps fly, and the bystanders flinch (pack: game-design, touch-answers-bigger-than-the-touch.md). Random tapping always produces something, and nothing is ever used up: whatever went in comes out again. A person watching sees within three seconds what the child is doing: feeding a hole.

**Idle.** With no touch the bunting flutters, the crowd sways, the mayor reaches for his hat, the drummer keeps silent time with his sticks, and the hole's rim laps slowly toward the nearest thing that fits. After a few seconds the hole and that thing glow, and then the ghost hand shows one move: a tap on that thing; or, as soon as a tap on the hole would stand one thing on another (two things inside, or one inside and one on the breath), and whenever there is something in the hole and nothing left that fits, a tap on the hole, while the thing that fits glows on; or, when everything rides on the breath, a tap on the bare street beside it, which calls the hole away and lets the stack down; or, on the bare street after a float, a tap on the gate keeper, who glows together with the gate.

## The object-by-action grid, and what is new on day 15

Six things by five actions. The things are named smallest first. Every cell looks and sounds different, and nothing is refused.

| | Touched where it stands | On the rim (the hole partly under it, or too narrow for it) | Dropped in | Burped out on top of others | Pushed up underneath, at the bottom of a stack |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pie** | The cream wobbles and the cherry hops, with a squelch. | The plate skates round the edge and the cream leans the other way, with a plate rattle. | It flips cream side down and slides off its plate, and the plate follows a moment later: a splat, then a small high bloop. | It lands as a dish on whatever is below, cream up, and the cherry lands last with a plip. | It is squashed flat with a squish, whatever stands on it sinks in to the ankles, and the stack slides along on the plate leaving a cream smear, with a wet scrape for each slide. |
| **Hat** | The brim flips up and down like a lid, with a hollow tok. | It rocks on its brim like a bell, knocking tok-tok on the edge. | It turns over and drops crown first with a hollow pop and a bloop. | It is worn: it sits on the thing below, tips to one side, and slides down over the eyes of a thing narrower than itself, with a soft fwump. | It stands upside down as a bucket with the next thing sitting in it, and the stack hops along like a frog, clop for each hop. |
| **Dog** (a sausage dog on wheels) | Its head nods on its spring, its tail twangs, and it squeaks twice. | Its front wheels hang over the edge and spin, its ears flap, and it whimpers in short squeaks over a wheel whirr. | It noses down and rolls in on its wheels with a long yelp that falls in pitch into the bloop. | It rides, wagging, and turns its head to wherever the hole goes, with a yap. | It carries the stack on its back and rolls along with the load swaying, wheels squeaking in time and a pant between squeaks. |
| **Balloon** | It bobs on its string and bounces twice, with a boing. | It bulges over the edge and rubs against the rim with a rubber squeal, its string pulled tight. | It is sucked thin through the mouth and springs round again inside: a squeal that slides up, then a pop into the bloop. | It floats up to the end of its string above the stack and pulls the whole stack onto tiptoe, humming a high wavering note. | It is squashed wide under the load and the stack bounces along on it, a boing for each bounce that is lower the heavier the load. |
| **Tuba** | Its bell swings toward the finger and it honks two notes, oom-pah. | It rolls round on the rim of its bell like a coin settling, with a ringing wobble that quickens. | It goes in bell first and swallows its own honk: a honk that slides down into the bloop and echoes. | It is played: it lies on the thing below with its bell up, the thing below puffs, and each bob honks one note, lower the bigger the thing that plays it. | It stands on its bell with the next thing sitting in it, whose sound now comes out deep through the tuba, and the stack waddles with a parp for each step. |
| **Drummer** (a bandsman with a big head and a bass drum) | He gives one extra loud beat and a cymbal crash with his cheeks puffed: boom-tish. | He marches on the spot faster and faster with his arms windmilling, and his beat stumbles: rat-a-tat-thud. | He falls in backwards still drumming, boom, boom, each beat more muffled; from inside he plays on, a muffled beat for every stretch of street the hole crosses, joined by a muffled oom-pah when the tuba is in there too. | He sits astride the thing below and drums on it, a steady boom from up high, lighter the smaller the thing he sits on. | He marches with the whole stack on his head and keeps time for it, a boom on every left foot. |

**How a stack reads.** A stack is named from the bottom up, in the order the things went in. Only the bottom thing behaves as in the last column: it carries the rest and gives the stack its walk. Every thing above it behaves as in the fourth column. So a dog, a hat and a tuba, fed in that order and burped in a row, come back as a dog that rolls along wearing a hat that plays a tuba. Fed the other way round they come back as a tuba on its bell with a hat sitting in it, whose fwump comes out deep, and a dog riding on top.

**The wrong use always works and is funny** (pack: game-design, liveliness-from-causing-and-comedy.md): the last column is the wrong use of each thing, the pie as a shoe, the hat as a bucket, the dog as a cart, the balloon as a cushion, the tuba as a seat, the drummer as a pedestal. Feeding the hole a thing too wide for it gets the teeter. Tapping an empty hole gets the hiccup. Tapping a bystander gets that bystander's own answer and brings the hole to the kerb at their feet, where they look down at it. None gets a buzzer or a refusal.

**Secrets that work every time, never hinted at or counted** (pack: game-design, hidden-never-counted.md): the drummer on top of the tuba on top of the dog plays a whole march as it rolls; a balloon at the bottom and a balloon on top lifts the stack clear of the street for one long bounce; the pie on top of the hat on top of everything makes the mayor cover his eyes and peek.

**On day 15** the child feeds the hole in an order chosen for what will come out. Each of six things is worn or used one way on top and carries another way underneath, so two things make up to thirty-four kinds of pair before any stack of three: every kind on every kind, except that a street has one hat and one drummer and neither can stand on itself. By then the child knows that the last thing in is the first out and ends on top, that the hole takes small things before it can take a big one, and that some of what is in the hole can be left there while the rest is burped; with that the child builds the stack they have in mind on purpose and sets it down in front of the bystander whose face they want to see. No new kind of thing arrives on day 15: the six kinds and the rules are those of day 1, and a fuller street only holds more of them (pack: game-design, depth-from-combinations.md).

## The error as a consequence

Nothing in the game is wrong, and two things can go other than the child meant. Each shows where and why in the world, costs nothing, and leaves everything where it is (pack: game-design, errors-show-as-consequences.md).

- **The thing is too wide for the hole.** The mouth lies at its foot and strains at it, the lip stretched under the foot and the thing teetering above, so the child sees the whole mouth beside the thing and that the mouth is the narrower of the two. It stays where it stood. The child feeds the hole something smaller, sees the mouth widen, and comes back.
- **The stack came out in another order than the child had in mind.** It stands there as what it is, behaving as its order makes it, and the bystanders react to exactly that. Swallowed whole it goes back in the same order; burped up and set down one thing at a time, then fed again in a new order, it comes out new.

No buzzer, no cross, no face turned to the child, no reset and no lost thing. A stack that works as the child meant is a consequence too: it walks, sounds and is looked at as that stack.

## The designed order, and what is stored

**The game has no learning goal and no order of challenges.** What follows is what a first visit starts with by age, what play changes, and what is stored.

**How full a street is.** A street is laid out with one of three sets of things. Their ids stand in `LADDER` in `config.ts` and name how full the street is, nothing else.

- `few`: three pies, the hat, a dog, two balloons, a tuba and the drummer.
- `some`: the same with a second dog.
- `many`: the same again with a fourth pie, a third dog and a second tuba.

On every street the empty hole is wide enough for a pie and nothing else, each thing fed widens it in proportion to the thing's own width, and the whole street can be eaten in some order. Whenever the hole is empty, a pie or a stack standing on a pie is on the street, so something always fits and no child is stuck; a test holds both for each set, the second from every state the model can reach. A fuller street looks fuller, and nothing else shows which set is laid out.

**A first visit, by age.** 5 and younger: `few`. 6 and older: `some`. No age: `few`.

**What play changes.** When a float has gone out of the gate, the street that comes in next is one step fuller, up to `many`. That is the only move, it happens between streets and never inside one, and nothing steps down: no street goes badly. A visit put away with no float leaves the set as it was. A saved set wins over the child's age. No clock is read.

**How the hole is steered, and what is not a move.** A press on the street, on a thing or on a bystander sends the hole toward that point, where it stops short at the foot of a thing too wide for it that stands there; a drag leads it; a press on the hole itself holds it still, also in the middle of a glide, and its lift, if the finger did not drag, is the burp. A touch in the grown-up's corner at the top right of the surface is asked for first and is answered by nothing: it calls nothing, ends no scene and does not count as the child's for the idle guidance, and no part of the gate that answers a finger lies there. A drag has no drop, so nothing is in the hand: when a drag is taken away (the game is parked under the finger, or the browser takes the touch) the hole stops where it is, and whatever it ate on the way stays eaten, since the child fed it. A press that is taken away is not a tap and burps nothing.

**Found as left.** Every field below is written as it changes, and the game opens exactly as it was put away, with no scene replayed. A put-away is the same as a load. Whatever is on its way when the game is put away arrives unseen and unheard: the hole at the end of its glide with what it takes there, a thing going in, a stack hopping down or walking, a street coming in, a float going out. No reaction and no note is left running, no tail of a note is heard on coming back, and the game that comes back to the child is the one a load of that save shows. Nothing starts on a load or on coming back: a thing the rim is under is found leaning over it as it leant, with no wobble, no tug and no sound, so no bystander has anything to jump at. A stack found riding plays on from its first frame, and not a bob late; before the first touch of a visit that is not heard, since the sound starts with the touch. The mayor is found level with his hat, or in the middle of his rail when no hat is on the street, after a put-away as after a load.

- A thing that is tilting into the hole is already in `belly`: the fall is a view. A stack that is dropping from the breath to the street is already in `street` at the spot where it lands. A thing wobbling on the rim is a view of `hole` and `street` and needs no field.
- A stack riding on the breath is at rest for as long as the hole stays under it, so it is saved as it is, in `breath`, and rides there again on load, with the hole under it. A stack the hole has left that still hangs, waiting for the street to stand still, is let down by a put-away and is found standing on the street where it would have come to stand; a save written while it hung, with no put-away after it, shows it riding with the hole under it.
- A set-down stack never stops on or against another thing, the hole, or the strip under the bystanders: it drops onto the nearest spot of the street clear of all of them, then walks a few steps in its bottom thing's walk to a spot that is clear as well, and what is saved from the moment it drops is the spot where it will stand. A spot is clear only if the stack standing there covers no other thing or stack, nor the gate keeper or the gate, as the child sees them. On a street so full that no spot is clear for a tall stack, it is set down where the least has to move, and not in front of the gate keeper or the gate while any other spot will do: each thing that stands where it comes down, behind it or in front of it steps to a clear spot of its own first, one thing after the other, on the ground where the way is clear, round the hanging stack where it can and over a low thing where it has no other way; the stack hangs where it rode until the last of them has set off, then hops down, over whatever stands between it and its spot, and each of those things is saved where it steps to. No spot where a thing or a stack stands or the hole can rest, and no part of the gate keeper or the gate, lies under the jam's round home control at the top centre of the surface. An overlap test holds both at 1180 by 820 and at each other surface size the game's tests name. Nothing the child made leaves the frame: for a stack taller than the frame the eye lifts, and then draws back, until the top of the stack is seen below the home control, and it comes back when the stack is gone.
- What a bystander does (the mayor leaning toward his hat, the gate keeper fanning the air) is rebuilt from the fields and is not stored. A reaction in progress is short-lived, gone on load and ended by a put-away.
- The cream smear a pie leaves, the scraps round the rim and anything else that marks the street are short-lived and gone on load.

**Every field of the saved state** (plain JSON, versioned, read defensively: a damaged field takes its default and the rest is kept; a largest street serializes far under half of the 64 KB cap, and a test says so):

- `v`: the version of the record.
- `position`: the id of the set the street on screen was laid out with (`few`, `some` or `many`), and after a float the set of the street that waits. An id the game does not know falls back to the first-visit default.
- `finished`: the float of this street has gone out, the street is bare, and the next street waits behind the gate.
- `seed`: the whole number the street on screen was laid out from, and after a float the number the waiting street will be laid out from.
- `hole`: where the hole is on the street, as `x` and `z` in street units.
- `belly`: the things inside the hole, in the order they went in. The last one is the next to be burped, and the width of the mouth is computed from this list.
- `breath`: the stack riding on the hole's breath, from the bottom up; empty when nothing rides.
- `street`: every thing or stack standing on the street, each with its `parts` from the bottom up and the `x` and `z` where it stands.

While `finished` is false a thing is in exactly one of `belly`, `breath` and `street`, and a record in which a thing is missing or doubled is repaired to the street's own set: the missing things are stood on free spots, and a doubled thing is dropped from the later list. While `finished` is true all three lists are empty, and a record that holds anything in them is emptied, so a finished street is never laid out again on load.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

The hole and the things are the child's. The bystanders have opinions, their faces and bodies are the only feedback, and their feelings are about what happens in the street, never about the child (pack: game-design, characters-with-opinions.md).

- **The mayor** stands on his stand at the far kerb: a huge head, a moustache, a sash and a bald crown. **He wants his hat.** His arm is always stretched toward wherever it is, his eyes are on it, and he shuffles along the rail of his stand to stay level with it. He cannot leave the stand, so he never gets it: he chases it for good and nobody is hurt.
  - Likes: his hat on top of anything. He beams, twirls his moustache and tips a hat he does not have at whatever wears it. His hat on the dog is his favourite and makes his belly shake.
  - Dislikes: his hat underneath anything, at which he clutches his bald head and his moustache droops. His hat in the hole, at which he leans out over the rail until his nose is above the kerb and no further, so that he never reaches in among the things on the street, calls into the mouth through his cupped hand, and hears it come back muffled; his call stops in the frame his hat is back out. The balloon's squeal, at which he jumps and his one tuft of hair stands up, every time.
- **The gate keeper** stands at the gate at the end of the street: tall and thin on stilts, with a small cap, a long nose and a big key. **He wants a float to open the gate for.** He holds the key up ready in one hand and the gate bar in the other, and his eyes follow the hole.
  - Likes: tall stacks. The taller the stack that is set down, the higher he rises on his stilts to look it up and down, with a whistle that climbs one step for each thing in it.
  - Dislikes: burps, at each of which he fans the air with his cap, bending further back the bigger the thing that came out. The drummer's noise, at which he puts a finger in each ear and drops his key.
- **The drummer** is a thing and a bystander at once. **He wants to keep time**, his sticks always raised between beats, and he plays on whatever happens to him: on the rim, falling, inside the hole, on top of a stack or under one. He has no likes or dislikes: nothing that happens to him changes his face or stops his beat, and that is his joke.
- **The crowd** along both kerbs has no tastes. Its big heads sway, and they turn to where the hole is.

A bystander who is touched answers as himself and changes nothing: the mayor harrumphs and pats his crown, the gate keeper jingles his key and salutes, the gate rattles its bars as it sounds, on any street, and a head in the crowd ducks and pops up again with a squeak. The one exception is a touch on the gate keeper or the gate on the bare street after a float, which lets the next street in.

## The scenes

Free play needs no first showing of an idea (pack: game-design, guided-discovery.md), so the game has two scenes, the ending of a street and the coming in of the next. Each is a list of timed beats filled in from the state of play, and each gives way to any touch outside the grown-up's corner (pack: game-design, endings-and-short-scenes.md).

**The float goes out** (about 8 seconds).

- Cause: the hole moves out from under a stack on its breath that holds every thing of the street, so nothing stands on the street and nothing is in the hole.
- Saved when it starts, at once: `breath` empty, `street` empty, `finished` true, `position` one step fuller (or the same at `many`), and `seed` set to the number of the street that will wait.
- Beats: the tower drops and sways, with its thud and the scraps round its foot in the frame it touches down. It tips over toward the gate, each thing clacking onto the next, and lies as one long float with its bottom thing in front, each thing turned and leaning a little otherwise than the one before it. The gate keeper swings the gate open and rises on his stilts. The float rolls out in its bottom thing's walk, quietly heard on that thing's wheels or feet at every second step, and each thing sounds as it passes the gate post, so the tune is this order and no other. The mayor follows his hat along the rail to the end of his stand. The gate closes, with its clunk in the frame the leaves meet, and behind its bars two things of the next street can be seen waiting, side by side between the posts.
- Filled in from: the order of the stack.
- A touch: the float is gone, the gate is shut and the next street waits, and no note of the scene starts after the touch. The touch is then an ordinary touch.

**The next street comes in** (about 5 seconds).

- Cause: a touch on the gate keeper or the gate while `finished` is true.
- Saved when it starts, at once: `finished` false, and `street` holding every thing of the new street at the spot where it will stand. No spot is under the hole's mouth or under a bystander.
- Beats: the gate opens with a creak. The things come in one at a time, biggest first, each in its own walk from the last column of the grid, or in one leap over the others on a street so full that it has no way round them, and each stops on its spot with its own sound from the first column, and one that hops or bounces in is heard as each hop lands. The gate shuts, with its clunk in the frame the leaves meet.
- Filled in from: `seed` and `position`, which give the things and their spots.
- A touch: the scene is ended whole. Every thing stands on its spot, the hole lies at its place on the new street and the gate is shut, and no note of the scene starts after the touch. The touch is then an ordinary touch from there, so the hole eats nothing on a way the child did not send it.

**How a street ends and the next begins.** A street ends only when the child has fed the hole everything, burped it all up in a row and moved the hole away. If the child does nothing, nothing ends and nothing starts. After the float the bare street stays as long as the child likes: the hole still glides and hiccups, the gate keeper holds his key up beside the shut gate with the next things in view behind it, and nobody hurries, complains or refers to the child. The next street begins when the child touches the gate keeper or the gate. On load no scene replays: a game put away during the float opens on the bare street with the next one waiting, and one put away while a street came in opens with every thing on its spot.

## The look

**Papier-mâché carnival**, the first look reserved for the game in `docs/art-direction.md`. Everything in the frame is a hard shell of pasted paper strips under matte poster paint, a little lumpy, as if made for a street parade: figures with huge heads, striped posts, tissue fringe and bunting. The second reserved look was not needed: the first is clear at 1180 by 820 and the frame budget holds.

**What is in the frame.** A carnival street seen from above the near kerb. Behind it a row of painted house fronts with shutters and awnings, lamp posts and four strings of bunting. Along the far kerb, from left to right: onlookers, the mayor's striped stand with its rail and fringe, two onlookers behind a rope on striped posts, the gate keeper on his stilts, the gate with its arch and bars, more onlookers. A striped tent stands at each end of the street, and tubs of paper flowers at the corners. Along the near kerb the backs of a row of big heads in hats fill the bottom edge. The street itself is pale sand cobbles inside a red and cream kerb, with confetti trodden into it, and on it stand the things and the hole. The mayor's stand is set back from the far kerb far enough that at his furthest lean his nose is at the kerb and his fingertips short of it: the street belongs to the things.

**Who is large and funny, and what stays plain.** The mayor is the largest figure in the frame: a head wider than his stand's rail is tall, a moustache that curls or droops, brows, pupils and a mouth that each move, one arm always stretched at his hat. The gate keeper is the tallest and thinnest: stilts, a long neck that stretches, a long nose, a key held high, which is a shaft with a ring for its bow and one square tooth (two teeth on a shaft read as a letter). The crowd's heads rock. The six things are the plainest objects in the frame: one or two flat colours each, a simple outline, no face except the dog's two dots and the drummer, who is a bystander too. They are painted at full strength and the setting is mixed toward pale sand (`quiet` in `view/sculpt.ts`), so what can be touched is the strongest colour on screen. The hole is the darkest thing in the frame, and nothing else is dark.

**Sizes, at 1180 by 820.** A pie is about 80 px across and the empty mouth a little more (`view/carnival.test.ts` holds both), the dog about 120 px long, the drummer about 165 px across his drum, and the mayor's head about 180 px. The street is 13 units by 5.2. Every thing is sculpted inside the round foot `kinds.ts` gives it (`stand` in `view/things.ts`), so where a thing stands is all the room it takes.

**What each thing is made of, so that it reads from above.** The pie: a crust, a dome of cream, a cherry, on a plate. The hat: a red top hat with a yellow band and a brim that lifts like a lid. The dog: a short orange sausage on two red axles, a head on a spring with two dots for eyes and flap ears, a tail that stands up. The balloon: a blue ball with a knot, a white string and a small sack that holds it down, set toward the eye. The tuba: two coils tipped back to face the eye with three white valves on them, and beside them a bell that opens upward and toward the eye, dark inside. The drummer: boots, a red coat, a bass drum on his belly with its skin toward the street, a cymbal on a stalk, a big head with cheeks that puff, and two arms with sticks.

**Palette** (display values, painted flat):

- The street: sand `#f4dfb3`, cobble joints a shade darker; pavement rose `#dbb39e`; kerb blocks `#db5c52` and `#f7edd6`.
- Carnival paints, used at full strength on things and bystanders and quieted on the setting: red `#d8343c`, yellow `#ffd23c`, blue `#2f7fe0`, green `#35b07a`, pink `#f08ab0`, cream `#fdf3dc`.
- The things: pie cream `#fffaf0` on crust `#d79a55`; hat red with a yellow band; dog orange `#f2a02c` with red wheels; balloon blue; tuba brass `#f5c22e`; drummer red coat, blue and cream drum.
- The mayor: purple `#6b3f96` coat, yellow sash, skin `#f6c49b`. The gate keeper: teal `#1f9a8a` coat, red and cream stilts, yellow key.
- The hole: a lip of cream, pink and yellow torn layers, then indigo `#120a29`. Shadows are soft violet, never black.
- Sky behind the roofs: `#8fd0ea`.

**Materials.** One material for every figure (`view/paper.ts`): the paint is in the vertices, the strips are one small tiling texture made in code (no image file), laid on from three sides by position, lit as low relief and showing through the paint as tone, with brush streaks. Stripes, bands and dots are drawn in the shader from the part's own shape, with an edge that wanders like a loaded brush. The lumps are pushed into each part once, when it is built. Tissue (bunting, fringe, the lip of the hole, the breath's streamers) is thin two-faced strips that flutter in the vertex shader. The street is one plane painted in its shader, with the hole cut out of it; the pit under it is a real open drum, so a thing really drops in and darkens with depth. No post pass, no shadow maps: every shadow is a soft disc, all of them one draw, cut away over the hole.

**Lighting.** One warm sun from the upper left and a cool sky, on a matte surface with no highlight. It never changes.

**Motion rules.**

- The setting moves only by itself and only a little: bunting and fringe flutter, the crowd's heads rock, scraps of paper drift down. None of it answers a touch except the bystanders.
- A thing has no motion of its own at rest. It moves when the hole or a finger makes it, each in its own way (`motion.ts`: six wobbles on the rim, six falls, six answers to a finger, six walks under a stack and six ways of sitting on one, no curve shared). The drummer alone keeps silent time with his sticks.
- Nothing passes through anything. A thing leans, spreads and slides only as far as the room to its neighbours allows (`inRoom` in `motion.ts`, `room` in `play.ts`); a stack is no wider than its widest thing; when a burp brings a stack up where something stands, that thing takes one quick step aside first, and where the stack would have no room the tap is a hiccup (`roomFor` in `play.ts`); a stack that rides is let down before a thing tips in beneath it, and only onto a street that stands still; a stack with no clear spot to come down on hangs while the things in its way and behind it step out (`setDown` and `stepOut` in `street.ts`); things that step go one after the other, each when no thing still on its way crosses its own, on the ground where the way is clear and over what stands in it where it is not, and a stack hops over whatever stands between it and its spot (`turns` and `setOff` in `play.ts`); the things of a float lie a little askew, so that no face of one lies in the plane of a face of the next (`ASKEW` in `scenes.ts`); the mayor's arm draws in as it points out over the kerb.
- The rim snaps to the finger: the first frame drawn after a touch that calls the hole shows the mouth lying along the way to the finger at its full reach (`lurch` is whole through that frame, `called` in `play.ts`), and it eases back from the next.
- The hole glides a little behind the finger, stretches the way it travels, bulges when it swallows or burps, draws in under a resting finger, and at rest laps toward the nearest thing it could take. At a thing too wide for it the mouth stops with a third of a unit under the thing's foot, on the side the eye sees (`berth` in `play.ts`), and strains: the lip reaches under the foot in quick tugs and stands taller, for 1.4 seconds after the hole rests, and the thing teeters with it; when the tug has died away, or the hole is led off, the lip lets go with its puff.
- A thing of a stack answers a finger by itself, where it sits; the bottom thing's answer moves the stack it carries. A thing touched while it hangs, hops down or walks answers in motion as well as in sound: its limbs move as they do when it stands, and it stretches or squashes upright, while where it is and how it leans stay the hop's or the walk's (`standing` in `view/carnival.ts`). A finger finds a thing where it is drawn at its height: one function, `liftOf` in `play.ts`, gives the height to the view and to `pick.ts`, the walk's own bounce included (the hat's hop, a balloon's bounce: `stepLift` in `motion.ts`).
- A thing that rides on the breath answers a finger as itself and alone, where it hangs, in the frame of the touch. The stack hides the mouth it rides over (with little inside it, the lip shows in front of the stack by less than a finger's width, and not at all under the drummer), so that touch is the hole's as well: the rim draws in, and on the lift the hole burps under the stack. The ghost hand taps the hole on the thing at the bottom of the stack (`holePoint` in `pick.ts`). A thing of a stack the hole has left calls the hole back under it.
- One clock on the breath. How far the riding stack is through its bob is one number in the model (`bob` in `play.ts`): the stack rises and falls on it, the tuba's note and the drummer's boom are heard in the frame it begins again, with both his sticks down on the drum and the thing under a tuba puffed in that frame. A stack found riding on a load or on coming back plays from its first frame, not a bob late; before the first touch of a visit that note is not heard, because the sound has not started.
- The drummer's sticks keep one tempo, 1.2 beats a second, whatever he does (`STICK_HZ` in `motion.ts`); how hard he beats changes only how high they lift, and in the frame of a beat that is heard (a bob of the breath, a step of the stack he rides or carries) they do not lift at all. Marching, his left stick is down as his left foot lands, which is the step his boom is on.
- Sound comes with what is seen: a sound belongs to the frame of the motion it is the sound of. Whatever comes down onto the street is heard in the frame it touches it (`landings.test.ts` holds every landing in the game to that): a stack's thump with the gate keeper's whistle and rise and the scraps round its foot, however long it hung; a leap over another thing, each hop of the hat and each bounce of a balloon, as it lands and not as it sets off; the long bounce of two balloons at its end; the float's tower, whose thud and scraps are a cue of their own pushed by the scene in the frame it touches down; each thing of the float as it tips over; each thing of a new street as it stops. A step that stays on the street is heard as it begins. The gate's clunk is heard in the frame its leaves meet, in the float and in the entry (`shutting` in `scenes.ts`). The puff comes in the frame the lip lets go. A burp that has to wait for a thing to step aside answers the tap in its frame with the rim's twitch and a low growl, and its pop, the thing's own sound, the sat-on sound, the scraps and the bystanders come in the frame the thing comes up (`cameUp` in `play.ts`).
- A note that starts a while after its cue (the cherry's plip, the whistle's further steps, a balloon's hum) waits on the game's clock and starts in the frame its time comes (`later.ts`), so a resting game holds it and a touch that ends a scene drops it. A note that belongs to something is dropped in the frame that thing is over: the notes of a stack on the breath when it is let down, of a thing on its way down when a burp cuts it short, and the mayor's wail when his hat is back out (`over` cues in `play.ts`, `heard` in `later.ts`). No note starts after its cause.
- Put away equals loaded. Going to rest ends a running scene whole and unheard, lets whatever is on its way in the street arrive (`rest` in `play.ts`), ends the bystanders' reactions and stands the mayor level with his hat, or in the middle of his rail when no hat is on the street, as a load does (`settle` in `bystanders.ts`), lands the scraps and drops the notes that wait; what comes back is what a load of the save shows. Every sound goes through one gain that a put-away closes at once and takes away, and the next touch opens a new one, so no tail of a note is heard on coming back (`shut` in `audio.ts`). A load starts nothing: a thing the rim is under is found leaning as it leant, with no cue (`found` in `play.ts`), and a thing that stood close against a riding stack is found where it stood, so the same save is the same street on every load.
- The grown-up's corner at the top right answers nothing: the Mount asks for it before anything else, so a touch there ends no scene, calls nothing and leaves the idle ladder alone. The gate answers a finger on its leaves and posts, up to just over its tallest bar (`GATE_TOUCH` in `pick.ts`); its arch reaches into that corner at 1180 by 820 and is scenery. A finger on the gate rattles its bars on any street (`hear` in `scenes.ts`).
- The eye is still while nothing tall has been made. For a stack whose top would pass the home control (48 px round, 10 px from the top edge, and 8 px of room) the eye lifts along the picture's upright, as far as the near corners of the street allow, and then draws back; it moves out quickly and comes back slowly (`look` in `view/carnival.ts`). The row of heads along the near kerb may slide out of the bottom edge while it is lifted.
- The mayor is slow and heavy (about half a beat a second) and all of him follows his hat. The gate keeper is quick and thin (more than a beat a second) and watches the hole. Each has three fidgets of his own and never does the same one twice running, drawn from a stream of his own that starts from the visit's seed (`bystanders.ts`).
- Paper is stiff: figures tilt, rock, hop and squash a little on landing, and nothing stretches like rubber except the balloon.

**How each tier keeps the look** (`TIERS` in `config.ts`). Every tier draws every figure, the whole setting and the same colours. Tier 0: pixel ratio 2, the strips lit as relief, up to 48 scraps in the air. Tier 1: pixel ratio 1.5, relief on, 32 scraps. Tier 2: pixel ratio 1.25, the relief off (the strips still show as tone and streaks), 20 scraps. Tier 3: pixel ratio 1, relief off, 12 scraps. Nothing is compiled at a tier change: the relief is a number the paint reads.

**Draws.** 31 to 46 draw calls and 25,766 to 35,748 triangles in the stills at tier 0, taken while the key still had two teeth (12 triangles more than now). The frame-budget test (`view/carnival.test.ts`) counts 46 draws and 36,728 triangles at the most over its heaviest stretch (the fullest street eaten, a tower on the breath, the glow and the hand, the float, and the next street coming in), against bounds of 50 and 40,000. What is drawn: the street, the setting, the crowd, the pit, its lip, the breath, the shadows, the scraps, three glow rings, the ghost hand, the two leaves of the gate, one draw for each thing, ten for the mayor and eleven for the gate keeper.
