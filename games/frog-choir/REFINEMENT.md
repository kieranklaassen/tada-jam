# Frog Choir: thirty refinement passes

Thirty record, critique, and fix passes after the first playable pond, with performance measured on every pass.

**How each pass was measured.** Every pass rebuilds the production bundle and serves it with `vite preview`. The page is `?chrome=0#/play/frog-choir` at 1180×820, DPR 2, with touch, on the default rising staircase. The perf number is the jam probe (`perf-probe.mjs`) on Chromium at 6× CPU throttle: 5 s of warm-up, then 15 s of sampling with twelve taps spread over the pond. It reports `cpuP95Ms` (update plus render submit, from `window.__jamPerf`) and the renderer's draw calls. The machine is a GPU-less cloud VM, so WebGL runs in software: frame rates here are fill-bound and far below an iPad's, and CPU time is the proxy that transfers. Look passes use a real-time screenshot after 6.4 s idle, pinned to the top tier with `?tier=0`. Motion and guidance passes use Playwright's fake clock, installed and paused and then advanced in 33 ms steps, with frames stitched at 30 fps. A pause is needed because the fake clock otherwise also flows in real time, and slow software screenshots would leak into `dt`.

**After the passes**

- **A test for sameness.** [`view/personalities.test.ts`](view/personalities.test.ts) runs each frog through a tap, a song, a resting finger, a carry, a hop, and a landing, twice (with the event and without), and compares the difference at 21 moments across every body channel. No two frogs may come within 0.3 of each other, and a touch must differ from a landing and a plain song. The closest pair is two landings, 0.61 apart; taps are at least 7.96 apart. It caught two real bugs. The sleepy frog's one-eye peek under a resting finger was erased by the singing lids on the next line, so it had no anticipation at all. The crooner's throat bubble was NaN until his first song, because the vibrato took the sine of an infinite age before multiplying by a zero envelope.
- **A frame-budget test.** [`perf.test.ts`](perf.test.ts) drives the busiest pond through the real controller and all five personalities: one frog carried across every pad, one dropped in the water, the other three tapped in turn, on the fastest tempo. It measures about 0.01 ms a frame against a 0.15 ms budget, and asserts the scenario really happened so it cannot pass on an idle pond.
- **Touch starts one tier down.** A coarse pointer starts at tier 1 and climbs to the full look after ten steady seconds, so an iPad's first seconds do not stutter while the monitor learns.

**Cold playtest proxy**

Run on the production build in WebKit, in real time, with fresh storage (recording: Project store `media/jam-10-games/frog-choir/cold-playtest.mp4`).

*10 s hands off: what the scene invites.* The firefly glides in from the sky and crosses the pond left to right, and every frog it passes puffs its throat while the light pools on it, so the eye follows the light and the loop reads as "the light makes them sing". The front frog puffs and bounces toward the child. At 3 s the five frogs' pads glow warm, and at 5 s a big ghost hand taps the front frog twice; it spins and sings. Nothing invites a drag yet, on purpose: the carry is demonstrated only after the first tap.

*The first 60 s as a newcomer.* Tapped the frog that bounced, the firefly, each frog, an empty pad, and the water; slid a finger a little on a frog by accident; waited; copied the ghost hand's carry; swapped two frogs; dropped one in the water. Every act got an answer on the same frame. The accidental slide lifted the frog and set it back home with a thump, which read as play, not a mistake. After the first taps, the ghost hand switched to carrying a translucent frog, and copying it worked first time. The moments that were not obvious:

1. **The first loop is silent.** Until the first touch the browser keeps sound locked, so the frogs visibly sing with no sound. The invite and the ghost hand both point at a frog, and that first tap unlocks the choir, so it resolves on the first act. It is a browser rule, not fixable in the game.
2. **Tapping the light while it sits on a singer.** Eight taps where the firefly had just been seen (0.1 to 0.4 s late, about a child's reaction time) were checked by counting the chime's own oscillator: seven caught it. The miss came while the firefly hovered right over the crooner's head, and the frog took the touch and bowed. That is still an answer, just not the firefly's; the frog is the bigger, likelier target, so it stays.
3. **Why the tune changed.** After a move, the next loop plays the new melody. It is heard, not shown; the only visible link is the pad tint by row. For 4 to 8 that is discovery rather than a blocker.

No fix was made: none of the three blocks the first act, and two are by design.

**Still weak**

- **No real iPad, no real child.** Every number comes from a GPU-less VM where WebGL is software and fill-bound. The tiers are tuned for a slow device, but no iPad frame time was measured, and the idle ladder's timings have never met a four-year-old.
- **Sound waits for a touch.** Browsers keep the context locked until the first real touch, so the first loop plays silently while the frogs sing along visibly. Whether the iOS ring/silent switch should mute the choir (Safari's default for Web Audio) is a shell-level decision, left alone here.
- **The frogs are the triangle budget.** Five frogs, drawn as toon plus hull, are 85k of the bottom tier's 138k triangles. That is why WebKit's software rasterizer spends 10 ms a frame. A lower-detail frog for the bottom tier would help the software path, but it was not built, because the silhouettes carry the look and real GPUs are fill-bound.
- **Outlines alias at DPR 1.** There is no antialiasing buffer, and at the bottom tier the plum hulls stair-step. MSAA would be cheap on Apple's tile GPUs, but it is a context-creation choice, so it can't follow the tier.
- **The sky is still.** Clouds and sun are painted once. A drifting cloud layer would add life at the top of the frame for one draw call, but it was left out in favour of a calm, cheap backdrop.
- **Two fingers were only unit-tested.** Carrying two frogs at once and swapping into a held frog's pad have controller tests, but no browser run, because Playwright's touchscreen taps with one finger.
