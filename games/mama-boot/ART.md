# Design sheet

Written before any game code, and checked by someone who did not write it before the game is built on the toy. The look follows the sheet at the end of this file.

## The band and its age rule

The manifest band is 2 to 5. The youngest age governs. The cue table has no row for two, so its row for 3 to 4 is the ceiling and is cut further: no text, numeral or icon, no spoken instruction, no verdict, one next act offered at a time (pack: game-design, ages-2-to-4.md). Everything essential works with taps, never two on the same thing in quick time; a drag is an extra way, survives a short lift of the finger, and counts when partly done: let go near the hen, a seat or the ground it was meant for is near enough. A thing is taken by another only where it is let go, never where it is carried past. A cancelled or lost finger puts the thing back where it was taken from. A resting hand never deafens the game, and a second finger takes nothing from the first and gets a small answer of its own. Targets are about 100 units across on a stage of 1180 by 820, nothing that must be touched stands in the bottom 60 units, and there are never more than five chicks and eggs together.

`ctx.childAge` sets one default, on a first visit only: three eggs at 3 and younger, five eggs at 4 and older. No age given starts with three. A saved yard always wins, and the hen lays the missing eggs for any child who taps her, so nothing is gated.

## The toy

Put a finger on an egg: in the same frame it squashes and creaks, and a finger that carries it on takes it whole. Tapped, it cracks as the finger lifts. The lid of the shell pops off, a pom-pom chick comes up with both googly eyes rolling, and the eyes land on whatever stands nearest. A thread of yarn springs taut between the chick and that thing, the chick cries out in a squeaky try at that thing's own sound, the thing answers, the thread snaps back and ties itself round the chick's neck, and the chick tumbles out and goes after its mother. Opening an egg is a pleasure with nothing to achieve, and tapping anything in the yard makes it do its own move and sound, which its chicks then do after it.

Two loose things set down side by side do something together, every time: the teapot pours into the boot; the pig sniffs the boot and keels over with its legs up; the farmer hugs the boot; the boot kicks the ball away; the boot kicks the bell and hops; the pig drinks from the spout and burps; the farmer drinks asleep and steam comes out of his hat; the ball hangs bobbing in the steam; the teapot and the bell take turns; the pig licks the farmer, who giggles; the pig noses the ball away; the pig dances to the bell; the farmer snores the ball away and back; the bell makes the farmer open one eye, look all round and shut it; the ball bounces off the bell. Whatever a thing does, its chicks do after it, one beat later for each step down the chain, so a tap on the first of a chain runs down it as a round.

Scenery that looks touchable answers as itself and changes nothing: the sheep bleats and chews, the cow behind in the barn door swishes her tail, the pasta sun turns, a cotton cloud puffs, the pom-pom tree shakes, the fence rattles. A touch anywhere else, the bare ground included, dents what is there under the finger with a soft pat and it springs back.

Whatever walks, rolls, is kicked, is laid or follows comes to rest on free ground inside the yard, above the bottom 60 units, and never in front of the middle of anything else that answers a touch. An overlap test holds this; the stage is fitted whole into every surface, so its units are the same at each size. A chick or egg that rides has its own touch area, apart from what carries it, except in the teapot, where a tap pours it out.

**Day 15.** On day 1 the child opens eggs and laughs at who the mother is. On day 15 the child stages it: carries an egg into the boot or sets a thing down in front of the rolling eyes, builds a parade of five behind one thing and plays it as a round, makes a ring by letting eggs see eggs, hangs a train of chicks on a chick that rides on the pig's back, stacks a tower, and knows which two things do what. The youngest is faster and surer and tries one new thing.

## The error as a consequence

There is no wrong attempt. A chick that has taken the wrong mother is as good to watch as one that has taken the right one, its thread shows whose it is, and a chick put under the hen is an egg again, so the child changes one thing and opens it once more. A thing let go where it cannot stand (on the fence, outside the yard, on top of another) is seen to hop to the nearest free ground with a soft bump.

## The designed order, and what is stored

There is no order of challenges and no position: the yard is open play, and what is new is reached by understanding. The saved state holds:

- `v`, the version;
- `things`: for each of the six loose things its place and which way it faces;
- `brood`: for each chick or egg, up to five, its stable id, whether it is an egg or a chick, its place, its mother (a thing, the hen, an id of the brood, its own id, or none), what it rides on or none, and whether its ring is sitting dizzy.

Nothing is saved in the air. A thing under a finger that is still down is saved where it was taken from; a thing the finger has let go, the short wait after a lift included, is saved where it was let go, with what that place does to it. An egg that is opening when the game is put away is found opened, with the mother that was saved last. A chick on its way under the hen is found as an egg beside the nest. A thing set on the hen does its piece with her and then hops down beside the nest, where it is saved from the moment it was let go. A lifted wing, steam, water, bubbles, shells, threads and every pose (the pig on its back, the farmer's hug, the ball in the steam) are short-lived and gone on load. A thing that is walking or rolling on is saved where it will stop.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

- **The hen** watches everything from her nest with two large googly eyes. Her want is eggs under her, and it always shows: one side of her skirt is held up over the bare hollow of the nest. Each mother gets a look of its own from her, always the same one: the boot makes her gasp behind a wing, the teapot turns her head upside down, the pig makes her faint backward, the farmer makes her shake her head, the ball makes her eyes go round, the bell makes her nod in time, herself makes her swell and shut her eyes, an egg makes her look from one to the other, a chick makes her look twice, and the puddle makes her lean over to look for what it sees. A ring makes her dizzy as long as it turns.
- **The eggs** want out: each leans toward what it would see and shows the tip of a beak through a small hole, always. One at a time, the asking egg, also knocks from inside, with a stop between the knocks.
- **The chicks** want their mother, and look at her wherever they are, from inside a teapot too.
- **The pig** wants a drink, and its tongue hangs out toward the teapot wherever that stands. It likes the bell, a drink and the farmer's nose, and cannot stand the smell of the boot.
- **The farmer** wants something to hug, and sleeps with his arms held open round nothing. He sleeps through everything but the bell.
- No feeling is about the child: nobody thanks, cheers, calls or minds being left.

## The scenes

There are no scenes that stop the play. Four short happenings run beside it and every touch is answered meanwhile. In the first three a touch on the one that is in it squashes it with a small sound of its own, save where the tap on the hen under "Under the hen" says otherwise; what was saved stands. Any touch at all ends the round, and what was touched does what it always does:

- **The opening of an egg** (about 2.4 seconds), caused by a tap. What it saves when it starts: the egg as a chick, its place, and for its mother whatever stands nearest at that moment. Until the eyes land, each change of who stands nearest is saved in its place, and when they land the mother is settled and saved.
- **Under the hen** (about 1.8 seconds), caused by a chick being set on her, or by a tap on a chick, the first while her wing is up. The egg and its place are saved at once, and any chick or egg riding on it hops down and is saved where it lands. A chick that had this one for its mother keeps it, as an egg too. After a tap her wing stays up until six seconds after the chick is under, and one tap on her at any moment from the tap on the chick until the wing comes down, the running under included, brings that chick out again as it was, with the same mother, and saves that; put away meanwhile, it is found as an egg.
- **The laying of an egg** (about 1 second), caused by a tap on the hen. The new egg is saved at once.
- **The round.** Every chick in the yard belongs in the end to one thing, there are five, and that thing is tapped: the round runs down the chain, and then the sheep bleats it, and the cow turns round in the door and moos it. It comes every time and is never hinted at. It saves nothing.

A want when there is no egg: the hen looks down under herself at the empty nest. Idle ladder: after 3 seconds a glow on the asking egg, after 5 the hand taps it. With no egg the glow is on the hen and the hand taps her, and if there are already five chicks it then taps one chick. From the second showing on the hand also shows a thing being carried to an egg or, with no egg, a chick being carried to the hen. The gaps double (10, 20, 40 seconds), there are at most four showings, and then the yard goes quiet: the knocking stops with them and the asking egg only holds its lean and shows its beak. Any touch clears it.

There is no cycle and nothing starts by itself. The calm way to tidy up is to put the chicks under the hen.

## The records

The game has no learning goal, names no record and claims no school skill.

## The look

**Craft-box collage** (canvas 2D). The yard is a thing a child could have stuck together at a table: sheets of card and felt, and glued on them dry pasta, buttons, pom-poms, lolly sticks, cotton wool, pipe cleaners, kitchen foil and googly eyes.

- **Materials, and what each is for.** Felt: bodies, with a light cut edge at the upper left, a dark one at the lower right and a few dashed stitches. Pom-poms: what is woolly and soft (pig, chicks, tree, comb). Lolly sticks: what is built (barn, fence, barrow, soles). Pipe cleaners: what bends (cuff, handles, tail, sleeves, and the yarn round a chick's neck). Buttons: snout, wheel, apples, the middle of the sun. Penne and pasta bows: the spout and the rays. Cotton wool: cloud, sheep, beard, steam. Foil: the puddle.
- **Light.** From the upper left. Every piece has one small hard shadow to the lower right in one blue-grey. Figures have a second one that falls on what is behind them and a flat soft one on the ground. Glue shows as a few clear shiny blobs.
- **Eyes.** The one thing every character shares, and what the game is about. White domes with a black disc that rolls on a loose spring: thrown about by the body's own movement and drawn to what it looks at. An egg's chick rolls them until they land.
- **Colour.** The ground is one mid-dark green-blue, so that all that is warm or pale stands off it. One strong hue for each mother, and her chicks wear it round the neck: boot red, teapot blue, pig pink, farmer green, ball purple, bell turquoise, hen orange, an egg not opened white, the puddle silver, a ring yellow.
- **Never.** Paint, torn edges, gradients on a body, a stitched or threaded cross on a button, words, numerals.
- **Next to its neighbours.** Who Made That Sound is painted tissue with torn edges; Felt Meadow and Critter Clay are solid 3D; Bedtime cut-outs have no thickness. Here the pieces are objects, they are thick, and they are glued.

The small print: `craft.ts` holds the materials, `figures.ts` the characters, `scenery.ts` the yard, `moves.ts` how each moves, `view.ts` only moves pictures that are cut once.

## The music

A rocking tune in G for nylon guitar, soft bass, wooden recorder and glockenspiel. It is played by live-mix (`vendor/live-mix`, whose README links to it): `music.worker.ts` plays the score once on its modelled instruments after the first touch, and `music.ts` runs the result as clips on three tracks. Nothing is a recording, and nothing is fetched but the instruments themselves from the same address.

The mood (`mood.ts`) decides how much is heard. A touch brings the accompaniment and the tune, several touches bring the answering instrument, and a child who stops hears them leave one by one until a trace of the accompaniment is left. The music goes down under each sound the game makes and comes back after it. It rests with the game.
