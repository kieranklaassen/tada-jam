# Critter Clay: thirty refinement passes

**Progress:** passes 1 to 30, the whole-game check, the final shots, the walkthrough, and the publish are done. Next: nothing; the PR body is with the coordinator.

The brief asked for 30 refinement passes on top of the first playable build, covering the look, clarity for a four-year-old, the guidance ladder, weight and squash, a motion personality per character, sound, and performance. Each pass: screenshot or record → honest critique (what reads badly, looks cheap, is unclear to the youngest child, or is slow) → one focused fix → re-measure. Passes 11 to 30 also apply the base branch's new lessons (one obvious want per scene, the cold playtest proxy, and motion personality per character) as they landed.

**How each pass was measured.**

- **Look.** A fixed showcase bench is seeded into storage: a sleeping lemon lump with an eye, a stub leg, and an ear on the turntable, and four awake critters (a six-legged scuttler, a two-legged waddler with a curly tail and floppy ears, a bold four-legged loper with a head, two horns, and a long tail, and a legless inchworm). Playwright installs a fake clock, runs it 3 seconds in 33 ms steps, and screenshots at 1180×820 and tier 3 (DPR 1 for the working shot, DPR 2 for `iter-NN.png`). Clarity passes also shoot a fresh bench a few seconds into idle (the idle-screenshot test), so the glow and the first demonstration are in frame.
- **Perf.** The shared probe on the production build (1180×820, DPR 2, touch): headless Chromium at 6× CPU throttle, three runs, and the median `cpuP95Ms` (update plus render submit) with the draw calls from `renderer.info`. This VM has no GPU, so absolute fps means nothing here, and its speed drifts by up to 2 ms between sessions. When a median moved without a plausible cause, the previous and current builds were served side by side and A/B'd with five alternating runs each, and that result is what the table reports.
- **Cold playtest proxy** (passes 15, 20, 23, and 30). A fresh bench, 10 seconds hands off, then a scripted clumsy newcomer minute: tap the lump's body, tap a tray part without dragging, drop a part on the empty board, tap the nose early, poke and drag an awake critter, pull a part back off. Every 33 ms frame is captured on a fake clock and reviewed frame by frame for moments a four-year-old could misread.
- **Per-character passes** (brief item 4). Contact sheets of all four temperaments doing the same moment side by side, asking "is this the same routine with different numbers?" Tests then require every pair to differ by more than 0.3 on some pose channel.
- **Deeper checks** where a question needed them: controlled builds that switch one layer on or off (pass 25), the sampling heap profiler plus a headless Node allocation bench (pass 27), and a full read of the diff (pass 29).

Pass screenshots are in the Project store under `media/jam-10-games/critter-clay/` (`iter-01.png`, `iter-10.png`, `iter-20.png`, `iter-30.png`, `hero.png`, `walkthrough.mp4`).

**Whole-game check after pass 30.** Checking the build against the base's performance lesson found two gaps in the tier controller, and both are fixed. Touch devices started at the top tier instead of one tier down. And stepping up counted only frames under 15 ms, which a 60 Hz display never delivers, so an iPad that stepped down once could never get the top look back. Headroom is now read from each frame's own work (an on-time frame under 8 ms of work counts), with a test that fails under the old rule. On the final production build, the shared probe's automatic tier gives `cpuP95Ms` of 2.5 ms at 4× and 3.8 ms at 6× in Chromium (medians of three). The scripted core loop (111 seconds at 6×: sixteen parts pressed on, a bold critter built and woken, every temperament poked, two critters carried, a part pulled off) gives 3.3 ms, with a worst frame of 22 ms of work. Draw calls are 18 to 22, with one full-screen pass at the top two tiers and no shadow maps. In WebKit, Critter Clay runs at 29.4 fps against Pebble Table's 16.8 fps in the same session (1.75×), and at 31 fps through the core loop. Part of that gain is the starting tier: Playwright's touch emulation matches `(pointer: coarse)` in both engines, so the probe now starts one tier down. In this software-GL VM the automatic tier then settles at tier 0 (DPR 1, no normal maps), so these numbers are for the lowest look. Pinned to the top tier (DPR 2 with the grain pass and normal maps), it runs at 11.4 fps against Pebble Table's 15.6. That is below the baseline, and the gap is fill, because the top tier shades 1.78 times the pixels of DPR 1.5. Pinned to tier 2 (DPR 1.5, the DPR Pebble Table starts touch devices at), it runs at 17.6 against 16.6. Pinned at tier 3, `cpuP95Ms` is 3.5 ms at 4× and 5.4 ms at 6×, with 20 draw calls. The kid side has no words (`npm run wordless:check`), nothing leaves the device (`npm run egress:built`), and the game's 112 tests pass.

## Still weak

- **No physical iPad was measured.** Every number comes from a cloud VM with no GPU, rendering WebGL in software.
- **The top tier costs fill.** At DPR 2 in software GL it runs below Pebble Table. A tile-based iPad GPU should handle it, and the tier controller steps down within about 3 seconds if it can't, but that is not measured on a device.
- **Some garbage is left.** Numbers boxed at call boundaries still make about 380 KB/s. The final 111-second core loop at 6× ran 37 minor collections (the longest 5.8 ms) and no major ones. The 150-second run on the pass 30 build had two incremental major ones (17 and 27 ms at 6×, about 5 ms unthrottled): under the 50 ms long-task line, but over an 8 ms frame.
- **Very slow CPUs.** At 20× throttle `cpuP95Ms` is 10.9 to 16.8 ms, over only 36 to 44 sampled frames (Pebble Table draws 1.7 to 1.8 fps there, against 5.9 to 7.2). The tiers cut fill, not the work of posing critters.
- **The first leg's glow sits by the nose.** On a sleeping lump the first leg folds forward under its chin, so its socket glow shows right beside the nose, and a child can read it as "tap the nose". A bare lump answers a nose tap by peeking at the tray (pass 17), so the misread is harmless, but it is not removed.
- **A lone leg is still small from behind.** Pass 30 shows its paw and shin from the front and the side; from behind, the body still hides most of it.
- **A critter set down in front of the turntable** takes up to a few seconds to walk out of the strip, and for that time it can cover the lump.
- **Sounds were tuned by reasoning and waveform, not by ear** on a tablet speaker.
- **Tier 0 loses the thumbprints.** The baked kneading keeps it hand-made, but the fine grain of the normal map is gone.
- **The playtests are proxies.** The cold playtest is a script playing a clumsy newcomer, not a child.
