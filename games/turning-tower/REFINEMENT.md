# Turning Tower: thirty refinement passes

Each pass: capture every diorama (or record a scripted scene) → honest critique → one focused fix → re-measure. The passes cover the look against the Geometric Valley reference, clarity for the youngest player in the age band (the idle-screenshot test: with no words, can a seven-year-old tell what to touch?), the guidance ladder, weight and squash, each character's own motion, sound, and performance.

**How each pass was measured.** Every pass rebuilds the production bundle (`npm run build`, served by `vite preview`). All five dioramas are then captured at 1180×820, DPR 2 in headless Chromium, 6.4 seconds after load, so the idle glow and the ghost-hand demonstration are in frame. Performance comes from the jam probe (`perf-probe.mjs`) on First Turn: Chromium with 6× CDP CPU throttle, DPR 2, 5 seconds of warm-up, then 6 seconds of sampling while 12 taps land across the screen. It reports the 95th-percentile per-frame CPU time from `window.__jamPerf.cpuMs` (update plus render submit), draw calls and triangles for the room on screen, and the tier the governor settled on. Motion passes use a recorder that drives Playwright's clock one 33 ms frame at a time and stitches the frames at 30 fps, so animation timing is exact.

## Intersection audit

The shared audit (`npm run check:intersections -- turning-tower`) plays ten moments on the production build at a quick child's pace, 68 s of game time in all. It starts hands off through the invitation and the ghost hand, then pokes the wanderer and the bird. Then it solves all five rooms by hand: every turning segment dragged round through its stops, the raft and the lift fetched and ridden, the bird sent along its bridge, and the wanderer walked over both impossible joins and in at every door. Before this pass it found 95 visible crossings (81 penetrations, 8 poses, 6 z-fights) and 6 hidden ones. After it, none is open and three are allowed. All 96 of the old close-ups replay clean. The audit is enforced in CI.

What was real, and what changed:

- Segments against the blocks beside them (4 penetrations, 6 z-fights). A turning segment's end faces lay in its neighbours' faces, and its corners swept through them as it turned. The threaded wheel's disc ran through its axle. Segments are now drawn fitted to their sweep (`armFit`), a touch thinner along a level axle. The wheel is a ring clear of its axle, and the perches and the drawbridge handle sit where nothing moves.
- The shadows (13: 6 in segments, 3 in blocks, 3 in the bird, 1 in the wanderer). A shadow's square corners reached into whatever stood beside its caster, and feet and shadows stood in the raised pavers. Crossing a perspective seam, the wanderer slid to its new level while its shadow still hung over the lift it stepped off. Shadows are now round, cut where their texture fades out, and scale with their caster. Every paver stands one shared height proud (`PAVER_RAISE`), and feet and shadows stand on it. The seam slide waits until the shadow is clear of both tiles (`SEAM_CLEAR`).
- The bird (10 in blocks, 1 in a segment, 5 in the wanderer, 1 in the door). Its head, wings and puff swung wherever the motion took them. A clearance model (`clearance.ts`) now keeps them in open air, and the bird lands facing the way it will stand. One set of dimensions (`anatomy.ts`) serves the drawn bird and wanderer and the clearance checks.
- The wanderer in the blocks (2) and a segment (1). A segment landing on its support dipped into it, and the wanderer riding it sank too. Segments now stop at their hard stops, only an unsupported landing dips, and it carries the wanderer with it.
- The wanderer and the door (7, plus 2 poses of the leaves in the frame). In First Turn, the Ferry and the Impossible Stair, the path reached the door from the side, so the wanderer walked in through a jamb. Those three doors now stand where the path meets them from the front. The Ferry gained a column under its new door, and First Turn's cone moved along the ledge to make room. At the door the wanderer now stands 0.2 of a cell toward the camera (`DOOR_STAND`) and steps in from there. Before, its cloak touched the frame and its lantern reached into the doorway. Its shadow stays on the floor in front of the door. The door's leaves are closed solids that swing in behind the light they open onto, not through the arch.
- The wanderer's head in its body (1 pose). The mask was an open plate, so points behind it read as inside the head. The mask is backed now, and the head is one closed solid. With that gone, a later run found the lantern swinging into the hood on the Ferry ride: a ride's sway could push it in past the hood. It now knocks back off the hood (`LANTERN_SWING_IN`), as a lantern on a staff would.
- The bird's head in its back (1 pose). Turned back over its shoulder and looking down, the bird's cheek sank into its back. The head now turns about the middle of its ball, and the farther it turns, the less it looks down.
- The ring of little rooms (4 poses). Each little model shares its room's geometry, so the same fixes cleared them.
- The glows (30) and the sky (7). The door and lantern halos now float toward the camera, so no wall cuts them. They are additive light that writes no depth, so the audit ignores them, and a test holds them in front of every block. The sky is one triangle drawn straight to clip space, so it is ignored too. The halo used to wash the lower half of the closed door, where it tilted through the door's plane. The closed door is now what the art direction names, warm leaves in a dark arch, with the glow around it.

Allowed, each with a cap a little above what it measures:

- The raised drawbridge against its path block (`room-crank` architecture × segment 0, penetration, 30% measured, cap 40%). Raised, the drawbridge stands flush against the block it lowers onto, and the paver on its hinge cell rests inside that block. It is hidden and still. The block has only its camera-facing faces, so the audit reads the depth against its far side.

## Still weak

- **No physical iPad was measured.** This VM has no GPU: headless Chromium renders through SwiftShader, and WebKit through a software rasterizer. So every fps number here is a relative comparison (Turning Tower against Pebble Table, measured the same way in the same session), not a device reading.
- **MSAA is the biggest cost on a software renderer.** Pinned to tier 0 in WebKit the game draws about 19 fps, and at tier 3 about 37–39 fps; nearly all of that gap is filling multisampled pixels. It stays on for the look, because tile-based iPad GPUs resolve MSAA cheaply, and the governor steps down within seconds on a device that cannot keep up.
- **The characters are small in The Crank.** The lift's top stop, three cells above the door, sets the frame height that the perspective join needs, so the wanderer is smaller there than in First Turn (pass 26).
- **The wanderer's greetings read more quietly than the bird's.** The tests check that each pair is a different movement (look-out and swing are the closest pair, at 0.41 against a floor of 0.35), but at a glance the bow is the least readable of the six answers.
- **The bird's exit happens in front of the door, not through it.** It brightens into the door's light and is gone; with no depth cut-out of the arch, it cannot visibly pass behind the frame.
- **Two near-misses stay under the audit's tolerance.** With its head pitched to 0.5, the wanderer's hood sinks 0.063 into its collar against 0.026 at rest; play pitches it no further than about 0.3, which keeps it within 0.025 of its resting depth. With the head turned away and looking up while the arm is half raised, the hood's tip can brush the staff. Neither showed up in the audit or in the in-process journey.
