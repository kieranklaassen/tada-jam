# Shadow Lantern: thirty refinement passes

> **Progress:** done. All thirty passes, the cold-playtest rerun, the flight fix, the final walkthrough and the perf re-measure on the final build are in. Next: review feedback only.

Thirty passes over the first playable theatre, one focused change each. Every pass followed the same loop: screenshot at 1180×820 (DPR 2), an honest critique of what reads badly or looks cheap to a six-year-old, one fix, re-screenshot, then a perf check. From pass 13 onward most critiques came from frame-by-frame captures of a whole wake, a tap or a demonstration, not from a single still.

**How each pass was measured.** Screenshots are clock-driven: Playwright's `page.clock` is installed before load and stepped in 33 ms slices, so every capture of the same scene is the same frame. The standard shot is the opening scene 2.5 s after load at tier 0 (full). Passes that changed something off the opening screen used seeded saves (a full sky, a creature half-woken, twins in the sky) or scripted taps and drags on the same clock. Perf comes from the shared probe on the production build (`vite preview`) at 1180×820, DPR 2, touch, headless Chromium with 6× CPU throttle, reading `window.__jamPerf`. The table reports the probe's cpuP95 and the draw calls on the tier the governor settles on at 6×, which is always the lowest (minimal). Repeat runs of the same build vary by about ±1.5 ms, so any jump was re-measured before it was believed (pass 15 first read 5.9 ms under a concurrent build and re-measured at 3.7 ms). A full sky is the worst case, so passes that touched the sky also give full-sky numbers in their change column.

Screenshots are in the Project store under `media/jam-10-games/shadow-lantern/` (`iter-01.png`, `iter-10.png`, `iter-20.png`, `iter-30.png`, `hero.png`, `walkthrough.mp4`).

## Cold playtest proxy

After the thirty passes, the proxy from the wordless-clarity convention was run on the production build with fresh state and a clock-driven recording: 10 s hands off, then about 60 s of play as a newcomer (turn and drag two shapes, tap the lamp, the sky, the sleeping outline and the floor, wait for the ghost hand, follow it, watch the wake and the next outline arrive).

What the scene invited with no touch: the dotted bird on the lit screen with a ring rising from its head, the lamp's glow, and seven shapes on stands beside the lamp. From 3 s the shapes breathe with cream rings; at 5 s the paper hand lifts a see-through copy of the big triangle and its pale shadow slides into the outline.

Unclear moments, first run:

1. Nothing at rest connected the shapes to the screen. Every home position throws its shadow wide of the screen, where it is clipped, so the first-open hop lifted a card and no shadow moved anywhere: a card jumped for no visible reason.

Everything else answered: a tap on the lamp flares it, on the sky or the floor it makes a burst of stars, on the sleeping outline its rings puff up, and the next outline arriving half under the leftover shadows reads as a head start rather than a mistake.

Fix: the half-disc that does the hop now stands forward of the right rack, where its shadow rests on the screen's right edge beside the outline without touching any creature's outline (a coverage test checks all six: nothing filled, and no shadow on the screen but this one). The hop now visibly lifts that shadow, and the game's own hints for the bird bring that half-disc in right after the big triangle. Rerun: the moment is gone and no new one appeared; the extra shadow does not compete with the outline, and the hints still wake all six creatures from the new rest layout. On the final build the opening screen measures 3.1 to 3.5 ms cpuP95 at 6× with 16 draws, and a full sky 4.2 to 6.1 ms with 48.

## Caught by the walkthrough recording

The walkthrough follows the game's own hints through all six creatures and starts on each one 1.5 s before the last is home, to keep the video short. Played back frame by frame, the sky ended with four friends, not six: the bird vanished just short of its home on the frame the fish woke, and the fox vanished the same way when the dragon woke. The next outline can wake about 3.8 s after a wake starts, but every creature is still flying then (their trips end 5.25 to 7.75 s after the wake). The controller had one waking slot and the new wake overwrote it, so the flying creature was dropped from the live sky. It was already saved, so it came back on the next open, which is why the seeded shots never showed the problem. A quick child whose leftover shadows already fill the next outline would lose a friend the same way.

Fix: once its page has turned, a creature flies home as a companion, so a wake can start at any moment without cutting another creature's trip short. A tap passes through a creature still in flight, and the loop keeps its full rate until the flight lands. A new test wakes the fish while the bird is still flying and expects both at home; it failed before the fix. Re-recorded, all six arrive and stay: bird, fish, snail, whale, fox and dragon are all at home by 51 s of the final walkthrough.

## Intersection audit

The shared audit (`npm run check:intersections -- shadow-lantern`) plays four moments on the production build at a quick child's pace, about 60 s in all: hands off until the demonstration, the bird, fish and snail woken by hand, the whale, fox and dragon, then taps on the sky, lamp and floor and stands pushed into each other. Before this pass it found 52 visible crossings (40 penetrations, 7 poses, 3 z-fights, 2 contained) and 1 hidden one. After it, none is open, one is allowed, and all 52 of the old close-ups replay clean. The audit is enforced in CI.

What was real, and what changed:

- A creature through itself (7 poses). The wing, tail, flukes or shell swung through the body and the backing card, because all the layers shared about 0.4 cm of depth. `CREATURE_STACK` now gives each paper layer its own depth with air between: drop shadow, backing, a hanging part, the body, the eye, a front part.
- Stands through stands (2 penetrations, 1 z-fight). Nothing kept a dragged or turned stand out of its neighbours; pass 14's hair of depth stagger only hid the flicker. Stands are now solid paper, with one set of dimensions for the view and the controller (`stands.ts`). A move that would carry one into another stops there, so it slides along what it meets. A set-down stops short of whatever is between it and the finger. A tap-turn steps aside, and a twist stops before the card turns into a neighbour. A saved layout with two stands in one place opens with them apart. The hint search no longer asks for a place or a slide a child couldn't do. The stagger is gone.
- The feet in the floor (7 with the contact spots, 4 with the stage). The stick was drawn in front of its foot and spot, and the sole lay in the planks' plane. Stick, foot and spot now share `STAND`'s numbers; the sole rests just above the planks and the spot lies between them, with a polygon offset.
- The flame through the lamp's cup (1). It stands on the cup's floor now.
- Paper things through the frame (2 sleep rings, 3 outline dots, 12 star bursts). Rings, dots and stars near the screen's edge grew past it into the frame. Each is now sized to the paper it lies on (`onPaper`), so it shrinks into the edge instead.
- Stars inside a creature (2 contained, 6 penetrations). The waking burst was drawn at one fixed depth, inside the creature as it lifted off. The stars now ride just in front of it, and a tap's stars fly in planes of their own (`SPARK_Z`).
- The shadows through the waking creature (1). It woke 0.3 cm off the paper, in among the shadows and the outline. It now wakes at 1.3 cm (`WAKE_Z`).
- The page turn through a stand (1). The peel swung the card out toward the child about its tail edge, so a big creature's backing swept into the front stand. It now turns over in its own plane, narrowing onto the tail edge and opening out mirrored; the dragon's barrel roll does the same.
- The demonstration's card copy on the real card (1 z-fight). The see-through copy now draws over everything without a depth test, like the hand.
- Scenery on itself (1 z-fight). The posts shared a front face with the top bar, and three grass tufts crossed their own outline. The posts end under the bar, and a tuft's base reaches past its outer notches.

The audit starts fresh and wakes six creatures in turn, so it never sees a full sky. The sky was checked in vitest instead, with the real creature geometry and the audit's own depth test over every home, idle, tap reaction and flight. That found three more: the dragon's barrel roll swept through the neighbour in the next home, and flights to the homes by the crest and the right curtain were pulled back through the proscenium near the end of the trip. A flight's depth now uses the creature's width and height plus its backing's reach, and eases back only once all of it is clear. A creature landing right by the frame arrives a touch short of its layer and settles onto it over 0.8 s (`SETTLE_S`). The same check now finds nothing.

Allowed: the shadows against the frame (`scenery` × `screen-shadows`, 102%). The shadows' material clips them to the screen's paper (`clippingPlanes`), which the audit does not model, so what it sees crossing the frame is never drawn.

Tests, each failing on the old code: `creature-layers.test.ts` (every idle and tap reaction in the sky keeps each creature in its layer; the peel turns about its hinge edge in its own plane), `motion.test.ts` (a flight keeps all its paper in front of the frame all the way home; the peel keeps every layer clear of the paper, the frame, the stars and the stands; the stack has air between its layers), `controller.test.ts` (stands never pass through each other or the front line, pushed about or following the hints; pushed, twisted and tap-turned into a neighbour; a saved overlap opens apart; a flight to the home by the crest stays in front of it and settles onto its layer), `projection.test.ts` (paper things shrink into the frame's edge) and `scenery.test.ts` (no self-crossing tufts, no two faces in one plane).

Perf: the stands' collision runs every frame a stand moves, so the cost was measured three ways. The frame-budget tests pass. The probe on the production build (bundled Chromium, 6× CPU throttle, eight interleaved runs of each build on a VM shared with other audits) reads cpuP95 6.3–15.6 ms before (median 7.9) and 6.3–18.9 ms after (median 8.5), with the same best run, 16 draws and tier 3 on both; the spread is the shared machine. A step-by-step timing of the controller with a full sky and stands dragged across each other puts the added cost at about 0.02 ms per step at full speed (p95 0.033 → 0.055 ms, about 0.15 ms at 6×). The idle hint search still keeps to its 0.6 ms slice (p90 0.61–0.64 ms in both builds).

## Still weak

- Not measured on a physical iPad. All numbers come from headless Chromium with CPU throttling and headless WebKit on the cloud VM.
- The sounds (paper rustles, the kalimba dots, each creature's voice) were tuned by reasoning about pitch, envelope and loudness, not by ear on a tablet speaker.
- The bird's and dragon's page turn still sweeps over the left curtain at nearly full size for a moment before they shrink; it reads as the card lifting off, but it does cover the curtain.
- Each companion's eye is its own draw rather than one instanced mesh; a full sky pays eight draws for eight dots.
- On the floor, the cream burst of stars near the lamp sits on the brightest planks and has the lowest contrast of the four burst surfaces.
- Landscape only: portrait shows the harness's turn-me-sideways card, and no portrait layout was designed.
- The worst frame is a full sky while the idle hint search is running; it holds under the 8 ms target at 6× after pass 22, but with less headroom than the opening scene.
- The wake tolerance (72% filled, at most 60% spill for ages up to 7) was tuned with tests and a solver, not with children.
- The browser intersection audit never sees a full sky; that is covered by the vitest geometry checks, which see CPU geometry only.
