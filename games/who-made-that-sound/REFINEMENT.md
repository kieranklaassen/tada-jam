A gap **for the template**: `tone` plays one steady or gliding note and cannot warble (a slow wobble of the pitch). `tone` also falls away from its peak at once, so a long note sounds short. `sound.ts` adds one held note, steady, warbling or gliding, for the long voices and the glides, and uses `tone` and `noise` for everything else; a held note and a `warble` beside them in the template would let the next game with a voice leave the file alone.

Two things **for the template**: a beat's `play` is not called before its time, so "has this beat begun" has to be kept by the game (a flag set on the first call); and a play that only shows something (a call from everyone, a gulp of air) uses the same class with a save that does nothing, which works and reads oddly.

### How the game meets the quality bar

- **Alive at idle.** Everyone who is out sways and blinks at its own tempo (idle rounds from 0.9 to 3.6 seconds, `motion.ts`); a heard hide's eyes look about and blink; the one who asks holds out its wings and, left alone, calls again three times at most, half a second after each of the ghost hand's first three showings is over (8.5, 21.5 and 44.5 seconds), and those still hidden answer in turn; the one who waits rocks on its feet. The one who waits never calls. All of it runs on the attended clock and stands still while the game rests.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** The game answers when the finger lands, on `press`. Every tap the model can receive returns something that happened, every play starts with a sound at the moment of the touch (`plays.test.ts`, over more than 600 plays of seeded games), a tap on someone on the hill gets a call and a trick, and a tap on nothing that can be tapped is answered by what it lands on: two leaves and a swish on the hill, two chips and a tick on the stone, two scraps of brown paper and a flick on the page and the ground; so is a touch in the top right corner, and a second finger or a palm that lands while one finger is down. Nobody has heard the sounds.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** Each kind has a weight that sets how it squashes under a finger, how long its way out of the egg takes and how it crosses the page; every trick starts with a move the other way and ends with a settle. Tests fail on shared or near-copy motion: no two kinds share an entrance, an idle, a join or a reaction, and no two of the eighteen tricks or the twenty-four reactions are alike (`motion.test.ts`, `reactions.test.ts`).
- **Kid-clear.** Few, large pieces of one bright hue each on a white page; plain hides, all alike, on one plain dark strip; at most four in the row and four on the hill. `overlap.test.ts` holds, in every state of seeded games from every place, that no two things overlap, that every target is at least 100 by 100 with at least 24 clear pixels to the next, and that none reaches into the bottom strip.
- **Wordless clarity for the declared age.** No word, letter, numeral or symbol anywhere; `npm run -s wordless:check` passes. Every scene has one want (`guide.test.ts`): the one who asks, or the one who waits. A new way of asking is shown once by a pair of characters before the child tries it, and any touch ends the showing.
- **Wordless guidance.** The template's ladder, whole: after three seconds idle a pale piece of tissue breathes under the one thing to touch next, after five a ghost hand taps it once, the demonstrations back off and stop, and any touch clears everything. `guide.test.ts` holds that the hand always has something to show and never shows an attempt while someone asks.
- **60 fps on a mid-range iPad.** Not measured: this machine has no graphics card, and no physical iPad was measured. What is known and held by `frameBudget.test.ts`: every frame is exactly one full-surface stamp (the cached page) and at most 179 cached pieces laid with `drawImage`; after the first 19 frames of a visit no frame makes a canvas, paints or cuts anything, even while a figure changes size. In software drawing here, at pixel ratio 2 on the production build, the first frame took about half a second, each of the next 18 about 65 ms, and after that the game's own work was under 1 ms a frame. The lead measures on a real card.
- **Procedural or committed assets only.** Everything is drawn and sounded at run time from numbers and a seeded stream. No asset, no web address; `node scripts/egress-check.ts`, `npm run egress:built` and `npm run education:built` pass.
- **Its own art direction.** Painted-tissue collage, the first look reserved for the game. The art guide is in `ART.md` under "The look", and the text of the registry row is at its end.
- **Found as left.** The world is saved at every change, at once, and read back field by field (`save.test.ts`). The outcome of every scene is in the world before the scene starts (`saves.test.ts` holds the list the sheet gives for each). `show.test.ts` holds that after any game from any place the page settles into exactly the picture a fresh load of the saved world gives, that a touch in the middle of any scene lands everything at once on that picture, and that a loaded world plays no scene again. The next one always waits for the child's touch.
- **Nothing passes through anything.** A canvas game's own tests (`overlap.test.ts`): 4,200 seeded taps from every place of the order, 1,680 distinct worlds in two shapes of surface and 5,792 plays. ### What the next builder should know

- **Two taps separate hearing from choosing with one gesture.** A band that starts at 2 has only the tap. Making the first tap on a thing "hear it" and the second "try it" gives a listening game a moment of decision without a second gesture, and a child who mashes still opens everything.
- **Let the wrong choice out.** A wrong hide that simply stayed shut would be a refusal. Letting its creature out to meet the asker makes the error a scene, keeps the state consistent (its own grown one finds it later), and needs one rule: someone alone on the hill is always still waited for. `play.test.ts` holds that rule after every random tap.
- **Compute the chances of blind play exactly.** The sheet's claim about a child who guesses ("no wrong attempt one time in six") is a test that walks every branch of the model, not a sample. It is what lets the "Guess" answer be checked.
- **Write the judging of a cycle so that a free action cannot dodge it.** A first draft let a hide be opened while nobody asked, which would have counted as no wrong attempt. A tap on a hide now brings the next asker in first and is only ever a hearing.
- **A voice you cannot hear is still testable.** `voices.test.ts` holds ranges and the steps of difference between voices. Keep one rule beside it: only a voice has a pitch, and every other sound is noise, so nothing between two calls can be taken for a call.
- **When the sheet names lengths, make them a test.** The scene lengths in the sheet were guesses until `beats.ts` computed them from the voices; two were wrong.
- **Keep what moves apart from what is saved, and test that the one settles into the other.** The show (`show.ts`, and `toyShow.ts` before it) is plain numbers on game time with no renderer, built standing still from a saved state. One test then covers found-as-left for every animation at once: play anything, wait, and compare with a fresh load.
- **Hit a creature where it is drawn.** The model had already put the one who came out on the hill while it was still standing by its shell. Asking the show where it is now, and not the model where it will be, is what makes "whatever looks touchable is touchable" true in the two seconds that matter most. In the game this is `bound` in `show.ts`: the figures of a running scene are asked first, and the saved picture after them.
- **Do not let a hidden thing show through the things around it.** A first version squashed each egg, and knocked on it, by the weight of the kind inside. Every shell now squashes and knocks alike; only the call differs.
- **Look for negative zero when a test compares two pictures.** A lean that eased back to rest came out as -0 and failed a deep comparison that printed two identical objects.
- **Make the page a function of the saved state, and a scene only the way there.** `picture.ts` turns a world into who stands where. A play (`plays.ts`) starts from the picture before the tap and has to end on the picture after it; the view hides the things a play shows on their way. Found-as-left, "a touch ends the scene" and "no scene replays on load" are then one property, and one test covers it.
- **Sort what happened before staging it.** The model reports what a tap did in the order it did it: someone made way on the hill between the meeting and the settling. The director looked for "settles" right after "meets", missed it, and a reunion on a full hill played as nothing but a fade. A test that says "nobody is suddenly there" found it; scripted tests on an empty hill never would have.
- **Count canvases in a frame-budget test.** Counting pieces was not enough: the piece count was fine while a walking figure was being cut anew at every size.
- **Keep two things that look alike apart by more than their place.** The basket (one egg the child may add) and the nest (the clutch that is brought) were the same bowl side by side, and a cold look read them as two baskets. Different paper and a different carrier fixed what no test could have seen.
- **Let another worker write the tests for what you built.** The overlap and frame-budget tests were written by someone who had not written the plays or the view, from the rules alone, and were told to report failures instead of fixing the modules. Four real faults came back.
