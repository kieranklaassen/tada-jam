## The cold playtest proxy, age 3

A proxy is a script: it cannot hear, and it taps where it decided to. What it did in the first thirty seconds:

- 0 to 5 s. It watched. A thing lay on the earth with one eye open a crack, looking at the caps. At 3 s the heap of caps glowed; at 5 s a ghost hand tapped it.

### For the owner to decide

First, what is nearest to the things the jam refuses.

- What is nearest to praise: the big wind child puffs once more when a light creature sails up; the small one spins itself round when a roller wakes; the toad croaks low at a slitherer and when a creature beds down. Each answers the thing made, at its waking or its settling, and none looks at the child or follows a tap as such.
- What is nearest to blame: the toad starts upright and glares at a scuttler or a roller, once each time one runs by its stump (for one woken beside the stump, once in its waking); the big wind child strains red at a heavy creature; the small one hides behind its hands from five legs and peeks over them; a creature shakes off a part too many.
- What is nearest to a count: nothing is shown as a number and nothing is tallied, but the limits can be felt as one. A child may learn that the sixth creature sends one away, that a fifteenth part or a fifth cap is shaken off, and that there are three acorns to dig up. Nothing shows how many are up, how many are left or how near a limit is, and neither hand in the game has fingers to count.
- What is nearest to a prompt: the idle hint. Three seconds after any touch the heap whose part comes next begins to glow, and after five the ghost hand taps it for three seconds; if nothing is touched it comes again, after ten, twenty and forty seconds more, four times at most, and then the glow goes too. It asks for nothing by name, it never points at an act that sends anything away, and it never rises while a part is in the hand or a scene the child caused is playing; but it does come back five seconds after every touch for as long as the child plays in bursts.
- The three ways a creature can go for good. One: a seventh waking. The sixth sends the eldest to wait behind the trees with its eyes showing, and a tap brings it back; when another walks off after it, the one that waited shuts its eyes and is gone, which is seen. Two: an eye dragged a cap's width clear of a creature that has two; it comes apart, its parts fly back to heaps that never run out, and that is the one way a child unmakes a creature on purpose. Three: the game put away in the half second a cap flies to a sixth creature while one already waits. On load the eldest is found waiting with its eyes showing, as the child would have seen, and the one that waited before is gone as it would have been, but its eyes were not seen to shut. Before pass 9 this third way dropped the eldest itself with nothing to show for it. Whether one waiting place is enough, and whether two eyes in the trees are a thing a three-year-old finds, is the owner's to say.

Then the rest.

- The look: Wax crayon on a dark slate-violet earth.
- The toy: whether lifting, carrying and pressing parts on, and waking a thing with two eyes, is a pleasure in the hand with no goal.
- The rule for a tapped part: it goes to the thing the child is making, not to a lone part set down elsewhere.
- The limits, and that each is answered by something seen to go: a twenty-fifth loose part sends the loose part laid longest ago fluttering back to its heap on the small wind child's puff; a fifteenth part, a fifth cap or a part too far out is shaken off. Each of these takes something the child made, and each can be had back from a heap with a tap.
- The heaps and the three who watch stand in front of the creatures, sleepers included, and take the touch first. A heap is touched as far as it is drawn, about 98 units to either side of its middle, so a creature's limb that lies over the outer end of a heap is not reached there.
- A carry survives a lifted finger for a third of a second, as the sheet and the pack say: the part quivers and sinks a little while it waits, and a finger that lands within about 140 units of where it lifted carries on. The grace is for a carried part only: any other slide is answered on the frame the finger lifts.
- The idle hint with five creatures up: it points at the creature woken last (a tap pokes it, or wakes it if it sleeps), never at the caps, because one more waking would send the eldest off and lose the one that waits. With twenty-four loose parts about it points at nothing.
- A creature that walks off is past the trees within eight seconds, a finger held on it or not: its own way of going runs faster, up to twelve times for a strutter that is all tail, and it bolts when only a bolt can still take it past the trees in time.
- A long creature keeps lower on the ground the longer it is, so that no limb passes under the home control or into the top-right corner; the very longest can turn right round only in the lower middle of the ground.
- A creature made of many long parts is large: no part lies further than 330 units from its middle, so it can be over half of the stage across.
- The glow comes by growing out from under its heap, never by fading in: laid thin over the dark earth a yellow turns grey. When the hint moves to another heap the glow draws back in under the one and grows out under the other.
- The kept pictures are made once before the first frame at a load, a put-away and a change of surface size (up to 180 stamps for the fullest clearing). That work is in no frame and is not in the draw number; on a device it may show as one longer wait before the first frame.

### Defaults taken for the owner

- A child of four or younger, or of no known age, finds a thing begun on a first visit; from five, bare earth. Age changes nothing after that.
- The limits: five creatures and one that waits, fourteen parts and four caps on one thing, no part further than 330 units from its thing's middle, twenty-four loose parts.

### What the next builder should know

- A hit test that takes "the nearest part" lets a creature standing close steal a part meant for another thing. A part that was sent somewhere names where it goes.
- What is drawn in front is touched first. Decide the drawing order of props and characters once, and make the hit test follow it.
- Bake a character's picture after the frame is drawn, when the frame's cost is known, not before it from a guess.
- Stopping the dev server by the PID of the wrapper leaves the server running; find the server's own PID.
- Compare what is found with what was left, not two reads with each other: two reads agree about a sleeper that both moved out of its bed.
- A hit test on unposed parts misses a creature at rest as well as one that tumbles. Test it against what the view drew.
- A kept picture that lags its creature by a frame shows a part twice or not at all. Draw a part live until the picture holds it, and test every frame of a waking for exactly one of each part.
- A grace after a lift belongs to a carried thing only. Given to every drag, it makes a poke late and lets two pokes add up to a stroke.
- An idle hint is an instruction. Check what copying it does in every state the limits allow, the full ones first.
- A wait counted in frames is a different wait on every display. Count it on the game's clock, and test it at two frame rates.
- What is saved ahead of the moment (a part in the air where it will land) must be saved with everything that landing would cause, or a load drops what the moment would have shown going.
- Tests of live strokes say nothing about kept pictures. Paint each picture with a recording crayon and hold its pressed lines.
