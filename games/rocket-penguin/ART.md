# Design sheet

Written before any game code, and checked by someone who did not write it before the game is built on the toy. The headings stay in this order. The look follows the sheet at the end of this file.

What each heading asks for is in the section "The design sheet" of `docs/solutions/conventions/building-a-jam-game.md`.

## The band and its age rule

- **Band.** The manifest says 8 to 12. The design is held to an eight-year-old.
- **The cue-table row.** The row for 7 and up governs. Its "Avoid" column is taken as a hard limit: no written word or letter, no symbol that stands alone so that play depends on reading it, no timer, points or verdict chrome, and no long chain of hints.
- **The pack's rule for the age** (pack: game-design, ages-9-to-12.md). The flight is a real system that behaves the same way every time: one model of pull, weight, drag, thrust, lift and bounce, in which every part is a force and nothing is scripted. A place can be reached by more than one route, and any route that gets there stands. A flight that misses is large, funny and free. The humour is dry and is not explained. Nothing is stored as a best and nothing is compared for the child. Difficulty comes with a way back in: the igloo close by can be reached by a bare penguin within the first few pulls, and it stays there.
- **Help is fetched, not sent.** The idle ladder shows what can be touched (the penguin in the pouch) and one move (a short pull, eased back, with no flight). With a rocket bolted on it shows a second move in turn with the first: the hand rests on bare paper in one long press, not a tap, and the rocket's flame flares for as long as it rests. After the first flight of a visit that had the rocket on and no finger held in it, the hand, the next time it comes, rests in the air instead, on the pencil line of that flight a little way along it; it does so once, and nothing of it is counted or saved. The help a child goes and gets is on the page: the pencil line of the last flight, and the ring where the pouch was last let go.
- **Symbols.** The band starts at 8, so numerals and mathematics signs would be allowed beside a quantity. This game draws none: no numeral, no sign, no letter and no word anywhere on the kid side, and the squares of the paper are never numbered. The `symbols.ts` the generator copied stays as copied and nothing calls it.
- **What `ctx.childAge` sets.** Nothing that differs by age. A first visit starts the same at every age, younger than the band and older than it included, and with no age (`null`): the bare penguin in the pouch, nothing bolted on, the whole kit beside the catapult. Every part and every place can be reached at every age from the first pull.

## The toy

**Pull the penguin back in the catapult, and let go.**

- **What the finger does.** It lands on the penguin in the pouch. In the same frame the penguin squashes down into the pouch, its eyes go wide, its feet grip, and the rubber gives one creak. Nothing waits for the finger to move or lift.
- **The stretch.** As the finger pulls, the pouch follows it and the two bands stretch from the fork. The bands thin as they lengthen, the fork bends towards the finger, the sledge under the catapult lifts its front runner, and the penguin leans back further the harder it is pulled. The rubber creaks in ticks, one for each small step of stretch, and each tick is higher than the last, so the ear hears the force. The pouch stops at the longest stretch, at the ice, and where the near seal and the ball on its nose begin, so that the seated penguin is never laid into them; pulled further, the bands only tremble.
- **The release.** When the finger lets go, the bands snap forward with a twang whose pitch follows the stretch, the fork whips past upright and rocks back, the sledge drops with a knock, and the penguin leaves the pouch along the line from the pouch to the fork, at a speed that follows the stretch. It stretches long as it leaves and gives one honk.
- **The flight.** The penguin flies by the model alone: its weight pulls it down and the air slows it. It tumbles slowly, its scarf trails behind, and a pencil line is drawn along its path as it goes. Every creature on the page follows it with its eyes.
- **The landing.** Where it comes down, the landscape answers (the bare-penguin row of the grid below). On bare ice a steep arrival sticks it in head first, upside down, with a thunk and its feet waggling, and a shallow one skids it along on its belly with a long scrape. Then a honk, muffled when its head is in the ice; then it pulls itself out with a pop, or gets up off its belly with a hop, shakes, toboggans back to the catapult on its belly along the back of the shelf and hops up into the pouch, which sags and creaks. The catapult waits loaded.
- **What stays.** The pencil line of that flight stays on the paper. When the next flight leaves, the older line is rubbed to a pale smudge, and the one before that is rubbed away. A pale tracing of the pouch, which this sheet calls the ring, stays in the air behind the fork where the pouch was last let go, leaning as the pouch leaned, so the child can pull to the same spot again or a square beside it. The lines are a view and are not saved; the ring is saved. A line is kept for being the latest, never for being the longest or the highest: the flight before last is rubbed away whatever it reached, nothing is drawn at the end of a line or beside it to mark how far it went, and no line outlasts the visit.
- **Other touches.** A tap on the penguin without a pull pokes it: it bounces in the pouch and squawks. A second finger on it, while the first rests elsewhere on the page, pokes it too. A finger that moves no further than a tap allows (14 pixels of the surface) pokes it and launches nothing. The shortest pull a finger can make past that, up to about a square, plops it out onto its face at the foot of the catapult; on an upright tablet, where the page is drawn smaller and the same finger pulls further on it, the shortest pull can reach the near seal's flank, and the seal tosses it on. A pull the wrong way works too: pulled up above the fork, the penguin is fired down into the ice at the foot of the sledge; pulled to the right of the fork, it is fired backwards: gently, onto the lid of the crate behind the catapult, which rattles; hard, over the crate and off the left edge of the paper. A press on anything else on the page is answered by that thing as itself, and changes nothing in the flight: the near seal barks and wobbles, the far seal snorts in its sleep, the chick peeps and hops, the walrus harrumphs through its moustache, the puffin ducks into its basket, the whale answers where it is pressed, each part of it in its own way (its eye rolls with a moan, its head puffs, its back rumbles under the finger's tickle, its tail swishes and stirs up bubbles), the igloo knocks hollow, the crate rattles, the iceberg rings, the cloud parts round the finger with a breath, and bare paper takes a pencil dot with a tick, which a rubber smudges out a moment later.
- **Why it is a pleasure with no goal.** The stretch is felt in the hand, the eye and the ear at once, and the release pays it back in the same frame with a snap, a honk and a body that flies exactly as hard as it was pulled. A harder pull is a longer line on the paper, so the child sees its own hand in the drawing. Any pull does something funny, the weakest included, and a person watching knows in three seconds what the child is doing.
- **In the first build of the toy** there is the catapult, the bare penguin, the whole landscape with every creature in its place and answering a press, and the bare-penguin row of the grid. The kit is a shut crate behind the catapult, which rattles when pressed and is solid: its lid can be landed on. No part can be bolted on yet, no fish is carried, and no friend is fed.

## The object-by-action grid, and what is new on day 15

Seven loads the penguin can be flung with (the six parts of the kit, and none) by five things in the landscape it can come down on. Every cell is a different sight and a different sound, and none is refused. Uses that go wrong are marked ✗: each works, follows from the model, and is at least as funny as the right one.

| | bare ice | a seal | the snowdrift | the whale's spout | the thin cloud |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **the bare penguin** | steep, it sticks in by the beak with a thunk, feet waggling; shallow, it skids on its belly with a long scrape | bounces off the belly, which dips under it and wobbles back, to a round boing and one bark from the near seal, a deeper boing and a snort from the far one | ✗ is swallowed to the feet with a soft whump; the feet waggle, and the honk comes out muffled | is lifted on top of the water, turning over and over, to a rush and a gargled honk | comes out wearing a beard of mist that trails off behind, with a breathy hush and a sneeze |
| **the rocket** | ✗ still burning, it ploughs along the shelf like a sledge, leaving a wet furrow, with a sizzle | ✗ the seal's whiskers curl in the heat; it bounces the penguin on and sneezes soot, with a hiss cut short and a cough | ✗ melts a tunnel: steam puffs from the top, the drift slumps, and the penguin comes out the far side sooty, to a kettle's whistle | the water puts the flame out with a spit and a fizzle; it lights again once clear, if fuel is left | punches a torn hole through the cloud each time it burns into it, which stays for that flight, with a soft pop |
| **the glider wings** | lands like an aeroplane: a long low run on the belly with the wingtips drawing two lines, to two thin squeaks | bounces into a long flat hop while the near seal claps twice (the far one waves a flipper at it in its sleep), to a flap like a shaken sheet | ✗ the wings stay stuck out of the drift on either side and it cannot sink; it hangs there kicking, to a creak of canvas | rides the water up like a kite, higher than anything else does, to a flutter that rises | ✗ the wings soak, droop and drip, and lift a third as much for the rest of the flight, with a squelch |
| **the umbrella** | comes down on its feet and stays standing, to two taps of feet; fast, the umbrella turns inside out with a fwop | ✗ lands on the open umbrella, which the seal balances on its nose for a moment, turned over with the penguin sat in its cloth, before tipping it on, to a held rubbery squeak; the near seal puts its ball aside onto its belly as the umbrella comes down, and back on its nose afterwards; each seal does this once in a flight, and bounces it like any other load after that | lands umbrella first and sits in it like a bowl on the drift, to a pat and a hush of sliding snow | the open umbrella catches the water and is driven up like a cork, drumming | keeps the penguin and anything else bolted on dry, to a patter on the cloth |
| **the balloon** | touches down on tiptoe and goes on in slow hops, each a little shorter, to small rubber squeaks | the seal noses the balloon like a ball and sends the penguin up slower and higher, with a hollow bomp | ✗ the penguin is swallowed and the balloon stays above the drift tugging at its string, until it pulls the penguin out with a plop | the water spins the penguin round its own string like a top, to a whirr | gathers dew, sags, and pulls up half as hard for the rest of the flight, with a wet rubbing squeak |
| **the spring feet** | bounces on, and on, each bounce three quarters of the last, to a twang that climbs as the bounces shorten | ✗ spring on seal: the seal is knocked flat out, its flippers flung wide and its belly dipping, comes back indignant, and the bounce is the highest in the game, to a deep wobbling twang and an oof | punches in feet first to the knees, and the coiled springs push it back out the way it came, one hop, with a crunch and a muffled spring | the coils fill with water, and squirt it out at the next landing, to a gurgle | ✗ pedals on nothing: the coils dangle and jangle loose all the way through |
| **the fish on a rod** | swings forward on landing and slaps the penguin across the face, wetly | ✗ the seal snaps the fish off the hook in passing, swallows and burps; the bounce is the same as ever and the rod is bare | the fish freezes into a block with a clink and stays frozen | the fish comes to life in the water and flaps in the penguin's face all the way up, slapping; it comes out wet | ✗ a gull that lives in the cloud flies alongside with its beak open as far as the cloud's edge and is left behind squawking; the fish comes out wet |

Beside the grid: the sea (any load goes in with a splash, the penguin floats there like a bath duck, and then swims back to the shelf), and the solid things, which the model treats as walls and floors: the igloo, the iceberg, the edge of the shelf, the whale's back, the kit's crate, whose lid is a floor, the skin of the weather balloon (a soft bomp) and its basket. The catapult is not one of them: its post, fork, bands and sledge stand a step further back than the flight, so the penguin leaves through the fork at every release, and a flight that comes back that way passes in front of the catapult without touching it and comes down on the ice or the crate beyond.

**How each thing flies** is the game's whole model, and it never changes. Any two parts bolt on together, and their forces add. A third part is never refused: it goes on, and the part bolted on first drops back into the kit with a clank.

- The pull. The speed at the pouch follows the stretch, along the line from the pouch to the fork. Every part has weight, and the same stretch gives a heavier penguin less speed.
- Weight pulls down, always the same. The air's drag grows with the square of the speed.
- The rocket pushes along the way the penguin points for as long as a finger is held on the page and fuel lasts: one tank a flight, about a second and a half of burning. It is the heaviest part. It shows that it wants the finger: with fuel in it and no finger held, it sputters a small pilot flame, in the pouch and in the air, which leans to where the last touch was and pushes nothing; in the pouch a finger rested on bare paper makes it flare; in the air a held finger anywhere makes it the whole flame with a roar, and a lift cuts it back to the sputter at once.
- The glider wings lift at right angles to the flight, more the faster it goes (with the square of the speed), up to what the canvas holds; past that they flutter. They add a little drag. Launched straight up with no speed left at the top, they lift nothing and the penguin comes down like a leaf.
- The umbrella is shut while the penguin rises and opens when it falls, and the whale's water holds it open from below: a large drag on the fall only. In a skid it is shut.
- The balloon pulls up steadily with four fifths of the bare penguin's weight, and adds drag. It never carries the penguin off: with any load, the penguin still comes down.
- The spring feet do nothing in the air. With them the penguin comes down feet first; off ice and the iceberg it keeps three quarters of its speed at each bounce, off a seal it keeps the whole of its speed where a load without them keeps nine tenths (with the balloon on as well, four fifths of either), and out of the snowdrift they push it back the way it came by one hop.
- The fish on a rod is weight, and it is cargo. It leaves the kit fresh, comes out of the spout or the cloud wet, and out of the snowdrift frozen; the last of these wins. A seal eats it in passing.
- The landscape is fixed. A seal sends the penguin on with nine tenths of its speed and never less than its own fixed toss, upwards, and never back the way it came; a balloon it noses first, and the penguin goes on with four fifths of that speed, still never less than the toss. The snowdrift stops everything dead but two: a rocket still burning melts through it and comes out the far side slower, and the spring feet push the penguin back out by one hop. The whale blows whenever something is above its blowhole, and only then, as hard each time. The cloud is thin: it slows a little and wets. Ice is slippery and the skid is long.

**On day 15** the child flies to places on purpose and by more than one route. It reaches the weather balloon with the rocket and the balloon straight up, or with the wings in one long climb, or with the spring feet off the near seal; it reaches the iceberg bare in three bounces off the seals and the whale's water, or across the water with the rocket; it sends the fish into the snowdrift on one flight because the walrus likes it frozen and carries it on to the iceberg with the rocket on the next, or under the umbrella through the cloud to keep it fresh for the chick. Fifteen pairs of parts, by angle and force, by five things to come down on, give more flights than a child has tried, and what the child found on day 3 is still true on day 15 because the model has not changed. The child's aim is better too, and the gain is seen on the page: the line lands where it was meant to.

## The representation

This game has no learning goal, so no school idea is represented and no claim rests on how the flight is drawn.

## The four mechanic questions

This game has no learning goal, so the four questions are not answered for a school skill; the verb is the pull and the release, and it is used in the most enjoyable moment of play.

## The error as a consequence

There is no wrong flight, only a flight that came down somewhere else.

- **What it does.** The penguin comes down where the model puts it, and that place answers as the grid says: beak in the ice, feet out of the drift, a splash and a swim. It is as good to watch as an arrival.
- **Where and why.** The pencil line of the flight stays on the paper and shows the whole path: where it rose, where it turned, what it struck and where it stopped. The older line under it shows the flight before, so the child sees what the last change did. The ring behind the fork shows where the pouch was let go.
- **The state stays.** The same parts are still bolted on, the ring is where it was, the landscape has not moved, and the penguin comes back and sits in the pouch. The child changes one thing (a square more stretch, a steeper pull, one part swapped) and lets go again.
- **Nothing gives a verdict.** No buzzer, no mark, no sad face turned to the child, no praise for an arrival. A friend's feelings are about the fish and the penguin in front of it. Nothing is lost by a flight that misses: no part breaks, the fish in the kit never runs out, and nothing counts flights.

## The designed order, and what is stored

**There is one place in the designed order.** Its id is `kit`, and it is the only entry of `LADDER` in `config.ts`. Every part is in the kit from the first visit and every place in the landscape is there from the first visit, so the game has no later place to move to, and no cycle is judged well or badly. The id names the open kit and no grade, level or stage.

**What a first visit starts with, by age.** The same at every age and with no age: the bare penguin in the pouch, nothing bolted on, the six parts lying in the kit, the three friends at their places with their wants showing, and clean paper with no line and no ring.

**What play changes.** Which parts are bolted on; the ring where the pouch was last let go; who is eating a fish; the fish on the rod as the last flight that came down left it, fresh, wet or frozen. During a visit, and not kept beyond it: the pencil lines of the last two flights, a hole burnt in the cloud, soot, wet wings, a sagging balloon, and what happens to the fish in a flight that is put away before it comes down.

**How a harder thing looks harder, and is chosen by the child.** The three friends wait at three places that look as hard as they are: the igloo close by on the ice, the iceberg far out across open water, the weather balloon high above the cloud. The child picks where to aim, and nothing asks for one before another. Nothing a finger works or presses sits under the jam's home control at the top centre of the frame: the weather balloon, its basket and the puffin hang wholly to one side of it, and the pouch at its longest stretch stays below it.

**Every field of the saved state.**

| Field | What it holds | Default, and how a damaged value is read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `v` | The version of the record, 1. | A record with another version, or no record, gives a fresh state. |
| `position` | The place in the designed order: always `kit`. | Anything else is read as `kit`. |
| `finished` | The template's mark for a finished cycle. This game judges no cycle and always saves `false`. | Anything else is read as `false`. |
| `bolted` | The ids of the parts bolted on, none, one or two of `rocket`, `wings`, `umbrella`, `balloon`, `springs`, `fish`, in the order they were bolted. | Not a list: none. An unknown id or a repeat is dropped; more than two keeps the first two. |
| `last` | Where the pouch was last let go, as its offset from rest in whole stage units, `{ x, y }`, or `null` before the first flight. It places the ring. | Not two finite numbers, or further than the longest stretch: `null`. |
| `eating` | Which friend is eating, and the fish it got: `{ who, fish }` with `who` one of `chick`, `walrus`, `puffin` and `fish` one of `fresh`, `wet`, `frozen`; or `null`. | Anything else: `null`. |
| `rod` | The fish on the rod, as the last flight that came down left it: one of `fresh`, `wet`, `frozen`. It is fresh again once a seal or a friend has had it, and when the rod is bolted on anew. | Anything else, or no rod bolted on: `fresh`. |

Nothing is saved in the air. A flight is a view of what was let go: put away in the middle of a pull, a flight, a landing or the way back, the game is found with the penguin in the pouch, the same parts on, and the ring where the pouch was last let go. A pull that was taken away (the game parked under the finger) is no release: the pouch eases back and the ring does not move. The largest state is a few hundred bytes.

The toy of the first build saves `v`, `position`, `finished` and `last`. `bolted`, `eating` and `rod` come with the kit and the fish.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

Each has one want that is always visible, and likes and dislikes that never change. No one's feelings are about the child, and no one refers to the child stopping, leaving or coming back. The pilot aside, each is drawn in pencil only; the one coloured pencil is kept for what the finger works.

| Character | Wants, and how it shows | Likes | Dislikes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The pilot** (the penguin in the pouch) | To be flung: it sits in the pouch with its goggles down, leaning back, looking at the finger | Height: in the air it spreads its flippers the whole way, and its eyes are wide as it leaves the pouch | Snow down its neck: out of the drift it shivers from the feet up. Its beak stuck in the ice it takes without complaint |
| **The chick** (in the doorway of the igloo, close by) | A fish: it holds a tin plate out towards the catapult with both flippers | A fresh fish: swallowed whole, and the shape of the fish goes down its front | A frozen fish: it licks it and its tongue sticks. A wet fish it eats, after the fish has slipped out of its flippers three times |
| **The walrus** (in a hollow in the face of the iceberg, far out) | A fish: a napkin is tied round its neck and an open tin stands in front of it | A frozen fish: it crunches it like an ice lolly, slowly, with its eyes shut | A wet fish: it wrings it out like a cloth with a look of pain, then eats it. A fresh fish it eats with great manners and no feeling |
| **The puffin** (in the basket of the weather balloon, high up) | A fish: it leans out with a spyglass on the catapult and a landing net held ready | A wet fish: it slurps it down like a noodle | A frozen fish: it raps it on the rim of the basket, finds it hard, and uses it as a hammer on its instruments. A fresh fish it lays crosswise in its beak and keeps for later |
| **The near seal** | Something to bounce: it lies on its back balancing a ball on its nose, nose up | Anything that lands on it. A fish, eaten in passing | Nothing. Singed whiskers get one sneeze |
| **The far seal** (at the edge of the shelf) | To sleep: it lies on its back, snoring, one flipper waving | Sleep. It bounces what lands on it without waking up, and a fish it eats in its sleep | Being landed on by the spring feet: it wakes, glares at nobody, and sleeps again |
| **The whale** | To blow: its eye watches the sky above its blowhole | Something overhead to blow at | Nothing |
| **The gull** (in the cloud; seen only when a fish goes through) | The fish | The fish | The cloud's edge, which it will not cross |

A friend reached without a fish answers the penguin in front of it and nothing else: the chick turns its plate over, looks under it and hops onto the penguin, onto its head where it stands and onto its back where it lies on its belly; the walrus sighs through its moustache so that the penguin's scarf streams out; the puffin puts the spyglass to the penguin from a hand's width away.

## The scenes

One scene plays at a time, on game time, and any touch ends it with every beat landed in order and silent.

**The landing.** Cause: the penguin comes to rest. Beats, 3 to 6 seconds: the place's own answer from the grid, with its sound; a honk and the feet waggling (the honk muffled when its head is in the ice or the snow, a squawk when it stands, floats or hangs on), while the nearest creature reacts in its own way (the near seal claps, the far seal snores on, the chick hops, the whale's eye goes wide, the walrus raises one eyebrow); the penguin pulls itself out with a pop, or gets up with a hop, and shakes; it toboggans back on its belly along the back of the shelf, behind whatever stands on the ice, and from the water it first dives, leaves a row of bubbles as far as the shelf, and leaps out onto it; from the iceberg it first runs to the edge and dives in, and from the far side of the basket it first drops into the water; the way back takes longer from further off; it hops up into the pouch, which sags and creaks. Filled in from the state of play: where and how it came down, what is bolted on, and how far the way back is. Saved when it starts: nothing; `last` was saved at the release, at once. A touch during it puts the penguin in the pouch at once, and that touch is then an ordinary touch, so a child who wants the next pull has it.

**The delivery.** Cause: the penguin comes to rest at a friend's place with the fish still on the rod. A friend's place is the ice round the igloo, the flat top of the iceberg, and the side of the basket of the weather balloon. The balloon hangs right over the basket, so nothing comes down into it: a penguin that meets the side of the basket gently holds on there, and one that meets it hard comes off again. Beats, 6 to 9 seconds: the friend takes the fish off the rod, and is seen to (the chick hops out to it with its plate, the walrus reaches a flipper to it on a long arm, the puffin takes it in its landing net); its answer to the fish as it is, fresh, wet or frozen, from the table above; it settles to eating; then the beats of the landing from the pull-out on, as one scene with it. Filled in from: which friend, and the state of the fish. Saved when it starts, at once: `eating`, set to that friend and that fish. A touch ends it as above, and the friend is found eating.

**The arrival without a fish.** Cause: the penguin comes to rest at a friend's place with no fish. Beats, 3 to 5 seconds: the friend's answer to an empty-handed penguin, then the beats of the landing from the pull-out on. Saved when it starts: nothing.

**Two secrets**, never hinted at, the same every time. A flight that leaves the paper by its right or left edge comes down off the page with a clatter of pencils, and the penguin walks back onto the paper with a curl of pencil shaving on its head. A penguin that sticks by the beak in the ice in front of the igloo's door gets the chick perched on its feet. Neither is counted or kept.

**How a cycle ends and how the next one starts.** A cycle is one flight. It ends with the penguin back in the pouch and the catapult loaded, and it stays so for as long as the child likes: nothing else starts, nothing counts down, and no one hurries. A friend who got a fish goes on eating it, slowly, and swallows the last of it at the next press on the pouch. The next cycle starts when the child pulls. On load no scene plays: the penguin is in the pouch, the parts are on, the ring is in its place, and a friend who was eating is eating.

## The look

Written after the style spike and rewritten when the frame was filled out, and not part of the sheet.

**The look in use is Squared-paper pencil**, the first of the two reserved for this game. The spike was the toy itself. It did not fail on clarity or on the frame budget, so the second reserved look was not tried. The owner found bare frames and small characters too minimal in other games, so this frame was filled out in the same look and not swapped: the first still of the second run is the filled frame.

**What it is.** The whole game is a drawing in a school exercise book: soft graphite pencil on cream paper printed with pale blue squares. Nothing is a filled colour. A thing is its outline, and tone is hatching inside the outline.

**The palette.**

| What | Colour | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | `#f7f4ec` | The page, and the inside of every figure, so that what is behind does not show through |
| The printed squares | a pale blue, `rgba(96, 148, 204, 0.4)` | The grid, 20 units to a square, over the whole surface |
| Graphite | `rgb(54, 57, 68)`, through a grain | Every line, every hatch, every eye |
| The orange pencil | `rgb(228, 98, 20)`, through its own grain | Only what the finger works: the pilot's beak, feet and scarf, the pouch, the six parts of the kit in the crate and on the penguin, the fish on its way to a friend, and the glow of the idle ladder |

**The materials.**

- **A line is gone over twice.** The second pass is thinner and a little off the first, as a hand goes over a line. Lines are 1 to about 3.5 units wide; an outline that is struck or stood on is 2.4 to 2.8, a detail 1 to 1.6.
- **Every stroke is drawn through a tile of grain**, so the tooth of the paper shows in it. The grain is one speck to a device pixel at any size.
- **Tone is hatching**, clipped to the shape: one set of strokes for a light tone, two sets across each other for the dark (the pilot's coat, the doorway of the igloo, the walrus's hollow). Hatches run at a slant, 3 to 13 units apart, and no two strokes are quite parallel.
- **The squares are printed, so they go over everything.** They are drawn last, as one stroke, and show through every figure.
- **The world is drawn in section**, as in a school book: the shelf is cut through, with a whole fish, a kettle and a bottle frozen into it; the kit is a crate sunk into the shelf and cut through like it, its six parts lying in six compartments; the sea is hatched level; the whale lies under the shelf nearly from end to end with only its blowhole out, its tail a figure of its own that swishes; the iceberg is cut off by the edge of the paper.
- **The paper is somebody's.** At the left edge a ragged hole is torn out where a ring pulled through, and lower down is the pale mark of a strip of tape; the two are unlike each other. A dot left by a finger on bare paper is rubbed out a moment later, and the rubbing shows.
- **No light and no shadow.** There is no lamp in a pencil drawing: the shade on a round thing is a patch of hatching on the side away from the moon, and nothing casts a shadow.

**A full frame.** The frame is a place from edge to edge. The sky is hatched all over in two directions; in it are two banks of shaded cloud, the weather balloon, a cratered moon, single stars, and three curtains of pencilled lights that come and go. The horizon is a range of mountains, shaded on the side away from the moon. Under the ice the page is the cut shelf, the whale and the hatched sea. Measured on the still at rest, in cells of one square: 6 in 100 hold almost no pencil (under 3 parts in 100 of the cell), and 15 in 100 hold under a tenth. On a surface that is not the stage's shape the page goes on past the stage: sky above, sea below.

**Sizes.** The stage is 1180 by 820. The pilot is 108 wide and 132 tall. A seal is about 170 long and its back 58 high; the chick is about 80 tall and fills its doorway; the walrus's face is about 110 across and fills its hollow; the weather balloon is 188 across and the puffin leans out over the rim of the basket under it; the whale is about 550 long. Against the toy of the first run the pilot is 1.65 times the size, the puffin 1.8, the seals about 1.45, the chick 1.4 and the walrus 1.3. They are not twice: the whole flight stays on one page with no camera, the seals, the igloo, the iceberg and the basket are the shapes a flight strikes, and the lanes between them are what a pull is aimed through.

**The working pieces are the plainest things in the frame.** The catapult's post, fork and sledge are bare ruled outlines in a paler pencil than anything else on the ice, so that they read as standing a step further back; the bands are two plain strokes; the post is tied to the sledge by one binding wound round it at a slant; the crate is a ruled box. Where the pouch was last let go there is a pale tracing of the pouch itself, cup and eyelets, leaning as the pouch leaned: the sheet calls it the ring. The fork is the catapult itself, two prongs wide of the penguin, and is half hidden by the penguin, the pouch and the bands whenever the catapult is loaded.

**No mark that reads as a letter, a numeral or a sign.** Every pencil mark was looked at for one. No star is joined to another by a line. No line crosses a mountain from flank to flank, and each mountain is shaded on one side, so that none is a bare triangle. The hole a rocket tears in the cloud has a ragged edge like the cloud's own, so that two of them side by side are tears and not rings. The fish frozen into the shelf is whole, not a row of bones on a spine. The cracks that were on the iceberg are gone, and so is the hut that stood far off. After the first reading four more marks were changed: the lone pencil ring where the pouch was let go is now the tracing of the pouch, a part of a figure and no ring; the two level bars across the catapult's post are one binding wound at a slant and hatched; the seal's ball has two hatched patches where it had two chevrons that crossed; and the crumbs of mist after a sneeze are dots, not a zigzag. After the second reading three more: the rocket's two level straps are one strap at a slant with a buckle on it; the two punched holes are the torn hole and the tape mark; and the darn on the weather balloon, which was a square hatched both ways, is a patch hatched one way with a stitch of a different length along each edge and no outline. One mark stays, and it is the weakest of them: the fork is the shape a fork is, two prongs on a post, whenever the penguin is away. It is the catapult itself, it is paler than anything else on the ice, and it is half hidden whenever the catapult is loaded. `symbols.ts` is never called: no numeral and no sign is drawn at all.

**Depth.** From the furthest back to the nearest: the page; the weather balloon, the puffin and its basket; the back lane of the shelf, where the penguin toboggans home behind whatever stands on the ice; the far seal; the snowdrift, which has blown up against the igloo's far side, and a penguin sat on it in its umbrella; the igloo and the chick, who is drawn in front of the penguin when it has hopped onto it; the near seal and its ball; the crate; the catapult, drawn pale; the pouch and the bands round the seated penguin; the plane of the flight. The cloud is thin and is drawn over what flies through it, and the printed squares are over everything. An umbrella balanced on a seal's nose is drawn in front of the seal and of the penguin sat in it, and the near seal's ball is put aside onto its belly before the umbrella's point comes down, clear of the cloth, and goes back to the nose afterwards (`nearSeal.test.ts`). A fish that a friend takes hangs where it hung until the friend's reach has come to it, and then moves only in that reach: the chick's plate, the walrus's flipper on a long arm, the puffin's net (`perch.test.ts`). A penguin buried in the drift or hung in it by its wings is drawn behind the drift.

**What is allowed to overlap, with its cap.** `intersections.test.ts` plays every load from pulls all over the grid at 1180 by 820, 1024 by 768 and 820 by 1180, and holds the penguin's round no more than half a unit inside any solid thing in any frame.

- The pilot is flown as a round of radius 54 and drawn 132 tall, so its head and its feet stand 12 units past the round: a tumbling penguin's crown or toes can lie that far over the outline of a thing it touches, and no further (under a test).
- Stuck by the beak, the head is in the ice and is not drawn below the ice. Lying on its belly, in the skid and after it, the tip of the beak ploughs the snow and is cut off at the ice in the same way. Floating, it is cut off at the water.
- **What is bolted on is kept out of the ice and the seals by how it moves, not by a clip.** Lying on its belly, the penguin folds the wing that is under it back along its side, where it trails, and carries the rod over its back so that it trails behind with the fish hanging above the ice. It faces the way it goes, so a penguin sent back the other way slides belly down and never on its back. Anything else that would meet the ice, the iceberg or a seal is turned about its joint by what it meets, by the least that frees it: a wing folds against a seal, the balloon leans off it, the fish lies over on the ice with its tail behind. The rocket is strapped round the penguin's middle and slides round on its strap. Between the shelf and the iceberg, where the open umbrella has no room, what it meets knocks it shut. `clearance.test.ts` draws every frame of whole flights, for every load from pulls all over the grid with the rocket held and not, and holds the outline of every part as it was stamped no more than 1 unit inside the ice of the shelf, the iceberg or either seal, in the air, on the bounce, in the skid, at the stop and getting up. It counts no clip.
- Not measured there, because the sheet says where the part is: in the pouch before the flight; in the water, where the penguin floats like a bath duck; in the snowdrift; and on the way home along the back of the shelf, behind what stands on the ice.
- Balanced on a seal's nose, the open umbrella is turned over and stands on the nose by its point, drawn whole with no clip, and the penguin sits in its cloth. The nose is on the seal's own round, so the cloth beside the point lies over the top of the round, in front of it, by at most 24 units (under the same test).
- A seal gives under a blow by at most 4 units from the round the model strikes, in every pose (under a test in `view.test.ts`). The blow shows in its flippers, its eyes, the ball and the strokes that stand off its belly.
- The seal's ball is knocked off its nose and the puffin is down behind its rim before a body reaches either (under a test).
- The pull stops where the near seal and its ball begin: the seated penguin's outline, as it is drawn and however it leans, is never in the seal's round or over the ball (`nearSeal.test.ts`).
- The chick, hopped onto the penguin, stands on what is drawn under it: its feet are within 3 units of the top of the penguin's outline as stamped (the head when it stands, the back when it lies, the rocket strapped on that back, the soles when it is stuck by the beak), for every load, and it gets there and home by a hop of more than a dozen frames (`perch.test.ts`).
- The walrus's reach for the fish is drawn over the roof of its hollow for the moment it lasts: an arm in front of what it reaches past.
- The catapult is not solid: the sheet says a flight passes in front of it.
- The cloud and the whale's water are passed through: the sheet says so. The snowdrift takes what comes into it.
- Sat in its umbrella on the drift, the penguin's round rests on the crest and the bowl is drawn in front of it; its feet are in the bowl and are not drawn. The igloo stands in front of the drift, so it can hide a part of the bowl.

**How things move.**

- The pilot is light and impatient: it swings in the sling, its feet kick in turn, its scarf follows it late. It squashes under the finger, stretches as it leaves, and tumbles in the air.
- The rocket wants the finger and shows it: with fuel in it and no finger held it sputters a small pilot flame, in the pouch and in the air, that leans to where the last touch was; a finger rested on bare paper makes it flare in the pouch; a held finger in flight makes it the whole flame, and a lift cuts it back in the same frame.
- The near seal is fat and slow: its belly rises and sinks, the ball on its nose is always about to fall, and it wobbles a long time after a bounce. Its clap is two beats, each heard as the flippers meet.
- The far seal is asleep: a long breath in and a quick one out, a bubble at its nose, one flipper waving at a dream.
- The chick is small and quick: it bobs on its toes and tips its head from side to side.
- The walrus is nearly still: its moustache twitches now and then, one brow goes up, and it blinks slowly.
- The puffin is stiff and busy: its spyglass goes out and in, and it ducks behind the rim.
- The whale is huge and patient: its eye drifts, and now and then it lets a breath go. Pressed, it answers where it is pressed: the eye rolls once right round and comes to rest on the penguin before it watches the sky again, the head puffs, the back shivers under a few strokes of tickle, the tail swishes.
- Each of the nine answers to a fish moves the fish its own way (`sights.test.ts`): the chick swallows a fresh one, drops a wet one three times, and lifts a frozen one off the plate by its stuck tongue; the walrus raises a fresh one in two polite halves, wrings a wet one, and bites a frozen one shorter three times with its eyes shut; the puffin lays a fresh one crosswise, slurps a wet one up like a noodle, and hammers with a frozen one. The chick turns its plate over without tipping over itself.
- The sky moves too: the two banks of cloud drift a few units against each other, and the lights come and go.
- No two of them share a curve (`motion.test.ts`). Everyone's eyes follow the flight, then the finger, then the penguin in its pouch.

**The tiers** (`config.ts`). Every tier draws the same picture. Tier 0 draws at a pixel ratio of up to 2, with the grain in the live lines and up to 14 crumbs of snow, water, soot and steam; tier 1 at 1.5 with 12; tier 2 at 1.25, with the live lines (the bands, the lines of flight, lids and brows) in plain graphite of the same width, and 9 crumbs; tier 3 at 1 with 6. The figures and the page are drawn once at the pixel ratio in use, and once again when it changes.

**The frame budget.** The draw count is the figures stamped plus the strokes and fills drawn live, which the view counts itself. The page is one full-surface stamp, laid first, and nothing else is as large. At rest the count is 39. `frameBudget.test.ts` plays every load through its landings and scenes with a finger held and a storm of presses: the quietest frame there is 37, the busiest 67 (the wings and the fish, with both lines of flight on the paper), and the test holds it under 80.

**What a finger meets.** Each target is measured as an area, at 820 by 1180 and at 1024 by 768 (`touch.test.ts`): the largest square that lies wholly on it, or for a round thing the largest round. A box-shaped target holds a square of 48 pixels: a place in the kit is 70 units square, and the crate's rattle is a box 216 units by 72 over its lid, so that a press a little low is still the lid and bolts nothing. A round target (the chick, the whale's eye and head, the far seal, the snowdrift) holds a round of 48 pixels; the chick and the whale's eye are rounds of radius 36. The near seal answers over the whole of its ball as well as its body. The snowdrift is in front of the far seal and answers there. One target is smaller than that by itself: the igloo's dome round the doorway is a band about 19 to 33 units wide, which is about 13 to 23 pixels on the upright tablet, because the chick fills the doorway and is a target of its own. The dome and the chick together hold the round of 48 pixels, a press there is answered by one or the other, each as itself, and neither answer changes anything. On a surface wider than the stage a flight goes on to the edge of the paper that is seen before it is gone.
