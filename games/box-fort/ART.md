# Design sheet

Box Fort is open pretend play with no learning goal: a den of cardboard boxes on the living-room floor, built for a cat, a baby and a grandmother who each use it their own way. The headings stay in the order of "The design sheet" in `docs/solutions/conventions/building-a-jam-game.md`.

## The band and its age rule

- **Band.** The manifest band is 4 to 8. Its youngest age, 4, governs the design.
- **Cue table.** The row for 3 to 4 in the wordless-clarity convention holds. Its "Avoid" column is a hard limit: no text, numerals or icons to decode, no spoken instruction, no verdict, not several activities live at once, and no tool on stage before it means something. One next act is offered at a time.
- **Pack rule for the age.** Pretend play with characters who react; tap, drag and tracing; a traced line survives a lifted finger; the funny things are tricks, mild naughtiness and someone who overreacts and is never hurt (pack: game-design, ages-4-to-6.md). Nothing here is babyish for an eight-year-old: the materials behave as cardboard, tape and cloth do, and the jokes are planned by the child.
- **Symbol rule.** The band starts below 6, so the kid side shows no word, letter, numeral or sign, and the game has no `symbols.ts`. A hole is whatever the finger drew; the game itself draws no shape that reads as a letter or a numeral.
- **What `ctx.childAge` sets.** Only how a first visit is laid out, never what can be reached:
  - 5 and younger: the big box and one small box already stand on the rug, side by side and touching, so that tape holds between them, the other three lie flat on the heap at the front of the rug, and the scissors lie open in front of the big box.
  - 6 and older: only the big box stands; the other four lie on the heap.
  - No age (`null`): the layout for 5 and younger.
- Every box, every material and every character is in the room at every age. The blanket, the cushions, the tape roll and the string of lights rest as furniture of the room and are not lit or offered until the child touches one; the one act the first frame offers is the scissors on the box. A saved fort always wins over the age.

## The toy

The finger is a pair of child's scissors, and the action is **cutting a hole in the face of a box**.

- **When the finger lands** on a box face the scissors are there in the same frame: the blades bite shut with a snip, a nick shows in the board, the box gives a small shudder, and the cat, the baby and the grandmother all look at the spot.
- **While the finger moves** the cut follows it exactly. The blades open and close by the length cut, and each closing is one snip, so a slow cut is a slow snip-snip and a fast one a quick run of them. A finger that lifts does not end the cut: the line waits on its face, with no limit of time, through a put-away and into the next visit, and a finger that lands on that face within 20 cm of either end of the line and drags carries on from that end; one that only taps there pokes a peep-hole beside the line, as a tap does anywhere, and the line still waits. Five things end it: the line closes on itself or comes back to where it began; it runs out over the edge of the box; the scissors bite anywhere else, which first finishes the waiting line the short way; its box leaves where it stands, let go to tip over, dragged back onto the heap or taken by a pull of the tape end, each of which finishes the line the short way before the box goes: the box hangs where it was let go, or stands where it stood, the cat gets off it, the blades run the line shut, the piece falls from the face, and only then does the box roll over or fold; or the line has grown to 1600 points, which is 15.9 m of line or more and over twice round the biggest face, and is finished the short way where it is while the finger cuts on with a new one. The scissors keep one line open, and none is ever dropped.
- **When the line closes**, on itself, back where it began or out over the edge of the box, the scissors run the short way back to where the cut began, the piece inside tips forward out of the board and slaps down on the rug, and the hole that is left is the shape the finger drew: a square window, a wobbly star, a slot.
- **A tap alone** pokes a round peep-hole with a pop, and its small disc drops out. The simplest use always works. Cutting is told from carrying by where the finger lands: a drag that starts on the lid of a box, the flap that stands up along the back of its top, or on the top in front of it, carries the box, and one that starts on its face cuts it; a box on its side is carried from anywhere on it. After a first hole the idle ladder's ghost hand shows the carry once, and once two boxes stand with a hole in one it shows the wall switch once; a child who has carried a box or flicked the switch already is not shown it. One finger works at a time: a second finger that lands while the first is cutting or carrying takes nothing from it and changes nothing, and gets a small answer where it lands (a box knocks, a cushion puffs, one of the three makes its own small noise, a fallen piece rocks, and anywhere else a scrap of board jumps), and a finger that rests (it landed where nothing is, or has lain still for a second with nothing moved) hands the work to the next finger that lands. A box, a cushion, the tape, the blanket or the lights let go hang where they were let go for 0.3 s, and a finger that lands on the thing again carries on; put away in that time, it is set down where it was let go. A character or a place takes a thing only when it is let go there, never while it is carried past.
- **Then the chain.** The cat, which was sitting on top of the box watching the blades, drops down behind the box with a thud and looks out through the new hole. Through a peep-hole it presses one eye. Through a middling hole it pushes its nose and one paw, which pats about. Through a big hole its whole head comes out, ears flattening and springing up again. The baby squeals and points; the grandmother's glasses jump.
- **Anywhere else** the scissors snip the air under the finger, and whoever is touched answers as itself: the cat mews and lifts a paw, the baby shakes its rattle, the grandmother clicks her needles, a sofa cushion puffs, a fallen piece rocks where it lies, the ball in the baby's hand boings and the block by its knee tumbles. A tap on the cat at a hole sends it back up on top of the box.

Repeating it is a pleasure with no goal because the board gives way exactly where the finger went, the sound is made by the finger's own speed, the piece that falls is the child's own shape, and the cat's answer depends on what was cut. Random tapping fills a box with peep-holes and the cat's eye goes from one to the next.

## The error as a consequence

There is no wrong answer, so there is no verdict to avoid; the materials still show what they do.

- A box let go half off another tips and lies on its side, where it is a tunnel. So does one let go on a stack that is already as high as stacks go. A box let go with the finger anywhere over another box goes onto that box, however low it was carried, and one let go where the rug has no room left for it goes onto the nearest box that can bear it. Only where no box can bear it either does it hop back to where it was taken from: it is the one thing that goes back for want of a place. The heap lies smallest on top, and a smaller box can stand wherever a bigger one can, so the box a finger takes off the heap has a place whenever any box on the heap has one. A thing handed to one of the three is back where it was once they have done with it, or at once, with their own small noise, when they are busy in or at a box. A cushion or the blanket let go on bare rug lies where it was let go.
- A hole too small for the one who tries it shows as that body stuck in it: the cat's back legs in the air, the grandmother's hat left outside. The hole is still there and the scissors make it bigger.
- A roof on one box slides off; a third cushion comes back out.
- Nothing is refused, buzzed, reset or lost. Each thing lies where it ended up and can be taken up again at once.

## The designed order, and what is stored

**The order.** The game has one place in its order, `room`: nothing is unlocked, judged or made harder by the game. What a first visit starts with by age is under the first heading. What play changes is the fort itself and nothing else: it stays as built from visit to visit. Harder work is the child's own choice and looks harder in the room: a second storey, a roof, a door wide enough for the grandmother.

**The records.** There are none: see "The records".

**Every field of the saved state.**

- `v`: the version of the save, 1.
- `position`: always `room`, the one place.
- `finished`: always false. Open pretend play has no judged cycle.
- `boxes`: the five boxes, each with
  - `id`: 0 to 4. The id fixes the size: 0 is the big box, 1 and 2 are middling, 3 and 4 are small.
  - `x`: where it stands along the rug, in whole centimetres from the middle; unused while it is on the heap.
  - `on`: the id of the box it rests on, -1 for the rug, -2 for the heap.
  - `side`: true when it lies on its side.
  - `holes`: the face turned to the child as a grid of cells 1.25 cm on a side, one bit a cell, set where the board is gone, packed as text. Any number of cuts fits in the same length, and what is drawn is always this grid.
- `tapes`: up to eight pairs of box ids that are taped together.
- `blanket`: the ids of the boxes it lies over; an empty list is the blanket folded on the sofa, or, while `lump` holds a place along the rug in whole centimetres from the middle, heaped on the bare rug where it was let go; a list of one is the heap it slid into beside that box.
- `cushions`: three entries, each the id of the box the cushion is in, -1 for the sofa, -2 for the rug in front of the sofa, where a cushion that was fired out always comes to rest, or -3 for the rug in front of the boxes, where a cushion let go on bare rug lies flat. With it, `cushionAt`: three entries, each where along the rug such a cushion lies, in whole centimetres from the middle.
- `lights`: up to twelve corners of boxes the string was draped over, in the order it passed them, each as the box's id doubled, plus one for its right corner; an empty list is the string on its hook. With it, `tangled`: true while the string lies in a tangle at the front of the rug, between the scissors and the heap, the list then empty.
- `dark`: whether the wall switch is off.
- `line`: the cut that waits: the id of its box and up to 800 points of the line, a longer line by every second point, in cells to a tenth of a cell; nothing when no line waits.
- `cat`: where the cat is: `top` (on the highest box), `rug` (lying on the rug beside the boxes, low enough to hide nothing behind it, also where it lies when no box stands), `look` (looking out of a hole from inside), `stuck` (in a hole with its back legs out) or `in` (inside a box), with the box's id and the cell of the hole.
- `baby`: the id of the box the baby is in, or -1 for the rug; and `toys`, three entries, each the id of the box that toy lies in, or -1 for a toy the baby still holds.
- `gran`: where the grandmother is: `chair`, `in` or `skirt`, with the box's id.

A largest state (five faces cut to nothing, every list full, a line of 800 points) is under 20 KB.

**Not stored, and gone on load.** The piece that falls out of a cut lies where it fell and rocks when it is touched, until the cat, on its way to a newer hole, sweeps it under the sofa with its tail, which is seen and heard as a scrape (up to five pieces lie there, and a sixth pushes the oldest under the sofa as it lands; someone who walks where one lies pushes it aside along the rug); the scissors, which lie open on the rug whenever no finger holds them; where each idle motion stands; which hole the cat last stuck in and looks at from a distance; and whatever a character does with the tape, the blanket, a cushion or the lights (a strip on a paw, the roll bowled over the rug, a corner over the baby's head, the blanket over the grandmother, the cat on a cushion or under the roof, a loop round the grandmother's neck). Each of these lasts a few seconds and ends with the thing back where the save has it and the character where the save has them.

**Where things come to rest.** Nothing the game sets down covers a face that can be cut or a thing that can be taken up: the fallen piece lands on the rug in front of and below the face it came from, a fired cushion in front of the sofa, the baby and the grandmother to the left and the right of the boxes. The grandmother in her chair behind a stack is reached by the arm of her chair, which answers for her. A character at or in a hole is its own target: a touch that lands on it is answered by that character and never starts a cut on the board behind it. No stack grows higher than the big box with a middling one on it. The room is framed so that the tallest stack this allows, with the cat lying on top, stays below the round home control at the top centre (48 px across, 10 px from the top), and the wall switch, the hook of the lights, the heap and the sofa are clear of it. An overlap test holds both at 1180 by 820 and at 1024 by 768, where the view opens a little wider so that the grandmother standing beside the boxes is inside the frame; the same test holds every character's reach inside the frame at both sizes.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

The feedback is the three people of the house, who use what is there by size and habit. None of them ever looks at, thanks, hurries or misses the child.

- **The cat.** Its one want, always visible: to get into, onto or through whatever is newest. It sits where it can see the scissors and follows the blades with its whole head.
  - Likes: any hole, the highest box, a bulb to swat, a cushion to knead, being under the blanket.
  - Dislikes: tape on a paw, and a hole it has stuck in, which it looks at from a distance afterwards.
  - It goes at any hole its head fits through, sticks in every one that is narrower than its body, and never learns which ones stick it.
- **The baby.** Its one want: to post things. It sits on the rug holding a toy out towards the fort.
  - Likes: the lowest box, a slot, a blanket over its own head, the tape roll as a wheel.
  - Dislikes: a box it cannot reach into, which it pats on the side, and a toy that springs back, which it frowns at and tries again.
  - It only ever crawls, so nothing above the floor exists for it.
- **The grandmother.** Her one want: a proper door to come in by. She sits forward in her chair with her handbag on her knees, looking the fort over for one.
  - Likes: a door she fits, a cushion to sit on, the lights as a necklace, tape pressed flat.
  - Dislikes: nothing she says; she never crawls and never ducks under a roof, so she always tries the door standing, and wears whatever is too small. A box she wears is hers until a tap sends her back to her chair: as she comes up in it its roof slides off, its cushions go back to the sofa, its toys to the baby's hand and its tape comes free; the cat, the baby, a roof, a cushion and another box keep off it, a thing let go on it is let go on her, and the scissors still cut it. A box she sits in by its door is not taken from under her either: its lid creaks under the finger and the box stays.
  - She carries on with her knitting whatever happens to her.

## The scenes

Open pretend play has no ending. A scene is the consequence of one act of the child, lasts 1 to 6 seconds (a thing handed back by someone who is busy is the shortest, the grandmother at a door too small for her the longest), and gives way to any touch: the scene jumps to its last pose without the sounds it had left, and the touch is then answered as an ordinary touch. Nothing starts by itself, and on load no scene plays: everyone is where the save has them. A tap on a character is answered when the finger lands as the toy says (the mew, the rattle, the needles), and the scene follows from that. Where the fort gives the character no hole to use (none the cat's head fits, none to post through, none that reaches the floor), the scene is that character's box cell of the grid: the cat on the highest box, the baby into the lowest box, the grandmother sitting down in a box and wearing it.

- **The cat looks.** Cause: a hole is cut.
  - Beats: the ears prick; the cat drops behind the box; it shows at the hole as the hole's size allows (an eye, a nose and a paw, or the whole head); it settles there.
  - Saved when it starts: `boxes` (the new hole) and `cat` (at that hole).
  - Filled in from: which box, where the hole is and how wide the widest round part of it is.
- **The cat goes through.** Cause: the cat is tapped while it is on top or on the rug.
  - Beats: it crouches; it goes at the nearest hole from outside; it walks through, or sticks and pedals; a tap on a stuck cat pops it out backwards.
  - Saved when it starts: `cat`.
  - Filled in from: the nearest hole and its size.
- **The baby posts.** Cause: the baby is tapped.
  - Beats: it crawls to the lowest box; it posts the toys it holds through the lowest hole, one by one; it crawls in after them if the opening lets it.
  - Saved when it starts: `baby` and `toys`.
  - Filled in from: which box is lowest, its lowest hole and that hole's size.
- **The grandmother tries the door.** Cause: the grandmother is tapped.
  - Beats: she stands; she walks to the largest hole that reaches the floor; she ducks in and sits, or goes head first and comes up wearing the box.
  - Saved when it starts: `gran`.
  - Filled in from: the door's width and height against hers.
- **Lights out.** Cause: the wall switch is flicked.
  - Beats: the room dims; each hole lights in turn, its shape landing on the wall; the bulbs come on; the cat's eyes shine.
  - Saved when it starts: `dark`.
  - Filled in from: every hole of every box, the blanket, the cushions and the lights. Flicked again, the afternoon is back at once.
- **Taking apart.** Cause: the loose tape end, which hangs beside the fort's right wall and over no face, is pulled a little way (a touch alone only pinches it), or a box is dragged back onto the heap, which does the same for that one box.
  - Beats, for each pull: one strip rips off; a line that waits on the highest box is run shut where the box stands and its piece falls; the box comes free, tips out what is in it and hops back to the heap; a cushion from it lands on the sofa, a toy from it rolls back to the baby's hand, the blanket and the lights slip off it onto what still stands, and a blanket left on one box with no room beside it for its heap is back on the sofa; the cat or the baby that was in it is on the rug, blinking, and the grandmother stands up out of it and goes back to her chair.
  - Saved when it starts: `tapes`, `boxes`, `cushions`, `blanket`, `lights`, `baby` with `toys`, `cat` and `gran`, each as it will be when the box is on the heap, and `line`, which waits no longer once its box is taken.
  - Filled in from: which box is highest, what is taped to it, and who and what is in or on it.
  - This is the calm way to tidy up. A box that goes back to the heap is folded flat and comes off the heap as fresh board.

**How it goes on.** There is no cycle to end and no next customer. The fort stands as long as the child likes, and nothing asks for a touch.

## The look

**Cardboard craft**, the first reserved row. The spike held on clarity, so the second row (quilted patchwork) was not tried.

### What is in the frame

A living room built as a cardboard model in flat workshop daylight. Everything is board with thickness: every edge shows a darker fluted strip, and paint stops short of the edges so bare kraft frames each piece.

- **Behind.** A wall painted dusty blue in two panels joined with torn tape, a skirting strip, and a window cut clean through it. Outside the window hang painted cloud cut-outs and rain drops on threads, which sway. A crooked picture hangs by one strip of tape: a fish in fat marker. A round portrait of the cat hangs top right, above the grandmother's hat, and a short shelf of books top left. Beside the portrait a round peg holds the string of lights in a coil: a dozen squares of coloured card on a black thread. Top left is the wall switch, a paper plate with one red knob.
- **Underneath.** A floor painted grey-green, and on it a round rug painted soft teal. The boxes stand on the rug, so bare warm kraft sits on a cool painted ground.
- **Left.** A slotted cardboard sofa with three painted cushions (green, yellow, coral) and a folded red check blanket over its back. In front of it the baby, large, in a mustard romper, holding a rattle up, with a ball and a block for posting.
- **Right.** A slotted armchair and in it the grandmother, the tallest thing in the room: a plum dress, a pink cardigan, a white bun, round glasses, knitting in her hands and a soft round handbag on her knees with its strap flopped to one side.
- **Middle.** The big box and a small box, bare kraft, the plainest things in the frame, with the ginger cat sitting on top of the big one, left of the middle so that it is clear of the home control above.
- **In front.** The scissors, the one bright red thing, lying flat and half open on the rug in front of the boxes, below every face. To their left the tape roll stands on its edge; to their right the flat boxes lie in a heap on a sheet of board taped to the floor, the smallest on top. A fort that is taped has one loose end of tape hanging beside its right wall, over no face.
- **The materials at work.** Tape is a pale strip laid a little crooked across a seam. The blanket is one sheet of red check that sags between two boxes and hangs down their fronts. A cushion in a box shows through whatever holes the box has. While the cat is at a hole of that box its cushions lie further back in it, behind the cat's head, and they come forward again once the cat has gone. The lights hang in loops from box corner to box corner; let go over nothing, the string is seen to fall, bulb after bulb, out to the front of the rug and down into its tangle. A box on the heap is folded flat, and one on its way there shows its face as it was, holes and all, until it lies there as fresh board; a box on its side shows its open end. Each box has a lid flap standing up along the back of its top, with a slot for a finger: it is what a box is carried by. A cushion let go on the rug lies flat in front of the boxes. The piece of a cut lies on the rug in front of its box, up to five of them, until the cat's tail sweeps them under the sofa. A cut that waits is its pencil line on the face, as far as the finger drew it; it is drawn there still while its box hangs or stands to have it run shut.

### The room dark

The switch turns the afternoon into evening: the whole room is painted over in deep blue, the window is night, and nothing is lit by a lamp. Light is flat pale-yellow card. Each hole shows the lit inside of its box as a flat yellow shape, the same shape lies as a cut-out on the wall behind and above the box, thin lines leak along the seams of a box with no hole, tape over a seam and the loose tape end are amber strips, the roof is a warm tent with its check showing through, the bulbs are bright squares in their own colours, and the cat's eyes shine. A cushion in a box blocks the light on the wall as high as the cushion reaches; no rim of light is drawn round a hole. The cut-outs are placed by one rule (up and back from the hole), not traced from a lamp, so they are a picture of light and not a simulation of it.

The hue split: what the child cuts is bare kraft and nothing else in the frame is; the room is painted cool and pale; the characters and the tool are painted warm and strong.

### Materials and light

- One board material for everything: a lit, matt surface with a procedural kraft tile (fibres and faint flute lines) multiplied by a painted vertex colour. The fluted edge strip is a second region of the same tile.
- One daylight from the upper left front and a pale sky fill. No lamp, no gloss, no shadow map: each thing has a soft dark blob on the ground under it.
- A box face is a board of two layers a board's thickness apart, with the holes cut out of both, so the cut edge is seen at its true depth.
- Faces are fat marker on painted board: round white eyes with a black pupil that follows the finger, brows as short bars, mouths as a few cut shapes that are swapped.

### Motion

- Board is stiff. Nothing bends: things tip, hinge, slide and hop, and land with a small squash of the whole piece.
- The cat moves in quick starts and long holds; its head leads and its tail lags. The baby moves all at once and overshoots, rocking back to rest. The grandmother moves slowly from the head down, and her hands never stop.
- The boxes have no idle motion. A box answers a cut with one short shudder, lands with a squash, and leans back while it is carried. The scissors lie still until the idle ladder makes them wriggle where they lie; nothing glows under them.
- Cloth is the one thing that bends: the blanket billows, sinks and sags, and a mound of it shows who is under it. A cushion puffs and squashes. The string swings and each bulb settles by itself.
- A character goes round a box, never through its board: the baby crawls round the corner and sits up down the side, the grandmother goes through a door on her knees or steps under the rim of a box lifted over her head and wears it, and the cat uses the holes and goes over the back edge of a lid.
- Nothing passes through anything. A box that goes to another place goes up, over whatever stands between, and down; carried, it stops against what stands in its way. The blanket goes up, across and down, above the cat, and its hem is above every box it passes. Down off a box the cat is out in front of the faces before it comes down. Over the cat the blanket ends where the tail begins, and the tail lies out from under its edge; under it the baby has its rattle at its mouth. The strip of tape in the finger runs in front of a head that looks out of a hole. A toy posted into a box runs along inside the face until it is beside its place. The grandmother walks a lane of her own in front of the faces, in front of where pieces and cushions lie. A piece that lies where someone will walk is pushed aside along the rug.

### Tiers

Each tier keeps the look and sheds cost only: tier 0 draws at a pixel ratio of up to 2 with the rain moving and the blob shadows; tier 1 at 1.5; tier 2 at 1.25 and the rain stands still; tier 3 at 1 with no blob shadows. Nothing that answers a touch is left out at any tier: a test draws every act at the lowest tier and at the highest and finds the same meshes but the rain's sway and the shadows.

### The frame

The camera keeps the height of the room on a wide surface. On a 4 to 3 surface it opens about 7 % so that the grandmother standing beside the boxes is inside the frame. The round home control at the top centre and the top right corner hold nothing a child can touch at 1180 by 820 or at 1024 by 768, and every target is 48 px or more at both.
