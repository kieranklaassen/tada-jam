# Design sheet

The first part is the sheet, written before the game. The look follows it.

## The band and its age rule

Band 3 to 7. The 3 to 4 row of the cue table governs: no words, letters, numerals, symbols or icons to decode, no spoken instruction, no verdict, one next act offered at a time, tools shown only when they mean something (pack: game-design, ages-2-to-4.md; pack: game-design, ages-4-to-6.md). Targets are about 100 units across, everything essential works with taps (hang, light, wind back), a drag survives a lifted finger and counts when partly done, and nothing essential lies in the bottom 50 units. `ctx.childAge` sets nothing, and `null` gives the same night: the first visit is the same at every age.

The loop is three acts in one line: pull, hang, press. A four-year-old could not follow the loops of the games before this one, so the sheet is held to one test: what I do, what happens and what I do next are all in the first picture, and a laugh is two touches away.

The first picture, left to right: the box with the fat bulb hanging out, the moose large leaning down until his great bare nose is within one hand's reach of the bulb, and the one switch, a fat red button as big as his nose on a post in the snow beside him, up, with nothing written or drawn on it. A thick cord runs in plain sight from the switch to the box. The house, the tree and the dog are behind or beside, lower in contrast. Nothing else looks like a thing to press.

## The toy

The finger drags the fat glowing bulb that hangs out of the box. The string follows, the next thing jams at the rim, the whole box leans and stretches after it with a rising creak, and it pops out with a pop of its own pitch, swings, and the box rocks back with the next thing already peeping. Pull on and another jams. Let go and everything swings. It needs no goal: the pull has a load and a release every hand's breadth, and what comes next is silly and half seen.

A tap on the box or on the end bulb does one such pull and hangs the end on the next free place, the moose's nose first. A tap on a free place, or on a host the string has not reached, hangs the end there. When no place is free, the next thing pops half out, looks round and plops back. A tap on the switch throws it. All of this works with the switch up or down. The end bulb is told from the other bulbs by its size and by being last.

## The object-by-action grid, and what is new on day 15

Objects: the things on the string. Columns: what it lies on when the switch is down. Every thing also wakes by itself when it hangs in the open.

| Thing | In the open | On the moose | On the house | On the tree | On the dog |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bulb | glows its colour, a ping | his nose glows that colour, a warm honk | the windows glow that colour, a chime | the tree glows from inside, a bell | his spots glow, a yip |
| Boot | stamps, a thud | the moose stamps | the house hops on its stones | the tree hops on its trunk | the dog drums his feet |
| Underpants | dance, a twang | he sways like a dancer | the house sways from its foundations | the tree sways | the dog wags all over |

| Kettle | steams and whistles | steam from both nostrils | steam from the chimney | steam from the tip | steam from both ears |

Sound: a thing's phrase (its tune and wave) is played in its host's timbre: the moose lowest, the house hollow, the tree bell-like, the dog highest, the open air plain. So no two cells sound alike. Where two cells are named with the same words, the body that does it differs in size, speed and part.

Acts on things with the switch up: a tap makes any thing but the end bulb swing and mutter; a tap on a host that holds string, and the string catching on a host, tickles that host in its own way (the moose crosses his eyes at what is on him, the dog's tail flicks, the house blinks a shutter, the tree shivers off snow).

**Day 15:** the child chooses who gets what. Every place added slides everything along, so they choose the order of places until the kettle lands on the nose and the umbrella on the roof, pull a loop from the middle to give the dog the trumpet too, and set off four hosts that answer each other in turn. None of it needs more content.

## The representation

No school idea, so none is claimed. What is represented is cause: whatever is drawn through the string ends at the one switch. Dark paper is asleep; bright paper is awake. The end bulb alone keeps a small steady glow with the switch up, so that the hand finds it. It throws no light on anything, and the press makes it blaze.

## The four mechanic questions

- **Swap:** no learning content to swap.
- **Attention:** where the string goes next, and what will lie there.
- **Fun:** the decision (where to hang it) is what makes the laugh.
- **Guess:** nothing is to be guessed; random taps hang the string and light it, and that is play.

## The error as a consequence

There is no wrong answer. An end let go in the open flies to the nearest free place, or lies down in the snow and can be taken up from there. A loop let go in the open springs back with a twang, and what it had drawn out goes plop, plop into the box. The switch pressed with nothing on a host lights what there is: the bulb at the box, and the moose looks at his bare antlers.

## The designed order, and what is stored

No ladder: all is there from the first frame, except the crank on the box, which turns up the first time the child, not the ghost hand, has pressed the switch with something hung.

Stored: the version; the seed of this boxful; the list of places the string hangs on, in order; the snow spot of the end or null; whether the switch is down; whether the opening round has been shown; whether the crank has turned up. A thing in the hand is saved where it came from. Which thing lies on which place is worked out from the seed and the list. Eggs, bubbles, rings, steam and shed snow are short-lived and gone on load. Nothing plays on load: with the switch down the string is found lit and steady.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

- **The moose** wants lights on his antlers. He is the one asker. From the first frame he leans to the bulb in the box, mouth a little open, and looks from the bulb to the child and back, his nose held out. While something lies on a place and the switch is up and has not been pressed since the last hang, he looks from the switch to the child and the switch rocks on its post. While the switch is down he looks at his own lights. Each step answers him: at a hang his head dips and his ears go up, at a press his body does what hangs there. The wanting is shown only while the child is idle and backs off. Big, slow, dignified, and everything happens to his nose. He takes every result as if it were the plan.
- **The dog** wants whatever hangs lowest; its tail does not stop. It is quick and small. It does not face the child and asks for nothing.
- **The house** wants to sleep: two shut windows as eyes and a door as a mouth. It likes the uncle (it snores with him) and dislikes the trumpet (one shutter opens and glares).
- **The tree** wants to stand straight, stiff and proud under its snow. It likes the umbrella and dislikes the underpants, which make it sway.
- The moose likes the bulb best and dislikes nothing but the fish, at which he shuts one eye. The dog likes the sausage best and dislikes the kettle, at which its ears go flat. These never change, and they play on top of the cell.
- **The uncle**, on the string by the back of his nightshirt, sleeps through everything but the switch. He is never hurt and never minds.

The string lies on an antler, a nose, a tail, the top of a head, a roof, a chimney, a branch. Never round a neck.

No feeling is about the child, and nothing thanks or praises.

## The scenes

**The opening round**, once, about 9 seconds: the ghost hand pulls the bulb to the moose's nose, the boot pops out, the hand presses the switch, the moose's nose lights and the boot stamps and the moose stamps, then the hand presses again and it is dark. It is made of the same acts as a finger's, so the world is left as play could leave it: the string on his nose, the end glowing. Any touch ends it at once, and nothing is undone. The mark is saved when it starts, and each act in it (the hang, the press, the press again) saves the list of places and the switch at that act, as the child's own would. A put-away in the middle is found at the last act saved, and the round is not played again.

**The lighting**, on each press: the light runs along the string from the box to the end, each thing wakes as it passes, then each host takes its turns with what lies on it, one thing at a time, for about 11 seconds, and then all glow steadily and only what is touched does its act. It blocks nothing.

A cycle ends when the child has lit the string and looked. Nothing new starts. The next begins with the child's touch: the crank winds the string back one place at a tap, and the end dropped on the box winds all of it. A wound-up box is shaken into another order.

**The guidance**, only while the child is idle: after 1.5 seconds a ring breathes round the one next thing, after 3 the ghost hand shows one act, then after growing gaps, four at most. With nothing hung, the act is the bulb to his nose, or to the nearest free place when his nose is taken. With something hung and the switch up and not pressed since the last hang, it is the press. Otherwise it is the bulb to another free place. Any touch fades it at once. The shown hand changes nothing in the world.

On load no scene replays.

## The records

None. The game has no learning goal and claims no school skill.

# The look

Gummed-paper shapes on black, the first reserved look, in canvas 2D.

- **Palette.** Red, yellow, blue, green, orange and white, and the black of the sheet (`paper.ts`). Each has a dark version, sunk into the night. Dark is asleep and pure is awake: lighting is a crossfade from one to the other and nothing else.
- **Shapes.** Circles, half circles, squares, triangles and strips, cut by machine and stuck a little crooked. Each has a thin pale gum edge, one broad gloss band and a small hard shadow. A few have a lifted corner.
- **Eyes** are always white and black, asleep or awake, so faces read in the dark.
- **Light** is paper too: a flat disc and eight stuck rays.
- **Code.** A picture is painted once for a surface in both tones and stamped (`pictures.ts`, `view.ts`); a frame paints at most one that has not been needed yet. The string, eyes, mouths, rays and what flies are drawn live.
- **Motion.** Nobody shares a tempo (`bodies.ts`): the moose is slow and heavy, the house stiff, the tree whippy, the dog quick. The string hangs by the same pose that is drawn.
- **Tiers** set the pixel ratio only. No answer to a touch is shed.

## Registry

The Endless String, gummed-paper shapes on black (canvas 2D).
