### How the game meets the quality bar

The game is built. **No frame rate has been measured, no physical iPad was used, and no one has listened to a single sound.** Those three are the lead's and the owner's.

- **Alive at idle.** The beetle is part of the way through smoothing a strip of tape when the page opens and goes on by itself, four idle actions, never the same twice running. The worm looks out of an empty pot now and then, three ways, and of one near the middle as the page opens. Each visitor lives while it waits and while it stands on the page, four idle actions each in its own tempo; none beckons, hurries or looks at the child. One end of a strip of tape lifts. The plants do not move at idle, by design: they are the working pieces. Every animal's eyes go to a finger on the page. Everything stops with the Mount's loop when unattended or hidden.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** Every one of the grid's thirty cells is reached by a touch and answers with its own motion and its own voice (`game.test.ts` holds that all thirty are heard), and no cell refuses. A flower answers as the finger lands, in that frame. One dab sets off a chain: a creak as the pod sets, a pop, six ticks up a scale as the seeds land, six notes as the flowers open, pitched by height and colour. Random taps and drags, five hundred late in the game and seven hundred on the first page, are each answered.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** A stem is a spring that swings past upright and settles; a plant squashes as its flower opens and as it lands from a hop or from the hand; a pot squashes when it is set down; a pod overshoots as it swells. Every character has its own weight: the snail and the beetle swing past each pose, the ladybird stops dead (`motion.ts`, `cast.ts`).
- **Every character moves like itself.** Seven characters, each with its own tempo, weight and funniest part, and no shared action: tests fail on a shared action and on one that is another with the numbers nudged (`motion.test.ts`, `cast.test.ts`). A visitor's like and its miss are its own for each trait, and a miss plays the same way every time.
- **Kid-clear.** The four traits read at a glance on white paper, which each plant brings with it wherever it stands: three petal colours (white carried by an outline and a cool wash), joints drawn as nodes, round against jagged leaves, dark spots. Pot plants share one scale. Every handle is at least 48 px at 1180 by 820 and at 1024 by 768, and dust or a carried thing let go anywhere in a slot lands on what stands there. One family's lines stand out at a time.
- **Wordless clarity for the band.** No text is drawn anywhere but one numeral, through `symbols.ts`: the number of plants alike, beside the plants of a wish that asks for two or three (`npm run wordless:check` passes). The wish is a pencil sketch of the plant wanted; an answer is the visitor's body on the plant; nothing rates, counts or praises.
- **Wordless guidance, and one obvious want.** With nobody on the page the want is the first visitor, waiting on the visitors' own ground with its wish sketch held open over its head; with one on the page it is that visitor's label, and the next one waits at the edge with its own sketch held open. The idle ladder: after three seconds a pencil ring on every flower, on the loupe where it lies, and on the one who waits when nobody is being served; then a pencil hand shows one possible move at a time, a dab, a touch on the one who waits, a plant carried to the visitor, or the loupe carried to a flower, picked by turn and never because it answers a wish. It backs off, stops after four, and any touch clears it. A brood in motion or a scene playing keeps it at the bottom.
- **Scenes.** Every answer to an offered plant, the ending, the beetle's two showings and the three secrets are lists of beats on `scene.ts`. Whatever changes the page is taken and saved as its scene starts; a touch ends a scene and is then an ordinary touch; nothing replays on load. With the real cast an ending lasts between five and a half and nine and a half seconds (`endings.test.ts`).
- **60 fps on a mid-range iPad.** Not measured. Counted (`frameBudget.test.ts`): 63 things laid down in the fullest frame the game has (a brood landing on a full page), 49 at most on a late page with few plants, against a budget of 80; one full-surface stamp; 186 paths drawn by hand; no kept drawing made again from frame to frame. What stands still is in a kept set, made again only when it changes (up to 32 drawings into a kept sheet on such a frame). The paper is painted once per size, each plant and creature is a sprite drawn once per look and size, a growing plant is that sprite revealed, and dust is drawn in one pass. No shadow, no post pass, pixel ratio capped at 2 by the tier table.
- **Nothing passes through anything.** A canvas game: its own model overlap tests (`intersections.test.ts`), played at sixty frames a second through moments that reach every state. Two contacts are allowed by name: a plant on its way to the border is drawn in front of the tray's board for under a quarter of a second a hop, and a plant that leaves the border reaches the beetle's back in one hop across the front of the tray's plants, under six tenths of a second, clear of the visitor and of the loupe. The beetle's own walks to a pot and home again, in its showings, are not covered: named under "Still weak".
- **Procedural or committed assets only.** Everything is drawn on a canvas at run time and every sound is synthesized. No file, font or address is loaded (`node scripts/egress-check.ts` and `npm run egress:built` pass).
- **Its own art direction.** Naturalist's field journal, written up in `ART.md` under "The look"; the text of the registry row is at the end of that file.
- **Found as left.** The page the game holds is always at rest: a plant in the hand still stands, in the page, in the pot it came from; a brood is in the page from the moment its pod sets; a scene's outcome is in the page when the scene starts. The random walks save and load the page after every touch and find it the same. On load every plant is grown, a pod that was set waits on its plant, a visitor that had its plant sits with it, and the next one waits for a touch. Put away with a finger still on it, the game makes no move: the Mount drops the touch and the game puts what was in the hand back where it came from (`putAway`), and tests hold that for everything a finger can carry.
- **The hidden position.** Nine positions by id in `config.ts`; a first visit starts at the second from age 11 and at the first otherwise, `null` included, with both ends open. It moves one step between cycles and never inside one, nothing shows it, and a new position shows first on the visitor after next.

### The learning claim

The claim as the sheet has it. Standings and check states were read with the lookup on 2026-10-03 and are read again on the day of the pull request.

Seed Lab is designed from two California standards adopted by the State Board, which California's preferred integrated course model places in grade 6, `us-ca MS-LS3-2` and `us-ca MS-LS1-5`, both confirmed, and from five statements of Dutch curriculum-institute guidance, which say what a school can offer and not what a child must know: `nl ojw/pdm/3/10/fase2` and `nl ojw/pdm/3/08/fase2` for fase 2, and `nl ojw/pdm/3/14/fase3`, `nl ojw/pdm/3/12/fase3` and `nl ojw/pdm/3/07/fase3` for fase 3, all confirmed. From the California standards it takes this and no more, each in part: from `us-ca MS-LS3-2` the use of a model that the game supplies and the child does not build, which shows why young by runner carry what their one parent carries while young from seed vary; and from `us-ca MS-LS1-5` the evidence that both surroundings and inherited factors shape how a plant grows, with the explaining asked for by nothing. From the Dutch guidance it takes this and no more: that a living thing comes from another of its own kind, that plants make new plants by seed and by runner and the two ways can be compared, that the characteristics of a kind are passed on to the young, and simple experiments with plants in which care for the living thing is kept. It names no record for a Californian child in grade 4 or grade 5.

- Choosing parents to get a wanted plant is the game's play and is in none of the records named. Which factor hides which is in none of the records named and is the game's own.
- For a child of 9 the passing on of traits is in none of the records named for the child's own level in either jurisdiction; the game is designed there from records for older children and claims nothing for that child from them.
- `us-ca MS-LS1-4`, which the wave's plan named, is not used: nothing the finger does in this game is that skill.
- The representation (pairs of factors, one from each parent) is school practice without a trial behind it in the pack's tables.
- Nothing in the game or about it says what a child has reached.

### Defaults taken for the owner

From the guide's list, as they bind this game:

- No symbol stands alone. The one numeral the game draws lies beside the plants of a wish, and play never depends on reading it; the other the sheet names, beside a group of a sorted brood, is not drawn while the owner's answer is awaited.
- No letters and no written words, ages 9 to 12 included. So the journal has no handwriting, not even scribble that looks like it, and the cross table in its school form, which writes factors as letters, is not reached.
- No reading on the object. A plant's height is seen in its joints and never measured against a rule. The count beside a sorted group is put to the owner (status block) and is not drawn meanwhile. The one numeral drawn names a quantity the child is asked for: how many plants alike a visitor wants.
- No camera shake and no impact pause. A pod's burst is carried by the chain, the sound and the squash.
- No speech. The visitors and the beetle make invented, synthesized sounds.
- The look is the lead's first pick for the game; it was shown to the owner at the toy checkpoint, and the answer was yes.
- The demo's verb is replaced: plant, harvest and buy becomes carry dust between flowers. Kept from the demo: plants that come up in seconds, and seed that is stranger than it looks.

From the sheet, the game's own choices, none set by a record:

- Tall hides short, round hides jagged, plain hides spotted, and red with white shows as pink; one pair to a trait; four traits; six seeds to a pod; heights of four joints and two; half the height from dry soil.
- A cycle that goes well is a wish met within a number of pods that differs by position (three to ten).
- A first visit starts at the second position from age 11 and at the first otherwise.

### What the next builder should know

- **A learning game about chance needs a route that is certain.** Inheritance is chance by nature, and the pack rules out a wanted result left to luck. The two fit when every wish has a cross that gives it for certain once the child has bred the right parents, and a test says so for every position and visitor (`order.test.ts`). Smoothing the chance instead would teach the very mistake the idea is known for.
- **Nothing wanted may sit in the starting material.** With true-breeding packets most first wishes were a packet plant as it stood, and the position moved with no cross made. Packets that hide each new factor in plants that all look alike fixed it, and a test holds that no wish is a packet plant.
- **Lay the waiting visitor out whole, with the position it was laid out at.** The guide's rule that a new position shows on the visitor after next falls out of storing that id in the visit, and what a position brings can then be carried in by the first visitor laid out there.
- **A long seeded walk of random steps, with a save and a load after every step, is a cheap test of found-as-left** for a state with many lists: four thousand steps run in about three seconds (`visit.test.ts`). It also stands in for random tapping: no step refuses and none leaves the page at a dead end.
- **State the idea so that it holds for the game's own edge cases.** The first sheet said two parents give young that differ and one parent gives a copy. A flower set with its own dust is one parent whose young differ, and two true lines are two parents whose young are alike, so the checker had the idea restated as seed against runner. Try the claim on selfing and on true-breeding parents before the sheet goes out.
- **A cloud builder's helper must be told to write one small file at a time.** A helper asked for five characters' motion in one go wrote nothing: its reply was cut off at the output limit. Asked again for one file a character, typechecked between files, it finished in a quarter of an hour.
- **Count the kept drawings a frame makes.** A counted frame budget that also counts sprites made anew caught a visitor whose size changed every frame of its walk and so made its sprites again every frame. Nothing on a software renderer would have shown it.
- **Give two builders one pure function for every place.** The visitor's place and size were numbers in the view and again in the logic until the overlap tests moved one of them; now both read `visitorSpot`, and a test holds the pod's and the loupe's handles to where they are drawn.
- **Keep the page at rest and put everything in motion beside it.** The toy never holds a half-done page: a rule step is taken at once and whole, and the flight, the growing and the hop are a view of the step already taken. Saving is then never a question, and the view's moving parts can be thrown away on load.
- **Give the view named moments.** Fixed moments of the page in motion, reachable from the address (a dozen for the toy, thirty-seven by the end), let the drawing be looked at and fixed while the logic that would produce them was still being written, and they stay as stills the lead can take.
- **Do not put a curve through the corner points of a routed line.** A spline through a short run and a long one swings wide of the corner; straight runs with each corner rounded inside a small radius keep to the route.
- **Have the folder read against the sheet by someone who has not built it, before the gates.** The first such reading of this game found nine promises unkept that four rounds of the sheet's check and three runs of building had not: cells of the grid that were only sounded, a secret that did not play every time, a character's move that was a pose with nothing in its arms, two beads that read as a plus and a nought, and a put-away in the middle of a drag that made the move.
- **"Every time" has to be tried with everything else that can be going on.** A secret that needs the beetle at home, or a visitor that is not settled, is not every time. Write the test with the beetle in a pot and the visitor settled.
- **A cell of the grid is a drawing and a sound.** A voice of its own over the ordinary motion is half a cell. Ask of every cell what a child with the sound off would see that is not the cell next to it.
- **Dust in the air must not count as the child being busy.** The ghost hand sheds dust of its own; counted as activity, it put off its own showing for ever.
- **A wrapper around the template's `state.ts` has to repeat its version check** to know whether a record was read or a fresh state came back (template notes).
- **A setting behind washes shifts every colour that is multiplied onto it.** Over a painted sky a pink petal read as mauve and a white one as blue. A drawing whose colours carry meaning has to bring its own paper: fill its shapes with the paper's white first and lay the drawing as it is.
- **A setting must not look like things.** A wash with a rim reads as a shape, and a shape reads as something to touch. Washes that run out, with no rim and no outline, stay a tone.
- **What stands still belongs in one kept drawing.** Laying thirty resting sprites every frame was most of the frame budget; a kept sheet that is made again only when one of them changes took the fullest frame from 104 things to 63, and left room for the setting, which then costs nothing.
- **Take stills while nothing else writes to the folder.** A helper that edits source while a dev server is up reloads the page under the still, and the still shows a game that has started again.
- **A working piece has no idle motion, a shared one included.** A breeze that leans every plant alike looked like the setting's motion and was the pieces'; the sheet's own line on the working pieces said so, and its check took it out. Put idle life in the characters and in things that are no part of a move.
