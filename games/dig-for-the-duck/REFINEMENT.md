### How the game meets the quality bar

No frame rate has been measured: every line about speed below is a count, not a result. The frame rate is the lead's to measure on a production build through the shared probe.

- **Alive at idle.** Two clouds drift, the pinwheel turns, the washing swings, the pond glints, the spring runs or hangs as a drop, and the four residents each keep their own beat: the duck brushes its back, the mole reads and turns a page, the worm polishes a boot, the badger pegs.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** Each of the thirty cells of the grid (six grounds by five actions) has its own motion and its own voice, and so have the duck, the tin, the plug and each resident's tap. One timeline names every scene's and every answer's move with its note; a test walks every cell and every row and fails when a note has no maker drawn. A touch in the grown-up corner is the one touch that gets no answer.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** Loosened sand falls and slumps; water falls, spreads and levels; a rock drops whole and everyone hops; the duck squashes and waggles after each move; the chair grounds with a bump; the washing sags and swings on.
- **Kid-clear silhouettes and tappables.** The residents are the largest things in the frame after the hill, each a plain shape in its own colour. Nothing lies under the home control, the chain hangs to one side of the duck, and tests hold both.
- **Wordless clarity for the band.** No word, letter, numeral or sign is drawn, and nothing drawn reads as one; the wordless check passes. Each new ground is shown once by the resident beside it on the first touch of its hill. The cold playtest proxy was run on a production build and is logged above.
- **Wordless idle guidance.** A glow where a rub would do most, then a ghost hand whose stroke ends on the glow; on a finished hill the glow is on the plug's knob and the hand pulls it up. The ladder rests while a scene plays.
- **Found as left.** Every scene saves what it changes when it starts, and a test holds each; a put-away at any instant, a scene or a rub included, loses nothing, and on load every resident is in the pose its stored field names and no scene plays. The next hill comes only on the child's pull.
- **Frame budget.** Pixel ratio capped at 2. One full-surface stamp a frame. 45 draws at rest with the hand, 41 to 54 through scenes and answers, up to 62 in the plug's scene, 68 at the worst the tests reach, against the bar's eighty; a model test holds every reachable state to 72. No shadow, no post pass.
- **Nothing passes through anything.** A canvas game: model tests draw every layout, every scene, every short answer, every showing, a run of rubs and the plug's whole scene on a recording canvas and hold that no figure's middle lies over ground or outside the pane, and that the duck and the chain never touch.
- **Assets.** Everything is painted by the game's own code; there is no file of art or sound.

### What the next builder should know

- A hill of cells wants a rule with no chance in it. Water that picks a side by a counter of ticks replays the same from a saved hill and can be tested cell by cell.
- A counter that the rules advance more than once a step never comes up on the remainder an "every nth step" test waits for. Count the steps apart from the draws.
- Water in a wide bowl does not level unless a cell may look far sideways for a lower place.
- Sand that a rub loosens falls into whatever lies below. If the thing below is the goal, count the sand toward the goal or the child's own digging blocks it.
- Painting only the cells that changed into a small picture and stamping that picture once keeps a whole hill of grains at one draw; softened edges then cost nothing, since they are painted into that picture.
- One table of cues that the model, the poses and the voices all read ends "sounded and not drawn" as a kind of fault; the test that walks it found a pose that was never a number at rest and two answers that never played.
- A recording canvas in the tests finds overlaps a still does not: a brush through a wall, a head in a roof, a slipper under a floor.
- A rule that feeds itself runs for ever: moss that drank its own drip, rain that ran back onto the stone that made it. Give each a place to end.
- A cause tested by setting cells by hand can be one that play never reaches. Test each scene's cause by the rubs a child would make, on every layout.
- Take a tap box from the pose that is drawn, not from where the figure stands at rest.
- Look at every still for shapes that count: stamps along a path, cells in a row and two bars side by side all read as beads, dots and lines.
