### The intersection audit

The game is 2D, so the audit is a test of the model: `way.test.ts`, "through a long, random play nothing overlaps anything, and no toy the game sets down lands on tape". Eight seeds of sixty seconds each of a child at play (every tape, toys carried, taps, peels, the box), every frame judged with each toy as a rectangle along its heading, or as the circle it sweeps while it turns. No pair overlaps by more than four units in any frame, no landing the game starts is on tape but the hop that takes a toy the rest of the way to its own tape, which is held to a strip of that tape and of which the eight seeds make more than eight, and no standing toy is off the boards. Allowed, with a reason and a cap: a corner that brushes a neighbour by between one and four units while a toy turns to set off, in at most twelve frames of the eight minutes. A duck under a bridge or a trestle is under whatever crosses it and is not counted against it. The arms of the gates are held by a test of their own: `gates.test.ts`, "through a long, random play no arm is ever down on a toy".

### For the owner to decide

### The cold playtest proxy

Run once on the game's own production build, at 1180 by 820, as a child of three who has been told nothing: a script that looks, waits, pokes what is biggest, smears a finger, holds, and pokes everything. It is the builder's script and not a child.

What the child did, and what answered: looked for three seconds (the town was already alive: the car on its road, every toy looking up); did nothing until the roll glowed and the hand pulled one strip; poked the car on its road (a parp and a hop); smeared a finger down the floor (a road, laid across the train, which strained and popped free, and the car revved); poked the big box (every toy went home, smallest first); waited (the hand tapped the box); poked the box again (they tumbled out); poked the yellow roll and smeared a finger over the train (it came up with a toot, went along with the finger and was set down); held a finger where the dog had been (a yellow stub); pulled toward the rug from where a peg had been (a short yellow strip that stopped on the boards); poked the cat (a prrp), the plant (its leaves shook), the basket (a pomf), the sock (a pff), the lid (a tok), the tray (a ting), and the wall under the home control twice (nothing, as it must be); poked blue and smeared across the road (a bridge).

### The still, in three answers

Asked of the first frame, before anything is touched (a child of four or younger, or of no known age).

- **How much of the frame is empty?** About half. - **What is funny already?** The toys lie where a box was tipped over and every one looks straight up at the child with big eyes on its roof: a duck larger than the car, a bus with a face as wide as itself, a train that puffs where it stands and will not move, a dog whose back half wags in bursts, three peg people of whom one rocks, one bounces in threes and one turns right round to look at everything. ### How the game meets the quality bar

Each frame rate comes with the engine, the throttle, the pixel ratio and the build it was measured on, and with whether a physical iPad was measured. None is measured yet.

- Alive at idle: the car on its road, the duck on its way to water, each toy's own idle and blink, the train's smoke, the leaf shadows, the cat. A town that is only watched grows quiet over about half a minute, in sound only (it moves and leaves its ripples and puffs as ever), and is as loud as ever at the next touch.
- Motion and sound on every touch: the floor (tape), a torn end (the peel), a roll, a toy (a tap, or a carry), the box, the cat, the basket, the plant, the rug, the sock, the lid, the tray, the skirting board and the wall each answer in the call that carries the touch. Only the home control, a finger's width round it, and the grown-up corner answer nothing.
- Weight, squash and follow-through: per toy, in `motion.ts`, for the idle, the tap, the hop, the carry and the landing; no two toys share a curve.
- Kid-clear silhouettes and tappables: rolls about 100 across and 130 apart, every toy at least 76 across, held in `layout.test.ts`.
- Wordless clarity for a three-year-old: no word, letter, numeral or sign; one move shown at a time; the lookalikes that stay are listed under "Reader". A cold playtest proxy was run and its unclear moments are listed above.
- Wordless idle guidance: the glow under the roll in use, then the ghost hand's one pull across bare boards; while the toys are in the box, the glow under the box and one tap on it.
- 60 fps on a mid-range iPad: not measured. Pixel ratio capped at 2, four quality tiers, the draw count 40 to 53 in the stills of reading 2's pass and after and 78 at the heaviest frame of the test, no shadow maps, no post pass, one full-surface stamp a frame.
- Procedural assets only: everything is painted with canvas paths and synthesized sound.
- Nothing passes through anything: the intersection audit above, and every frame of the 180 first strips of `opening.test.ts`. A toy that goes the rest of its way in one hop goes through the air, and whoever is where it lands hops out of the place in the same moment and is down no later.
- Found as left: `found.test.ts`. Put away at any instant and opened again, the town is the one a load would find, nothing replays, and nothing sounds until it is touched.
- No engagement mechanics: nothing is counted, scored, timed or released, and a test holds that the model has no tally.

### What the next builder should know

- Queue a sound in the model, in the same call or step as its motion, and let the Mount play what it takes after every touch and every step. Then a test can step the model and hold each sound to its frame, and no note needs a delay.
- A hop out of the way that looks only a moment ahead lands the toy back in the way. Trigger on the next half second of the mover's way and land clear of the next two seconds of it.
- Circles are too kind to long toys: a bus is twice as long as it is wide. Rectangles along each toy's heading found the overlaps the circles passed.
- Every rule that makes one toy wait for another needs its other half: who stops waiting, and when. A random-play audit finds overlaps and does not find two toys that stand still for ever; a cold script with a burst of stills at one place did.
- What lies on top where two things cross is a fact of the pair. Deciding it by the order they were made gives two looks for one town, and the model and the view each need the same answer.
- Whether a toy is under a thing is a fact of what it travels, not of where it is: a flag set by place hid a duck that walked over the bridge. Set it from the ride, and let the view and the finger ask the same function.
- A fading loudness must gate sound and nothing else. Gating the call that also leaves a ripple or a puff took the motion away with the sound.
- Count a wait in seconds, summed from the step's own length, and carry what is left over into the next wait. A count of frames is right at one rate only, and a test at rates that divide the wait evenly (30, 60, 120 for half a second) does not find a wait that drifts: step it at 45 and 75 too.
- Stopping a toy needs its other half, like making one wait: who may still move it. A toy held still under a finger was hopped away by the next mover and called away by the next strip until each of those asked first whether a finger rests on it.
- Touch a long toy as a rounded bar along its heading, and test it through the Mount's own path at its nose and its tail.
- Replay the stills script in the model when a still looks wrong. It says at once whether the fault is in the model or in the view.
- Replay the town as it opens with a dozen different first moves and count how often each toy gets what it wants. The tests of single rules all passed while the opening town was jammed: a rule that stops a toy from doing harm (never through anybody, never a second toot) also stops it, and only a count of who arrived shows that.
- A log of sounds cannot show that a floor is calm once the sounds have faded. Count hops and steps too.
- Asking a finger in the order the view paints, top first, is one line where both read one list of layers; keep that list in one function.
- Hold the first thirty seconds by a count over many seeded first moves, with a bound in seconds, before any single rule is tuned: ninety towns found in one run what thirteen replays and two readings had circled.
- Do not use the town as it opens as the crowd of a test. It stops being that crowd the day the opening is fixed; build the crowd by hand.
- Count a clock that two towns must agree on in frames, not in seconds: a town put away keeps its time and a loaded one starts at nought, and a half second ends one frame apart in the two.
- A toy called while it is in the air, stuck or in the middle of a turn must remember the call. Every such state that drops it is a first strip that gets no toy.
- A toy on its tape is not yet a toy using it. Count how far it travels as well as whether it arrived: the duck was in every first river within ten seconds and paddled fifty units of five of them, kept there by a peg that had no free floor within a hop.
- A toy wider than its tape is stopped by whoever stands a few units off that tape. Land whoever makes room for it well clear of the tape, not just off it.
- Before a still is called a still of a state, ask the model whether the toy is in that state at that moment. A still of "the dog lying down" was reported from a town in which the dog never lies down, because the lying dog and the standing dog were nearly one drawing; a state a child must see needs a drawing of its own, and a test that the two differ.
- "Nobody does this to a held toy" is one sentence and many code paths: every rule that moves, calls, boards or answers a toy must ask the same one question first. Write the test once for each kind of toy, not for the easiest one.
- What a load must find is which way a toy was going, not only which way it faces. Compare a town put away in the middle of a run with the same town played on, over the next seconds; a comparison of two settled towns cannot see it.
- A flag that means "this step or the last" is a count of steps. Written as a fraction of a second it holds at one rate and fails where a step is as long as the fraction.
