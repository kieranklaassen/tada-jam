# Design sheet

Written before any game code, and checked by someone who did not write it before the game is built on the toy. The headings stay in this order. The look follows the sheet at the end of this file.

What each heading asks for is in the section "The design sheet" of `docs/solutions/conventions/building-a-jam-game.md`.

Tape Town has no learning goal. It is open pretend play: a floor, four rolls of tape and a shoebox of toys with fixed habits.

## The band and its age rule

The manifest band is 3 to 7, and its youngest age, three, governs the design.

- **The cue-table row.** The row for a youngest age of 3 to 4 in the wordless-clarity convention. Its "Avoid" column binds the game: no text, numeral or pictorial icon that has to be decoded, no spoken instruction, no verdict, never several activities live at once, and no tool present before it means anything. One next act is offered at a time: the idle ladder shows one move and nothing else, which is a pull of tape across bare boards or, while the toys are in the shoebox, a tap on the box.
- **The pack's rules for the range** (pack: game-design, ages-2-to-4.md; pack: game-design, ages-4-to-6.md). The band starts at 3, so the rule for the youngest binds what is essential: a tap always works (a tap on the floor sticks down a stub of tape), a drag survives a slipped finger and counts when partly done (a strip is laid from wherever the finger went down to wherever it lifts, however short), the four rolls are about 100 stage units across and 130 apart, and no roll sits in the bottom strip where wrists rest. No pinch, tilt, shake or double tap. For the older end the game is pretend play with characters who react: the child supplies the town and the plot.
- **The symbol rule.** The band starts below 6, so the kid side shows no word, letter, numeral or symbol, and the game has no `symbols.ts`. The rule follows the manifest band: a seven-year-old sees none either. Road, river, hedge and rail are told apart by the colour of the tape and by what the toys do on it, never by a printed sign.
- **What `ctx.childAge` sets.** Only what a first visit opens on. Four and younger: one strip of grey tape already lies on the floor and the car is driving along it. Five and older: the floor is bare and the car waits beside the tipped shoebox. In both the box is tipped, every toy is out, and the grey roll is the one in use. Age locks and hides nothing: every tape, toy and crossing is there for every child from the first touch.
- **What no age gives.** `null` opens as for the youngest child: one grey strip with the car on it.

## The toy

**The action.** Drag a finger across the floor and a strip of tape comes off the roll behind it. That is the touch the finger performs most.

**In an empty scene.** A wooden floor seen from straight above, one roll of grey tape and one toy car, and nothing asks for anything.

- When the finger lands, in that frame, the free end of the tape is stuck down under it with a tick, and the roll jumps to the finger.
- As the finger moves, tape comes off the roll with a zip that follows the hand: a short tick for each finger-width pulled, higher the faster it comes. The strip is drawn straight from where the finger landed to where it is now, lifted a little at the roll's end so its shadow shows, and the roll turns as it gives.
- When the finger lifts, in that frame, the tape tears with a rip: the roll springs back to its place, the torn end drops, and a press runs along the strip from the first end to the last like a thumb smoothing it down, ending in a pat. The strip is flat, matte and a little see-through, with torn fibrous ends.
- The moment the pat lands on a grey strip the car sets off: it scoots across the boards to the strip's nearer end with a clack at each gap, turns onto it and drives along it with a purr. At the far end it makes a three-point turn with two tyre squeaks and comes back. Where two grey strips touch it takes the turn onto the other strip, toward that strip's longer side, every time.
- A tap, with no drag, sticks down a stub one tape-width long with a tick and a pat. A stub is tape like any other.
- A finger that lands on a roll picks that roll: it hops, turns a quarter with a creak and is the tape in use.
- Nothing a finger can do is refused. A strip may cross another, lie on top of it or run off toward the rug, where it stops 48 stage units short of the edge of the bare floor, so that a toy that travels it stays on the boards. When the floor holds as many strips as it can keep (sixty-four), laying one more lifts the oldest with its own small rip, seen as it comes off the boards, and the new one goes down; nothing counts strips or roads.

**Why repeating it is a pleasure with no goal.** The answer starts when the finger lands and is bigger than the touch: a tick, a zip that follows the hand, a rip, a press that travels, a pat, and a car that takes the new road at once (pack: game-design, touch-answers-bigger-than-the-touch.md). Random dragging covers the floor in a tangle of roads that the car drives anyway, and random tapping leaves a trail of stubs, so no child is stuck and nothing is punished (pack: game-design, toy-first.md). The same strips make things: a square, a star, a long road with a branch, a face. Someone watching can tell in three seconds what the child is doing: sticking tape on the floor for a car.

Five toys by the four tapes, the bare floor and a tap of the finger. Every cell looks and sounds different. The habit of each toy is fixed: what it does on a surface is the same every time.

**Where two tapes cross, the pair decides.** It does not matter which was laid first. Four tapes give six crossings, and each is one thing, the same every time:

| Crossing | What it is | What happens there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Road and river | A bridge | The car and the bus drum over it, bom-bom, on hollow boards. The duck swims under, is gone for a moment and pops out the other side with a quack. The dog stops halfway and woofs over the edge at the water. A car or a bus that floats down the river goes under as the duck does, drawn under the road and sounding as it does afloat, with no drum of boards; the same holds under a trestle. A duck that walks the road is on top of the bridge and seen. A finger on the road lifts out whoever is under it, where nobody who is seen is on top: a finger on a car or a bus that stands on the bridge gets that car or bus, since a tap meets what is seen |
| Road and rail | A level crossing | The train hoots before it and clatters over, clack-clack: a clack as its first wheels come onto the road and a clack as its last leave it. The two gates come down across the road on the frame of the hoot and go up on the frame of the second clack; an arm never comes down on anybody, and stays up over whoever stands under it. A car or a bus that reaches it while the gates are shut brakes with a squeak and idles, put-put-put, at the arm, or where it is if it is past the arm and short of the rails, until the gates open, then drives on; whoever walks the road waits at the arm too, with no squeak. Nobody stops on the rails: whoever is on them when the gates shut drives on, a car or a bus drives onto them only when it can drive off them at the other side, and where the road ends too soon beyond the rails for it to stand clear of them it turns back at the arm. The dog sits and howls along with the hoot, then watches the train by |
| Road and hedge | A jam | The car stops at the hedge and honks. The bus pulls up behind and honks lower. They honk in turn, nobody minds, and then each makes its turn and drives back the way it came. Boxed in between two hedges, or between a hedge and the end of a short road, neither honks on and on: back at a hedge after less than 400 units of road it stands there quietly for ten seconds and then turns back, until the child taps it or a strip is laid or peeled |
| River and rail | A trestle | The train rattles across on hollow tonks and drips. A duck swimming under as it passes gets a puff of steam on the head and sneezes |
| River and hedge | Reeds | The duck hides in them with only its tail up, and pops out with a quack at whoever passes next. A car floating by snags, bobs twice with a gulp and floats on |
| Rail and hedge | A tunnel | The train goes in, the hedge shakes and its chuffing goes muffled; it comes out with leaves on its funnel and hoots them off. A car, a duck or a dog that travels the rail goes through under the hedge as the train does, however the hedge slants |

**Three peg people** are not one of the five: they stand where they are put and are part of the bus's habit. A peg beside a road is a bus stop. The bus stops, the peg hops aboard with a hup, and it hops off again at the next peg the bus reaches, so two pegs make a bus line.

**The wrong use of each thing works and is funny.**

- A toy set on the wrong tape does what its row says: the car floats, the train steams up, the duck walks the rail.
- A strip pulled across a standing toy sticks it to the floor for a moment: it strains, pops free with a thwip and stands on the tape, bewildered and unhurt.
- A stub is a whole road, river, hedge or rail one tape-width long: the car spins on a grey stub like a top, the duck sits in a blue one as in a bath.
- A road that leads nowhere is still a road: the car reaches the end, turns round and comes back.
- A toy set down in the shoebox when the box is tipped rolls straight out again with a rattle.

**On day 15** the child builds a town that works and plays it on purpose: a road that crosses the river so the duck goes under the bus, a rail laid through a hedge to see the train come out in leaves, a peg at each end of a road so the bus runs a line, a jam built to hear the honking, the dog set behind the train to make it howl. None of that needs new content: it is four tapes and their six crossings, by five toys whose habits never change (pack: game-design, depth-from-combinations.md; pack: game-design, liveliness-from-causing-and-comedy.md).

## The error as a consequence

Nothing in the game is an error and nothing gives a verdict. What a child may see as a town that does not work shows in the world, where and why:

- A road that stops short of another road is seen as the gap of bare boards between the two torn ends, and the car turns round at the end and does not cross it. One more strip over the gap joins them: the car comes to the new strip at once, as it does to every new road, and takes the turn each time it comes by after that.
- A toy on a tape it cannot use does the funny thing in its row, on that tape, every time, so the child sees which tape was wrong for it.
- A hedge across a road makes a jam at the hedge. Peeling the hedge up, or laying the road round it, lets the traffic through.

The state stays: no strip is taken away because of how a toy fared on it, no toy is sent home, nothing resets. The child changes one thing and watches again. A wrong tape costs nothing: a strip peels up by its end.

## The designed order, and what is stored

**The order.** The game has one place, with the stable id `town`, and no order of challenges: it is open pretend play, and no cycle is judged, so the position never moves. What is harder is something the child makes harder in the world: a longer road, a second tape, a crossing.

**What a first visit starts with, by age.**

- Four and younger: one strip of grey tape lies across the middle of the floor, the car drives along it, the box is tipped, the other toys stand outside it, and the grey roll is in use.
- Five and older: the same, with no strip on the floor and the car waiting beside the box.
- No age: as four and younger.

**What play changes.** Everything on the floor is the child's from the first touch, and a saved town wins over the age. Nothing is locked, counted or released by play.

**Every field of the saved state.**

- `v`: the version of the record.
- `position`: always `town`.
- `finished`: always false, since no cycle ends.
- `strips`: the strips on the floor in the order they were laid, at most sixty-four, each as its tape (grey, blue, green or yellow) and its two ends in whole stage units. A later strip lies over an earlier one. Crossings and joins are not stored: they are worked out from the strips.
- `roll`: which of the four rolls is in use.
- `out`: whether the shoebox is tipped, with the toys out, or upright, with the toys in it.
- `toys`: for the car, the bus, the duck, the train, the dog and the three peg people, where each stands in whole stage units and which way it faces in whole degrees, and for a toy that travels its tape tail first (the train on its run back, a toy backing away to turn) that it does, written with that toy, so that it is found going the way it was going. A toy on the move is saved where it is, on its tape. A toy in the hand is saved where it was picked up. A toy in the air by itself (hopping out of another's way, tumbling out of the box or going home) is saved where it will land.
- `riding`: for each peg person, whether it is aboard the bus.

**What stays and is not stored.** A strip being pulled is not a strip until the finger lifts: put away under a finger that is still down, it is not laid. Leaves on a funnel, a hat of leaves, drops on the boards, steam, who passed last for the dog to follow, and a jam's turn of honking are short-lived and gone on load. On load every toy stands where it was saved and takes up its habit from there in silence until the first touch, as it does after the game was parked: one that was travelling a tape travels on, one found on a tape where it does a funny thing waits for that touch before it does it again, a duck saved under a bridge is under it, a train saved with its wheels on the road at a level crossing has the gates shut over it, which open at its second clack as they would have, and the dog waits for the next one to pass.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

Each toy has one want that always shows, and likes and dislikes that never change.

- **The car** wants road. It looks along whatever grey tape there is, revs once as a grey strip starts to come off the roll, with a shake of the car and a jiggle of its eyes for as long as the rev sounds, where it stands or drives (in the air, in the hand, under a bridge or under the hedge of a tunnel, or in the middle of a turn or of some other business, it does not rev), and takes every turn and every road it is offered: a grey strip just pressed flat calls it off the road it is driving, at once or when its turn is done, and it scoots to the nearer end of the new strip. The bus stays on the road it is driving, and the duck in the river it swims. It does not mind water, hedge or rail: it tries them all and is surprised each time.
- **The bus** wants passengers. It leans toward the nearest peg person and will not pass one by. It dislikes turning: it goes straight on.
- **The duck** wants water. It faces the nearest blue tape, or the blue roll while there is none, and stretches its neck toward a blue strip as it is pulled. It likes hedges to nest in and is in nobody's hurry.
- **The train** wants rails and only rails. It faces the nearest yellow tape, or the yellow roll while there is none, and leans toward a yellow strip as it is pulled. It looks down its nose at every other tape and hoots at everything that crosses its line.
- **The dog** wants company. It looks at whoever moved last and follows them, whatever they are on. It likes the hedge for its own reason and loves the train's hoot.
- **The peg people** want a ride. They look up the road for the bus.
- **The cat on the rug** is not a toy and wants to sleep. An ear turns at every rip, one eye opens when something honks, and its tail flicks. A tap on it gets a prrp and a stretch, and changes nothing.

A toy that stands in the way of a moving one hops aside with its own sound, a short hop to the nearest free floor beside the tape and off all four colours, and stands where it lands, so nothing drives through anything. Nobody is sent hopping by the same toy again and again: the second time the same toy sends it off it lands well clear of every tape where there is such a place within two hops' reach, and after that the one who comes stops short of it without a sound and turns back or goes another way, until a finger taps or picks up the one that was sent, or a strip is laid or peeled. A toy that sits on a stub of its own tape, the duck in its bath, is at home there and hops aside for the train alone; whoever else cannot get by it waits and turns back without a sound. One that was on its way across the boards to its tape when it hopped aside stands aside until whoever it hopped for has gone by, and then sets off again; hopped aside twice, or six seconds on its way and not there, it goes the rest of the way in one hop, over whoever is between and never through them, onto its tape. Where its tape is full it stays on its feet, looks again every half second, and waits longer each time it has been hopped aside before it walks on, up to a minute. A toy stopped on foot by somebody who cannot step aside goes round where there is a way round, a few steps to a place at one side that it can walk straight to and on from there, three times running at the most and then not again for twenty seconds; where there is no way round it goes over, in the one hop onto its tape, and it goes back, as the next sentence says, only where its tape is full. It does not stand and wait for a toy that only stands in its way and cannot step aside: it toots once and goes round or over within half a second, or, stopped again by the same toy in the same place, without a sound and within two seconds. Where no free floor is near, the one who comes waits and toots once while the one in the way shuffles, and then goes back the way it came; when it comes by again and finds the same toy in the same place, that toy hops to free floor further off, up to two hops' reach and well clear of every tape where there is such a place, and nothing is said (the dog is not hopped so: it trots off, as this sheet has it); where there is no free floor within that reach either, the one who comes says nothing, waits four times as long and tries again, and it toots there again only when something has changed (the toy in its way stands somewhere else, the child has tapped it or the toy in its way, or a strip was laid or peeled). A toy that sets off across the boards for a tape says so once, and not again each time it is stopped on the way, and no toy's own two toots, or its setting off and its toot, come within a second of each other; the dog, who got there on its own legs, whines and trots off, nose first, to free floor further away. Back where it stood, it is not asked again until twenty seconds have gone by, and then it goes without a whine. Two who each wait for the other do not stand there: the one who ranks lower gives way within a second (the train gives way to nobody, the dog to everybody), and where it cannot, the other does. A toy with no room to make its turn backs away tail first and turns where there is room. A toy the child sets on a tape it travels is set along that tape, at the nearest place on it where nobody stands. A strip just pressed flat calls whoever wants that tape and stands on the bare floor, or sits at home on a stub of that tape where the new strip is longer than a stub (a stub calls nobody off a stub), and one who is in the air or in the middle of a turn answers when it has landed or is done. The hop that takes a toy the rest of the way to its tape lands at the nearest place along that strip where nobody is; where there is no such place, whoever is at the nearest place hops out of it in the moment the newcomer leaves the floor and is down no later than the newcomer, as far as it takes and well clear of every tape where there is such a place, and whoever was going somewhere goes on when the newcomer has gone by. The newcomer waits for anybody in the middle of a turn or of some other business, never sends off a toy that is on that tape as its own (the bus on the road the car is coming to), never lands where the strip goes under a bridge, under a trestle or through a tunnel, never lands on a strip that is not yet pressed flat, and asks the same toy to make way twice at the most. So in the town as it opens the first strip of grey, blue or yellow tape, laid anywhere a finger can lay it, has a toy that wants it on it within about ten seconds, with nothing else touched; green is nobody's want. Wherever the game sets a toy down (a landing from the box, a hop aside, the end of a drive) is on the bare floor, or for the one hop that takes a toy the rest of the way to its own tape on that tape, and never on a roll, the shoebox, the cat or another toy, and a tap on a toy is never taken for a roll or the box behind it. A finger that lands on a moving toy stops it where it is in that frame, and stops the bus under a peg that rides it: lifted with no drag it was a tap and the toy goes on, and dragged the toy is carried from where it is, as far as the finger goes, and nothing jumps into the hand. A toy caught in the air is in the hand at once, where it is. A toy a finger rests on is left alone, every toy: nobody sends it hopping, backs it away or calls it away, the dog under a finger is not sent trotting and does not howl at a hoot, and a peg under a finger does not board the bus. Whoever comes up behind it or towards it, where it stands or where it was stopped on its way, toots at it once and waits or turns back as for a toy with no free floor near, and a strip that called it is answered when the finger lets it go. A toy caught in the air on its way onto its tape, when the touch is taken away and not lifted, is where it was going and takes that tape up in silence. The bare floor stops short of the jam's round home control at the top centre (48 px across, 10 px from the top), so no strip, toy, roll or box is ever under it. An overlap test holds both at each surface size the game is tested at.

No feeling of any of them is about the child: none thanks, praises, asks, hurries or minds being ignored. While the child is idle the car looks at the grey roll and back at the floor in front of it, which is the scene's one want.

## The scenes

The game is open pretend play with no cycle and no ending. It has two short scenes and a calm way to tidy up.

**Tipping out.** Caused by a tap on the upright shoebox. Beats: the box rocks and goes over onto its side with a thump; the toys tumble out one after another, each with its own landing (the car bounces on its tyres, the bus lands heavily and sways, the duck bounces twice with a squeak, the train lands with a clank, the dog skids and shakes, the pegs roll and stand up); each then sets off by its habit. About three seconds. Saved when it starts: `out` as true, and each toy's and each peg's place in `toys` as the place it will land. Filled in from the state of play: the toys head for whatever tape lies on the floor. A touch ends it with every toy standing where it was to land.

**Calling home.** Caused by a tap on the tipped shoebox. Beats: the box is stood upright with a knock; every toy stops, and they go home one after another, smallest first and the bus last, each hopping in with its own sound; a peg aboard the bus gets off first; the box settles, open, with every toy looking out over its rim. About three seconds. Saved when it starts: `out` as false, each toy's and each peg's place in `toys` as its place in the box, and `riding` as false for every peg. Filled in from the state of play: each toy starts from wherever it had got to. A touch ends it with every toy in the box.

**What a peel is.** A finger that lands on a torn end of a strip lifts that end in that frame with a creak. A strip too short to have two ends a finger can tell apart (shorter than three times the reach of an end) is one thing: a finger anywhere on it lifts it, and it comes off by a pull along it, either way, of its own length and at least a tape-width, so a peel is always a pull that is seen. Pulled back along the strip, over its own length, the strip comes off with a long rip while the finger pulls and is gone when the finger lifts; a toy on it stops where it stood and is settled in that call as a load would settle it: whole on the boards and in silence, from where it takes up its habit as it does after a load. The same holds for a toy in a hedge or in reeds that are peeled from round it, and for one under a road or a rail that is peeled from over it, which is seen again in that call; one that would be left standing on another toy hops to the nearest free floor. Pulled back only part of the way and let go, what had come up lies down again with a pat, the strip is as it was and nothing new is laid. Pulled any other way, or lifted with no pull, the end drops back with a pat and a new strip or a stub is laid from there like any other, so joining two roads end to end never peels one. It is an answer to a touch and not a scene. Saved when the finger lifts: `strips` without that strip, and the place of each toy that stopped. Put away under a finger that is still peeling, the strip is not peeled and lies flat on load.

**How play ends and begins.** Nothing ends by itself and nothing new starts while the child does nothing. The town runs for as long as it is watched and stands still while the game is parked. While the child only watches, the sounds the toys make by themselves grow softer from fifteen seconds after the last finger lifts and are gone after thirty-five, never while a finger is down, and are whole again at the next touch. Only sound goes quiet: every habit is still done, with its ripples, bubbles, steam, leaves and puffs, every time, and the dog still sits and howls when the train hoots. The child tidies up by standing the box upright, which calls the toys home, and by peeling strips; the floor is never cleared for them. On load no scene plays: the floor, the box and the toys are as they were saved, a box that was tipped lies tipped, and a box that was upright holds its toys until it is tapped: it stands open with no lid on it, the toys are seen in it from above, and each looks out over the rim at what it wants (the car at the grey roll, the duck at the blue roll or the nearest blue tape, the train at the yellow roll or the nearest rail, the bus at the pegs, the dog at whoever moved last, the pegs at the nearest road).

## The look

Written after the style spike, not part of the sheet.

**The claimed look: washi tape on floorboards.** The first look reserved for the game in `docs/art-direction.md`, spiked at 1180 by 820 and kept. The room is seen from straight above and the subject is tape: strips of matte paper tape in four colours, with torn fibrous ends and a little of the wood showing through, on an oiled oak floor. No look reserved after it was tried.

**The frame is a place.** A corner of a playroom, filled to every edge: the skirting board and a strip of wall along the top, a basket of wool and a pot plant in the two top corners, the shoebox and its lid down the left with tissue paper and a stray sock below them, a tray with the four rolls down the right, and a woven rug with a sleeping cat along the bottom. A patch of sun lies across the boards, slanting down and to the right the way the shadows fall, with the shadows of leaves drifting in it. The tissue paper lies in full view beside the sock, creased by three short folds of unequal length that do not meet. The bare boards between are the child's, and the toys stand on them large: the car is 137 stage units long, the bus 205, a peg person 76, on a stage 1180 wide.

**Palette.** Warm wood carries the frame and the tape and the toys are the only saturated things on it.

- Floor: five oak tones round `#c9955a`, darker gaps and a knot here and there; the patch of sun is a pale wash over them.
- Tape: grey `#99a1ab` (road), blue `#4a9bd8` (river), green `#5cad55` (hedge), yellow `#f3c62f` (rail), each with a lighter fibre tone. No tape is red, purple or teal: those belong to the toys and the box.
- Toys: the car red `#d8402f`, the bus purple `#7b62c9` with yellow trim, the duck yellow `#ffd84a` with an orange bill, the train navy `#2c3e63` with red and one brass band round its boiler, between the funnel and the buffer beam and clear of the funnel's ring, the dog cream `#f6f1e7` with brown patches, the three pegs in teal, lilac and coral coats.
- Room: the shoebox and lid a dusty teal, the rug brick red with an ochre pattern, the cat black and white, the plant deep green.
- Line: one warm dark, `#3a2a22`, three stage units wide round every toy and never pure black. Pupils `#2b2622`.

**Materials.** Paper tape is matte, a little see-through (the boards' gaps read faintly through it), with fibres along its length and ends torn by hand, never cut square. Tape that is not pressed flat yet is paler and lifted, with a soft shadow under it. The toys are chunky painted wood and rubber seen from above, with rounded corners, a flat colour, one lighter panel, and big eyes on top that look up out of the floor. Nothing is glossy, nothing has a gradient but the soft shadows.

**The four rolls are four different rolls.** Each is a ring of tape round a card core with a loose end, and no two share a size, a core or the way the end hangs: the grey is a full fat roll on a narrow buff core, its end up and to the right; the blue is half used, a thin ring round a wide white core through which the boards show, its end hanging long down the tray; the green is nearly full and a little out of round, on a brick-red core with a small hole and a short end; the yellow is almost gone, a thin band on a wide brown core with a dark hole, its end sticking out to the left. Every core is a plain ring with nothing drawn in it: after the hole and its outline no roll has another mark, and a test holds that.

**Light.** One light, from the window at the top left: every toy, roll and loose strip throws a short soft shadow down and to the right, and a toy that leaves the floor parts from its shadow, which stays on the boards and fades a little. The patch of sun slants the same way. No other light, and no glow but the idle ladder's: a soft warm patch with no drawn edge, under the thing the ghost hand will touch, which is the shoebox while the toys are in it and the roll in use once they are out.

**Motion rules.**

- Every toy moves like itself and no two share a curve (`motion.ts`, with a test that fails on a copy). At rest the car shivers in fits, the bus sinks and rises on its springs, the duck bobs and looks about, the train swells with each chuff and puffs smoke, the dog wags in bursts, one peg rocks, one bounces in threes and one turns right round. Each blinks on its own period.
- Weight shows in landings, and no two toys land alike: the bus sinks once and slowly, the duck wobbles, the dog lands running, skids stretched out along its way and then shakes itself from nose to tail, and a peg rolls round on its foot before it stands.
- Eyes follow the finger while it is down. With no finger each toy looks at what it wants. The dog that was hopped out of the way twice and lay down beside the road has its eyes open, and its pupils follow whoever moves nearest; a dog that lay down with nobody to follow is asleep, lids shut. Lain down, the dog is another drawing than the dog that stands: lower and wider, its chin on the boards, its forelegs stretched flat in front of it and its hind legs out to the sides, its ears spread on the floor and its tail curled along its flank and still.
- No sound without sight: the car's rev at a grey strip being pulled is said once for each strip and is a fast shake of the car and a jiggle of its pupils, where it stands or drives, for the third of a second the rev sounds and no longer; where it would not show (in the air, in the hand, under a bridge, under the hedge of a tunnel, in the middle of a turn) there is no rev. The sounds a toy makes on its way move that toy for half a second: at its hoot the train swells across and draws in along its length, twice; on the drum of a bridge the car bounces twice on its shadow and the bus sinks once on its springs; at its horn on the water the bus rocks from side to side.
- Waits are in seconds on the model's clock, whatever the length of a step.
- A touch is answered in the call that carries it. Sound and motion share one clock: a sound with two parts is two sounds, each queued on the frame its motion happens, and `town.test.ts` steps the toy and holds each sound to that frame.
- The room is alive without a touch: leaf shadows drift, the cat's side rises and falls and its tail tip flicks, the train puffs. However long the child only watches, and after a load, every habit still leaves its ripples, bubbles, steam, leaves and puffs; only the sounds the toys make by themselves fade, from fifteen seconds after the last finger lifts to nothing at thirty-five and never under a finger that is down; the dog still howls at a hoot nobody hears, and the cat's eye still opens at a honk nobody hears.
- Nothing passes through anything: a toy standing in a mover's way makes a short hop to the nearest free floor, off every tape, before the mover is there. Where there is no free floor near, the mover waits and toots once and the one in the way shuffles, and the mover goes back; when it comes by again and finds the same toy there still, that toy hops to free floor further off, without a sound; where there is none it says nothing, waits long and tries again, and toots again only when something there has changed or the child has tapped it or the toy in its way, so a full floor is a calm one. Nobody is sent hopping by the same toy more than twice: the second hop lands well clear of every tape where it can, and after that the mover turns back before it without a sound. A toy on a stub of its own tape is at home there and hops aside for the train alone. A toy that sets off across the boards says so once, and that sound is its toot for the moment: no toy's own two toots come within a second of each other, and whoever is in the way shuffles all the same. Hopped out of somebody's way while it was on its way to its tape, a toy stands aside until they have gone by and then sets off again, and hopped aside twice, six seconds on its way, or stopped with no way round, it goes the rest of its way in one hop through the air, onto its tape at the nearest place where nobody is; whoever must make room there hops well clear of the tape in the same moment, and is down no later. Where its tape is full it waits longer each time, up to a minute. A strip just pressed flat calls whoever wants that tape, from the bare floor or from a stub of that tape where it sat at home: the car leaves its road for a new one, and the train hops onto a new rail. Stopped on foot by somebody who cannot step aside, it goes round where there is a way round: a few steps to a place at one side that it can walk straight to, and on from there, three times running at the most and then not for twenty seconds. Kept on the boards within its own reach of tape that lies along their edge, it is on that tape and takes to it. The dog trots off on its own legs with a whine, nose first, where a straight way to free floor is clear; back where it stood it is not asked again for twenty seconds, and then goes without a whine. Hopped out of the way twice, however long apart, it lies down where it lands and watches until it is tapped, a strip is laid or peeled, or the toys have been home in the box; it howls at the train's hoot and lies down again. Two who wait for each other never stand there: the lower rank gives way within a second. A mover that finds itself against another toy may always leave: it is held only by a way that takes it nearer. A toy backing off a tape to the floor is a mover too: whoever has come to stand where it is going hops aside, twice at the most for the same toy, and where they cannot it stops where it is.
- A finger that lands on a moving toy stops it where it is in that frame, and the bus under a peg that rides it; lifted with no drag it was a tap, and the toy goes on. Dragged, the toy comes up from where it is and keeps its place under the finger, so nothing jumps into the hand. A toy caught in the air is in the hand at once. A toy a finger rests on is left alone, every toy: nobody sends it hopping, backs it away or calls it away, the dog under a finger is not sent walking and does not howl, and a peg under a finger does not board the bus. Whoever comes up behind it or towards it on its road toots at it at once, once each, and waits or turns back. A toy caught in the air and then let go by the browser and not by the child takes up its tape, or stands on the boards, as if it had been set down there.
- A carried toy lifts off its shadow and is drawn above everything on the floor and round it: the rolls, the cat and the box. Where the child lets it go on a tape it travels it is set along that tape. Tape in the hand, coming off the roll or peeled up, is drawn over the toys on the floor.
- A tap meets what is seen: a finger meets the toys in the reverse of the order they are painted in, so on a bridge it gets the car or the bus on top, and whoever is under only where nothing seen is on top of it.
- A toy is touched over the whole shape it is drawn as: a rounded bar as long and as wide as the toy, turned the way it faces, so the bus's nose, the train's eyes and the dog's snout are the toy. A tap there never lays tape.
- Every toy's own part shows in its eyes as well as where it is drawn for that toy (the dog's tail, the bus's doors, the train's smoke ring): the car's shiver, the duck's looking about, the train's chuff and a peg's rocking all move the pupils a little.
- A strip is peeled over its own length: the tape comes up as far as the finger has gone back along it and no further, and let go before the end it lies down again. A strip too short to have two ends is one thing: a finger anywhere on it lifts it, and it comes up by a pull along it of its own length and at least a tape-width.
- A toy whose tape is peeled from under it, from round it or from over it is whole on the boards in that call, as a load would find it, and stands on nobody; so is one that sat at home on a stub of its own tape.
- As a town is opened again, a train on the bare floor hops onto the nearest rail that lies flat, without a sound, and a train whose wheels are on the road at a level crossing has both arms down over the road. A toy that was travelling tail first when the town was put away, the train on its run back or a car backing, is found going on the way it was going.

**Where two tapes cross, the pair decides what lies on top**, never the order they were laid: a road lies over a river, rails lie over a road and over a river, and a hedge lies over a road, a river and a rail. Each crossing is built as the place it is, so that none is two bars of colour:

- **The bridge** has a dark parapet down each side with a lit top, a square post at each of its four corners, and its shadow on the water to both sides.
- **The trestle** stands on three splayed timber legs to each side, each of its own lean and width and with no foot across its end, in the water, and throws its shadow on it.
- **The level crossing** has boards laid along the middle of the rails for the road to cross on, the yellow showing to each side of them as the two rails, and two gates beside the road, one before the rails and one after. Each is a square post, part of the floor, with the pale end of its arm on it while the gate is open. On the frame the train hoots the arms come down across the road, alongside the rails: each a pale boom with a red edge and two red bands of unequal width, longer as it comes down, with its shadow nearer as it does. They go up on the frame of the train's second clack. An arm is drawn over the toys on the floor and never comes down on one: it stays up over whoever stands under it.
- **The jam** is at a real hedge: bushes of unequal size bulge out over the tarmac down both sides of the green tape, with a few lighter leaves scattered on them and a shadow on the road.
- **The reeds** stand out over the water as blades to both sides.
- **The tunnel** has two dark mouths where the rail goes in, and whoever travels the rail goes in at one and comes out at the other.

Each crossing is painted once into the floor layer in the colour the tape has on wood, so it costs nothing a frame. Only a toy in the river is under what crosses it: the duck, the car or the bus afloat under a bridge or a trestle is painted first, and the road or the rails are laid over it again with their parapets or legs. The duck is drawn small there: at the middle it is gone, and at the edges its tail or its bill shows. A duck that walks the road or the rail is on top and seen. Whoever travels the rail through a tunnel (the train, and the car, the duck and the dog on a rail) has the hedge laid over it again, over the whole of its body however the hedge slants across the rail, and the hedge shakes while the train's middle is under it. A strip with an end in the hand keeps its crossings, built as they were, until the lift reaches them, and then they go with the tape; what lies flat of a river in the hand stops at its bridge and starts again past it, so the river is never shown over the road. The rail is plain yellow tape with no sleepers drawn on it, held by a test that paints a short rail, a long one and a road and counts the same marks; the boards at a level crossing lie along the rails, not across them.

**The bus's doors** are two leaves hinged at the body on the side of whoever waits for it. They swing out as it comes up to a peg and shut when the peg is aboard, and clap twice at a tap.

**How it is drawn, and the tiers.** The room and every strip that lies flat are painted into one layer, which is painted again only when a strip is pressed flat or the surface changes size, and stamped once a frame. Each toy, roll, the box, the cat and the ghost hand is a sprite painted once for the surface's density, and the dog has a second one for when it has lain down. What is live is a few fills and strokes: the strip in the hand, the press running along a new strip, a strip being peeled, the patch of road or rail over a toy afloat under it, with its parapets or legs, and of hedge over whoever is in a tunnel, the arms of gates that are down or moving, pupils, lids, the dog's tail, the bus's doors, smoke, leaves, drops, the cat's tail and eye. Patches of one tape over several toys are one set of fills, and so are their parapets, their legs and every gate arm. The leaf shadows are one fill. The draw count is those stamps plus those live fills and strokes, counted in `view.ts` where each is made, and `frameBudget.test.ts` holds the count to what reaches the surface and to the budget of about 80. The draw number is 78: the heaviest frame the test can build, with three toys afloat under three bridges, the gates of three crossings moving, every toy tapped and three strips being pressed flat at once, which one finger cannot do. The same floor is 59 with a strip being pulled, 64 with a strip being peeled or an end lifted, and 66 and 72 with one and two strips being pressed. No frame of a busy scripted stretch or of two minutes of random play from three seeds is heavier, and the stills of reading 2's pass and after report 40 to 53.

The stage is fitted to the surface as a whole: on a surface too short for the bare floor to clear the home control it is drawn a little smaller and set lower. The floor stays in stage units, so a strip or a toy saved on one surface is where it was on any other and none is moved or taken away.

- Tier 0: pixel ratio 2, five leaf shadows, fibres on the tape.
- Tier 1: pixel ratio 1.5, the same.
- Tier 2: pixel ratio 1.25, three leaf shadows, plain tape with torn ends and no fibres.
- Tier 3: pixel ratio 1, no leaf shadows, plain tape.

Every tier keeps the floor, the torn ends, the toys, their shadows and all motion: a lower tier is softer, never barer.
