# Light Garden: thirty refinement passes

Each pass: a screenshot at 1180×820 (DPR 2) → one honest critique → one focused fix → a new screenshot and a perf check. Passes 1 to 24 judged the look and feel against the brief and the reference photo of a Montessori light table. Passes 25 to 30 judged the game against the compounded docs and the "Before you show the owner" checklist in `AGENTS.md`, and against two runs of a cold playtest proxy.

**How each pass was measured.** The screenshot is a fresh garden at age 7 in WebKit on a fake clock, 6.4 seconds after load, so the idle glow and the ghost hand's first demonstration are in frame, at the top tier (DPR 2 with the glow pass). A second shot seeds a busy table (both lamps, the prism, a mirror, and the red filter out; the moth and snail awake). Perf is the jam's shared probe on the production build in headless Chromium at 6× CPU throttle, once on the automatic tier and three times pinned to the top tier (the median is logged). `cpuP95Ms` is the 95th-percentile CPU time of a frame's own work (controller step, view update, render submit) from `window.__jamPerf`; draw calls come from `renderer.info`. The machine is a cloud VM with no GPU: Chromium renders through SwiftShader and WebKit through a software rasterizer, so frame rates here are a floor and CPU time is the number to compare. On this machine the automatic tier settles at tier 0 at 6×, because software fill is the bottleneck; that says nothing about an iPad's GPU.

The last column is: auto-tier `cpuP95Ms` / draw calls · top-tier median `cpuP95Ms` / draw calls, all at 6×. Budget: under 8 ms at 6× and under 80 draw calls.

Screenshots are in the Project store under `media/jam-10-games/light-garden/` (`iter-01.png`, `iter-10.png`, `iter-20.png`, `iter-30.png`, `hero.png`, `walkthrough.mp4`).

## Intersection audit

The jam's intersection audit (`npm run check:intersections -- light-garden`, config in `scripts/intersections/games/light-garden.ts`, enforced in CI) plays 66 s of game time. It covers the opening, pieces and the snail carried over other pieces and sleepers, a lamp left at the panel's edge and turned, every creature poked twice, pieces sent home, a full garden, and awake creatures set down beside pieces.

**Found (6 open).** A carried mirror went through the lit lamp (14%), and a carried filter went through the sleeping fish (12%) and jelly (10%). A lamp dropped at the left edge pushed its knob out over the frame (12%). The snail's "who is there" poke pitched its head into the panel (9%). The lamp's lantern bars z-fought with the lantern room (8 px²). Porting the glass shader's creature moves to TypeScript found two more that the audit cannot see: the moth's wings dipped under the panel at take-off, and the snail overshot into it when set down.

**Changed.** Pieces and creatures now have bodies measured from their meshes (`bodies.ts`: each piece's top and reach, the knob, and each creature's tallest and widest drawn pose). Every frame the controller raises what is moving over what is under it: a carried piece over everything, a carried creature over resting pieces and sleepers, a piece flying home over whatever rests in its path. It rises 2.5 cm early and keeps 0.4 cm clear, so it floats up rather than pops. No creature is drawn lower than the panel in its pose. A dropped, tapped, knob-turned or loaded piece settles with its knob 0.5 cm inside the panel and clear of its neighbours, sliding there. The snail's second poke answer rears up, as it was meant to. The lantern bars are 1 mm shorter. The tray knob folds as a morph target instead of in the shader alone, so the audit sees the fold, and the lamp's lens is a thin solid disc, so the lamp stays one closed shape when the knob folds.

**Allowed.** Nothing. One finding stays hidden without a rule: a static z-fight on the table body's front face, 5 cm under its top and off screen in every frame. The shader-instanced shadows, eyes, and light were checked by eye. Awake creatures were checked against pieces and each other offline, with the audit's own geometry code on the shader-deformed creatures over 72 s of a full garden: no crossings.

**Perf.** Chromium at 6× CDP throttle, 1180×820, DPR 2, interleaved with `main` in one session. Pinned top tier: 4.9, 4.9, 5.1, 5.6, 12.0, 4.3 ms against `main`'s 4.4, 4.6, 5.7, 6.4, 8.9, 4.4 (medians 5.0 and 5.15; the 12.0 and 8.9 pair ran while other workers loaded the VM). Auto tier: 2.7, 2.4, 2.5 against 2.7, 2.5, 2.6. Draw calls unchanged (23 top, 19 auto). The CI frame-budget test's busiest garden costs the controller 0.017 to 0.018 ms a step against `main`'s 0.011 to 0.016 (five interleaved runs, budget 0.15 ms).

## Cold playtest proxy

The checklist asks for a cold playtest before showing the owner; no child was available, so a scripted proxy stands in, run twice with the same script.

Cold playtest proxy (same script both runs: fresh garden at age 7, 10 s hands off, then about 60 s as a newcomer: poke the moth, tap the lamp, drag the red filter, drag the prism into the beam, tap it, drag the filter into the fan, drag a mirror in and turn it twice, then hands off to 70 s; WebKit, fake clock, frames every 100 ms).

Run 1 (after pass 25), unclear moments:
1. The ghost hand presses the lamp and nothing moves, so the demonstration shows no consequence.
2. Poking the sleeping moth gets a twitch too small to see from the child's seat.
3. Once the prism fans the colours out, nothing connects them to the sleepers, and nothing shows that a sleeper can be carried.
4. The moth falls back asleep when the prism takes its white beam. Judged fine: it is the table telling the truth, and it wakes again when white returns.

Run 2 (after passes 26 and 27):
1. Fixed. The ghost press swings the lamp's beam down onto the moth and back, so the demonstration shows what a tap does.
2. Fixed. The moth flurries up in a loop with its eyes open, and its dream orbs brighten to show it wants white.
3. Addressed. In the late idle stretch the ghost hand now lifts the snail toward its red-and-green overlap (run 1 brought another mirror from the tray). The snail faces the child while it is the want. Whether a child follows the carry is untested: the proxy script does not act on hints.
4. Unchanged and still judged fine.
No new unclear moments.

## Still weak

- No physical iPad was measured. Every number here comes from a cloud VM rendering in software, so treat it as a floor check. The grown-up overlay (triple-tap the top-left corner) is how to read the real frame rate and tier on a device.
- The sounds, including the eight new poke answers, were tuned by reasoning about shape and pitch, not by ear on an iPad speaker. They need a listening pass.
- The cold playtest proxy is scripted: it finds moments where the screen says nothing, but it cannot tell whether a seven-year-old follows the carry demonstration or understands why red and green make yellow.
- Where the prism's fan begins, the three colours still overlap for a few centimetres and add up to white, so a sleeper placed there wakes as if in white light. That is honest additive light, but a child may not see why.
- The awake snail's coil keeps its amber, but its spiral face only shows from the side; from above it reads as an amber dome on a cream body.
- Awake creatures each play one continuous routine of their own, with nudges and two poke answers as variants, but they have no separate spontaneous delights yet (the motion doc's grooming, glancing, or sneezing moments).
- While the garden rests at half rate, awake creatures keep playing at 30 fps. It looked fine in captures on the fake clock, but it has not been judged on a device in real time.
