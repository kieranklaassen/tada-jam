### For the owner to decide

One line each. The nearest things to praise, to blame, to a count, to a prompt and to a punishment come first, in that order.

- **Nearest to praise: the likes, and the raised fist at the ending.** A fair-goer given a thing it likes shows it: the bundle squeals and hops twice, the candle-maker hums and lifts her fist, the baker hums, the tuba's note rings clear. - **Nearest to blame: her fist sinking with one whistle at a thin candle.** It is the one answer to the child's own work: when a candle has melted thin under the wax, a falling whistle sounds once and her fist sinks, once. Nobody else answers it, and nobody answers the wick going bare. With it belong the dislikes, which are about a thing given: the baker says bleh and holds it away, the goat sneezes, the tuba's note sours, the bundle tips over and rocks back up.
- **Nearest to a count: six basket places.** The basket holds six things; a seventh sets it rocking and flies back. Nothing marks an empty place and nothing says how many are full. - **Nearest to a prompt: the ladder, four times at most.** After three idle seconds the piece in work glows; after five an open painted hand shows one move, four times at most, further and further apart, and then nothing. The halo on the basket in the snow swells three times as it comes up, each time less, over about eight and a half seconds, and then stands still for as long as the fair is left; a fair found finished finds it still.
- **Nearest to a punishment: melting, warned and slow.** A part of the candle left under the wax for 1.6 seconds is warned: two slow bubbles rise at the candle, with a sound now and then, and nothing is lost. From 3 seconds under it melts, at 2 steps of thickness a second, where one coat is 4 steps and one dip makes it: a coat takes two seconds to go, five quick bubbles cross the pot, and the wax rises with what melts into it. A dip of two seconds, or of nearly three, leaves every part as fat as it found it and adds the coat. A part that did melt comes out hot and takes no coat from that dip. Whether there should be melting at all is the owner's call: it is the only way back to a bare wick, and the only thing in the game that un-makes work.
- **A slip that gives work away.** Where the finger ends decides what a drag on a piece was, so that a four-year-old who drags a biscuit to a hand gives it. The cost is that a stroke or a rub that slips off the piece onto a hand or a body gives the piece away as it is. It can be taken back from the hand, with whatever mark the fair-goer left.
- **Faces while the child works.** Nobody's mouth is turned down before the child has touched anything. At a bare wick and at a thin candle she waits with her brows up; she grins as the candle fattens. The tuba player is stern by his brows, and his mouth is a straight line. ## For the pull request

Written as the game is built and kept at the end of this file: the pull request is made from it.

Advent Market is a winter fair in one hall, for ages 4 to 8. The child dips a candle, folds a paper star, binds a wreath and cuts and ices a biscuit, lays what is made in a basket, uses any thing at any other stall, gives any thing to any of five fair-goers who each do their own thing with it, and carries the basket out into the snow when the fair is done. It comes from the demo of the same name, which lends the idea and the feel and no code. It adds no package.

### How the game meets the quality bar

- **Alive at idle.** Flames and their pools flicker, snow falls, each of the five has its own breath, blink and three habits, and all eyes follow what moves. All of it stops while unattended or hidden (`folk.test.ts`, `clock.test.ts`).
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** Every thing in the hall answers a touch when the finger lands: the working pieces as themselves, a painted prop at the place it is touched with the nearest fair-goer looking, bare wall with a breath of air, and the window with a swirl of snow at every tier. No answer is a sound alone, and a thing that is sent back flies back in sight (`dipping.test.ts`, `carrying.test.ts`, `benches.test.ts`, `answers.test.ts`, `drawn.test.ts`, `giving.test.ts`).
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** The candle is a pendulum that slows as it fattens; the stick overshoots its hooks; each bench gives under the finger and springs back; a pulled piece comes after the finger with slack; each fair-goer is a spring of its own weight and tempo.
- **Kid-clear silhouettes and tappables.** The working pieces are flat and plain and drawn after the live light. Every target, the five held-out hands included, is 48 px or more at 1180 by 820 and 1024 by 768 and answers where it is drawn; nothing a child must touch lies under the home control or in the top right corner (`places.test.ts`, `drawn.test.ts`, `visit.test.ts`).
- **Wordless clarity for age 4.** No word, letter, numeral or sign; one stall in view at a time; a tap and a drag; the wordless check passes. What could be mistaken for a sign is listed under "Reader".
- **Wordless idle guidance.** A glow after three idle seconds, an open painted hand that shows one move after five, four times at most and then quiet. Every move it shows works (`guide.test.ts`).
- **Frame budget.** 65 stamps, strokes and fills at the busiest legal moment of any place, at both sizes, with one full-surface stamp; pixel ratio capped at 2; no blur in a frame, counted by a test; the pictures are painted with blurred edges when the surface takes its shape, and a tier change paints nothing. One made thing is painted, without blur, in the frame it changes, and that is not in the count (`frameBudget.test.ts`). **No frame rate has been measured**, on this machine or on a tablet.
- **Procedural assets only.** Everything is painted at load and every sound is synthesized. No file, no address (`hygiene.test.ts`, the egress check).
- **Its own art direction.** Candlelit gouache, as the art guide in `ART.md` has it.
- **Found as left.** Every scene is saved as it starts, ended by any later touch, and never replayed on load; a put-away at any instant leaves the fair as a load of its save finds it; a thing the child let go just before the put-away is found where it was let go, and a thing under a finger that was still down is found where it came from; the next fair starts only on the child's tap on the basket in the snow, once the ending is over (`scenes.test.ts`, `foundAsLeft.test.ts`, `grace.test.ts`). An unfinished coat is not kept: a candle under the wax at a put-away is found without the coat of that dip.
- **Touch-first.** A tap and a drag; a tap never gives anything away; one finger works, and a palm or a thumb at rest hands the work to the next finger that lands (`hands.test.ts`, `visit.test.ts`).
- **No engagement mechanics.** No score, count, timer or streak. A like and a dislike are each fair-goer's own business with the thing (`scenes.test.ts`, `folk.test.ts`). The nearest things to praise and to blame are named under "For the owner to decide".
- **Age is a hint.** `ctx.childAge` only decides whether the cloths lie on a first visit; a tap lifts a cloth at any age, and `null` is as the youngest (`save.test.ts`).

### Defaults taken for the owner

- A melting candle, the basket of six, the fifth fair-goer, all five fixed tastes, the chord and halo at the end, and the three things that cannot be undone, as under "For the owner to decide".
- No camera shake and no impact pause, as the guide's default.
- A lifted finger's moment is a fifth of a second and 90 stage units; the basket in the snow is ready when the ending's nine seconds are over.
- A hand takes a thing only at a let-go, and never at a tap. Where the finger ends decides what a drag on a piece was: on a hand or a body the piece is given as it is, with the work of the way there on it; on the piece it is a stroke or a rub. The ring and the star go by the biscuit's rule.
- A held-out hand answers before the bench behind it; the piece in work answers before the hand where it is drawn over it, which for the sheet of dough is most of his hand.
- A contact is at rest after one second within 14 px, or at once where it landed on nothing, and then gives way to the next contact that lands. A finger that holds the stick, a thing or the basket never gives way.
- A thing let go and still hanging at a put-away lands where it hangs, the basket over the sill included, which finds the fair finished.
- The wax rises 8 stage units at most for a candle standing in it and 5 more for what melts into it, and sinks back within a few seconds of the candle coming out.
- A tap on a bundle adds its twigs to those on the ring and takes none off. A smaller sheet is not taken while more points are folded than it has. A smaller tin keeps the icing beyond its edge.
- Melting warns at 1.6 seconds under, starts at 3, and takes two seconds a coat. The warning is two slow bubbles and a sound nearly a second apart; melting is five quick bubbles and five sounds a second.
- A walk starts when the finger lifts from a walking lantern. The halo swells three times and stands still.
- The pictures are painted once for the top tier's pixel ratio; a tier change paints nothing.
- The dev shell's child of no age is treated as the youngest.

### What the next builder should know

- Paint a figure flat, then let one shared pass light it from where the flames are: two strokes each side and a falloff over the whole sprite were enough, and stronger strokes read as glass.
- Draw the live light before the working pieces.
- Batch what is small and many (spots, drops) into one path a colour, or the live count climbs past the stamps.
- A path of several ellipses needs a `moveTo` before each, or the fill joins them with a wedge.
- Keep what the Mount does around the game (whose touch, what holds the ladder, how sounds are played, the put-away) in a module a test can reach.
- A thing a character holds outside its own body needs its own place to be touched, and so does the hand it holds out: test every drop on the hand as drawn, at both sizes, through the Mount's path.
- A game on one clock cannot use the touch tracker's own grace, which runs on the event clock. Keep the grace in the model and test it with pointer events.
- A scene that a touch can end must not be the only thing that guards what comes after it. Give the wait its own clock.
- Judge a let-go by where the hand is, not by where a swinging thing happens to be: a still made twice the same way gave the candle to two different places until it was.
- Count the draws with a literal table in the test. A snapshot assertion writes into the test file.
- Let a hand act only at the let-go. Every rule that made a hand act under a moving finger (hold the candle up, lift the piece) took something from a child who was only passing.
- A blur is paint, and paint in a frame is a hitch: paint with blur only when the surface takes its shape, and give what must be painted in a frame an edge that needs none. Count blurred fills in the recording context, so a test can say none ran in a frame.
- A shade under a round button is a smile. Look at every small fix in a still.
- Decide what a drag was where it ends, not where it starts or by what it did on the way: a rule that asks "did this touch make anything?" cannot tell a child who is giving from a child who is icing, and takes the work away from one of them.
- Ask of every target that lies inside another's radius which of the two is drawn in front, and let that one answer. A hand inside the dough's radius answered as dough for three readings.
- When a level, a glow or a size is driven by a number that can grow, keep a still for its largest value. The last still of this game showed the wax floating over its pot, and there was none left to show the fix.
