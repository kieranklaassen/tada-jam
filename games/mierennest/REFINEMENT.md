## For the pull request

Written as the game is built and kept at the end of this file: the pull request is made from it.

### What changed from the child's design, and why

The game comes from a child's own design, passed on by the owner. It is kept recognisable as that child's game: an ant farm seen from the side, a small ant led by the finger that digs and carries, sand, mud and stone that each behave in their own way, a kingdom that grows, other ants, beetles and flies that want it, a catapult and a cannon, and a last army of dung beetles led by a dung fly. What differs, and why:

- **Levels are the kingdom's growth, seen in the nest.** The design has levels that make the kingdom bigger and stronger. Here the growth is chambers, workers, their things, the hill on the surface and the party that waits at the log, and never a number, a bar, a name or a badge: the jam shows no score and counts nothing at the child.
- **A raid is a test the child starts.** The design has attacks. Here the invaders camp in plain view and come only when the child taps the dewdrop bell; nothing attacks by a clock or while the game is away, and the same raid against the same nest plays the same way twice, so that changing one thing and ringing again shows what that thing did.
- **A raid runs on a copy, and failure is free.** What a raid knocks down the workers put back as the child built it. The child never rebuilds by hand what an invader broke, and the place that gave is marked until the next raid.
- **Defeated means turned back.** A hit buries, blinds, tumbles or gums an invader and it goes home; nobody is hurt or removed, the child's ant does not fight, and the kingdom cannot be lost.
- **Every raid ends by itself.** An invader that reaches a chamber does its own act there once (a nap, a seed eaten too slowly, a taste of its own foot, a ball polished, an army conducted that is not there) and goes home. One invader does its act in a chamber at a time and a later one of a kind does a short one, so that the longest raid is under 45 seconds and each act can be seen. A raid that the clock had to end with invaders still on their way is not judged and moves nothing; a test plays every party on 27 nests nobody planned, and none is cut off.
- **The machines are the child's, by name.** The catapult and the cannon arrive once each, stand where the child sets them, are loaded with a lump of the ground, and are fired by the finger in a raid. Sand, mud and stone are three different shots, and each kind of invader answers each in its own way.
- **Finished, and not over.** Turning back the dung beetle army is the end the design asks for. It is a scene that plays once (a leaf for the dung fly, a glow-worm in every chamber, the queen's egg set down, dusk); after it the kingdom is as it was left and a round of six parties can still be called.
- **Nothing is written.** No numeral, letter or sign is drawn, though the band would allow numerals: nothing in this play needs one. The name lives in the manifest.
- **Rules settled in the building**, each in the sheet: a chamber is a clear block four cells wide and three high that air reaches; a raid went well when no invader finished its act in a chamber, and the position only moves forward; a raider ant drops where it has nothing to hold; every kind climbs back out of the shaft a new nest starts with, and only a pit the child dug holds a beetle; a dung ball pushes 6 and leans on lumps under the mouth like on any wall; a lump is not shoved from under one of the party nor onto one.

### How the game meets the quality bar

As built, at the end of the run that answered the code review.

- **Alive at idle.** Every creature idles in a way of its own (`motion.ts`, one row a kind, held apart by a test); the bell sways; the worker on the hill mutters; the party at the log holds its want in plain sight; at the camp each kind that is not in the party is caught out by its own habit; one who waits at a door in a raid fidgets as its kind does at the camp.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** A touch on the ground is answered in the call the finger lands in: a mouthful, crumbs, a crunch whose pitch follows the finger's speed, and the ant turning to the finger and running. Each material answers the dig, the jaws, the carrying, a set-down, a push, a shot and a rolling stone in its own look and voice (the eighteen cells of the sheet's grid, each drawn and heard); a poke of any camper, the bell or the setting is answered at once, a camper by acting out its own habit. In a raid a touch on a loaded machine fires it at once, a touch on one with nothing in it yet gets a cough, and a touch on the ground brings the ant to look. Voices are played inside the touch handler. Nobody has heard them.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** Sand pours and slumps, mud holds, a stone drops and its landing is felt at the camp. A beetle leans and strains before a wall gives; a stone puts it on its back, rocking; a dung ball rolls as far as it turns, digs its way wider and jams; a machine recoils, smokes or twangs and its shot trails and leaves a puff or a splat; a wall that gave leaves a scuff and footprints until the next raid.
- **Kid-clear silhouettes and tappables.** The three materials are flat and far apart in lightness, each leaning its own way, held by a test, mud in a tunnel included; the kingdom's ants are told from the raiders by colour and outline; the child's ant wears the only blue in the nest; each kind of invader is one hue. A touch within 34 stage units of the bell rings it, and a test holds the bell clear of every camper and of the hill at its largest; every camper is found by a target of 48 or more; a machine is two cells square. The machines are a leaf green nothing else under the grass has, with the load seen at a glance. In a raid one invader is in a chamber at a time and no face is ever under anybody, held by a test on every party of the order.
- **Wordless clarity for 9 to 12.** No word, letter or numeral is drawn; the game has no `symbols.ts`. The wordless check passes. Each kind is shown once, large, when its party first sits at the log, at the first touch whatever it lands on, and every kind but the raider ant, which is the first party, is seen at its habit at the camp before it is met. No shape stands for a sign (the art guide lists how the ones that could be read as one are drawn).
- **Wordless idle guidance.** After six seconds of stillness (ten for a child of 10 or older) the ant looks out and scratches at the earth beside it, which glints, as does a lump in reach; once the kingdom has a chamber the bell sways and its drop is lit. Then a ghost hand makes one short drag through plain earth that touches nothing built. It waits twice as long each time and stops after three. The hand never rings the bell, sets a lump or loads a machine.
- **Nothing rated, counted or praised.** A raid that went well brings the next party to the log and nothing else; one that did not leaves the same party there. The position is shown nowhere. The ending is a scene in the world (a leaf for the dung fly, glow-worms in the chambers, the queen's egg set down, dusk) with no confetti, star, numeral or letter in any frame, held by a test, and it leaves two small marks.
- **Found as left.** The nest is saved as it changes, as it will come to rest; a lump in the jaws stays in its cell until it is set down; a raid runs on a copy, so the nest is as built the instant the game is put away, the raid is not judged, and the invaders are at their camp. A judged raid and a showing are saved the moment they start, and no scene plays on load. Played on the production build: a raid put away seven seconds in and the page loaded again. The ending's fields are saved when the great raid is judged: put away in that instant or in the put-back, the game is found with the kingdom open and plays no scene. A shot in the air over the nest lands at once when the game is put away, so no lump is ever lost.
- **Frame rate.** No frame rate has been measured, on any machine, and no physical iPad. The game's own work a frame on software drawing, production build, tier 0 pinned, pixel ratio 2, six times CPU throttle: 3.2 ms at the median and 6.7 ms at the ninety-fifth percentile idle and 7.7 ms and 18.6 ms digging fast without pause (both on the build before the code review); 4.3 ms and 11.0 ms in a raid of the full camp with both machines firing and 4.2 ms and 8.7 ms in the great raid (on the build after it, one run each). Digging and both raids are over the bar of 8 ms; with the canvas's pixel calls made to do nothing they are 4.7, 4.3 and 4.3 ms at the ninety-fifth percentile, so what is over the bar is software drawing on the processor. Nothing was measured on a graphics card. By tests each of the three fullest frames is under the budget of 80 draws; a frame repaints at most 24 cells of the ground.
- **Procedural assets only.** Everything is canvas paths and synthesized notes; no image, font or sound file, and no network request.

### What the next builder should know

- The stage is 40 by 21 cells of 28 units. That cell size is the only one at which the finger's mouthful (two cells) and a creature a tenth of the frame wide both hold.
- A raid is a pure simulation at twenty ticks a second on a copy of the ground (`raid.ts`), and the view only reads it. That one choice gives the put-away, the same raid twice and the nest as built for nothing; keep everything a raid does out of the view.
- Write a picture test for a ground rule by hand from the rule, then run it: several first pictures were wrong (the edge of a picture is rock; sand on a single post slides off; a stone bedded in earth is a lintel), and the rule was right.
- Simulate a scripted play against the rules before turning it into touches for a still, and print the ground it leaves: a dig a cell too deep made a pit that changed a whole raid.
- A saved nest for a still that has a machine in it needs a position at or past the machine's place, or the machine is dropped on load.
- On software drawing, measure once more with the pixel calls made to do nothing: it tells the game's work from the drawing's in one run.
- Play the rules on nests nobody planned, thousands of them, and ask only that every raid ends by itself: the code review did, and found invaders waiting for each other at a dip in a floor; the test that came of it found two beetles passing one lump of sand to and fro for as long as the raid ran. No picture drawn by hand had either.
- Play the raids from the game's own classes on nests a child would dig, not only on the test pictures: a room right under the shaft kept every raider ant out for four runs, and one lump in the mouth's left column kept everyone else out, and no test said so.
- Name every creature in the list of things to draw from the first day: the test that nobody is drawn twice is then a few lines, and it covers scenes nobody thought to look at.
- When a shot, a lump or a ball can be in two places for a moment (in the air, in the jaws, at the mouth), write the test that counts the kit at every frame before the rule: three of the last points were lumps counted twice or not at all.
- A rule that sounds like drawing ("no face under anybody") is cheapest as a rule of play plus one of drawing: here a door rule in the raid keeps the heaps from forming, and a duck in the view covers the moments two must pass. Either alone would not have held the test.
- A raid's chambers are the ones the kingdom had as it was called. The first dung ball that dug a roof made a new room round itself and did its act in it.
- A stone in a roof holds only between two firm neighbours: a ball that digs the earth beside it lets it fall. A test nest that is meant to jam a ball beds the stone in mud.
- `git fetch origin <branch>` in a shallow single-branch clone updates `FETCH_HEAD` and not the branch's own ref: name the ref in full, or the look for a new answers file reads an old tree.
- A subagent painting creatures needs the palette frozen before it starts, and no file it owns touched while it works.
- Run the cold playtest on the production build before the look passes, not after: it found three things in one minute that four strips of chosen moments had not.
- Look for an answers file on the base branch before the last push of a run, not only at its start.
