### The cold playtest proxy

Run twice by a script on the production build, with a fresh slot and no knowledge of the game but what the screen offers: ten seconds with the hands off, then a newcomer's minute that copies the ghost hand, pokes about, waits for the next glow, copies that, and watches. It is a proxy: a script and a reader of stills, not a child.

First run, before the fix sets of passes 3 and 4. In ten seconds: the camp is alive, the three trays glow at three seconds and at five a hand pulls a short way out of the first; nothing else asks for anything. Unclear moments:

1. The pin that marks where the fire went out lay under the cursor and could not be seen.
2. The scout read as a hat lying on the map.
3. The pencilled strip under the ruler was too small to read.
4. At dawn the cursor covered the handle that unfolds the ruler.
5. A tap on the amount card flipped it from 1 and 3 to 2 and 6, and nothing said why.

Second run, on the build of commit `06eb07d`, after which no drawing or rule has changed. In ten seconds the same, and the hand is copied without a miss: a short pull lays 12 logs with the count at the row's end. The glow then moves to the cursor and a hand pushes it; copied, the night glides, the fire goes out at the fourth hour, the pin stands above the cursor, the raccoons close on the ring, the row has shortened to nothing, and in the morning the scout stands at the rods while the card stamps 3 and 6 under the ruler. Afterwards the glow is on the logs' tray, the supply that ran short. Of the five, the first four are gone. Still unclear:

### How the game meets the quality bar

Each line says what is in place and what is not.

- **Alive at idle.** Every camper breathes at a tempo of its own and does its own act now and then, in whatever pose the night has put it (a page turned, a bobble twitched, a moustache curled or a spoon twirled, a feather swayed, ears wiggled; `look.test.ts` holds that each shows in every pose); the dog's tail, the frog's throat, the mule's ear and tail, glints on the stream, and at night the fire, the moths at each lit lantern and the eyes in the dark. A seeded director starts each act at moments of the character's own, never in step, and it all stops when the game is unattended or hidden. `motion.test.ts` fails on a shared rhythm or a near-copy curve.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** The answer starts in the call that lands the finger, before any frame. Each piece of a row has its own note and every piece is heard, however fast the row is pulled; a poke is seen as well as heard, in each camper's own way and in every pose; every cell of the sheet's grid, wrong uses included, has a show and a voice of its own, and no two fixed voices share their numbers (`game.test.ts`, `voices.test.ts`, `lookShows.test.ts`). No one has listened yet.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** A log pops out past its size and settles; a row let go settles in a wave that runs back to the pile; oil follows as a stiff band and water as a soft one that lags and sloshes; a heavy character answers a poke late and a light one at once; a thing held throws its shadow further.
- **Kid-clear.** Five campers told apart from above by hat and bag, upright or lying, and a hue split that does the work: everything that is kit is red, white, black or steel on a map that uses no red. The working pieces are single flat colours on plain rods. Everything that answers a touch is at least 48 pixels across at every size of surface, and the top right corner is left bare (`board.test.ts`). Not yet judged by the owner.
- **Wordless clarity for the declared age.** No letter and no written word is drawn. Numerals come only from `symbols.ts`, each on or beside the quantity it names: along the rods, at the end of a row at dusk, on the ruler's hours, on the amount cards, on the pencilled strip (the running total at each stamp, and the hours of a stamp that ends half way between two hours) and on the sled's bed. Two cards stamped along the one ruler are read hour under hour. The wordless check passes. Errors are consequences on the map: a fire that goes out, a pin where it did, ash that stops, the dark closing and raccoons coming exactly as far as it reaches, a camper doing what its taste makes it do.
- **Wordless guidance.** The whole ladder: after three idle seconds a violet halo breathes round the one thing a child would want next (the empty piles, then the cursor, after a short night the pile that ran short, after a night that held the fold's corner), and after five a ghost hand shows one move there and never a plan. It changes nothing and vanishes on any touch (`game.test.ts`). The cold playtest proxy copied it without a miss.
- **60 fps on a mid-range iPad.** Not measured. The builder's machine has no graphics card, and the frame rate is the lead's to take. As counted on a production build: 27 figure draws at dusk on the first site and 79 in the heaviest night looked at; 93 in the heaviest frame the counted test plays; one cached map layer and at night one film layer stamped once each, the pixel ratio capped at 2 and stepped down by the template's governor, no post pass. `frameBudget.test.ts` fails above 140 figures, at a third layer stamp, or if a long night draws more at its end than at its start.
- **Nothing passes through anything.** A canvas game, so by model tests: `overlap.test.ts` plays eight scripted nights and fails if two solid things overlap deeper than 14 design pixels or for longer than 300 ms; whoever walks is routed round tents, campers, lanterns and the fire (`paths.test.ts`), and the raccoons' lines keep clear of tents, campers and pins.
- **Procedural or committed assets only.** Everything is drawn at run time from seeded generators; every sound is synthesized; no asset is committed and the egress checks pass, on the source and on the build.
- **Its own art direction.** A printed survey sheet with contours, hill shading and fold creases, and the field kit lying on it. No other game in the jam is a map.
- **Found as left.** The plan, the cards and strips, the lanterns, the hidden position and what has been shown are saved through `ctx.storage` as they change and read back defensively. A night is a pure function of the plan and is never saved: put away in the middle of one, the camp is found at dusk with the plan as it was. A morning is saved as its scene starts and is found finished, with no scene replayed. Each of the five scenes has a test for what it saves at its start (`game.test.ts`). A put-away, or a press that the browser ends, does nothing the finger had not done: no dial turns, no card flips, no night starts and no sheet turns; and a put-away ends a night on its way and finishes a scene, so the camp is found as a load would find it.
- **No engagement mechanics.** Nothing is counted at the child. Two hidden numbers move only the hidden position, and the owner is asked about them above.

No physical iPad was measured, and no frame rate is claimed.

Gates run on the builder's machine at the end of the game run, of the closing run and of the finishing run: `npx tsc --noEmit`, `npx vitest run games/night-camp test/games.test.ts`, `npm run -s wordless:check`, `node scripts/egress-check.ts`, `npm run build`, `npm run egress:built`, `npm run education:built`. All passed. Not run: `npm run perf:jam` (no graphics card), the listening, the owner's checkpoints.

### The learning claim

### Defaults taken for the owner

Each default the game took in the owner's place.

- From the guide: no symbol stands alone; no letter and no written word, a unit abbreviation included; no reading on the object; no camera shake and no impact pause; no speech; the look is the lead's first reserved row.
- From the toy: every figure answers a poke; the first piece jumps out of an empty pile on touch-down; a pull past the end of a rod is followed by a heap that is never saved.
- From the sheet: the numerals on the strip and the cards are read as quantities laid, not readings; the night can be slid both ways by hand; a hidden count of nights feeds the judgement; every number range is the game's own choice, since no record sets one.
- From the game: the fold is pulled past half way, not tapped; a count is shown at the end of a row at dusk only; the kettle is not a target of its own; a lantern put on a taken pin goes back where it was; a camp put away in the middle of a night is found at dusk; the first visit of a child of 11 or older starts at `birchwood`, of anyone younger or of unknown age at `meadow`, and nothing is gated by age after that.

### What the next builder should know

What this build taught that the guide and the template do not say.

- **A test that plays the ladder finds bad numbers before a child does.** `world.test.ts` holds, for every site, that a plan exists that is tight, fits the sled and lies on the rods, that a sled never holds everything at its highest, and that the numbers stay inside the sheet's ranges. The first draft of the sites broke the sheet's own range on the amount cards, and only the range test said so.
- **When rules are written before the check, keep a list of where they had to be more exact than the sheet.** Writing `camp.ts` showed sentences of the sheet that could be read two ways. They go in the status block as exact replacements, and the sheet part is left alone while its check runs, so the hash the checker reads still matches; here they were pasted into the sheet in the closing run, with a round of their own.
- **What stays on screen after the plan changes has to be stored.** The first rules kept only the plan and worked the morning out again from it. The first check showed the hole: once the child changes the plan, the pins and the judgement belong to a night that is no longer in the save. Whatever outlives the thing it was computed from needs a field of its own.
- **A range the child can stretch has to be checked at its far end.** The ruler unfolds and the dials turn, so a test now supplies every site with the ruler fully unfolded and every dial at its highest. It cut the nights with two lanterns to eight hours.
- **A night that is a pure function of the plan needs no saved clock.** The cursor's place is the only time there is, so a put-away in the middle of a night loses nothing, the morning is worked out again on load, and the night plays backwards for free.
- **Give the model and the view one sentence for each number they share.** The frame said "0 to 1" for a poke; the model sent how far out the answer was and the view read how far through it was, and shaped it again with a curve of its own, so every camper would have moved alike and nothing failed. Each channel now says which of the three it is: how far through, how far out, or a strength that dies away.
- **Geometry in one pure module pays at once.** `board.ts` (first `stage.ts`) is what the view draws by and what a touch is read against, so a test can ask that every target is found at its own place, is 48 pixels across at five sizes of surface, and that the dog's way to a row crosses no tent.
- **Two workers can share a folder if one file is the contract.** The view was written by other workers against `frame.ts` and `board.ts` while the model was written against the same two files; they drew their stills by importing the modules into a page and painting hand-made frames, before the model existed. For the game the view was split into three files with one owner each (figures, kit, shows), and each fix set went to the owner of the file it touched.
- **Keep the saved outcome and the state on screen apart while a scene plays.** Packing up saves the next site at once, but the sheet has not turned yet. With one field for both, the old board met the new camp and the game crashed in the middle of the scene. The next camp now waits beside the one on screen until the beat that swaps them, and `saved()` returns the one that waits.
- **Write the overlap tests before the last look.** They found raccoons crossing lantern pins and a pocket between tents that no path could leave, neither of which showed in a still.
- **A row that the night uses up has to be seen to shrink.** The first night kept the laid row whole while the fire went out beside it, and the proxy child asked where the logs had gone. What the plan is and what is left of it now are two pictures; the rows show the second as soon as the cursor moves.
- **Have the folder read against the sheet by someone who did not build it, before the gates.** The lead's reader found seven promises unkept and the builder's own reader found more, after six passed rounds of the sheet and three logged passes. A sheet's check reads the sheet; nobody had read the code against it sentence by sentence.
- **Test the view with what the game really sends.** The figures drew a poke and an idle act only for a camper with no act, the view's tests gave it campers with no act, and the game never sends one: every test passed and no camper answered. And a number the model had already shaped was shaped again by the view after a rewrite, though a note in `frame.ts` said which it was. A test that paints frames taken from the running game, poked and idle in every pose, holds both.
- **A press that ends is not a tap.** `input.ts` says so and the Mount still sent it down the same path. Whatever a tap does must have a second way out that does nothing.
- **Count every card in a range test.** The tests that held the order and the ranges listed the fire's and the lanterns' cards and left the kettle's out, so the one card that broke both went unseen.
- **Check a chained command's last link.** Twice a commit went out with a red test because the chain ended in a filter whose own exit code was read. The tip was mended each time by the next commit, but a branch that must be green at every commit needs the test's exit code and nothing after it.
