# Design sheet

## The band and its age rule

Ages 4 to 8, for two people in one room with one tablet. The youngest age, four, governs.

- **The cue-table row.** The row for a youngest age of 3 to 4. Its "Avoid" column is a hard limit: no text, numeral or picture that has to be decoded, no spoken instruction, no verdict, never several activities live at once, no tool before it means something. While the curtain is shut there is one thing to do (make the twin); the sacks come only when the curtain is open.
- **The pack's rule for the range** (pack: game-design, ages-4-to-6.md). Pretend, characters who react, tap and drag, slapstick, no reading, no double tap, quantities to six.
- **Symbols.** The band starts below 6, so no word, letter, numeral or symbol is drawn and there is no `symbols.ts`.
- **What `ctx.childAge` sets.** Only where a first visit starts in the designed order: 4 and younger `a-few`, 5 `small-and-large`, 6 `bodies`, 7 and older `odd-places`. `null` starts at `a-few`. A saved place wins.
- **No microphone and no voice.** Talking happens between the two people. Nothing in the game hears it, waits for it or depends on it.

Two children share one tablet. The curtain is lifted only while a finger holds it, so the turn passes by itself: hold and look, let go and tell. The mole shows the other job: it covers its eyes for as long as the curtain is up. Nothing enforces it, and a child who looks loses nothing. A younger brother or sister needs a grown-up or an older child as the other person. One child alone plays it as remembering (look, let go, build) or with the ostrich, who peeks and mimes.

## The toy

A finger takes a plastic magnet off the shelf and slaps it on a monster. At the touch-down the magnet squashes and does its one act with its own voice: the eye goes round, the mouth chomps, the nose sneezes, the ear flaps, the hand claps, the foot stamps, the tail swishes. Dragged, it hangs from the finger and swings. Let go near one of the monster's six places it jumps the last bit and clacks on, and the whole body wobbles like jelly and every part already on it rings a little. A tap on a part that is on makes it pop from large to small or back, with a pop that is low for large and high for small. A tap on the body pops it into the next of the three shapes, and all its parts ride along. Sticking things on something that wobbles is a pleasure with no goal, and a monster with a foot on its head is funny before anybody has said a word.

In the first build of the toy there is the board, one body and the seven magnets.

What stands around answers as itself. The string twangs and a bump runs along it. The sacks, when they are there, hop and their lumps knock. The cord twitches and the curtain shivers at a tap, and works only when pulled. The small drawings along the top squeak and wobble. Bare board takes a squeaky marker line that fades to a smear in six seconds, and the wiper slides along the shelf with a rub and wipes the lines off at once.

On day 15 a child can make a monster that is hard to say (a small foot between two large mouths, on a tall body), know which question to ask first, and know which body will faint at what.

## The representation

The idea is that words carry what a thing is like to someone who cannot see it. In the objects: a monster is a body and six places, so that saying it needs what (the part), how large (two sizes, side by side they differ by nearly half) and where (on top, above, under, at the two sides, at the bottom, between). Speech between the two people is the representation itself. The pieces are plain: one plastic colour for each kind, no face, no pattern, no idle motion, on a white board. School practice without a trial behind this game: barrier games, in which one child describes and the other makes, are a common speaking exercise; the pack's tables hold no trial of one for this age, and the game claims none.

The comparison is in the objects too: the two monsters stand side by side, and the place where they differ is where they stare.

Alone, the ostrich's mime is a picture of the same three things at once: she points her wing at a place on herself, the part appears there as a marker outline, large or small, and acts with the voice of its magnet; she stretches for large and crouches for small. The outline squeaks on and is wiped off. A body she mimes by swelling round, stretching tall or sinking wide. A bump runs down the string and the mole pats the same place on itself. It is a game of looking and remembering, with no talk in it, and no record is claimed for it.

There is object and picture, and no symbol.

## The four mechanic questions

- **Swap.** No: if nothing had to be described, nothing would be left to do, since the one who builds has nothing else to go on.
- **Attention.** One looks at the monster and thinks which words will tell the other what is where; the other listens for what, how large and where, and asks for what is missing.
- **Fun.** The telling is the play, and the surprise it leads to is the meeting.
- **Guess.** Seven kinds in two sizes or nothing, in six places, on three bodies, is more than thirty million monsters, so random sticking makes a fine monster and hardly ever the same one. A child who peeks while building can copy; that is the younger child's way in.

## The error as a consequence

A twin that differs is not wrong: it is a stranger with the same house. At the meeting every place where either has a part is looked at in turn. Where they agree, both parts act together on one note. Where they differ in kind, in size or in being there at all, both monsters lean to that place, each part acts alone, the two notes rub, and the body shows how it takes it. Nothing is taken away. Afterwards both stand as they were, both can be changed, and a child who wants them alike can make them alike; a part made alike then is answered by both acting together on one note. What was misheard is in plain sight, at its place.

## The designed order, and what is stored

The order, as it stands in `config.ts`, is of the game's own monsters, one new thing at a time:

1. `a-few`: three parts of different kinds, all large, where a child expects them, on the round body.
2. `small-and-large`: three parts, at least one small and one large.
3. `bodies`: three or four parts, both sizes, on any body.
4. `odd-places`: four parts, at least two of them where nobody expects them.
5. `crowd`: five or six parts, kinds may come twice, anything anywhere.

Where a child expects them (the game's own choice): ear on top; eye above; mouth or nose under it; hand or ear at the sides; foot or tail at the bottom.

A harder one looks harder and is the child's pick: of the two sacks the small smooth one holds a monster of the stored place, and the large lumpy one, with a lump for each part, a monster of the next place (of the same place and six parts at the last). Either can always be picked.

A cycle is judged only when the monster was the game's own and something was stuck on the twin. Each place where either has a part, and the body, counts one; the same kind in the other size counts half. Three quarters or more moves the place one up, under a third one down, anything between leaves it. The place moves when the meeting starts, and the two sacks are filled then from the new place, so the one who waits is already of it. A monster the child hid moves nothing. Nothing shows the place.

Stored: the version; the monster behind the curtain and the twin (body, and for each of six places nothing or a kind and a size); whether the curtain is open; whether the hidden one was the child's own; the place; the two waiting monsters when the curtain is open; what the ostrich and the mole wear; the last three pairs as they met, drawn along the top; whether the first showing has been given; the number the next monster is drawn from. Nothing in the air or in the hand is stored: a magnet in the hand is stored where it came from, a lifted curtain as shut, a meeting as over, a sack in flight as arrived, the ostrich at rest. Marker lines, a ready magnet and the ostrich's next fact are short-lived and gone on load. No clock is read.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

- **The monster behind the curtain.** Wants to be looked at and is shy: the curtain bulges where it leans, and at its foot a toe of plain violet sticks out. Every body, on either side, takes a difference in its own way for ever. **Round** is in love with whatever is different, leans over and tries it on (the part flies across, sits on it, flies home). **Tall** is appalled, and tips over stiff and comes up again. **Wide** laughs until it slides.
- **The ostrich.** Wants to peek: her neck is always turned a little to the curtain. Loves noses, strokes one she wears. Cannot look away from a mouth.
- **The mole.** Wants to build: its free hand is always half out towards the shelf. Loves hands, shakes one it wears. Cannot bear an eye. It covers its own eyes for the curtain with great obedience.

Nobody looks at the child in disappointment, cheers, hurries or refers to leaving.

## The scenes

- **The first showing**, once ever, 1.5 s into the first visit if no finger has landed: the ostrich peeks, mimes one part, the bump runs down the string, the mole pats the place and reaches for the shelf. 5 s. The mark is stored when it starts. Any touch ends it. After it the idle ladder may show a move; it never mimes.
- **The mime**, an answer and not a scene, 3 s, caused by a tap on the ostrich. Another tap goes on to the next fact, from the top of the monster down and then round again. Everything else can be touched while it runs.
- **The meeting**, caused by pulling the cord while the curtain is shut. The rings zip, the ostrich ducks, the two lean in; every place where either has a part is compared for 0.7 s; then 2.4 s of how they take it: all alike, body included, they dance in step, each part acting; otherwise each body in its own way. 5 to 9 s. Stored when it starts: the open curtain, the place, the two sacks, the pair as the newest drawing (the fourth oldest is wiped, and shown to be). A touch while it plays lands it at its end and is answered as usual. Then it stays. The sacks come in and wait with their lumps turning, and nothing starts.
- **The next one**, caused by carrying a sack to the stage, or by its being let go past halfway there. The curtain runs shut, the sack hops behind it, the shapes bump the cloth, the empty sack flies off. The twin's parts jump home to the shelf in a patter, and its body pops to round. 1.8 s, all stored when it starts; a touch lands it. A sack that is tapped hops and knocks; let go short of halfway it hops back.
- **Hiding one's own**, caused by pulling the cord while the curtain is open: the curtain runs shut over the monster as it stands, the sacks slide off, and the twin's parts patter home as above. Stored when it starts; a touch lands it.

On load no scene replays.

## The records

### us-ca

Levels: `preschool-tk` for a child of 4 (age mapping official; the two printed bands overlap for the whole of age 4) and, for a child of 5, `kindergarten` beside it (age mapping official). Gap: none printed.

- `edu.us-ca.preschool-tk.reading-language.objective.language-and-literacy-development-foundational-language-development-strand-1-0-listening-and-speaking-1-7` (`us-ca 1.7`): department-published-foundation, confirmed. Taken in part: describing things to others. Explaining and giving an opinion are not taken.
  Limits taken: no topic, no length and no number of details is named. The number of parts, three to six, is the game's own choice.
- `edu.us-ca.preschool-tk.reading-language.objective.language-and-literacy-development-foundational-language-development-strand-1-0-listening-and-speaking-1-3` (`us-ca 1.3`): department-published-foundation, confirmed. Knowing and saying words for how large a thing is and where it is.
  Limits taken: the words in it are examples and no list; no measuring with numbers or units; both understanding and using are asked. Six places and two sizes are the game's own choice.
- `edu.us-ca.kindergarten.reading-language.objective.sl-k-3` (`us-ca SL.K.3`): state-board-adopted-standard, confirmed. Asking and answering questions to find something out and to make clear what was not understood. Taken in part: asking for help is not taken.
  Limits taken: it is about talk with other people and names no topic.

### nl

Level: `fase-1`, sub-band groep 1 for a child of 4 (age mapping convention). The `einde-po` lane is returned beside it, labelled as the end-of-primary goals, and nothing in it is named.

- `edu.nl.fase-1.reading-language.objective.inhoudskaart-nederlandse-taal-fase-1-mondelinge-taalvaardigheid-gesprekken-voeren-2` (`nl Gesprekken voeren / 2`): curriculum-institute-guidance, confirmed. Responding to each other, asking and answering questions.
  Limits taken: it names no kind of question, no topic and no group size, and says what a school offers, not what a child must be able to do.
- `edu.nl.fase-1.mathematics.objective.inhoudskaart-rekenen-wiskunde-fase-1-meten-meetkunde-meetkunde-orienteren-in-de-ruimte-2` (`nl Oriënteren in de ruimte / 2`): curriculum-institute-guidance, confirmed. Investigating and describing where objects are, relative to each other and to oneself.
  Limits taken: it names no position words and no number of objects. Taken in part: the places are places on a drawn board, relative to each other.

For describing how a thing looks, none of the `fase-1` records named here speaks, and no `nl` record is named. The nearest read, `nl Spreken / vloeiendheid, verstaanbaarheid en grammaticale beheersing / 5` of the `peuters` level, is about something from the child's own surroundings and is not used.

### Where the two differ

The `us-ca` records name size words and the `nl` records named here do not. At that point the game follows `us-ca`: sizes come in from the second place on. The `nl` record asks for places relative to oneself as well, which `us-ca` 1.3 does not name; nothing in the play depends on that difference.

### The claim

Monster by Telephone, played by two people who talk, is designed from two California preschool and transitional kindergarten foundations (1.7, the describing part, and 1.3) and, in part, one California kindergarten standard (SL.K.3), and from two pieces of SLO guidance for the Dutch fase 1 (asking and answering questions; and, in part, describing where objects are). Describing what a thing is like and size words are taken from California only. Every record was `confirmed` when it was looked up on 2026-11-24. The partner in these records is another person, and in this game it is the other person in the room; with one child and the ostrich, no record is claimed.
