- **For the template:** as generated, the Mount ends a touch that is still down at a put-away as if the finger had let go (`touch.clear()` gives a `dragEnd` or a `pressEnd`, and the Mount acts on it). - **For the template:** the overlay opens on three quick taps in a corner where nothing answers a touch. A child who drums there opens it and sees letters and digits. Here the corner must be held for a second first; that belongs in `overlay.ts`.
- **For the template:** a canvas game has to write its own stand-in for a 2D context before it can count a frame's draws or measure what overlaps what. - **For the template:** nothing in it draws a seeded stream of chance, and both a painter and a dealer of layouts need one. This game has `rng.ts`; if a second game writes the same, it belongs in the template.

### How the game meets the quality bar

- **Alive at idle.** Each of the four customers breathes and every few seconds does one small thing of its own, never the same twice running, and no bit of one is a bit of another (a test compares them). The tufts of a mane sway, each at its own pace, and left alone a wave runs through the whole mane now and then. The pair at the door blink behind its glass and, in an empty salon, look about and rock on their heels, turn about; they never knock or wave. Under the cape the customer looks from its lock to the friend's every few seconds and pats its own. Rain runs down the glass and somebody goes by in the street. The looking glass shows the customer's face as it changes. The three strips are working pieces and are still until touched. All of it stops while unattended or hidden.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** Every press is answered as it lands: hair is caught, or the scissors are in the hand. All thirty cells of the grid answer with their own notes (tests hold one voice a cell and that no two are alike) and with the motion the sheet gives them. With the cape off, each customer makes its own noise for what it does about its lock, its mane, a bow and what is on its face. A test plays hundreds of random touches through scenes and finds a sound for each. Nobody has heard the notes.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** A head squashes at a bump and springs back taller; the whole mane trembles at scissors and droops when a foot is stamped under a lock that is too long. A caught lock squashes; let go, it bounces and swings, more slowly the longer it is; two strips knock each other; a snipped stump twangs; a poked tuft boings and its neighbours ripple later and less; a head rides a soft, heavy spring; each customer walks in with its own gait and lands.
- **Kid-clear.** One big figure, one large flat cape, and on its cool blue the two strips of strong colour side by side with their tops level. The room behind is full and is soft wash throughout, lower in contrast than anything that can be touched; nothing new in it looks like a thing to pick up. White paper is left round every painted piece. The things that move the game on (the door, the knot, the empty seat, the chair) are large; the door and the knot give under the finger.
- **Wordless clarity for the declared age.** No text call anywhere, in the painting or in any frame of whole visits (tests hold both); the wordless check passes. The band starts at 4, so there is no `symbols.ts`. Nothing rates, counts or praises: a lock that is too long, too short or as long is acted out on the two ends and sounds as the two lengths.
- **Wordless guidance.** The idle ladder: with nobody under the cape, a breathing glow on the door, and on the chair as well when a finished pair stands by it; under the cape, a glow on the lock, a ghost hand that snips or pulls a tuft of the mane (the verb, not the answer), then the ghost hand on the cape's knot. It backs off and stops. Each of three moves is shown once by a character, on something that is not the problem in front of the child.
- **60 fps on a mid-range iPad.** Not measured, and not measurable on the machine the game was built on. - Hit-test where things are at rest and draw them where they are: a long tuft that flops over has to flop in the shared geometry (`poses.ts`), not only in the view, or the finger misses what it sees.
- A strip that hangs under a chin is a tie. Hair has to come out of hair.
- A check whose output goes through a filter loses its result. Run each check for its own exit code.
- A wash that is soft because it was drawn small and scaled up needs two stages each way; one stage shows as blocks at the edge.
- A fade in a canvas gradient must end in the same colour at no strength. Ending in transparent black leaves a dark ring.
- Transparent paint laid with `multiply` darkens over whatever is beneath it. Put bare paper back under a figure before painting it, and it keeps its colour and a white halo.
- A still cannot show two things passing through each other for a quarter of a second, or a customer painted whole in the frame the door opens. Play the game with a scripted finger at sixty frames a second into a context that keeps what was drawn, and measure. Here it found nine faults that the stills had not shown.
- A budget test is only as good as its seeds. Two seeds held 80 draws and a third made 81.
- Whatever a character does to something, give it a limb of its own to do it with, and do it where nobody stands in front. Scissors that float to a tuft behind the friend's head read as an attack on the friend.
- A demonstration that holds one thing beside another needs every other thing of that shape out of the way. The friend's own lock beside the ribbon and the tail made three strips of two.
- When a figure leaves, it leaves with what the child did to it.
- A fuller frame need not cost a draw. Everything that stands still goes into the one sheet that is stamped; whoever only waits is one sheet with its eyes on top; and a second view of a character (a looking glass) is its pieces laid together in one sheet, not painted again.
- Give the hair, or whatever the game is about, feelings of its own. A mane that trembles at the scissors says more than a face does.
- A touch that draws nothing out has to let go of what it touched, or the thing stays stiff for good and nothing says so.
- Have the folder read against the sheet by someone who has not seen the work before calling it done. The builder's own list of what is weak is not the list of what the sheet promises: here nine promises were off it.
- A put-away is not a let-go. What a lifted finger does, a surface taken away must not do.
- When a character's move in the sheet names a limb, draw the limb. A tilt of the head is not a paw.
- Chain the checks and the commit with `&&`, never with `;`. One commit on this branch (`4279730`) was pushed with a failing test of this game's own because of that; the next commit (`b6e99c4`) fixed it.
