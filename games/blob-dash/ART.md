# Design sheet

Written before any game code, and checked by someone who did not write it before the game is built on the toy. The headings stay in this order. The look follows the sheet at the end of this file.

What each heading asks for is in the section "The design sheet" of `docs/solutions/conventions/building-a-jam-game.md`.

Blob Dash has no learning goal. It comes from the lab demo of the same name, which lends its idea (a jump that lasts exactly one beat) and its feel in the hand, and none of its code. The demo's death and retry, its attempt count, its progress percentage and its rising tempo are gone: the course is a loop with no way to fail, and the child's own jumps write it.

## The band and its age rule

The manifest band is 8 to 12, and its youngest age, eight, governs the design.

- **The cue-table row.** The row for a youngest age of 7 and up in the wordless-clarity convention. Its "Avoid" column binds the game: no written word or letter, no symbol standing alone that play depends on reading, no timer, points or verdict chrome, no long hint chain. Several things may be live at once when each reads at a glance.
- **The pack's rule for the range** (pack: game-design, ages-9-to-12.md). A real system that behaves truly: the loop is a small rule language of six pieces and a spring, and what a piece does never changes. Any loop the child builds stands. Being wrong is large, funny and free: the blob flattens on a wall and peels off unharmed, and the loop never stops. The machine is left running and is worth showing to someone in the room. Nothing is babyish: the cast is dry, the tools look like tools, and nothing praises. No competition and no stored best.
- **The symbol rule.** The band starts at 6 or above, so numerals and mathematics signs would be allowed on or beside a quantity. The game draws none, and it has no `symbols.ts`: nothing in it is a quantity to be read. No letter, numeral or sign is drawn anywhere, and the stencilled paint forms none: a beat is marked by what stands on it and by nothing drawn on the line, a spring is a coil, shut eyes are arcs.
- **What `ctx.childAge` sets.** Only the tempo a first visit starts at (see "The designed order, and what is stored"). A saved tempo wins over the age. Age locks and hides nothing: the dial turns the whole way for every child.
- **What no age gives.** `null` starts a first visit at the slowest of the starting tempos, as for the youngest child.

## The toy

**The action.** Tap the wall and the blob jumps for exactly one beat. That is the touch the finger performs most, and it works anywhere on the wall that is not a control.

**In an empty scene.** A blob, a rounded square with a face, runs round the inside of a ring sprayed on a brick wall, sixteen beats to the lap, at a steady tempo, with nothing in its way. The ring's line is sprayed by hand in one pass and is not broken anywhere: a beat is marked by what stands on it, a spring or a piece, and by the line lighting there when the blob lands. Nothing asks for anything.

- **When the finger lands,** in that frame, a burst of spray paint spits onto the wall under the finger with a short hiss, and the blob squashes into a crouch. The burst stays on the wall and fades.
- **The launch is on the grid.** The loop has thirty-two places where a jump can start: the sixteen beats and the sixteen halves between them. A tap is taken for the nearest place it can still reach: up to three tenths of a beat early the blob holds its crouch and launches as it crosses the place; up to two tenths late it launches at once, as if from the place. The blob stretches off the line with a boing in the note of that place, turns half over in the air, and lands flat exactly one beat later with a thump, a squash and a ring of dust.
- **A spring is left** where it took off: a coil that stands on the line. On every later lap the blob jumps there without being asked, with the same boing, and the child taps over the top of what is already laid. A tap that lands on a spring already there gives that jump an accent for this lap: a brighter boing and a bigger stretch.
- **On the beat and off it are two different jumps, and neither is wrong.** A jump from a beat lands on the next beat, where the thump and the blob's own footfall fall together as one fat sound, and the line under it lights. Its spring is black and upright. A jump from a half lands halfway between two beats with a light scuff. Its spring is the second colour, shorter, and leans forward, and its boing is an octave up and clipped. A jump off the beat costs nothing.
- **A tap while the blob is in the air** cannot start a jump, and still gets an answer: the hiss and the burst under the finger, and the blob kicks an extra half turn with a whip of air. It leaves no spring. A tap in the last three tenths before a landing is held, and the blob bounces straight off the place it lands on, so tapping in time hops it round the ring. A blob thrown by a flip is in the air too, and a tap kicks it or is held in the same way. A blob that is flat on a wall, on the rim of a mouth it came down on, or changing sides inside a pipe cannot leave the line: the tap gets the hiss and the burst under the finger, and the blob stays as it is.
- **The lap plays what was laid.** With sixteen notes round the ring, the springs a child has left are a tune that comes round again, and each tap adds to it.
- **Two controls stand off the ring.** The springs' release is a pull cord: pulled down past its catch and let go, every spring comes off the wall ("The scenes"); let go short of the catch, it snaps back with a tock and nothing is cleared; tapped, it swings with a clink. The tempo dial turns under a finger with a ratchet click for every step, and the loop speeds or slows at once; nothing else ever changes the tempo. The release, the dial and the rack stand clear of the ring and of each other, and nothing a finger must reach (the cord's grip, the dial, the rack, any beat of the track on either surface) lies under the jam's home control, a round 48 px control at the top centre, 10 px from the top. A spring and a piece on the same beat are drawn so that neither covers the other, and a finger on the piece lifts the piece. An overlap test holds all of this at each surface size.
- **The cast watches.** Three large stencilled animals, a camera and a neighbour at a lit window are on the wall round the ring from the first frame. They keep time each in its own way, and each answers a poke as itself ("The characters and their fixed tastes"). A tap on one of them is still a tap on the wall: the blob jumps as well.

**Why repeating it is a pleasure with no goal.** Every tap is answered when the finger lands, and the answer is bigger than the touch: a hiss and a mark on the wall, a crouch, a boing, a turn in the air, a thump, and a spring that goes on jumping for the child on every lap after (pack: game-design, touch-answers-bigger-than-the-touch.md). Random tapping lays a rhythm that is always in time and in tune, since every place has a note of one five-note scale, and nothing a finger can do is punished or refused. The simplest use, one tap, always works (pack: game-design, toy-first.md). The pleasure of the demo is kept: the jump is one beat long at every tempo, so a tap in time lands in time, and the child feels it land.

## The object-by-action grid, and what is new on day 15

Six kinds of piece are dragged from a rack that never runs out onto the track, on the floor (the ring's outer line), one to a beat, or the ceiling (the rim of the hub), one to every second beat, since a beat there is shorter than a finger is wide. Each acts and sounds when a blob meets it. Three of them are switches that the same piece switches back: a flip sends the blob to the other surface, a splitter makes one blob two or two blobs one, and a tunnel mouth hushes the blob until the next mouth. So a piece can be met in five ways, which are the columns. Every cell looks and sounds different, and no row and no column has one sound across its cells.

| Piece | Run into on the floor | Landed on from a jump | Met on the ceiling | Met by twins | Met while hushed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drum pad | It sinks under the blob with a low doom, and a ring of dust jumps off the wall | A cracking tak; the pad kicks back, and flecks of paint spit sideways | It hangs and swings, with a hollow tock like a knock on a door | A flam, da-doom: the pad is still wobbling from the first twin when the second lands | A thud felt more than heard, mmf, and the pipe bulges where the pad is |
| Chime step | The blob trips up the step with a ting in the note of that beat, and the step shivers | It rings on, tiiing, an octave up, and hums until the next beat | The bar hangs like one key of a glockenspiel and gives a low tung, an octave down, swaying | Two notes a third apart, ti-ting, and the bar swings both ways | A dull tk with no ring, like a spoon on a mug held in the hand |
| Flip | It scoops the blob up with a rising whoop; the blob turns over in the air and lands on the ceiling one beat on | A harder throw, whee-bap: the blob comes off too fast, hits the far side flat, sticks a moment and runs on | A falling woop, and it drops back to the floor feet first | Whoop, whoop, in canon: for a third of a beat one twin is up and one is down, and the pigeon's eyes go one each way | A breath, hff, and the lump in the pipe changes sides, the pipe with it |
| Splitter | It tears the blob in two with a zip; a twin in the second colour peels off and runs a third of a beat behind, and everything they meet sounds twice, the second time a third higher | The blob bursts on it like a dropped egg with a splot, and two blobs wobble out, the twin on its head until it rights itself | The zip runs downward, and the twin hangs from the ceiling by a string of paint before it catches up, with a plip | Met by two, it zips them back into one with a shlup, and the blob is fatter for a beat | A zip with its teeth out, fft, and the lump in the pipe becomes two lumps. Two lumps that meet it there become one again: the twin stays a lump in the pipe until it is drawn in, the fft is the same, the shlup is heard through the pipe's wall, low and soft, and the one lump is fatter for a beat |
| Tunnel mouth | The blob dives in with a gulp; a pipe is painted on from here to the next mouth, the blob travels it as a lump, and whatever it meets inside is hushed | It comes down into the mouth from above like a coin into a slot: a clink, then the gulp | The mouth hangs open downward and swallows upward with a slurp | Gulp, gulp, and two lumps go down the pipe a third of a beat apart | Reached from inside, it is the way out: the blob is spat out with a pop and a puff of grey, loud again |
| Wall | The blob runs flat into it with a wet slap, spreads like a pancake, peels off from the top with a squelch and is back in step by the next beat | It comes down on the wall's top edge and folds over it like a towel with a flump, then slides off the far side | A slap upside down; it peels off by its feet and hangs a moment, and one drip falls the wrong way with a plink | Slap, slap: the second twin flattens onto the back of the first, and they peel off one after the other with two squelches | A soft bump, bmp, and the pipe gets a dent that pops back out |

**The spring** is not on the rack: the child's own jumps lay it, as "The toy" says, on the floor or, after a flip, on the ceiling. A hushed blob's spring gives a boing with a hand over it, and twins take the same spring one after the other.

**The wrong use of each thing works and is funny.**

- A piece let go off the track, on the wall away from the rack, sticks to the wall where it was dropped, slides down it leaving a smear, and clonks back into the rack. A piece let go over the rack is put back: it settles into its own place at once with a small knock of its own, and nothing sticks or smears. A piece dropped on a beat that already holds one takes its place, and the old one pops out and back to the rack with a pok.
- A piece dropped on one of the cast is dealt with in that animal's manner and then goes back to the rack: the pigeon wears it as a hat until its next bob, the rat sniffs it and flicks it home with its tail, the cat bats it off the ledge without opening an eye.
- A wall set half a beat after a spring on a half is hit from the air: a bigger splat, and the blob slides down the wall's face before it peels. A flip on the floor one beat before a place of the ceiling, and one in that place, play ping-pong with the blob. A splitter alone on the track is met again a lap later by the twins it made and zips them back into one, so the loop plays single and in canon lap about, which is a figure and not a fault. Twins run for good only when a flip has taken them to the other surface from their splitter.
- A spring that a longer jump now flies over does not fire. It ducks as the blob passes above it, and the blob's faint trail shows the arc going clear over, so the child can see why that note has gone quiet.
- The dial turned to its fastest with a wall on the track leaves a print of the flattened blob on the wall, which fades by the next lap.
- The release pulled with no spring on the wall clacks its catch, and the cat's ear turns to it.
- A mouth lifted off the track while the blob is in its pipe leaves the pipe without an end: the blob is spat out where it is, onto the line, with the pop and the puff of grey, loud again, and the pipe fades off the wall. A lump that is changing sides at that moment is spat out in the same frame, where it is between the two lines, and flies on to the far one as a thrown blob. Any other piece lifted or set down while the blob is hushed changes the pipe ahead of the lump in that frame: the part it loses fades off the wall, and the part behind the lump stays as it was painted. So a lump is never outside a pipe, and a twin that is being drawn into a lump is a lump in the pipe too.

**On day 15** the child is composing: six pieces by sixteen beats of floor and eight places of ceiling, in layers of their own laps, at a tempo they chose, with a machine worth showing to someone in the room. They know that a jump from a half makes the next beat a rest, that a splitter turns one note into a figure of two, and what each of the cast makes of each sound. None of that is new content: it is the same seven things in new orders. What does not change is honest too: there is one wall, one ring and one cast, and a child who wants a new place will not find one (pack: game-design, depth-from-combinations.md; pack: game-design, liveliness-from-causing-and-comedy.md).

## The error as a consequence

Nothing in the game can be done wrongly, and nothing gives a verdict. What a child can get is a loop that does not sound as they meant, and the world shows where and why.

- **Where.** Every sound has a place on the ring and a thing standing there. A spring that no longer fires ducks under the arc that flies over it. A piece no blob meets stays still and silent while the blob's trail passes clear of it. A jump from a half lands visibly halfway between two beats.
- **Why.** The blob leaves a faint trail of its last positions, so the path of the last half beat can always be read off the wall.
- **The state stays.** The loop keeps running and nothing is taken away. The child changes one thing (a tap one place earlier, a piece moved one beat on, the dial a little slower) and hears the difference on the next lap.
- **The crash.** Running into the wall piece is the largest thing that can go "wrong", and it is a joke that costs nothing: the blob flattens, peels off unharmed and is back in step by the next beat. The loop never stops and never restarts.

## The designed order, and what is stored

**The order.** Blob Dash is open play with one place in its designed order, stored as `loop` in `LADDER`. There is no order of challenges and no cycle to judge, so the position never moves and nothing is easier or harder by the game's choice. What is harder is something the child picks in the world: more pieces, a faster dial.

**What a first visit starts with, by age.** Only the tempo, and it is a default and never a limit:

| `ctx.childAge` | Tempo of a first visit |
| --- | --- |
| 8 and younger, or `null` | 90 beats a minute |
| 9 or 10 | 100 beats a minute |
| 11 and older | 110 beats a minute |

A first visit of any age opens on an empty ring with the blob running and the cast keeping time.

**What play changes.** The child's taps lay springs, their drags put pieces on the track and take them off, their pull on the release clears the springs, and their turn of the dial sets the tempo between 60 and 160 beats a minute. Nothing else changes any of it: not the age, not a clock, not the number of visits.

**The saved state.** Plain JSON, versioned, read field by field, a damaged field taking its default.

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `v` | The version of the record. |
| `position` | Always `loop`, the one place in the order. |
| `finished` | Always `false`: open play has no cycle that ends. |
| `tempo` | Beats a minute, a whole number from 60 to 160. A first visit takes it from the table above. |
| `springs` | The places that hold a spring, as whole numbers in ascending order with none twice: 0 to 31 are the thirty-two places of the floor in loop order (even numbers are beats, odd numbers are halves), and 32 to 63 the same places on the ceiling. |
| `pieces` | The pieces on the track, as a list of records `{ kind, beat, ceiling }`: `kind` one of `drum`, `chime`, `flip`, `splitter`, `tunnel`, `wall`; `beat` 0 to 15; `ceiling` true or false. At most one for each beat of the floor and one for each even beat of the ceiling, so at most twenty-four; a record on an odd beat of the ceiling is left out when the state is read. |

- Where the blob is in its lap, whether it is in the air, on the ceiling, split or hushed, and every pose of the cast are views of the running loop and are not saved. The spray bursts, the trail, the smear a dropped piece leaves, the print on a wall and a loose feather are short-lived, are not saved and are gone on load. On load the blob starts from the first beat, on the floor, single and loud, and the loop plays from there as the pieces on the track make it. A blob that was on the ceiling, split or hushed because of a piece since taken off the track is not found so: what was laid on the other surface is still on the wall, and is silent until a flip is set again.
- A spring or a piece is saved when it is laid, at the throttle; a clear and a let-go of the dial are saved at once. A piece in the hand is saved where it came from. A put-away under a finger that is turning the dial puts the tempo back to what it was when the finger landed, and one under a finger that holds the release pulls nothing.
- The loop runs on attended game time only. It plays only while the game is attended and visible, and no clock is read.
- A largest legal state (sixty-four springs and twenty-four pieces) is far under half of the 64 KB cap, and a test says so.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

The cast is painted on the wall round the ring, large, and is there from the first frame. Their feelings are about the sounds and the blob, never about the child. Each keeps time in a way no other does, and each has one want that can always be seen.

- **The pigeon**, in the hub of the ring. Its want is the beat: its head thrusts forward on every beat, and its eyes follow the blob round. It likes a landing on the beat (its chest puffs and it coos) and the drum pad (it struts on the spot). It cannot stand the wall's slap (it ducks, and one feather comes loose and drifts down) or the dial at its fastest (it goes cross-eyed trying to follow, and has to shake its head straight).
- **The rat**, standing at the left with headphones on and a spray can in its paw. Its want is the off-beat: its foot taps on the halves, never on the beats. It likes a jump from a half (it grins and shakes the can, which rattles) and the splitter (a double-take at the twins, then a slow nod). It cannot stand the hush of a tunnel: each time a mouth swallows the blob it lifts one cup of the headphones off its ear and frowns into it. The frown is at the blob going quiet and never at a finger: setting a mouth on the track draws none.
- **The cat**, lying along a ledge at the right with its tail hanging down. Its want is quiet: its eyes are nearly shut and one ear is turned to the ring, and its tail swings one way on each beat like a pendulum. It likes the chime step and anything hushed (a slow blink, and the tip of the tail curls). It cannot stand the drum pad, the slap or the clatter of a clear-out (its fur stands on end along its back, then settles hair by hair).
- **The camera**, on its bracket at the top left, has no taste. It turns to keep the blob in view, and nods when the blob lands.
- **The neighbour**, at the lit window at the top right, has no taste either. It sways once across in four beats, sips from its mug and leans out now and then, and when poked it ducks behind the sill and draws the curtain.

A poke is answered by each as itself: the pigeon flaps and resettles with a ruffle, the rat starts and pushes its headphones back on with a squeak of foam, the cat swipes a lazy paw with a mrrp, and the camera swings round to look at the finger with a whirr and then back to the blob.

## The scenes

Open play has no ending. The loop runs for as long as the child attends, a finished machine stays as long as the child likes, and nothing new starts by itself. There are two short scenes, and each gives way to any touch.

**The clear-out.** *Cause:* the release is pulled past its catch and let go. *Beats:* the catch clacks; the springs come off the wall one after another round the ring, starting from the blob's place, each with its own boing a last time, and drop down the wall and out of the frame; the cat's fur stands; the last spring gone, the blob runs on over a bare ring. It lasts up to four seconds, by how many springs there were. *Saved when it starts:* `springs`, as an empty list. *Filled in from play:* which springs lay on the wall, so the scene plays the child's own tune once through as it takes it down. *A touch:* the springs still on the wall drop at once without a sound, and the touch is then an ordinary touch.

**The pigeon's lap.** A secret that is never hinted at and works every time. *Cause:* the blob finishes a lap in which it left the ground from every one of the sixteen beats. *Beats:* the pigeon stops bobbing and stares; it takes off with a clatter of wings and flies once round the ring ahead of the blob at more than twice its speed; it lands back in the hub out of breath, chest heaving, and takes up the beat again. It lasts half a lap, which is between three and eight seconds by the tempo. *Saved when it starts:* nothing, since it changes no field. *Filled in from play:* the tempo, and the springs it flies over, which duck. *A touch:* the pigeon drops straight back to its perch, and the touch is then an ordinary touch.

**How play ends and starts again.** It does not end. The calm way to tidy up is the release, which clears the springs; pieces go back to the rack by hand. Put away at any instant and opened again, the ring is found as built, at the tempo it was left at, with the blob starting its lap, and no scene replays.

## The look

Written after the style spike, not part of the sheet. The look in use is the first one reserved for the game: **spray stencil on brick**. The lead passed it after Run 1 (the wall is full, and the rat, the pigeon and the cat are large and funny); the owner has not seen it yet. The second reserved look has not been tried.

**The place.** A brick wall at the back of a yard, seen square on, with a strip of pavement at its foot. The wall fills the frame from edge to edge and goes on past the stage on a surface of another shape: brick in seven near shades with dark mortar, a band of old blue-grey paint along the bottom third, a drainpipe down the left edge, older paint under everything (two mustard swaths, three faded leaves, and by the camera a burst that ran and the dustings of two cans that were nearly empty), weeds and a puddle on the pavement, and a soft darkening into the corners. In the middle someone has rolled a patch of pale primer in overlapping roller widths, and the course is sprayed on it. At the top right an arched window is lit from inside, and a neighbour in a red hat stands in it with a mug.

**The course.** One line sprayed by hand in a single pass round the patch, not through a stencil: it is fatter where the hand slowed, it has drips under its lower half and specks thrown past it, and it is not broken anywhere. The blob runs on its inside. The second surface, the ceiling, is the rim of a dark round niche in the middle of the patch, where the pigeon lives. The niche is 112 units in radius and the ring 312, so the corridor between the two lines is 200 units: a blob on one of them, at the top of its jump, clears a piece standing on a pressed spring on the other. Nothing marks a beat but what stands on it: a spring, a piece, the place the line lights when a blob lands there.

**The one rule of the look.** Everything else is paint that came through a cut stencil, in flat layers, at most three colours to a figure over a white undercoat. So every shape has the marks of the method and nothing else: a soft edge of overspray round each layer, a run or a drip under a heavy one, specks thrown past the edge, a slight slip between two layers so a sliver of the undercoat shows, and bridges, which are the gaps a stencil must leave so that its middle does not fall out. No figure has an outline drawn with a pen, a gradient that shades its form or a cast shadow. What is not a stencilled figure is drawn otherwise, and that is all of it: the ledge, the sill and the rack's plank stand out from the wall and each has a band of shade under it, painted into the wall once; the niche is dark towards its middle; soft things are soft (the glow, a burst under a finger, dust, a smear); the ghost hand is not on the wall at all, and is white with a soft dark edge so that it is not taken for one of the figures; and the light is the wall's own darkening at the corners, the lamp in the window and one warm glow sprite.

**The hero.** The blob is a rounded square 96 units across on a stage 820 high, the brightest thing on the wall, with a face for every pose: running, a wide grin with one tooth and eyes that look the way it goes; crouched, eyes squeezed to arcs; in the air, mouth wide open; flat on something, a wobbly mouth with its tongue out and eyes looking two ways. The face turns with the body, so on the top half of the ring it is upside down, and on the ceiling it is mirrored so that it still faces the way it runs. Off the line it is drawn stretched, taller and narrower by what the launch gave it, further for an accent, and round again by the top of the jump. Flat from the harder throw of a flip it wears the flat face, and sticks where it hit: it is held flat for a fifth of a beat, comes away slowly and then fast, and is at its place in the loop again by the next beat, while its sounds stay where the loop has them (`pieces.test.ts`). A twin is the same stencil in the second colour, printed a little behind the first.

**The pieces and the spring.** A spring is one coiled wire seen from the side, a zigzag of uneven turns with round elbows: black and upright on a beat, teal, shorter and leaning the way the blob runs on a half. It stretches when it fires, flattens when something flies over it, and is pressed flat under a piece or a foot. The six pieces are tools, each with a silhouette of its own: a drum pad (a gold skin on a dark shell with two splayed feet), a chime step (a stair of two treads with a teal bar), a flip (a gold ramp, a solid wedge low where the blob runs onto it, with one shallow curve from its tip to its top), a splitter (a pale curved blade on a block), a tunnel mouth (the end of a grey pipe seen at an angle: one barrel, a lip that bulges to the front, and one dark inside set back from it) and a wall (a stub of brickwork that steps down behind, with what came off it lying on the beat as grit and one shard: a scatter, never a bar, so the stub has no foot at any place of either line). A low piece is 62 units wide and 41 tall, a spring 46 by 42, and a wall's stub 52 tall. On the floor a piece stands on any beat. On the ceiling it stands on every second beat, so that its place there is 88 units long, which is 61 px on the narrowest of the three surface sizes, and a finger on one never lifts its neighbour (`layout.test.ts`). On the rack and in the hand a piece is drawn three tenths larger than on the line. On the rack each piece stands with the middle of its drawn shape on the middle of its place, so the wall, whose stub is ahead of its foot, stands whole on the plank, which runs 56 units past the middle of the first and the last place. On the ceiling a piece is the mirror of itself on the floor, so that it faces the blob that runs at it; the flip alone is not mirrored but turned round with the rim, so it is a wedge at every place of the niche and at none of them a foot with an upright. The wall is mirrored like the rest, and has to be: its stub stands 40 units ahead of its beat with its flat face towards the blob, and turned round instead it would stand behind the beat with its stepped back to the blob. A pipe is a pale grey body the lump shows through, with a harder line for its roof, 56 units off the surface.

**No mark forms a letter, a numeral or a sign.** A stencil look leans towards lettering, so every figure was read for it at the stills' size, in both runs and again by a fresh reader, and what was redrawn is listed under "Reader" in `REFINEMENT.md` with the lookalikes that stay and why each is weak. The rules that came out of it: no ring is broken into equal arcs and no row of equal marks stands at even steps; nothing is two or three level strokes standing by themselves (the two mortar lines of the wall's stub are cuts inside one solid shape, of two lengths); two or three strokes that belong together, the whiskers of a cheek or a snout and the feathers of a wing, are curved, of unlike lengths and slants, and leave roots that are apart, so they are neither a pair of bars nor an arrowhead; no thin upright stands on a thin foot, a ramp's inside corner is filled until it is a wedge, and what lies at the foot of an upright is a scatter and not a bar; a round thing has no second ring inside it unless it is a wheel one turns, so a lens is one oval with a glint to one side, the inside of an ear is a half shape with a flat edge, and an open mouth is one shape with its undercoat slipped to one side and a tongue to the other; a run of paint never ends in a separate dot; old paint is never one wavy stroke by itself; and a shell is a solid shape with a band, never a bare spiral.

**Palette.** Seven cans and the wall.

**How it is drawn.** The wall is painted once, when the surface is sized, into one canvas, and every figure is sprayed once into its own sprite, in parts where it has joints. A second canvas, the set, is a copy of the wall with every piece that is at rest stamped on it, the rack with them. The springs at rest are on a clear layer of their own, the size of the ring, and there are two such layers. A frame stamps the set and the springs' layer whole, then only what moves: the blobs, the cast, the controls, a spring that has just fired, a piece that has just been met, a lump in a pipe (drawn before the pieces it passes, which are stamped again over it, and a twin that is being drawn into a lump with it), and what a touch leaves. When a spring or a piece starts or stops moving, its patch is put right (a spring's is wiped on its layer; a piece's has the wall copied back and whatever at rest reaches into it stamped again), two patches at most in a frame, and a thing that waits its turn is drawn live meanwhile exactly as it is. So a full ring costs a frame what an empty one does. In a clear-out every spring that has come off the wall is stamped in every frame until it is under the bottom edge of the surface, however many there are; they come off a twentieth of a second apart and fall fast, so a dozen or so are in the air at once. A spring falls harder on a surface that goes on below the stage, by as much as the way down is longer, so a clear-out is as long and as full on a standing tablet as on a lying one; a spring is forgotten in the step after it was drawn under the edge. A piece that stood on a spring is laid again on the line in the frame its spring comes off. When a touch drops the rest, their layer falls whole as one stamp and the other layer takes the springs laid after. Nothing is sprayed in a frame. There is one draw count, the one the overlay and the report give: stamps, plus live strokes and fills, plus draws into the layers. `frameBudget.test.ts` holds it under 80 in the fullest frames it can reach, at 1180 by 820, 1024 by 768 and 820 by 1180, and the worst frame found is 77 at each: twins out of their pipe and in the air over a ring of springs with a piece in the hand, and on a standing tablet a clear-out of a full ring with all six pieces out. Each case asserts that it reached the state it is named for.

**Motion rules.**

- Paint does not bend, so nothing morphs. A figure moves by its cut parts turning on their joints, by the whole stencil shifting, and by squash and stretch of a whole sprite.
- What paint does leave is a ghost. The blob leaves faint copies of itself on the wall behind it, which fade, so a jump's arc can be read after it has happened. In a pipe, on a wall or thrown by a flip it leaves none, and the last ones fade. A ghost is drawn where the blob was, on top of the piece it was rolling or flying over, so the trail never prints through a piece (`body.test.ts`).
- Every touch leaves a burst of spray under the finger, in a colour of the palette, which fades off the wall. At most eight such short-lived marks are on the wall at once, and the oldest gives way. A spring that is falling is not one of them and is never dropped to make room.
- A landing knocks a ring of dust off the wall, and on a beat the line lights under it.
- The blob never goes through the line, the rim of the niche, a piece or a wall: it rolls up and over a low piece, winces before a wall and meets it with its front, and presses a spring as it comes over it. A jump that ends on a wall comes down the way the wall will be met: over the top when the blob is alone on the floor, in front of the wall's face on the ceiling and as twins. A blob thrown by a flip flies to where it will stand on the far side, which is on top of whatever stands there: on a pad, a step or a blade, over the top of a wall of the floor, in front of the stub of a wall of the ceiling. A ramp there throws it again and a mouth swallows it. `body.test.ts` holds each of these to two and a half units, in every step of every throw.
- Two that meet a splitter are zipped into one: the twin is drawn into the blob from where and as it was drawn, smaller as it goes. Inside a pipe it is a lump the size of its lump or smaller, between the pipe's walls, under the pipe and behind the piece it passes, for as long as the blob is in the pipe; the shlup that ends it there is low, soft and short, heard through the pipe's wall, and the one lump is fatter for a beat along the pipe (`pieces.test.ts`, `voices.test.ts`, `frameBudget.test.ts`).
- A twin's sound is the blob's a third higher, and what is noise in it (a breath, a zip, a slap) is a fifth softer and three tenths shorter as well, so two are heard as two in every cell (`voices.test.ts`).
- A meeting moves as the cell it sounds as. The five ways a piece can be met are tried in one order (hushed, twins, ceiling, landed, run into), and the sound, the piece's motion and the blob's are all taken from the one that holds (`pieces.test.ts`).
- A tap in the air kicks the blob an extra half turn every time, in every part of the jump and however many there have been. Thrown by a flip the blob is in the air too: a tap kicks it, and one in the last three tenths of the throw is held for the place it lands on. Flat on a wall, on the rim of a mouth or changing sides inside a pipe it cannot leave the line: the tap is the hiss and the burst, and nothing is set on the blob that is not drawn (`toy.test.ts`). A lump running in its pipe takes a tap as a bump with the hushed boing, and the spring is laid (`pieces.test.ts`).
- Each of the cast keeps time on a curve of its own (the pigeon's head snaps out on the beat and draws back; the rat's toe lifts into each half and slaps down on it; the cat's tail takes two beats to swing over and back; the neighbour sways once across in four beats; the camera does not keep time and pans after the blob), blinks on its own period, and has its own idle habits. A test holds the curves apart, and another that every number of every pose is read by the drawing.
- Sight and sound are one clock. A sound is raised by the loop in the step its motion starts, and no voice holds a delay. The harder throw of a flip is half a beat long, so that its bap, its dust and the flat body come in one step, on the half; every other throw is one beat. A piece that flies home to the rack sounds in the frame it lands there, the small knock of one put back as much as the clonk (`sync.test.ts`).

**Rules completed where the sheet is silent.**

- The rack never runs out: a piece taken from it leaves its like behind, so the same kind can stand in many places (sixteen on the floor and eight on the ceiling).
- A piece is taken by a finger on it as drawn or within 16 units of that; of two within reach the one the finger is nearer is taken. On the rack it is the same with 12 units: a finger on any drawn part of a piece there lifts that piece, the wall's stub and grit included, and never jumps the blob (`layout.test.ts`, and `play.test.ts` through the path the Mount uses). A wall is one thing from the back of its grit to the back of its stub. A piece let go over the ceiling goes to the nearest of its eight places.
- A piece let go over the rack is put back: over the plank, from the top of the tallest piece on it down to its bricks, or within reach of any piece there, it flies to its own place in about a seventh of a second and knocks as it lands, a small knock of wood of its own, and nothing sticks, slides or smears. The joke of a piece let go off the track is for the wall away from the rack. The track's margin outside the ring reaches down to the tops of the rack's flip and splitter, and there the rack wins: where a finger would take a piece of the rack, a piece let go is over the rack, and no beat is marked for it (`pieces.test.ts`, `layout.test.ts`).
- A pipe is from a mouth to a mouth. The pipe a lump is in is laid again in the frame the track changes: when a piece is lifted, set down, set back, tapped or let go by a finger that is taken away. What is behind the lump stays as it was painted; what is ahead is what the track now makes; and the part that is taken away fades off the wall in half a second, once when twins share the pipe. When either mouth of that pipe is lifted, the pipe has lost an end: the blob is spat out where it is, onto the line, with a pop and a puff of grey, loud again, on top of whatever stands there, and the pipe fades off the wall. A lump that is changing sides at that moment is spat out in the same frame, where it is between the two lines: it goes on across without a jump in where it is drawn, growing from the lump's shape to a blob's, takes a tap as a thrown blob does, and lands loud on the far side. A twin on its way into a blob that is spat out comes out with it. So a lump is never outside a pipe. A blob sitting on the rim of a mouth that is lifted from under it is on the line and is not swallowed (`pieces.test.ts`, `body.test.ts`).
- The camera is poked where it is drawn. It turns on its joint after the blob, so a finger is on it when it is within ten units of its box and hood as they are turned now, and the place it pointed at before is bare wall. All it sweeps is clear of the ring, the rat and the ghost hand (`toy.test.ts`, `layout.test.ts`).
- The stage is fitted into the surface whole and centred wherever the jam's home control then ends above everything a finger must reach, which is every tablet size. On a short wide surface the control, which does not shrink, would lie on the top beat: there the stage is made a little smaller and set lower, down to the bottom edge, so the control ends above the reach of a piece on the top beat. The draw and every touch use that one fit. It is held at ten surface sizes, three of them short and wide: 1000 by 600, 844 by 390 and 568 by 320 (`layout.test.ts`, `frameBudget.test.ts`).
- The snail and the hare beside the dial are paint, not buttons. A tap on one is a tap on the wall, with its burst and the blob's jump, and the knob rocks towards that end with its knock, which turns nothing and shows what is turned (`toy.test.ts`).
- A second finger is a tap where it lands on wall and nothing where it lands on a control, the rack or a piece.
- A finger in the top right 72 px of the surface is a grown-up's: the game answers it with nothing, and it does not count as a touch to the idle guidance.
- The pigeon and the rat answer their own kind of jump at most once in two beats, however many land, and neither answer grows. The cat's and the neighbour's habits do not follow the child at all.
- The rat's frown is its taste at the blob: it comes when a mouth swallows the blob, each time, and at nothing a finger does. Setting a mouth on the track, moving one or taking one off draws no frown (`toy.test.ts`).
- The pigeon has two eyes, a large near one and a smaller far one. Both follow the blob; they part, one each way, at twins in canon over a flip, and cross at the dial's fastest.
- When a touch ends the pigeon's lap, the pigeon goes home in a straight move a third of a second long, which is seen, and the touch is an ordinary touch from the frame it lands in.
- The idle guidance counts a child's touch and a clear-out and nothing a character does by itself. The pigeon's lap, which a ring with a spring on every beat starts every lap, does not hold it back (`play.test.ts`).
- The cat's tail, which hangs below the ledge and swings, is the cat for a poke. In its strut the pigeon rocks about the foot it stands on, each foot in turn, and the other foot leaves the perch (`cast.test.ts`).
- The neighbour has no taste. It sways in fours, sips, and ducks behind the sill when poked, drawing the curtain; a tap on the window is still a tap on the wall.
- The idle guidance glows on the blob and on the rack, shows a tap on bare brick in its first and third showings, and in its second and fourth carries a drum pad from the rack to a beat at the bottom of the ring.
- Sounds are mixed by the frame: where the voices of one frame would add up past full scale all of them are turned down together, so with a full ring every spring is still heard over the footfall (`voices.test.ts`).

**Overlaps that are allowed, each with its reason and its cap.**

- *Twins print over each other.* A twin runs a third of a beat behind, which is less than a blob's width on either surface, and on the ceiling a beat is under half a blob wide. They are two colours of one stencil printed off register, which is what the look does. Cap: the lag is a third of a beat and never less; the first is always drawn in front.
- *The ceiling is close-set.* A beat of the ceiling is 44 units. A piece there, 62 wide, has a place of two beats, so two pieces never touch and a wall's stub ends before the next piece begins. A spring can stand on all thirty-two places of the ceiling, 22 units apart and 46 wide, so springs on neighbouring places there print over each other, black and teal by turns, a spring on the beat beside a piece prints over the piece's edge by about ten units, and one on the half beside it over the piece's near half. A blob coming off a wall of the ceiling is still coming down its far side a beat on. The ring cannot be larger on the stage, and the niche is as small as the pigeon, drawn at two thirds of its sprite, allows. Cap: one piece to two beats of the ceiling; springs are stamped over pieces, never under; no finger has to pick a spring.
- *A lump in a pipe passes a piece as a bulge.* A hushed blob is a lump under the pipe's roof; it does not climb over a pad, it goes by behind it, drawn before the piece, and the pipe bulges or dents. A twin that is drawn into a lump is a lump too, no wider and no taller than one. Cap: the lump is under the roof (56 units) at every beat, and so is the twin in every frame it is drawn in (`pieces.test.ts`).
- *A pressed spring under a foot or a piece.* A spring that something stands on is drawn pressed to 7 units and the thing stands on it. Cap: 7 units, and the spring is never taller than the room there is (`body.test.ts`).
- *A blob on one surface and a twin on the other, at the same place.* For a third of a beat after a flip in canon one is up and one is down, and the corridor between the lines is 200 units for two bodies of 96. Cap: a third of a beat; a jump on one surface still clears whatever stands on the other (`body.test.ts`).
- *The blob's body against the two lines and the pieces.* Its outline is a round-cornered square and the model rolls a plain oval over things, so it can read into a line or a corner by a hair. Cap: 2.5 units, held in every state a visit reaches.

**Tiers.** A tier changes drawing only, never the tempo, the grid or a sound.

Every tier keeps the wall, every layer of every figure, the overspray, the bursts under the finger and at least two ghosts, so the look is the same wall at a coarser grain. A change of tier repaints the wall, the set, the springs' layers and the sprites once, at the new pixel ratio.
