### Template notes

One entry a copied file.

- `tea-time.tsx` (the Mount). Changed, as it must be: the table's view is made in the effect, the game's controller (`play.ts`) is made when the slot has been read, `draw` takes the frame's seconds, a gesture goes to the controller, and what the controller says changed goes to the save cadence. At a put-away the touch in progress is called off, not ended. **For the template, two things.** `draw` has no way to be given the step the frame plays, and a three.js game needs it for springs and liquid; here `draw(seconds = 0)` is called with the clock's step in the loop and with nothing from `resize` and the load. And the template ends a touch at a put-away as a drag that ends where the finger was (`act(touch.clear())`), which puts the thing in the hand down there: a cup over a head becomes a hat, a led guest changes seats. A game whose sheet stores a thing in the hand where it was picked up must call the touch off instead; a word on that beside `touch.clear()` would save the reader's finding.
- `input.ts`. Used as copied. **For the template, two things.** There is no hold: a finger held on a thing for three seconds drifts more than `TAP_SLOP` (14 px), so the tracker reports a drag, and a game whose verb is a hold has to treat `dragMove` within a slop of its own as still holding and `dragLift` as the release (`HOLD_SLOP` in `play.ts`). And a finger that comes back within `LIFT_GRACE_MS` and `REGRAB_RADIUS` carries on the same drag with no new `press`: a game whose press does something (here it pours) must treat the first `dragMove` after a `dragLift` as that press, or a quick second press does nothing; and a script's quick strokes become one drag, so each scripted drag is followed by a pause longer than the grace (the `carry` helper in the audit's config).
- `audio.ts`. As the template has it on the base branch, with its count of the fingers on the glass and the test for it. **For the template:** it plays one-shot voices only. A sound that lasts as long as a touch (a running stream) is made here of short grains, one every 90 ms, each built from the level at that moment. A voice that can be held, steered and let go would be simpler and cheaper for any game with a continuous sound.
- `guidance.ts`. Used as copied. **For the template:** `handPose` shows a tap or a drag. A hold and a rub are drawn by the game's own `guide.ts`; a hold pose would say it outright.
- `state.ts`. Used as copied, and wrapped by `save.ts` as its top comment says. **For the template:** `deserialize` gives no way to tell "not this game's record" from a fresh result, so the wrapper repeats its two tests (is a record, the version matches) before reading its own fields.
- `scene.ts`. Used as copied: every scene in `scenes.ts` is a list of its beats. **For the template:** nothing in it says what a scene saves when it starts, nor what is stored while a scene that undoes itself is playing; here each scene writes the state it ends in before its first beat, a showing stores the table as it found it for as long as it plays, and `play.test.ts` holds both.
- `overlay.ts`. Changed: it opens on a finger held a second in its corner and lifted there, followed by three taps there (`HOLD_MS`, `ARMED_MS`, `lift`), and touch-downs closer together than 60 ms are one tap (`APART_MS`); the Mount passes it every lift as well as every touch-down. **For the template:** the template opens on any three touch-downs in the corner, which a child who drums on a tile there makes, and counts every pointer. Its comment asks for backdrop under its corner, where nothing answers a touch; the wall round that corner is that here.
- `config.ts`. Changed where it is meant to be: `LADDER` holds the ten position ids and `FIRST_VISIT` its two rows. The tier table is as copied.
- `perf.ts`, `quality.ts`, `attention.ts`, `saveCadence.ts`. Frozen and untouched.

### For the owner to decide

- **The toy**: the pot pours for as long as it is held.
- **Where the finger goes.** The brief says hold to pour, let go in time. This build puts the finger on the pot, which waits beside the cup, and never on the cup: a finger on the cup would cover the tea. A tap on a cup, a saucer or the cloth calls the pot over.
- **The pot never runs out.** It is where tea comes from and may go back to. A pot that empties would add "enough for everyone", and a dead end.
- **How much of the claim California carries.** The judging of a pour against a painted ring, the first six positions, is designed from the Dutch records only. The California records come in where two cups are compared, three are ordered and a place is laid. Pouring as a skill of the hand rests on no record in either list, and nothing is claimed for it. The sheet says so; the alternative is a design that compares containers from the first position.
- **Two of one guest in view.** The next party waits at the gate while the old one still sits, and may hold the same guest. Keep it (they are figurines, and there can be two), or show the waiting party only once the old one has left, or have the order never seat a guest twice running (not possible where four of the five sit).
- **A puddle stays for the next party.** It is the sheet's rule, and it makes the sponge matter. The other way is a cloth that is dry for every new party.
- No default awaiting the owner had to be broken: no symbol, letter or word, no reading on the object, no camera shake or impact pause, no speech.

### How the game meets the quality bar

No frame rate has been measured: the game was built on a machine with no graphics card, and no physical iPad was measured.

- **Alive at idle.** Steam rises from the spout of a resting pot. Each guest breathes, blinks and looks from its cup to the pot in its own tempo, and a guest with no saucer looks at the stack. Now and then, at a quiet table, one guest in turn does a small joke of its own: the Bear's belly rumbles, the Mouse hiccups, the Hen nods off and starts awake, a Duckling yawns itself over. All of it runs on the attended clock and stops when the game is unattended or hidden.
- **Motion and sound on every touch.** Every one of the thirty cells of the sheet's grid (six kinds of thing by tap, hold, drag, put-on and rub) is drawn and heard, held by `grid.test.ts` and `play.test.ts`: a tapped pot hops its lid, puffs steam and gives one drop, a cup rings at a pitch set by how full it is and calls the pot, a saucer spins down like a coin, a spoon flips and swings behind the finger, the sponge squelches, swells and streaks, and each guest has its own poke, tickle and walk. Every press is answered in the frame it lands. All sound is synthesized from numbers. Outside the grid, the whole gate rings its bell at a touch, and a touched tile of the wall comes loose and does what its picture would, with a ring of its own.
- **Weight, squash and follow-through.** Pottery squashes a little and rings back on a stiff spring; a thing leans after the finger before it follows; the tea leans in a carried cup and rocks in a nudged one; the pot rises as it tips, hops in an arc and lands with a knock; its lid chatters while tea runs and drops home after; a last drop falls after the stream stops. A carried thing rides over what it passes.
- **Every character moves like itself.** The Bear, the Mouse, the Hen and the two Ducklings each have their own actions for a poke, a stream, a tickle, a step, a reach, the three ways a sip can go, a wait, the clink and settling (`motion.ts`); `motion.test.ts` fails on a shared or near-copied action. The second Duckling does everything half a beat after the first.
- **Kid-clear.** Few, large, white pieces on a plain blue cloth; the wall is the only busy thing and is behind everything. What is touched to play carries the one warm colour; the wall's tiles answer a touch too, as something a child finds.
- **Wordless clarity for age 4.** No word, letter, numeral or symbol on the kid side, and no `symbols.ts`; `npm run wordless:check` passes. An amount is read as tea against a painted ring, against a twin's cup held rim to rim, or against the size of the cup. An error is only a consequence: tea runs over, a guest finds too little or too much and shows it, a guest will not drink by a puddle. Nothing buzzes, resets or is lost. Nothing is on the table before it means something: the tray comes at the third position, the sponge with the first spill, the bowl with the first cup too full.
- **Wordless guidance.** The template's idle ladder: a ring breathes round the one thing to touch now (`hint.ts` picks it: the pot, a cup to call the pot to, a saucer and then a spoon for each place, the sponge and a puddle, the gate), then a ghost hand shows the one move (a tap, a hold, a carry from here to there, a rub), backing off and stopping. A guest plays each new idea once, as its party sits down and before the child first tries it, and never again.
- **One obvious want per scene.** A cup that is not to its guest's taste, with the pot beside it; a place that is not laid; a party at the gate. The cold playtest proxy was run on the production build with a fresh slot, fixed and rerun (pass 7), and once more after the closing run.
- **Found as left.** Put away at any instant and opened again, the table is as it was: tea, puddles, things, hats, who sat where and what each found (`save.ts`, read defensively, well under 64 KB at its largest). Every scene writes the state it ends in before its first beat, and a showing stores the table as it found it, so no scene plays twice and none leaves a mark the child did not make. Put away with a finger down, a pour stops, a thing in the hand goes back where it was picked up and a led guest goes back to its seat. The next party waits at the gate for the child's touch.
- **Nothing passes through anything.** The intersection audit is enforced: 1072 samples over seven moments, 0 open, every allowed contact with a reason and a cap. The first run had 98 open.
- **60 fps on a mid-range iPad.** Not measured. Built to the budget and held by `frameBudget.test.ts`: at most 78 draw calls on the fullest table with a party of four at the gate and a tile of the wall loose, a count that does not grow with play; pixel ratio capped at 2 by the tier table; no shadow maps (one instanced draw of blob shadows); no post pass; one material for all pottery; the loop paused when unattended.
- **Procedural or committed assets only.** Every texture is painted on a canvas at load from a seeded stream. Nothing is fetched; `node scripts/egress-check.ts` and `npm run egress:built` pass.
- **Its own art direction.** Blue-and-white glazed pottery, the row reserved for this game; the guide is in `ART.md`, with the registry row's text at its end.

### What the next builder should know

- **Put the finger where it does not cover what the child must read.** The first design had the child press the cup. The finger then sits on the tea. The pot waits beside the cup and is what is held.
- **Stop a spout short of the cup.** A spout that hangs over the cup hides the level as surely as a finger. The tea lands further out than the spout reaches, and the stream arcs the rest.
- **A lathe form that is painted from an atlas must keep every row inside its region.** A face that runs from the region to a plain patch elsewhere in the atlas samples everything between them, and shows as rings of other pieces' paint.
- **Spill past the edge of what overflowed, and never under a thing.** A puddle that starts under the saucer cannot be seen until it is large, and tea under a thing cannot be wiped.
- **A matcap can keep a glaze's highlight over painted colour** with one line in the material: where the matcap is nearly white, the output is white whatever the map says.
- **Put a type between the rules and the view, and test the controller with a stage that draws nothing.** `stage.ts` is all that passes between `play.ts` and three.js; every scene, every cell and found-as-left are tested without a renderer.
- **Judge everyone before anyone acts.** Two guests who compare their cups must both be judged on the table as it stands; the first to drink otherwise leaves the second an empty cup to compare with, and the sitting never ends. It showed only in a still of a full table.
- **What is worn follows the head, not the seat.** A hat placed from the seat's numbers is in the head as soon as the guest hops, stretches or nods. Take the head's own place and turn each frame.
- **Something that eases toward "above whatever is near" must not count things that are doing the same.** Two saucers flying home each rose over the other, for ever.
- **A model test can hold "nothing stands in anything" before there is a scene to audit:** footprints as circles, every pair of standing things on every table the designed order can lay. The audit then finds what moves: carries, flips, leans, walks.
- **Write the audit's moments from saved tables,** and give the game a fixed random stream in the audit's `query`, or two runs lay different first tables.
- **A scripted drag needs a pause after it** longer than the input tracker's grace for a finger that comes back, or two strokes become one.
- **Check that each audit moment reaches the state its comment names.** The ending moment's saved table lacked one cup, so the sitting never ended and the moment audited a table at rest for thirty seconds, clean every time. Read the last sample's stills, not only the count.
- **A walker that lands exactly on its spot has still arrived.** "The gap is above zero and below a hair" misses the step that ends at zero: the guest stays "on its way in" for ever, its party never counts as seated, and nothing that waits for that starts.
- **Give everything that can stand on a thing a place to stand there, and audit it.** "It is on the cup" is a rule; where its bowl and its handle are is a pose, and without one a spoon is drawn through the wall of the cup. The audit finds it only if a moment puts it there.
- **Move what rests on a thing after that thing, in the same frame.** A rider that eases after its base trails a carried saucer and is a frame late in a lifted cup, which is long enough to be in its wall.
- **Judge a pair on which of the two was touched last.** "More than the other" blames the cup that was filled first for a pour that is still being made into the second.
- **Play it cold on the production build before believing it.** The pot standing behind the cup, where a child would not look for it, passed every test and the audit.
- **Have the sheet read against the code before the gates, not after.** A sheet's grid says what each cell looks like and sounds like; the build gave every cell a sound and a third of them no picture, and every test passed. A reader who has not seen the build finds in a quarter of an hour what the builder no longer sees. Write the sheet's sentences as tests of the stage ("the spoon swings", "a drop falls") while building.
- **"What is under the spout" must be asked of the table as it is drawn, at every press.** A relation remembered from when the pot arrived pours into a cup that has since been carried off.
- **Keep the tracker's quirks in the controller's tests:** a finger that comes back in the grace has no `press`, a release of a long still hold is a `tap`, and a put-away arrives as a drag's end.
- **On software GL a still at pixel ratio 2 can outlast the screenshot's default timeout.** Give it three minutes, and take it at device scale.
