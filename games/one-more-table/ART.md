# Design sheet

Written before any game code, and checked by someone who did not write it before the game is built on the toy. The look follows the sheet at the end of this file.

## The band and its age rule

The manifest band is 3 to 6. The youngest age governs. From the cue table's row for 3 to 4: no text, numeral or icon, no spoken instruction, no verdict, one next act offered at a time, and big targets. The pack's rules for ages 2 to 4 and 4 to 6 both apply. Everything needed works with a tap as well as a drag, a drag counts when it got half way, and no double tap means anything. The knob and the bell are never under 100 units across on a stage of 1180 by 820, whatever the zoom, and no target stands in the bottom 60 units.

`ctx.childAge` sets nothing: every age, and no age, opens the same table. A saved table always wins.

## The toy

A wooden table with a bellows under its top, like an accordion, and a huge striped knob on its right end. A finger on the knob squashes it in the same frame with a breath of reeds. Pulled, the table wheezes longer, the reeds climb, every head leans after it and a gap opens with an empty stool in it. Pushed, everyone is squeezed narrower and taller, cheeks bulge, lips purse and the reeds sink to a squeak. Let go, they breathe out with a long wheeze and wobble to rest. Pumping it in and out plays it, and that is a pleasure with nobody arriving.

## The object-by-action grid, and what is new on day 15

Seats are places in a row. There are twelve who come, each as wide as they are. Each has one act, done to the neighbour on the right (to the one on the left from the last seat), and one way of taking an act. Whoever newly sits beside someone does it once, and a tap does it again, so every pair of two is a joke of its own.

What each dish does to whoever eats it, and it stays until they go home: corn rattles in at the mouth like a typewriter and pops off the ears; mash sits on the nose; milk is a white moustache; jelly makes the eater wobble; sausages go in as one long slurp and the cheeks whip; gravy is a brown beard; pancakes stretch the eater tall; rolls fill the cheeks round; pumpkin makes the belly round; cheese sends the neighbours leaning away; honey sticks the mouth to the plate and it twangs loose; pie covers the face and two eyes blink through. A liked dish is eaten with shut eyes and a bounce, a hated one with a shudder from the hair down, and both are eaten up. A guest with nothing before it leans over and takes one bite of the neighbour's.

Scenery that looks touchable answers as itself and changes nothing: the cuckoo, the cat by the stove, the pot, the curtain, the hen on the roof, the sun, the apple tree, the mouse family under the garden with a table of their own. A touch anywhere else leaves one painted brush flower that fades, with a soft dab.

The dishes, when the knob is pulled up or down instead of along: the table tips, and every dish slides one place toward the low end; the one at the end flies back over all the heads to the other end. A long scrape and a whistle. A pull counts as tipping only when it is within 25 degrees of straight up or down and longer than 120 units; every other pull is along, and the first 40 units along fix it as a pull until the finger lifts.

**Day 15.** On day 1 the child pulls and laughs at who plops in. On day 15 the child seats people: the goose by grandpa's ear, the dog by the sausages, the baby between the bear and the moose, gives the pie to the cow who hates it, puts the jelly on grandpa's head, slides every dinner one place along, and rings the bell to see it. The same seats always do the same thing. The youngest is surer and pulls it all the way to the moose.

## The error as a consequence

There is no wrong attempt. A pull let go before half way wheezes back and the one who waits sinks on its heels again, so the child sees that a longer pull was wanted. A guest let go away from the table is seen to hop to the nearest seat with its own plop. A dish let go away from any guest slides back where it stood.

## The designed order, and what is stored

There is no order of challenges. Who comes is a fixed order: baby, aunt, uncle, dog, tall cousin, goose, little cousin, cow, bear and moose. After each feast that ends in going home, the order of those between the baby and the moose moves on by three, so another visit starts with another guest at the door; the moose is always last.

Stored: the version; whether the first round was shown; the rounds gone home (only to know the order, shown nowhere); the seated guests in order; for each dish whose place it is at, whether on the table or the head, and whether it is full; for each guest what it last ate; whether they have eaten. Who waits, the length of the table and the camera follow from these.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

The likes and hates are in the first table and never change. The one who waits has one want, a seat, and shows it: it stands large at the table's right end, beside the knob, its dish held out over the table. Every few seconds of attended idle its foot nudges the knob outward: the table opens a crack, the edge of an empty stool shows, and it springs shut. After 1.5 s without a touch the knob also glows, and after 3 s the ghost hand pulls it. The nudges end with the ladder's last showing, any touch removes the help, and every new guest starts this ladder again. It never sighs, complains or looks at the child.

## The scenes

- **The first round** (first visit, about 4 s). Granny and Grandpa are packed at the table and the baby waits. A ghost hand presses the knob and pulls it; the gap opens; the baby somersaults in; the hand fades and the aunt fills the door. Saved as shown, with the baby seated, when it starts. A touch lands it there.
- **Someone sits down** (0.8 s). They jump in an arc, land with a squash that runs down the row as a wave, their dish lands after them, and the two new neighbours do their acts. It is saved when they jump, and it never holds back the next touch.
- **The feast** (4 to 10 s, the more the longer, the ending). Caused by the bell. Beats: everyone sits up; from the bell outward each eats what is before it, with its own tempo; then all lean back with round bellies, and the odd hiccup goes down the row. Eaten dishes and what each ate are saved when it starts. A touch ends it there. Nothing follows by itself.
- **Going home** (2.5 s). Caused by pushing the table shut after a feast, or, once nobody waits and all have eaten, by tapping the knob. While someone waits a tap always seats, before or after a feast; only a push home ends the round early. They pop up one after another and sail over the row out at the right, each with its own cry, and two stay: Granny and Grandpa. Saved when it starts; all dishes are full again.

With nobody left to come, the want is the bell: all look at it, and it swings by itself; then the ghost hand taps it. When all have eaten, eyelids droop, heads lean right, toward home, and every few seconds the table closes a crack and pulls back; then the ghost hand pushes the knob inward.

## The look

**Folk-painted panel** (canvas 2D). The world is one long board painted by a village hand with a loaded brush: every stroke starts round and full and ends in a point, and carries a second, lighter colour along one edge.

- **The strokes.** Comma, dab, petal, leaf and twist, in `folk.ts`. A roof is rows of dabs, a post is a twisted cord, a fence is upright commas, a beam is a scroll. No outline anywhere: shapes end against a ground of another value.
- **Grounds.** Deep, flat and matte, one for each place the table grows into: barn red for the room, bottle green for the kitchen, night blue for the sky over the garden, earth brown below. They are darker than any face.
- **Colour.** Scarlet, ochre and gold, cream, sky blue, leaf green, with dark brown for the darkest. Faces and dishes are the lightest things in the frame. The knob is the one large scarlet and cream wheel, the bell the one large gold thing.
- **People.** Large heads, round eyes with a live dark pupil, two painted cheeks, clothes of one colour with folk motifs. Each has a silhouette of its own: the bun, the beard, the tower of hair, the bowler, the neck, the antlers.
- **Motion.** Everything rests on a spring. A squash runs along the row, heads lag behind bodies, shut together they grow tall and their eyes bulge, and each has one act and one way to take one. The painting itself does not move except where it is a thing that would: leaves, steam, curtain, cuckoo, sun.
- **Never.** Words, numerals, pilgrim or Native figures, a hard black line, a gradient on a body, a shadow cast on the wall.
- **Next to its neighbours.** Dressed in the Dark is gold line on lacquer with flat jewels and arches; wild washes and papers are other games'. Here there is no line at all and the brush stroke is the unit.

The small print: `house.ts` paints the world into tiles once, `figures.ts` paints each guest and dish once, `moves.ts` is how each one moves and eats, `view.ts` moves the pictures and draws the faces.
