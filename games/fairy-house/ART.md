# Design sheet

Written before any game code, and checked by someone who did not write it before the game is built on the toy. The headings stay in this order. The look follows the sheet at the end of this file.

What each heading asks for is in the section "The design sheet" of `docs/solutions/conventions/building-a-jam-game.md`.

## The band and its age rule

The manifest band is 4 to 7, and its youngest age, four, governs the design.

- **The cue-table row.** The row for a youngest age of 3 to 4 in the wordless-clarity convention. Its "Avoid" column binds the game: no text, numeral or pictorial icon that has to be decoded, no spoken instruction, no verdict, never several activities live at once, and no tool present before it means something. One next act is offered at a time, and one finger works.
- **The pack's rule for the range** (pack: game-design, ages-4-to-6.md). Pretend play with a place, props and characters who react, and the child supplies the plot. Tap and drag. Funny through wrong uses, tricks and a victim who overreacts and is never hurt: the fat one sits down in a heap of snapped twigs and blinks. No reading and no double tap.
- **The symbol rule.** The band starts below 6, so the kid side shows no word, letter, numeral or sign, and the game has no `symbols.ts`. The rule follows the manifest band: a seven-year-old sees none either.
- **What `ctx.childAge` sets.** Only what lies in the hollow on a first visit ("The designed order, and what is stored"). A saved house wins over the age. Age locks and hides nothing: all seven kinds lie on the forest floor at every age.
- **What no age gives.** `null` gives a first visit what the youngest child gets.

## The toy

**The action.** Carry a found thing from the forest floor into the hollow between the roots and set it down. That is what the finger does most.

**In an empty scene.** The hollow is bare and the found things lie on the forest floor in front of it.

- When the finger lands on a thing, in that frame, the thing hops up under the finger and stretches, with its own small sound: a pebble clicks, a leaf rustles, a feather breathes. The three folk who sit round the tree turn their eyes to it.
- While the finger moves, the thing is carried just above it and swings a little behind, and a soft pencil shadow in the hollow shows where it would come to rest.
- When the finger lifts over the hollow, the thing drops down and comes to rest on what is under its middle: the ground, or a thing already lying there. What lies wholly above the height it is let go at is over it, and is not what it comes down on: let go under a roof, a table, a perch or a thatch it goes under that when it fits there by its height, and lies on the ground with its shadow under it. Where it does not fit it slides to the nearest free place at its own height, as a thing with no room does, and it is never set on top of what it was let go under. Where it has no room to lie there (no more than its edge is over a leaf, an acorn cap or a feather, or a thing its middle is not over stands in its way) it comes down on that thing if that thing carries, and otherwise slides to the nearest free place it can be seen to reach without passing another thing, with a sound of its own; only where there is no such place does it tumble home, and near a wall, where the arch comes down low, it is set down a little further in. Each kind falls by its own weight. A pebble drops hard, a feather drifts down from side to side. It lands with a squash and a sound that is made of the thing and of what it landed on, where it lands on the ground a few specks of pencil dust come off it (a thing that lands on another thing raises none), the things lying near it hop, and the folk answer in their own ways.
- It stays exactly where it was put. Nothing in the hollow moves again until a finger moves it or one of the folk uses it at evening.
- What it rests on decides how it lies and what it is: a leaf sags into a moss cushion and is a bed, stands tented on two twigs and is a roof, lies flat on bark and is a tablecloth.
- A finger that lifts without having moved has tapped: the thing drops back where it was and nothing has changed. A thing with other things resting on it is carried with them, so a bed is moved whole. A twig that shares its load with another support (a roof, a table, a perch or a thatch across two) stays down under it when touched, stretching with its sound, and carried off it leaves that thing on the other, where it comes to rest in sight.
- A thing let go outside the hollow, with its middle over something that carries nothing (a leaf, an acorn cap, a feather) or higher than the hollow, tumbles back to its own place on the forest floor in two bounces, at no cost.
- A touch on bare ground raises a puff of pencil dust with a soft pat. A touch on the bark is a dry knock, at which the owl shuts its eyes, and a touch on the sky is a breath of air, at which the moth flutters off; neither raises dust. A touch on one of the two big roots is a low knock of wood, and something stirs under it; a touch on a bush is a rustle, and a few leaves come out of it; a touch on the far hill, or in the air just over its back at the right of the frame, is a small, far answer, and birds go up off it. None of the three answers as the sky. A touch on one of the folk, on the snail, the owl or the sun is answered by that one as itself.

**Why repeating it is a pleasure with no goal.** The touch is answered when the finger lands, and one set-down sets off a chain: the fall, the squash, the dust where it lands on the ground, the neighbours' hop and three different faces (pack: game-design, touch-answers-bigger-than-the-touch.md). The same thing sounds and lies differently on each of five supports, so setting things down is also finding out. Nothing a finger can do is refused or punished, the simplest use (carry one thing in and let go) always works, and what is made stays (pack: game-design, toy-first.md).

## The object-by-action grid, and what is new on day 15

Seven kinds of found thing by five supports: the one action is setting a thing down, and what it comes to rest on is the choice. Every cell lies differently and sounds differently. The sound of a cell is heard at the moment the thing touches down, and a cell that rocks, rolls or swings sounds again each time it visibly knocks.

| Thing | On the ground | On moss | On a pebble | On twigs | On bark |

| Acorn cap | A bath: cup up, and dew wells up in it, with a plip and a gurgle | A cradle: cup up and tilted, rocking to rest, with a creak at each rock | A table lamp: cup down on the stone, and it lights warm, with a glassy ting | A bell: it hangs from the tip and swings, with a ding at the end of each swing | A soup bowl: cup up, spinning on its rim like a coin, with a rattle that quickens and stops in a tok |

Whatever is set on it lies on its slope: a thing small enough to sit on an end lies where it was let go (on the low end, over the stone, or up on the high end where that end cannot come down), and a wider thing (a leaf, a twig, moss, a second slab) lies along the slab on the middle of it. | Feather | A broom laid ready: it drifts down from side to side and lies flat, with a breath and a faint tsk | A quilt: nestled into the moss, with the softest hush | A tickler: it stands in a crack of the stone and quivers, with a fluttering frrr | A flag: tied at the tip, it streams once and hangs, with a flap | A sail: it stands up from the slab, with a whup like cloth filling |

**The wrong use of each thing works and is funny.**

- A thing set on a leaf is flipped off with a flap, on an acorn cap it bounces off with a clonk (out of a bath, with a splash), and on a feather it slides off with a whoosh. Each then tumbles back to its place on the forest floor.
- A thing set higher than the hollow topples off the pile and rolls home.
- A roof is a bed to the fat one, who climbs on and snaps the twigs. A table lamp is a hat to the tiny one. A bath set on the root step floods what lies below. A flag is taken down to sweep the ceiling.
- Whatever is wrong for one of the folk still gets that one's own answer, every time ("The characters and their fixed tastes").

**What the folk do with it, at evening.** Each of the three keeps to one kind of thing, and each of its five cells gets a different act and sound.

| The one who comes | Its thing on the ground | On moss | On a pebble | On twigs | On bark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The fat one tries every leaf, from the left | Lies down on the rug, finds it hard, and gets up with a hmpf | Sinks into the bed and snores at once | Lies on the slide and slides off on its belly with a zip and a bump; on a leaf that lies flat on its stone, which is no slide, it lies down as on a rug, finds it hard, and gets up with a hmpf | Climbs onto the roof; each twig that still stands snaps with a crack, one after the other (two under a roof, one under a parasol, none over stumps), the roof comes straight down, and it lands sitting, blinking: on the roof where the roof lies on its stumps, and on the ground, dropped there in sight, where the roof had no room to lie and slid aside or went home | Lies on the table, which creaks; it rolls off wrapped in the cloth with a flump |
| The tidy one sweeps with the first feather it reaches, from the right | Sweeps the floor in quick strokes, swish, swish, and the dust makes the fat one sneeze | Plumps the quilt with pats, and fluff flies | Dusts the stone, tickles its own nose and sneezes | Takes the flag down, sweeps the ceiling with high swishes and ties it back | Polishes the slab with squeaks; where a bath above has spilt over the ground the slab lies on this evening, it sails off standing on it, with whatever else stands on the slab, as far as the way is free, and stays where something stands in its way on both sides |
| The tiny one gets into every acorn cap, from the right and the cradles last | Jumps into the bath with a splash and scrubs; the water spills over and soaks what lies below | Climbs into the cradle and rocks, creak, creak; in the last one it comes to, it rocks itself to sleep | Lifts the lamp onto its head, walks into things with a bonk and sets it back on its stone | Swings on the bell, ding, ding, ding, and the fat one snorts awake | Sits in the bowl and spins with a rattle until it is dizzy |

Whoever ends the evening in no bed and no cradle sleeps on the floor, content: all three in a heap when the fat one has found no bed (with no leaf in the house it looks for one, shrugs and lies down there), and the other two side by side when it has. With no feather the tidy one sweeps with the tip of its own hat. With no acorn cap the tiny one washes its face like a cat.

**On day 15** the child furnishes for a particular evening, because the three can be relied on: the bath goes on the root step above the bed to give the fat one wet feet, or well away from it; the roof is set on twigs on purpose, to hear them snap, or a moss bed is put to its left so that the fat one is asleep before it gets there; the bell is hung to wake the sleeper; a bark with a feather is left on low ground to see it sail. None of that is new content: it is seven things by five supports, for three folk with fixed habits (pack: game-design, depth-from-combinations.md; pack: game-design, liveliness-from-causing-and-comedy.md).

## The error as a consequence

Nothing in the game is wrong, and what does not work shows why in the world (pack: game-design, errors-show-as-consequences.md).

- **A thing that cannot rest.** A leaf, an acorn cap and a feather carry nothing. What is set on one is shed in that thing's own way and tumbles back to its place on the forest floor, where it can be picked up again. The same happens above the hollow's arch and outside the hollow. The thing below is not disturbed.
- **A support taken away.** A thing that lay across two supports and loses one settles on the one that is left, and is what that cell says: a roof becomes a parasol.
- **A house that goes wrong at evening.** The twig bed snaps under the fat one, the bath floods the bed below, the bell wakes the sleeper. Each is seen happening, at the place and for the reason it happens, and the morning shows the house as the folk left it. The child changes one thing and pulls the evening down again.
- No buzzer, no cross, no sad face turned to the child, and nothing is lost: a snapped twig and a soaked thing are whole and dry again once they are back on the forest floor.

## The designed order, and what is stored

The game has no learning goal and no order of challenges: it is open pretend play with an evening the child calls. The ladder in `config.ts` has one place, `hollow`, and it never moves.

**What a first visit starts with, by `ctx.childAge`.** A default, never a gate: every thing is on screen and can be moved at every age.

| Age | In the hollow on a first visit |
| --- | --- |
| 4 and younger | A moss cushion with a leaf on it (a bed) on the floor of the hollow, and a pebble beside it |
| 5 and 6 | One moss cushion on the floor of the hollow |
| 7 and older | Nothing: the hollow is bare |

With no age (`null`) a first visit starts as for 4 and younger. A saved house wins over the age.

**What play changes.** Only the house: which things lie in the hollow and where, and at evening what the folk did to them. Nothing gets harder, nothing is unlocked and nothing is counted.

**The harder option the child can see and pick.** Height: things stacked two and three high, a roof over a bed, a bath on the root step. It looks harder in the world and nothing asks for it.

**Every field of the saved state.**

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `v` | The version of the record, 1 |
| `position` | Always `hollow`, the one place of the ladder |
| `finished` | True from the moment the sun is let go below the hill until it is let go above it again: the evening stands |
| `things` | The things that lie in the hollow, in the order they came to rest (a thing let go under another comes before the one that is over it, and that one is then kept at a place that gives its rest with the thing under it), each as its number (0 to 17, fixed for each found thing) and the place along the hollow where it was let go, or, where it came to rest somewhere else (it slid aside, came down on the thing beside it, or leans to its other side), a place that gives that same rest when the list is set down again. Every other thing is at its own place on the forest floor. The house is rebuilt from this list by the resting rule, so how each thing lies and what it rests on need no field |
| `snapped` | The numbers of the twigs the fat one has snapped. A snapped twig is a short post; back on the forest floor it is whole and its number leaves the list |
| `soaked` | The numbers of the things the bath has soaked. Back on the forest floor a thing is dry and its number leaves the list |
| `asleep` | For each of the three folk at evening, the number of the thing it ended on, or none for the floor, and the place along the hollow. Empty by day |

A thing in the hand is not saved in the air: a put-away under a dragging finger sends it back where it came from. A thing falling or tumbling is a view of the saved state, which already holds where it comes to rest. The pencil dust, the hop of the neighbours, the folk's faces by day, the snail, the moth and the owl are short-lived and are not saved. The water a bath spills at evening sinks into the earth before the three settle and is not saved either: what it soaked is in `soaked`, and a bark that sailed is in `things` where it came to rest. The largest state, all eighteen things in the hollow with four twigs snapped and eighteen things soaked, is under one kilobyte.

No customer waits in turn: the same three folk sit round the tree by day, and the evening lays out nothing new.

## The characters and their fixed tastes

Three folk sit round the tree by day, large, facing the hollow and the child. Each holds what it wants in plain sight, and its eyes follow the thing in the child's hand. They never ask, thank, praise or hurry, and nothing they feel is about the child (pack: game-design, characters-with-opinions.md).

- **The fat one** wants a bed. It hugs a pillow and its nightcap hangs over one eye. Slow and heavy; its belly is its funniest part and wobbles at every thud. It likes a leaf anywhere and loves one on moss: its eyes go round and it squeezes the pillow. It dislikes hard things landing (it flinches at a pebble) and wet. At evening it tries every leaf from the left and breaks the twig one.
- **The tidy one** wants to sweep. It holds a small dustpan, upright as a bottle with a stiff collar. Quick and exact; its long nose is its funniest part and twitches. It likes a feather anywhere, and things set down level. It dislikes dust (it sniffs at every puff) and anything that tumbles (it tuts with its nose). At evening it sweeps with the first feather it reaches from the right.
- **The tiny one** wants a bath. It has a towel round its neck and a scrubbing brush in its fist, and a hat far too big for it. Fast and bouncy; the hat is its funniest part and slips over its eyes whenever something lands. It likes an acorn cap anywhere and bounces for one on the ground. It dislikes nothing: a tumble makes it laugh. At evening it gets into every acorn cap.

By day each of them answers what the child just set down, by these tastes and in its own motion, and that is the feedback. Each also answers a poke as itself: the fat one's belly rings like a drum and it hiccups, the tidy one's nose is pressed flat and springs back with a sniff, the tiny one's hat drops over its eyes and it pushes it up with a squeak. Round them, and nothing to do with the house: a snail on the left root, a moth, and an owl in a knot hole of the trunk. Poked, the snail pulls in its horns with a blip, the moth darts off with a flutter, and the owl ducks with a hoot.

## The scenes

- **The evening.** Cause: the child drags the sun below the hill and lets go. The sun stands over the hill at the right of the frame at every surface size, and neither it nor any found thing is ever under the home control at the top centre. Beats: the light turns to dusk and fireflies come out; the fat one climbs down and tries every leaf from the left; the tiny one gets into every acorn cap; the tidy one sweeps with the first feather from the right; the three settle where they end, or in a heap on the floor; the lamps stay lit. Each folk's part is filled in from the house: which cells of its thing exist, in what order along the hollow, and what lies below a bath. It lasts 10 to 25 seconds, as an ending may. **Saved when it starts:** `finished` (true), `snapped`, `soaked`, `asleep`, and `things` where a thing was moved (a roof that fell, a cloth pulled off, a bark that sailed). Any touch ends it: every beat lands where it was going, without its sounds, and the touch is then an ordinary touch.
- **The morning.** Cause: the child drags the sun back above the hill and lets go. Beats: the light comes up, the three wake, stretch each in its own way and climb back to their places. Each one's part is filled in from `asleep`: where it lay and what it wakes on. It lasts 5 to 8 seconds. **Saved when it starts:** `finished` (false) and `asleep` (empty). `things`, `snapped` and `soaked` stay as the evening left them. Any touch ends it the same way.

**How a cycle ends and the next begins.** The evening is the ending: the house lived in, lit and still, for as long as the child likes. While it stands no thing in the hollow is carried, since one of the folk may lie on it: a touch on a thing makes it hop with its own sound, a touch on a sleeper is answered by that one as itself, and neither changes the house or the saved state. The sun is the one thing that can be dragged. Nothing follows it by itself. The next day begins when the child raises the sun, which lies on the hill in plain sight. By day the house is as the folk left it and can be changed; a thing that is moved back to the forest floor is whole and dry. A game put away during the evening opens on the finished evening, and one put away by day opens by day; no scene plays on load.

## The look

**Coloured-pencil storybook**, the first look reserved for the game in the ledger of `docs/art-direction.md`. The spike passed on clarity at 1180×820, so the second reserved look was not tried. Frame rate is measured by the lead on the target, not here.

**What the frame holds.** One place seen from the front, filled from edge to edge: a great tree whose trunk leaves the top of the frame, the hollow between its roots in the middle, pale far trunks and crowns behind, bushes with flowers, a hill at the right with the sun over it, ferns coming in from the sides and corners, toadstools, and the forest floor along the bottom with the found things lying on it. On a surface of another shape the wood goes on past the stage's edges: more trunks, crowns, bushes and toadstools, never a blank band. The three folk are large and stand in the frame from the first still: the fat one at the left on the root, the tiny one on the shoulder of the right root, the tidy one beside it. An owl looks out of a knot hole, a snail creeps on the left root and a moth flies over the left of the wood. The sun stands over the hill near the right edge of the stage. The round a finger takes it by runs on an upright track that ends behind the hill; the sun as it is drawn leans out to the right inside that round as it goes down, so that its whole disc is seen beside the tidy one's hat all the way to the hill (`overlap.test.ts`). No cloud, far tree or fern lies on that track, and no part of the sun's touch shape reaches the top right corner. Under the sun the hill's line is the same at every width of surface.

**Two places are kept plain on purpose**, since they are where the child works: the back wall of the hollow and the forest floor. Both are hatched all over in a pale warm tone, so neither is bare paper, and neither has anything on it that could be taken for a thing to pick up. The floor's marks (needles, crumbs of earth, clover) are a quarter of the size of the smallest found thing and far paler. The upper half of the back wall carries the tree's own marks in the wall's own pale browns: a drop-shaped knot with the grain bending round it, the small angular stub of a broken branch, a root hanging from the top of the arch and two shelf fungi on its rim. The root step at the left of the hollow is drawn as a root: rounded, with grain along it and a toe that runs down to the floor.

**The hollow is seen through its opening.** It is wider inside than its mouth, so the end of a thing that reaches past the opening is behind the trunk and is not drawn over the bark (`view.ts` clips what lies in the hollow to the arch). A thing in the hand, in the air or on the forest floor is never clipped. A thing is touched only where it is seen: behind the trunk the bark answers.

**Materials.** Everything is coloured pencil on cream paper (`pencil.ts`).

- A shape is filled with hatching in three pressures of one pencil: a light layer over all of it but the lit part, a second layer across the first where the form turns from the light, and short strokes pressed hard in the deepest shade. Strokes are short runs with the hand lifting now and then, a little uneven in spacing and strength.
- The edge is a soft contour in the pencil's own dark, drawn twice a little apart. There is no ink outline and no flat fill. The exceptions are the faces (eyes, brows and mouths in one dark brown pencil, since they move) and the ghost hand of the idle cue, which has a firm line so that it reads over anything. An eye that shuts is a lash: one stroke that bows down, its outer end lower than its inner end, with no white and no pupil. An open eye is never drawn lower than three tenths of its width.
- Light comes from the upper left. The lit side of a round thing is left as paper.
- White is the paper: clouds, the spots on a toadstool, the gleam on a pebble and the whites of eyes are paper left or laid back.
- The found things are the strongest pencils on the page, one hue each: amber leaf, brown acorn cap with a pale inside, blue-grey pebble, red-brown bark with a pale split face, dark twig, bright green moss, jay-blue feather. They have no face and move only when moved. Each of the four twigs is its own: one bowed right with a long shoot high on the right, one bowed left with a stub low on the left and a bud under its tip, one straight with a long shoot on the left halfway up, one with a dog-leg and a small shoot each side (`marks.ts`). A twig is drawn as the one it is wherever it stands, whole or snapped. The wood behind them is pressed more lightly and in quieter hues, so a thing reads against it anywhere.
- The folk are saturated and large: an orange-red coat and a teal nightcap, a violet coat and a dark green hat, a blue coat under a red toadstool hat with a yellow towel. No two share a hue or a silhouette: a wide egg, a tall bottle, a small ball under a wide cap.
- A lamp's light is yellow pencil pressed lightly in three rings. Water is a pale blue oval with a paper gleam. A shadow is soft violet-grey hatching.

**Palette.** All of it is in `HUES` in `pencil.ts`, each pencil at three pressures. Paper `#fdfaf2`. Sky blue, lilac far trunks, grey-green far crowns, two greens for bushes and ferns, a yellow-green hill, warm browns for trunk, root and the rim of the hollow, cream for its back wall, straw for the earth, a yellow sun.

**Motion.** Nothing is hatched in a frame, so the paper does not boil: a sprite keeps its strokes and moves whole.

- A found thing squashes and stretches about its underside, and each kind springs back at its own rate: moss slow and soft, a pebble hardly at all. A carried thing swings a little behind the finger. A feather drifts down from side to side, a leaf falls slowly, a pebble drops.
- Each cell that rocks, rolls, swings, spins or tips does so in its own number of turns, at its own pace and dying away (`cells.ts`). It knocks at each turn and is still with its last sound: a bell dings at the end of every swing, a cradle creaks at every rock, a bowl spins on its rim faster and faster and stops in a tok, a see-saw tips until its far end touches the ground with a donk, and tips over the other way, with a donk again, when a thing is set on its high end. Whatever lies on a see-saw lies on its slope, square to the slab's face, and turns with the slab as it swings: a small thing where it was let go, a wide one along the middle of the slab, and what is set on such a thing with it. Nothing lies level on a leaning slab. A bell or a flag on a mast that leans there hangs plumb from the mast's tip, where the tip is. A ball, a bowl or a rolling pin set on the slope lies still, and sounds its touch alone: the rumble, the spin and the purr belong to a level slab, where they are seen.
- A flag streams out level once and then hangs down beside its twig. The twig under a balanced pebble bows to the side the pebble was set down on and nods with it. Dew wells up in a bath from the bottom. Moss bulges at the ends of a bed and round a pillow stone, and a thatch sags between two tops.
- What sheds a thing decides how it leaves: a leaf flips it up high, turning right over; an acorn cap bounces it straight up, and it comes down on the cap once more; a feather lets it slide off sideways, low. A thing is shed only by the thing its middle is over, from that thing's top.
- A thing with no room where it was let go comes straight down, knocks where it first touches, and slides along to the nearest free place, where it lands with its own sound. It passes no other thing on the way. A thing held under a roof, a table, a perch or a thatch has its shadow on the ground under it, and goes there when it is let go; held over it, its shadow is on the roof.
- Nothing is in another place from one step to the next. A thing one of the three takes up is seen to go onto its head or into its hand and back; whoever stands on a thing that leaves drops off it and lands on the ground in its own way, the fat one with a thump and dust, the tidy one stiffly, the tiny one with a bounce.
- Each of the three moves in parts, on springs of its own stiffness (`folk.ts`): the fat one slow with a belly that wobbles on, the tidy one quick and exact with a nose that twitches, the tiny one bouncy with legs that never stop swinging and a hat that slips over its eyes. Their eyes follow the thing in the hand, each at its own speed, and each has three idle delights of its own, never the same one twice running.
- The sun bobs when touched, the owl ducks, the snail pulls into its shell, the moth darts. A touch on the bark shuts the owl's eyes for a moment and a touch on the sky sends the moth off; neither raises dust. Dust rises from the ground, where something lands on it or drops back onto it, and the tidy one sniffs at each puff by day; the one other dust is the ceiling's, which rises at the broom when the tidy one sweeps up there with a flag. A touch on a big root shows a beetle's legs and feelers stirring under it, a touch on a bush shakes a few leaves out of it, and a touch on the far hill, or in the air just over its back, sends up four far birds, small: bent wings, no two of a length or as high (`marks.ts`). A found thing has no motion of its own: a lit lamp's light is steady and a flag hangs still until something knocks it.
- A twig or a slab laid on one twig alone lies tipped to the side it was set down on, and levels when something is set on it.

**The evening.** The dusk is a second drawing of the same wood, made once with the first: the day's wood under two open layers of blue-violet pencil, with a few stars as dots of paper, and inside the hollow the day's wall laid back under one thin layer, so that the house reads as a warm room. Dragging the sun down brings that drawing down from the top of the frame with it, and under its edge three narrow bands of it thin out so that the light has no hard line. The sun sinks behind the hill's own outline. Fireflies are small yellow dots, each on a loop of its own. The found things and the three keep their day colours, so they are what is lit.

- The three walk into the hollow at 0.62 of their size round the tree. On its way from place to place one of them is drawn behind what stands in the hollow; where it stops it stands in front, on a side of its thing where the ground is free. What one of them is in, wears or holds (a bed, a bath, a lamp as a hat, a feather as a broom) is drawn in front of it. So is whatever lies over the bed where the sleeper lies: a roof that came down on its stumps beside the bed and reaches over it, the edge of a table. The sleeper is under it.
- What one of the three holds or wears is part of it to a finger: the tidy one's dustpan and nose, the tiny one's hat and brush are touched where they are drawn (`HELD` in `folk.ts`), so a new held thing is given its box there.
- With no feather the tidy one steps to one end of the widest free floor and sweeps toward the other with its hat, leaning only as far as that floor is free.
- Each act of the second grid is its own motion (`acts.ts`): where the body goes, how it turns, and what its face and its funniest part do. Each of the three has its own walk, its own way of sleeping (the fat one sunk and snoring, the tidy one stiff, leaning on the fat one or nodding toward the tiny one beside it and never out over a neighbour, the tiny one under its hat) and its own way of waking.
- A soaked thing has three drops under it, of different lengths. The water a bath spills lies along the ground as one puddle, thick in some places and thin in others, and fades. A snapped twig is a short post with a split top, splintered in its own way. A roof whose twigs snap comes straight down where it stood.

**Tiers.** A tier sets the pixel ratio and nothing else (`config.ts`). The two drawings of the wood and every sprite are made once when the surface is sized, at the finest ratio any tier uses, so a lower tier stamps the same pictures smaller and the look does not change; a tier change redraws nothing. A frame is one stamp for the wood, one for each sprite and a few live paths (the faces in three, the idle cue, the dust, drops, fluff, leaves, stirring legs and far birds by kind, the fireflies, the drips, the spilt water): 37 draws in the first frame, 42 in the fullest frame by day and 47 at the fullest moment of the evening, counted as sprite stamps plus live paths (`frameBudget.test.ts`). While the sun moves the wood is stamped in two parts that add up to one surface, and the three bands under the dusk's edge add a tenth of a surface.

**What would break the look.** A flat fill, a hard black outline round a body, a gradient or a glow made by blur, a drop shadow, hatching redrawn each frame, a found thing with a face or a motion of its own, a dusk laid on as one flat tint, or a mark that reads as a letter, a numeral or a sign (even rows of equal strokes, two like strokes or two like ovals side by side, a lone ring, a level bar). That holds outside a face. Inside one, two brows over two eyes and a round open mouth under them are those very marks and read as a face; they are never drawn apart from the eyes, and they move with every answer. The small marks that could be drawn evenly by mistake have their geometry in `marks.ts`, and `marks.test.ts` holds each of them uneven: the nightcap's stripes, the hand's creases, the wall's marks, the puddle, the lashes, the four twigs, the brush's bristles, the towel's fringe, the far birds and the grain by the wall's knot (one bowed curve over it, and no stroke under it).
