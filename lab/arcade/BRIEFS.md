# Arcade round: the 30 briefs

Each prototype borrows a loop children already play in large numbers (see `RESEARCH.md`). A brief fixes the verb, the fantasy and the payoff. The builder owns everything else and is expected to make it feel good in the hand: if a detail here fights the fun, the fun wins, and the builder says so in the report.

Ages are the intended band. "Hooks" are scores, upgrades and the like; the lab allows them.

---

## Ages 2 to 5: toys with a payoff

### feed-the-monster
**Feed the Monster** · 👾 · ages 2 to 5 · like Toca Kitchen, My Talking Tom

A big, round, hungry monster fills the middle of the screen with a huge mouth that opens when food comes near and eyes that follow the finger. A shelf of silly foods sits along the top or side. Drag anything into the mouth: it chomps, swallows with a gulp that travels down, and reacts differently to each thing. Chilli: goes red, steam from the ears, breathes fire. Ice cream: brain freeze, turns blue, shivers. Sock or fish: disgusted face, spits it out across the screen. Beans or soda: a burp that blows its cheeks out. Cake: hearts and a happy wiggle. The belly visibly grows with each bite until a giant screen-shaking burp (or a fart, this age loves it) resets it with confetti. Foods regrow on the shelf with a pop.

Feel: the mouth tracks the food, the chomp is crunchy and on time, the body jiggles like jelly (springs). At least eight foods with at least six distinct reactions; a few rare foods appear later (a whole watermelon, a rainbow, a boot). No wrong food, no fail. Poking the monster itself tickles it.

### wild-hair-salon
**Wild Hair Salon** · 💇 · ages 3 to 7 · like Toca Hair Salon

One big animal face (a lion, then others) with a lot of hair made of strands that sway. Tools in a row at the side, big: scissors, grow tonic, colour sprays (three or four colours), a hairdryer, a shaver. Pick a tool by tapping it, then rub the hair: scissors snip strands where the finger passes and the cut ends fall; tonic makes hair under the finger grow long fast; spray colours it; the dryer blows it away from the finger; the shaver buzzes it to stubble. The animal reacts the whole time: giggles when tickled, sneezes from the dryer, looks wide-eyed when suddenly bald, admires itself. A "done" bell (a mirror or camera button) flashes a photo with a fanfare and brings the next animal with a different hair shape (a poodle, a yak, a porcupine with spikes).

Feel: hair is the toy. Strands are short chains or curves with springy follow-through; cutting is instant and makes a snip per strand with falling clippings that stay on the floor. Hair regrows slowly by itself so it is never ruined. No wrong haircut.

### muddy-truck-wash
**Muddy Truck Wash** · 🚜 · ages 2 to 5 · like PowerWash Simulator, car-wash toddler apps

A big friendly vehicle with a face (a digger first; then a tractor, a monster truck, a fire engine, a bus) drives in caked in mud. Rub with the finger to scrub: mud wipes away under the finger to reveal bright shiny paint, with foam, bubbles and squeaks. Two or three big tools at the side change the rub: sponge (foam), hose (a water jet that blasts mud and splashes), and at the end a polish cloth that makes sparkles. When the vehicle is clean it beams, honks, shines with a glint sweep and drives off; the next one rolls in dirtier, with mud in new places (and sometimes leaves, bird poo, or paint splats).

Feel: the reveal is the payoff, so the mud must come off in satisfying soft-edged swaths (draw mud on an offscreen canvas and erase with `destination-out`), with a progress feel but no meter needed. The vehicle reacts: giggles, closes its eyes when sprayed in the face, bounces on its suspension. Before washing, the child can also tap puddles on the ground to splash more mud on: getting it dirty is half the fun. No fail.

### surprise-eggs
**Surprise Eggs** · 🥚 · ages 3 to 7 · like surprise-egg videos, gacha capsules, Adopt Me hatching

A nest or conveyor of big wobbling eggs, each with its own pattern. Tap an egg: it cracks a little more with each tap (three to five taps, louder and shakier each time), then bursts open and a creature pops out with a flourish and its own sound and little animation, then hops to a shelf or meadow behind where the collection lives and keeps idling. Most are common (chick, frog, turtle); some are rare (a unicorn, a dragon, a robot, a tiny dinosaur) and get a much bigger reveal: glow through the cracks beforehand, slow-motion, rainbow, fanfare. Occasionally a gag: an egg inside an egg, a sock, a grumpy old rooster.

Feel: anticipation is the mechanic. The egg wobbles on its own, rattles when something rare is inside, the cracks spread visibly, and the final tap has a beat of hit-stop before the pop. New eggs roll in immediately. The collection visibly grows (twenty or more possible creatures, a simple "new!" marker), and tapping a collected creature makes it do its trick. No fail, no timer.

### boo-boo-vet
**Boo-Boo Vet** · 🩹 · ages 3 to 6 · like hospital and doctor games on the under-5 chart, Sago Mini

A sad animal patient sits in the middle with one or two obvious problems drawn big: a thorn in a paw, a bump on the head, muddy fur, a runny nose, hiccups, a splinter, a cold (shivering), spots. Four or five oversized tools wait on a tray: plaster, tweezers, ice pack or blanket, sponge, thermometer or medicine spoon, tissue. Drag a tool onto the problem and it fixes it with a clear little animation (thorn pops out, bump shrinks under the ice, plaster sticks on with a pat). The "wrong" tool still does something funny and harmless (a tickle, a squirt, a plaster on the nose) and the animal giggles. When every problem is fixed the animal jumps up, does a happy dance, gives a heart hug and trots off; the next patient walks in.

Feel: sad-to-happy is the payoff, so the face change must be big and immediate. The problem spot glows softly; the matching tool wiggles as the idle hint. At least five animals and seven problems, two problems at once after the first few patients. No fail, no timer.

### balloon-pop-parade
**Balloon Pop Parade** · 🎈 · ages 2 to 5 · like balloon-pop and pop-it apps, Bluey keepy-uppy

Balloons of many colours and sizes drift up from the bottom in a steady, generous stream. Tap one: it pops with a snap, confetti, and a pitch that climbs while the child keeps popping. Many balloons carry something that falls when popped: a toy that bounces on the ground, a creature that parachutes down and waves, a splash of paint that lands on the scenery and stays, a shower of stars. Special balloons appear more often as play goes on: a giant one that needs several taps and explodes into many small ones, a water balloon that drenches a character below, a long wiggly one that zooms around deflating, a golden one that pops every balloon on screen in a chain.

Feel: generous hit areas (the whole balloon plus margin), pops on touch-down, dragging a finger across pops everything it crosses. Balloons wobble, bump each other softly and turn to look at the finger. The ground fills up with the things that fell and they react to new arrivals. Never an empty sky, no fail, nothing to miss that matters.

### fire-truck-hero
**Fire Truck Hero** · 🚒 · ages 3 to 6 · like PAW Patrol Rescue World, DUPLO rescue

A row of little houses and trees; small cartoon fires pop up on them (on roofs, in windows, on a barbecue, on a birthday cake). A fire truck with a face sits at the bottom with a hose. Touch and hold anywhere: the hose sprays a thick arcing jet of water that lands where the finger is. Drag to sweep it. Water on a fire makes it hiss, shrink and go out with steam; the building sighs with relief. Water on anything else does something too: flowers bloom, a dog shakes itself dry, a cat scrambles away indignantly, windows go squeaky clean, a person opens an umbrella. Every so often someone waves from a window and a tap or a spray near them lowers the ladder for a rescue and a cheer.

Feel: the water jet is the toy: a stream of droplets under gravity with splashes where it lands, a continuous hiss, and the truck leaning back from the recoil. Fires are never scary (round, orange, with little faces) and nothing burns down; they only get a bit bigger and sillier if ignored. New fire types arrive over time (a run of fires in a row, one that hops between roofs, a dragon who keeps sneezing them). No fail.

### choo-choo-draw
**Choo-Choo Draw** · 🚂 · ages 3 to 6 · like BRIO World Railway, Thomas track builders

A green landscape with stations where animals wait with luggage. A little steam train with a face sits on a short piece of track. Drag from the train (or from the end of the track) and track is laid under the finger as a smooth curve of sleepers and rails, as long as the drag lasts. The train follows the track at its own pace, puffing, its carriages swinging behind. When the track passes a waiting animal it hops aboard with a cheer; when it reaches a station, the passengers hop off and dance. The train keeps going to the end of the track and waits there, tooting, for more. Drawing loops, zigzags and hills is the fun: the carriages follow every wiggle.

Feel: the line appears exactly under the finger with a clackety sound, smoothed so a wobbly toddler line still becomes nice track. The train's motion along the curve is the payoff: lean into corners, smoke puffs, a whistle when tapped. Things to discover along the way: a tunnel that plays an echo, a bridge over water, cows that moo as it passes, a level crossing. Old track fades away behind the last carriage so the screen never fills. No fail: track can go anywhere.

## Ages 4 to 9: one verb, big payoff

### fruit-slicer
**Fruit Slicer** · 🍉 · ages 4 to 9 · like Fruit Ninja (1B downloads)

Fruit is tossed up from the bottom in arcs. Swipe through it to slice: the fruit splits into two halves that tumble away, juice sprays along the direction of the cut, and a splat of that fruit's colour stays on the back wall and slowly fades. The swipe leaves a bright blade trail. Slicing several fruit in one swipe is a combo with a climbing pitch and a big floating number. There are no bombs; instead an occasional grumpy cactus or stinky sock flies up, and slicing it is a funny "ew" moment that breaks the streak and nothing worse.

Feel: this lives or dies on the slice. Detect the swipe segment crossing the fruit (generous radius), cut along the swipe angle, a few milliseconds of hit-stop on a multi-slice, juice particles, wet splat sound with pitch by fruit size. Waves escalate: singles, then fans of five, then a giant watermelon that takes many slices and bursts into a fruit shower, then a "frenzy" when fruit pours from both sides. Missed fruit just falls; nothing is lost. Hooks: a streak counter and best combo.

### slingshot-smash
**Slingshot Smash** · 🏰 · ages 4 to 9 · like Angry Birds

A slingshot on the left, a wobbly tower of blocks on the right with a few smug creatures sitting in it (jelly monsters, not pigs). Drag back from the slingshot: the band stretches, a dotted arc shows roughly where it will go, the projectile creature squints and squeaks louder the further you pull. Release to fling. The tower takes the hit and collapses with real physics (use matter-js): blocks tumble, creatures go "wah!" and poof into stars when knocked hard or when they fall off. Clear the creatures and a new, different tower builds itself with a little construction jingle; shots are unlimited, so there is no failing, only how few shots it took.

Feel: anticipation (the stretch), release (whoosh and a trail), impact (hit-stop, shake scaled to impact speed, dust, wooden clonks pitched by block size). Three materials that behave differently (wood, ice that shatters, stone that is heavy). After a few towers, new projectiles rotate in: one that splits into three when tapped mid-air, a heavy one, one that explodes on a tap. Towers are generated from a handful of templates so each one falls differently.

### hungry-hole
**Hungry Hole** · 🕳️ · ages 4 to 9 · like Hole.io (420M), Donut County, Katamari

A top-down picnic park (or toy-strewn bedroom) full of things of every size. The child drags a hole around the ground; it follows the finger with a slight lag. Anything smaller than the hole that it slides under tips in, shrinks and falls with a "bloop", and the hole grows a little. At first only crumbs, berries and ants fit; soon sandwiches and balls; then picnic baskets, dogs (who yelp comically and are fine), benches, trees, cars, and finally the whole bandstand. Things too big to swallow wobble on the rim and teeter, which shows what to come back for.

Feel: the fall-in is the payoff: objects tilt toward the hole, slide, spin and drop with a pitch that drops by size; the hole's rim pulses when it grows and a "size up!" moment zooms the view out a step so the world feels bigger each time. Eat everything and the level ends with everything the hole swallowed fountaining back out as confetti, then a new, denser scene. Some objects run away (a squirrel, a ball that rolls) for a little chase. No fail, no timer by default. Hooks: size level, and a gentle "everything eaten" completion.

### snack-merge
**Snack Merge** · 🍩 · ages 5 to 10 · like Suika Game

A tall glass jar. A snack hangs at the top under the finger: drag left and right to aim, lift to drop. Snacks are round and have little faces. Two of the same that touch merge with a pop into the next bigger snack (a chain of about ten: blueberry, cherry, cookie, donut, burger, pizza, cake and so on, ending in something absurd and huge). Merges can set off chain reactions as the pile shifts. The pile wobbles and settles with soft physics.

Feel: the chain merge is the payoff: each merge in a chain plays a higher note, the new snack pops in with overshoot and shoves its neighbours, faces react (worried when squeezed, delighted when merging). Circle physics can be hand-written (positions, overlap resolution, a little damping) or matter-js; either way the pile must feel squishy, not jittery, and never explode. When the jar overflows, do not end the game: the top snacks spill out comically and the jar burps, clearing some room. Show the next snack. First time making each new snack gets a named fanfare ("DONUT!"). Hooks: score from merges, biggest snack reached.

### animal-tower
**Animal Tower** · 🦒 · ages 4 to 9 · like Animal Tower Battle, Stack

A small platform. An animal hangs from a crane at the top, swinging gently; drag to position, lift to drop. Animals have funny, awkward shapes (a long giraffe, a round hippo, a flat crocodile, a spiky hedgehog, an elephant with a trunk) and real physics (matter-js compound or polygon bodies), so the tower is wobbly and ridiculous. The tower's height is the goal: a height line rises, and new heights get a cheer. When the tower topples, animals tumble off with comic yelps and bounce away unharmed, and whatever remains on the platform stays; the child just keeps stacking.

Feel: the wobble is the fun: soft landings with squash, animals blink and look nervous as the tower sways, a creak as it leans. Tapping the hanging animal (or a rotate button) rotates it a quarter turn for older children; younger ones can just drop. The camera slides up as the tower grows. Surprises in the queue: a tiny mouse, a huge whale, a wobbling jelly, a bird that flaps off after three seconds. Hooks: best height. No game over.

### whack-a-mole
**Whack-a-Mole** · 🔨 · ages 3 to 7 · like the arcade cabinet and every toddler tap game

A garden with nine big holes. Moles pop up with a cheeky look and a raspberry; tap one and a big soft hammer bonks it: the mole squashes flat, stars circle its head, it sinks with a dizzy face. Moles stay up long enough for a four-year-old (generous at first, a little shorter as the streak builds, easing off again after misses). Variety arrives steadily: a mole in a hard hat that needs two bonks, a golden mole worth a shower of coins, a mole that holds up a carrot to swap holes, a giant mole boss that takes ten fast taps and explodes into little moles, and a bunny that should not be bonked (bonk it anyway and it just looks offended and boxes the hammer away).

Feel: the bonk is the payoff: a hammer swings in an arc to the tap point, hit-stop, squash to a pancake, "bonk" sound with random pitch, screen nudge. Tapping an empty hole thumps the lawn and a worm peeks out. Moles taunt (tongue out, wiggle) before ducking. No timer that ends play; rounds roll on with a little intermission dance every twenty bonks.

### claw-machine
**Claw Machine** · 🕹️ · ages 4 to 9 · like arcade claw machines, gacha capsules

A claw machine full of plush toys and capsules in a heap. The claw slides along the top following the finger (drag anywhere to move it left and right); lift the finger (or tap the big button) and it drops, closes, and rises with whatever it grabbed, swinging. Then it carries the prize to the chute and drops it in. Plush toys go straight to a shelf of prizes; capsules crack open on the way for a surprise, sometimes rare. The grab is kind: it nearly always holds something, but heavy or awkward prizes can slip on the way up with a groan from the machine, which is the suspense.

Feel: the wait on the way up is the mechanic: the claw sways, the prize dangles and wobbles, a rising drumroll tone, then a jingle at the chute. The heap is physical enough that prizes shift and tumble when one is pulled out (simple circle physics). Rare golden prizes sit half-buried so digging toward them takes several grabs. The shelf of won prizes fills visibly, and each prize does a little trick when tapped. Refills pour in from the top when the heap runs low. No coins, no fail.

### monster-pizza
**Monster Pizza** · 🍕 · ages 4 to 8 · like Papa's Pizzeria, Toca Kitchen, Bluey's pizza oven

A pizza base sits on the counter. A monster customer waits with a thought bubble showing two or three toppings as pictures. Drag sauce across the base to spread it (it paints on), then drag or tap toppings from big bowls (cheese, pepperoni, mushrooms, and silly ones: worms, eyeballs, socks, slime, gummy bears); they land with a plop where dropped. Push the pizza into the oven: it bakes in two seconds with a glow and a ding, cheese bubbles. Slide it to the monster: it gobbles the pizza in three bites and reacts. If it matches the bubble: delight, a coin tip, a happy dance. If not, it is never "wrong": the monster pulls a face at the sock, shrugs, eats it anyway and burps. Next customer: a different monster with a different order and its own silly favourite.

Feel: the making is the toy (sauce smears, toppings plop and wobble, the oven roars) and the reaction is the payoff. Orders grow from one topping to three; later a fussy customer wants "no mushrooms" (a crossed-out picture) and a huge monster wants a pizza with everything. Hooks: tips that unlock two or three new silly toppings.

### draw-a-bridge
**Draw a Bridge** · ✏️ · ages 5 to 10 · like Crayon Physics, Brain Dots (50M), Draw Bridge games

A little car with a face wants to reach a flag (or a friend) across a gap, a pit or a step. Draw any line with a finger: when the finger lifts, the line becomes a solid crayon object that falls under gravity and lands (matter-js: a chain of rectangles or a compound body). Draw a bridge across the gap, a ramp up a step, a wedge to stop a rolling boulder. Then the car sets off by itself (or on a tap of a big "go") and drives over whatever was drawn. If it falls in, it lands in a ball pit with a "wheee", hops back to the start and the drawing stays or can be wiped with a tap on an eraser.

Feel: the child's scribble becoming a real, heavy object is the magic, so the crayon line must look hand-drawn, land with a thud and dust, and bear the car's weight believably. Ten to twelve short levels that teach one idea each (gap, step, two gaps, a hill that needs a ramp, a ball that must be pushed onto a button), generated or hand-laid, cycling with variations. Any solution that works counts; silly ones are celebrated. Success: confetti, honk, next level in under two seconds.

### dig-for-the-duck
**Dig for the Duck** · 💧 · ages 5 to 9 · like Where's My Water, Sandspiel

A cross-section of earth. Water sits in a pool near the top; a rubber duck in an empty bathtub waits at the bottom. Rub with a finger to dig tunnels through the dirt; water pours through whatever is dug, following gravity, pooling and spilling. Get enough water into the tub and the duck floats up, quacks, and the tub overflows with bubbles. Then the next dig: rocks that cannot be dug, a second pool, mud that turns water brown (the duck does not mind, but a clean tub gets a sparkle), a sponge that soaks water up, a plug of grass to dig around, a little mole who digs his own random tunnel.

Feel: the falling water is the toy: a cellular-automaton grid (cells of about 8 pixels; water falls, spreads sideways, settles) drawn smooth enough to look wet, with a trickling sound that scales with how much is moving. Digging is instant under the finger with crumbling dirt particles. There is always more water than needed and it slowly refills, so a level cannot become unwinnable. Hidden in the dirt: gems, bones and a buried treasure chest that pop out when uncovered.

### hop-across
**Hop Across** · 🐸 · ages 5 to 9 · like Crossy Road (200M), Frogger

A frog (or chicken) on a lane-based world scrolling upward: grass, roads with toy cars, rivers with floating logs and lily pads, train tracks. Tap to hop one lane forward; swipe left or right to hop sideways (a tap on the left or right third of the screen also works). Getting bumped by a car is slapstick, never grim: the frog goes flat as a pancake, peels off with a "boing", and is back at the last safe lane within a second. Falling in the river is a splash and a fish spitting it back out.

Feel: the hop is the verb and must be perfect: a quick arc with squash on landing, a different sound per surface, instant response even mid-hop (buffer one input). Cars honk and drivers look shocked. Lanes are generated in gentle bands that get busier with distance; slow lanes first. Collect flies or coins along the way; every ten lanes is a checkpoint flag with a cheer, and retries restart from the last flag. Rare sights to discover: a golden car, a whale in the river, a UFO lane. Hooks: distance, best distance.

### peg-blaster
**Peg Blaster** · 🔮 · ages 5 to 10 · like Peggle, pachinko

A cannon at the top centre, a field of round pegs below arranged in a pretty pattern, and a bucket sliding back and forth along the bottom. Drag anywhere to aim (a dotted line shows the first bounce), lift to fire a ball. It bounces off pegs with real restitution; every peg it touches lights up, plays the next note of a rising scale, and vanishes once the ball is gone. Orange pegs are the ones to clear; clear them all and the final hit goes into slow motion with a zoom, a drumroll and a rainbow finish. Land the ball in the moving bucket for a free ball.

Feel: the long bounce chain with its climbing scale is the payoff. Ball physics hand-written (circle against circle), lively but not frantic. Special pegs: a green one that splits the ball in two, a purple bonus, a bumper that kicks. Ten or so layouts (circles, smiley, waves, a heart), picked in turn. Balls are generous: run out and the board just refills some pegs and carries on, so there is no losing, only fewer-shots-is-better. Hooks: score with a multiplier as orange pegs run low.

## Ages 6 to 12: mastery, mischief, numbers going up

### egg-heist
**Egg Heist** · 🦆 · ages 6 to 11 · like Steal a Brainrot (25.8M concurrent), Untitled Goose Game

Top-down farmyard. The child is a sneaky raccoon; home is a den on the left. Across the yard are nests with eggs of different rarity (white, spotted, golden, a glowing dragon egg far away behind hedges). A big goose guards them, patrolling with a visible vision cone; a sleepy dog and a farmer join later. Drag (the raccoon runs toward the finger) to sneak in, touch an egg to grab it, and run home: carrying makes the raccoon slower and the rarer the egg the heavier. If the goose sees it, an exclamation mark, furious honking and a chase; if caught, the raccoon drops the egg, gets comically pecked and scampers home empty-handed (no other penalty). Eggs brought home hatch into little pets of matching rarity that sit by the den and earn coins over time; coins buy speed shoes, a decoy (a thrown tin can), and a bush costume.

Feel: tension and cheek: tiptoe sounds, the goose's head whipping round, a heartbeat when the cone is near, triumphant jingle on a steal. Hiding in bushes breaks the chase. Hooks: coins, pet collection with rarities, upgrades. The far golden and dragon eggs are the "one more try".

### mutant-garden
**Mutant Garden** · 🌱 · ages 6 to 11 · like Grow a Garden (22.3M concurrent)

A garden of a dozen soil plots. Tap a plot to plant the selected seed; crops grow in seconds (not minutes) through visible stages and bounce when ripe. Tap or swipe across ripe crops to harvest: they fly into a basket and turn to coins. A seed shop along the bottom sells a ladder of stranger seeds (carrot, tomato, pumpkin, then candy cane bush, dragon fruit, moon melon); it restocks every 30 seconds or so with a chance of a rare seed. Weather rolls through: rain speeds growth, a rainbow can turn crops golden (worth ten times), a thunderstorm can make one giant, a frost makes them sparkle. Mutations stack and are announced loudly.

Feel: planting is a satisfying dig-and-pat, growth pulses, harvest is a swipeable cascade with a climbing coin scale. Numbers go up fast: prices and yields roughly triple per tier so a new seed is always a few harvests away. Things to hope for: a rare seed in the shop, a mutation, a wandering crow to shoo (or a friendly bee that doubles a plant). More plots unlock with coins. Hooks: coins, seed tiers, mutations, a small "collection" of every crop and mutation seen.

### deep-sea-fishing
**Deep Sea Fishing** · 🎣 · ages 6 to 10 · like Tiny Fishing (Coolmath top 10), Ridiculous Fishing

A little boat on the surface. Tap to cast: the hook sinks fast to its maximum depth while the view scrolls down past layers of sea; on the way down, steer left and right with the finger to avoid fish (touching one ends the descent early). When it reaches the bottom of its line, it reels up, and now steering into fish hooks them, up to the line's capacity; rarer, more valuable fish live deeper. At the surface the catch flies up into the air and each fish pays out with a coin burst, biggest last. Coins buy a longer line, more hooks, and an income for fish caught while away from the rod; each depth band has new creatures (sardines, clownfish, jellyfish that tangle, an anglerfish, a treasure chest, a kraken at the very bottom).

Feel: the two-phase dip is the hook: tense dodging down, greedy gathering up. The payout fountain is the payoff and scales with the catch. Upgrades are priced so every cast or two buys something and the next depth band is always visible as a silhouette. Hooks: coins, depth record, a fish collection with rarities.

### lane-runner
**Lane Runner** · 🏃 · ages 6 to 11 · like Subway Surfers (#1 on Poki, 191M downloads in 2025), Temple Run

An endless run down three lanes drawn in simple perspective (things grow as they approach). The runner (a kid on a skateboard or a fox) runs by itself. Swipe left or right to change lane, swipe up to jump, swipe down to roll under; taps on the left and right halves also change lane. Obstacles: barriers to jump, high beams to roll under, parked carts to switch lanes around, moving trains. Lines of coins pull the player along good paths. Speed rises gently. A crash is a comic tumble with stars and a one-second rewind to just before it, keeping the coins but resetting the speed and streak; there is no game-over screen.

Feel: lane changes must be instant and snappy (a lean, a swoosh, about 100 ms), jumps floaty and forgiving, near-misses rewarded with a "whoa!" slow-mo blink and bonus. Power-ups: a magnet, a jetpack stretch above the lanes raining coins, a x2 star. The scenery changes zone every 30 seconds (city, beach, candy land). Hooks: distance, coins, best run.

### blob-dash
**Blob Dash** · ⚡ · ages 7 to 12 · like Geometry Dash (17.4M monthly), Run 3

A square blob with a face slides right at a constant speed through a neon course of spikes, blocks and gaps, synced to a driving beat the prototype synthesizes (kick and hat on a timer; obstacles placed on beats). Tap or hold anywhere to jump; jump pads, double-jump rings and a gravity flip arrive in later sections. Touch a spike and the blob pops into squares with a "bwomp" and is back at the start of the current short section in under half a second, the music continuing. Each section is about eight seconds; clearing one is a checkpoint with a flash and a rising stinger, and the attempt counter for that section shows how stubborn it was.

Feel: the jump arc is the whole game: fixed height, tuned so jumps land exactly on the beat, with a rotation of the square through the air that lands flat, squash on landing, a trail. Input buffering and a few frames of coyote time so it feels fair. The background pulses to the beat. Sections are hand-authored as short obstacle strings, ordered from trivial to tricky, and looped with higher speed. A ghost of the best attempt in this section runs alongside. Hooks: progress percent, attempts.

### micro-mayhem
**Micro Mayhem** · ⏱️ · ages 6 to 11 · like WarioWare, Dumb Ways to Die (360M)

A rapid string of four-to-five-second microgames, each announced by one shouted word and a picture, then played with one obvious gesture: "POP!" (tap all the balloons), "CATCH!" (drag a basket under falling eggs), "WAKE UP!" (tap the snoring dog fast), "DODGE!" (drag the chicken away from falling anvils), "SWIPE!" (swat a fly), "BALANCE!" (keep a wobbling plate stack centred), "FEED!" (drag the sandwich into the right mouth), "STOP!" (tap to stop a spinning wheel on the star), "SCRUB!" (wipe the window), "PICK!" (pull the one weed among the flowers). At least eight distinct microgames. Win or lose, a one-second funny result flashes and the next begins; speed rises every four games with a "FASTER!" jingle.

Feel: surprise and pace are the fun: the child never knows what is next, and every ending, win or fail, is a joke. Fails cost one of four hearts shown as a row of mascots; when they run out, the mascot gets a pie in the face and the run restarts immediately at slow speed, keeping the best streak on show.
