# Builder notes

What each prototype's builder reported when it finished, and what the independent skeptic found. These are the builders' own words, kept as written; they are not panel findings. The per-prototype `SPEC.md` files carry the panel results.

## broom-on-a-fingertip

- **Characteristic moment:** Three moments, each reached by a scripted controller in sim.test.ts that reads the snapshot like a child reads the screen. (1) Balance: a full-state feedback controller slides the fingertip under the lean (the finger is placed with the closed form of the fingertip's 40 ms spring follow) and keeps the mid broom up for over 12 s with 0 falls. A finger that grabs and holds still gets 1 fall. (2) Walk and plant: the same controller leans the broom toward the flag, holds it upright and still over the flag for 30 ticks, and the `plant` state event and `flags` hook event fire. The next flag lands at least 200 px away. (3) Swing-up mystery: a shaker feeds a hanging broom (pivot moved in time with its swing, stopped near swing energy 1.9) until it swings over the top and is caught, and the `swing-up` event fires. The signature then ends in `/swung`. Also asserted: the short broom falls faster than the mid one and the mid faster than the long one, rack taps swap brooms, an empty hooks list gives no hook events, no flag and no timer, same seed and script give the same signature and snapshot, and hints appear only when on and never change the outcome. This is a mastery engine, not variation, so no two-seed signature test is needed. Verified: `npm run lab:typecheck` clean; `npm run lab:test` passes 342 of 342, including the shared contract suite over this folder (meta, determinism, fuzz, affordance bounds, signature bound, hook switching, source hygiene, recipe files). In fuzz sessions of random play, 4 seeds produced 0 problems and 20 to 38 distinct signatures (bound 48). One of the four found the swing-up by chance.
- **Hooks declared:** flags, best
- **Builder-reported weaknesses:** 1) Control demand versus persona input. Keeping the broom up needs continuous, well-timed `move` events every tick or two. A persona that emits sparse jittery taps and drags will fall almost every time. The panel may then read the loop as never reaching its ceiling, which is a persona-model limit and not a sim limit. Check watch mode before trusting a low depth score. 2) Cue-blind touches only change the sim on the broom's column (150 px either side, top to bottom) and on the rack. A touch on a flag or empty field does nothing. The `flags` affordance is kind 'hold' at the flag, but the hit-test does nothing there unless the broom is near, so the clarity flag may read low. 3) The `best` hook changes no affordance and no signature, so the panel will report it as inconclusive by construction. The `flags` hook does change both and is measurable. 4) The rack reset is the cheap way back up after a fall. If personas always use it, the swing-up trick is found only by accident (it did happen once in four fuzz sessions). The `/swung` latch in the signature counts finding it as novelty. 5) The idle 'shake' hint (a wiggle line under the fingertip once the broom hangs) gives the trick away. It appears only with hints on, and the brief has return runs turn hints off. 6) Balancing from a lean above about 0.7 rad (40 degrees) is out of reach for a linear controller within the 1000 px rail. Real recoveries from big leans are hard by design, but very forgiving tuning was not attempted. 7) The `falls` counter re-arms only after the broom has stood again for 10 ticks. A broom that keeps swinging through the top counts one fall, not many. 8) The view was only smoke-checked against a fake canvas context in Node (155 draw calls, no throw). It was not looked at in a browser, and `lab:smoke` was not run.
- **Skeptic verdict:** pass
- **Skeptic's minor notes:**
  - INSTRUMENT FIT (read the panel result with this in mind): the panel personas cannot reach the balance moment, so a panel verdict of 'no depth' will be structural, not evidence against the idea. Persona gestures are a tap, a hold of at most 40 ticks (1.3 s), or a 3 to 6 step drag that ends in 'up', and 'up' releases the broom. Continuous correction is impossible, and bestRun needs `held`, so it is capped at about 1.3 s for any physics. I tried G=1100, 600, 350 and 200 with the persona driver and bestRun stayed at 1.0 to 1.3 s in every case. The builder's SPEC notes already say this. Do not 'fix' it with an assist, which would make the loop hollow.
  - uprightTime is the only `objective: 'up'` feature and can be maxed with no skill. The strategy is: tap a rack slot, put a finger on the broom, hold still about 1.6 s, repeat. It gave uprightTime 150 of 180 s on the long broom, 127 on mid and 100 on short, with bestRun only 1.1 to 1.6 s. Greedy self-play and self-set aims that push this feature would read a hollow strategy as progress. Fix in meta.ts: put `objective: 'up'` on bestRun (capped by the fall time under reset spam, over 60 s for a skilled child) and drop it from uprightTime. Alternatively, in sim.ts only count uprightTime for a run longer than about 3 s.
  - The swing-up latch is looser than 'stood up'. It fires when the broom stays inside 20 degrees for 10 ticks after 3 s or more low. Roughly half the events I sampled were fly-bys with energy 2.4 to 3.2, where the head is going over the top at 2 rad/s or more and cannot be caught. Random flailing sets `/swung` in 4 of 5 fuzz sessions, so the mystery half is cheap and the signature bit is nearly free novelty. Fix: at confirm time also require `holder !== null` and energy at most about 2.3, or require about 30 ticks upright instead of 10. Keep the 3 s low requirement.
  - Skilled play sits on one signature, `mid/up/open` (or the broom size it picked). Repeat-play change shows in the signature only through broom size, at-flag, and swung, and progress shows only in features (bestRun, wobble). A signature that folds in a coarse run-length class, such as 'held-up under 3 s / over 3 s / over 10 s', would let the panel see mastery. Optional.
  - The flags hook adds a 'hold' affordance on the flag rectangle, and a hold there does nothing unless the broom is already over it. Personas will aim holds at it and read them as ineffective. The builder documented that cue-blind touches only act on the broom column and the rack. Optional: leave the flag out of `affordances()`, or make a hold on the flag pull the fingertip toward it.
  - Random-play signature union is 48 of 48, so the bound is tight but honest and cannot be exceeded (3 brooms x 4 poses x 2 flag states x 2 swung states). The first uncontrolled fall of the mid broom takes about 2.0 s for seed 1, versus the idea note's 'a few seconds'. Naive chasing lasts about 1 to 2 s, so the first try is short but a plausible learner with a 100 ms delay holds it. Consider a slightly lower G if a longer first try is wanted.
  - Housekeeping: view.ts draws a permanent instruction line and a flag progress ring regardless of `hints`. That is allowed in the lab and the sim-level `hint` is correctly null when hints are off. Source hygiene is clean: no Math.random, Date or performance, only `../../kit` imports with `.ts` extensions, and `import type` for types.

## chant-rope

- **Characteristic moment:** One held leap hangs over BOTH quick swings and lands back on the slow beat (signature stand/pair-quick, state event pair-quick, streak 4, reach above 1). The test in lab/protos/chant-rope/sim.test.ts reads the snapshot to time the child's fingers. It holds the jumper to start the chant and lets go at beatTick 8, which hangs over the first slow swing. It gives the second slow swing a 2-tick hop, early in its window. Then it crouches with about 5 ticks of charge and lets go 2 ticks before the first quick swing. That leap hangs at least 17 ticks, past the second quick, and lands in beat 0 with the chant still turning. Other tests show that a plain hop for the first quick swing trips on the second, that holding on through a swing trips, and that a finger pressed in mid-air keeps charging through the landing. lab:typecheck and lab:test pass (12 files, 347 tests), and the contract suite runs for chant-rope.
- **Hooks declared:** streak, tempo
- **Builder-reported weaknesses:** Known weaknesses, from my own probing with noisy scripted players (not the panel).
(1) The three styles are only weakly separated once the player is noisy. At about 4 ticks (130 ms) of reaction noise, per-swing tapping, the quick-pair chunk and the long leap all trip on roughly one clear in three. A precise player gets a clear win for chunking, about 3.7 clears per trip for tapping, 10.7 for the quick-pair chunk and no trips at all for the long leap. So play-5 chunking may not beat tapping for a noisy 4-year-old persona.
(2) The long leap over slow swing 2 plus both quicks is the most reliable style, more than the quick-pair chunk the play-5 line names. It still hangs over both quicks and lands on the slow beat, so it fits the line, but it is a different signature (long, not pair-quick).
(3) The first press both starts the rope and crouches, so the very first tap is a wasted whiff hop. The chant restarts from beat 0 after every trip, so a child who trips often replays the easy first beat.
(4) Tempo level 2 (slow 21 ticks, quick 11) is nearly impossible for hop-per-swing play because the 7-tick landing squash does not scale down. It pushes toward big leaps, but a hop-only persona will just fall back to level 0.
(5) The streak hook changes no affordance and no signature, so the panel will read it as inconclusive.
(6) The seed only changes the turners' colours and cheers; the rope timing is identical every session by design, so there is no variation-engine signal.
(7) Wordless clarity is weak: the view relies on text (chant bar, help line), and there is no telegraph beyond the rope arc and the bar fill.
- **Skeptic verdict:** pass
- **Skeptic's minor notes:**
  - Without the `tempo` hook there is a rope-blind winning line. Press again the moment the jumper is airborne, hold a fixed 5 to 20 ticks, release, and repeat, after one opening hold of about 6 ticks. With hooks off, 470 of 1392 such settings never tripped in 3000 ticks, for example `h0=6, hr=5` gave 133 clears and 0 trips in a hop-slow/pair-quick cycle. Its period is about 30 ticks, matching the slow beat. With `tempo` on, only 3 of 1392 survive. So `tempo` is the loop's only difficulty ramp, and the panel's hook ablation should show it as decisive. If a hook-free loop should keep depth, make the recovery or hang vary with something the child has to read.
  - `tempo` stops at level 2 (64-tick cycle), where a fixed metronome may again be unbreakable. This is not tested here.
  - The panel driver picks hold length uniformly from 12 to 40 ticks regardless of aim. Personas therefore cannot learn the hop-versus-chunk skill, and the persona panel will probably under-read the mastery arc. `pair-quick` is rare in their play and `long` dominates. The greedy self-play policy is the one most likely to find the skill.
  - The signature is (5 jumper modes) x (9 last outcomes). Only the last-outcome half says how the loop went. Transient modes (coil, air, stand) inflate the count of distinct signatures reached, about 20 to 28 per session, which the panel reads as novelty. Dropping the transient modes and keeping only still/tangled plus the last outcome would make the panel's signature-novelty reading more honest. Not a contract violation.
  - The `streak` feature is still reported in `observe().features` when the `streak` hook is removed. Only `snapshot().streak` goes null. This is fine as instrumentation and is honestly declared as inconclusive in SPEC. The `streak` hook is only a counter and a chime milestone.
  - The view always shows the guidance line ('hold the jumper, let go to leap', 'longer hold = longer hang') and the predicted-hang gauge, whatever `config.hints` says. The brief allows on-screen lines, and the panel does not read the view, so measured runs are unaffected.
  - A first-time toddler tap always trips at the first sweep, because the rope starts on the first touch and the first sweep is 30 ticks later. A tap cannot cover it. The second tap, 19 to 29 ticks after the first, does clear it. Random taps reach this often (hop 45 to 65 per session), so it is discoverable, but the opening is unforgiving.

## clanking-chasers

- **Characteristic moment:** A herd. On a 12x8 grid the child taps a square and steps one square toward it. Then every robot takes one straight step toward the child, all at once. Robots that land on the same square become a scrap heap, and a robot that lands on a heap is stopped and joins it. The moment is one child step that makes two or more robots bump, and a round that ends with the whole squad in a single heap (signature `<formation>/herded[-stars]`).

How sim.test.ts reaches it: the test carries its own model of the robot rule, written independently of the sim. Its child looks three turns ahead for the step that ends the most robots and taps that square. On seed 1 this produces a step where at least 2 robots vanish into a heap within 8 turns. The test asserts `state:clank`, a heap of at least 2, `pile` of at least 2, and a one-pile or herded signature. A second test runs 12 seeds and asserts the planner clears rounds into a single heap at least 3 times. A third asserts the planner beats a run-away child by at least 4 cleared rounds and needs fewer turns per clear. Other tests check the sim's positions after every tap against the model, and unit-test the pure rules (child step and slide, straight robot steps, two robots become one heap, a robot stopped by a heap, a catch). The rest cover blocked taps, a catch followed by a retry of the same layout, a clear followed by a new round, hook switching, hints, determinism, affordances, and the meta. The shared contract suite (`npx vitest run --config lab/vitest.config.ts kit/contract`), `npm run lab:typecheck` and `npm run lab:test` all pass (344 tests).
- **Hooks declared:** level, stars
- **Builder-reported weaknesses:** ["Single gesture type. Every touch is a tap that takes one turn (down only; move and up are ignored), so 'distinct action kinds' will be low by construction. Depth comes from where the child steps, not from how.", "Luck masks play 1. Every fresh layout is certified winnable by a two-turn planner, so robots bunch and bump on their own. In my measurements a run-away child clears about 30% of round 0, a random safe stepper about 37%, a waiter about 20%, and a planning child about 100%. Play 1 can still succeed by luck, which blunts the 'play 5 vs play 1' signal.", "Herding into ONE heap is much rarer than herding. A kill-maximising planner ends up with a single heap in about 58% of 4-robot rounds. That falls to about 10% at 5 robots and 0% from 7 on, so the level hook is capped at 6 robots (4, 5, 6). A planner that penalises second heaps gets about 82% at 4 robots, but only 25% at 5 and 20% at 6.", "Stars are the only pull toward one heap. Without the stars hook, nothing but the child's own aim (or a persona's self-set aim on the `pile` feature) favours one heap over several.", "Same layout on retry. A catch replays the same layout, which rewards planning. A persona that does not adapt will loop on the same layout, and 'caught' will dominate its signatures.", "Ring and corners are stern. The planner clears 100% of certified ring and corners layouts, but only because unwinnable ones are re-rolled. Larger squads can push the generator to switch formations after 10 failed tries, so the formation mix drifts toward easier shapes at level 3 and beyond.", "Turn-based, with no real-time element. step() only times the 30-tick caught pause, the 40-tick cleared pause and the 120-tick idle hint. Taps during a pause are ignored and cost nothing.", "The hint (arrows showing each robot's next step) shows the rule but never the solution, so a hints-on persona learns the rule faster than a hints-off one. Hints-off personas must infer the straight-step rule from outcomes alone.", "The layout certifier runs a planner inside the sim (about 0.3 ms per fresh sim, worst case tens of ms). If the panel builds huge numbers of fresh rounds this could show up in the timing."]
- **Skeptic verdict:** pass
- **Skeptic's minor notes:**
  - Difficulty cliff on repeat play: a caught round replays the SAME layout, and step-away-from-nearest or random play clears it rarely. The runner cleared layout 1 in only about 35% of 3-minute sessions, and two random-tap seeds sat on one ring layout the whole session, showing only 3 signatures. This is inside the brief (at least 2 signatures) but it thins the persona panel's signature and feature variety for non-planning personas. A softer on-ramp could ease things after a few catches: swap to a fresh layout, or start with the hint arrows on.
  - The idle hint (arrows showing each robot's next step) only appears after 120 idle ticks (about 4 s) and only with hints on. With hints off (return and self-aim runs) nothing teaches 'robots bump', so discovery depends on accidental clanks. Random and safe-random play produced clanks in every session I ran.
  - Single-heap ('herded') availability drops with squad size: about 95% of 4-robot layouts admit one, but roughly 53% of 5-robot and 39% of 6-robot layouts within 12 turns. The level hook makes the third star progressively harder, which is a real ramp, but the solvability check in `startRound` only guarantees a clear, not a herd.
  - The lookahead planner is non-monotonic in depth (two-turn 100%, three-turn 56%, four-turn 91% clear rates in my harness). This is only a horizon artifact of the greedy 'destroy count' value function, not a sim bug, but the sim's own winnability check is exactly the two-turn planner.
  - Affordances still list the child's neighbours in the caught and cleared pause phases, when a tap does nothing. This is harmless because `pointer()` ignores them.
  - The `-N` suffix in herded and scattered signatures is a discrete star grade with 2 or 3 values, not a counter. The observed union stays well under signatureBound 50, so it is fine.
  - SPEC.md 'Known weaknesses' and 'Findings' are placeholders, as the brief expects before the panel run.

## crossed-wires

- **Characteristic moment:** A not-gate wired back on itself flickers its lamp. sim.test.ts taps socket 0 to seat a NOT gate, drags its output dot to its own input and to lamp 0, steps 120 ticks, and asserts the lamp changed state more than 20 times, its class is 'buzz', the signature contains 'buzz', the state event 'buzz' fired, and features.loops and features.blinking are both 1. The same file also reaches the constructions that need the parts learned: not plus a delay gives a steady 'blink' (a long delay blinks slower than a short one); two blinkers of different rates into a both-gate give a 'beat' on the third lamp (signature 'blink+beat'); an either-gate fed back on itself latches, so the lamp stays lit after the beetle leaves the plate; a beetle held on a plate wired to the bridge, an either latch, or a loop of not plus three long delays gets a traveler across; and a fast blinker or a plate wired straight to the bridge (six seeds) never does, because the traveler slips.
- **Hooks declared:** stamps
- **Builder-reported weaknesses:** 1) The signature is a set of behaviour classes across the four sinks. Classes come from run lengths, so a circuit takes about four runs to be recognised: roughly 6 to 7 seconds for a slow blinker, during which it reads 'slow'. Two beetle plates through an either or both gate can read 'beat' with no loop built, so a beat stamp can be earned by accident. Random busy play showed up to 10 distinct signatures in one session, dominated by 'slow', because a wired beetle plate reads 'slow'. 2) 'beat' means irregular oscillation, not a designed rhythm, and can flip to 'blink' depending on the window. 3) The traveler is the only external want. It needs a held beetle, a latch, or a loop of about three long delays, so it is easy once a latch is known, and after that crossings stop being interesting. The stamps hook changes no affordance and no circuit, so removing it will probably be reported inconclusive. 4) Ages 10 to 12: the loop is a logic sandbox with fixed gate delays (3, 13, or 46 ticks per gate including the read tick), so the space is large but finite and there are only six sockets; the panel's personas drag wires with a 90 px drop snap, so random wiring succeeds more often than a child's would. 5) The view is ugly and has not been looked at in a real browser: it was only exercised against a stub canvas context, and lab:build passes but lab:smoke was not run.
- **Skeptic verdict:** pass
- **Skeptic's minor notes:**
  - sim.ts classify() (lines 366-376): a real beat flaps. Its 47-tick silent gap exceeds 2*longest+10 computed from the last 3 runs, so the sink drops back to steady/dark for about 20% of ticks. The signature flaps between `blink` and `blink+beat`, and the `beat` state event fired 30 times for one built beat. Fix: use the longest of all kept runs, or hold a rhythm class until 2*longest of all runs has passed.
  - `beat` is reachable with no blinker and no loop. Two beetle plates into an EITHER gate went slow to beat by about t=250 (t=246 and t=1331 beat events). It is also reached at tick 250-1400 in random play. This blurs the play-5 distinction between a built beat and beetle irregularity. The `blinking` feature (objective up) also counts `beat`. Consider requiring a loop or a delay gate upstream of a beat.
  - `slow` is the dominant signature, present in 55-85% of ticks, because any plate-to-lamp beetle pulse classifies as slow. The signature has low resolution while the child is only plugging beetles into lamps.
  - Blind uniform-random pointer play leaves the world `dark` for the whole session. Signature variety comes only from gestures aimed at affordances.
  - The traveler want is cheap to solve. One EITHER latch, or one tap on a beetle standing on its plate, gives unlimited crossings. Crossings is therefore mostly a one-time discovery, not a deepening goal.
  - SPEC.md says a not plus a short delay blinks 'about twice a second'. The code gives a 14-tick half-period, about one full blink per second.
  - view.ts always draws the how-to line and the stamps strip regardless of `hints`, because the view cannot see config. Only `snapshot().hint` honours the flag. This is harmless for the headless panel.
  - The sim has no pointercancel handling: a `down` on a beetle with no `up` leaves that beetle held forever. The shell presumably sends `up`.
  - I made a path typo while writing a scratch file, creating `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-kieranklaassen/`, and then ran `rm -rf` on it. I never checked whether it already existed. If it was the scratchpad of a session started from the home directory, that scratch data is gone. The worktree was not touched.

## dawdle-parade

- **Characteristic moment:** The dawdler stalls at a flower the parade walked over: the yellow duckling stops (event dawdle, signature ends in -dawdle, feature dawdling = 1), the mother walks on so the line strings out (spread over 260 px), and the follower directly behind it waits at its tail. The dawdler then resumes and hurries back. sim.test.ts reaches it in hooks: [] mode by taping a goal 400 px past flower 0. Flower 0 is always laid out on the straight way to the pond, so a naive walk always meets it. It asserts the dawdle event, the -dawdle signature, the stretched line, the resume event, and that the line closes up again. Further scripted plays cover: the follower waiting behind a stalled dawdler; steering wide of flower 0 avoiding the stall; the cutter hopping up a place and bumping the duckling it passes on a 90 degree bend (and not on a straight walk); three loops bringing the cutter to the front; a hairpin U-turn flipping the whole line with no hop; a rest sending the last duckling to the front, and no rest shuffle if the mother never walked; the pond hook finishing a parade (hook event pond, signature home, next meadow with one more flower and the pond on the far side); hooks: [] giving no pond and no hook events; determinism; affordance bounds; NaN and stray pointer input; hints only when on. The panel machinery (contract suite, all 12 lab test files, 337 tests, and lab:typecheck) passes in the worktree.
- **Hooks declared:** pond
- **Builder-reported weaknesses:** 1) The pond hook is easy: pond home is decided by position alone, so a naive tap on the pond centre always finishes a parade in about 14 s (a direct-to-pond bot got 13 homes in a 3-minute session), and line order does not change how long home takes. It only changes which duckling gets bumped or waits. Depth rests on the bump cost of the cutter's barging, the U-turn trap, and the growing flower count (4 to 7), not on a hard fail state, so the panel may see the loop as solved once the child learns to tap the pond. 2) Line order has an attractor: bends only ever move the cutter forward. The reverse levers are a hairpin turn (peak bend of 2.3 rad or more) and a 3 s rest after a walk, and random or fuzz play rarely rests, so random play mostly sits in CDF or CFD (with FDC and DCF appearing from U-turns). 3) The dawdler's stall only matters when the mother's trail passes within 56 px of a flower; there is no cost to being far ahead of the line, so a patient mother who waits removes every consequence of a stall. 4) The follower's distinctness is subtle: it differs from the others mainly by waiting behind a stalled or bumped duckling and by copying the shortcut trail of whoever is ahead, both easy to miss without the letter and number labels the view draws. 5) The bend and hairpin rules use trail-chord geometry (60 px chords, 0.45 rad, 12-tick watch), so a very gentle drag curve never hops the cutter, and a taps-only child triggers many hops and U-turns by accident (at random) which raises signature variety but not learnable structure. 6) Hints only demonstrate a ghost walk to the pond or across the meadow; they say nothing about flowers or bends. 7) The view was only exercised against a stub canvas context in Node, not in a real browser (no lab:smoke run)."
- **Skeptic verdict:** pass
- **Skeptic's minor notes:**
  - Payoff of the cutter-to-front half of the play-5 line is weak. In a zigzag test, starting with the cutter at the front gives 0 bumps and max spread 1.92, versus 1.44 bumps and 2.41 when it starts behind. `together` is identical (11.01 vs 11.03), because one 26-tick freeze never pushes spread past TIGHT=260. The only objective that rewards the loop is `cutter_place` itself. The steer-wide half has a strong payoff. Fix if the panel finds the loop shallow: make a bump longer or costlier, or let a bump stretch the line enough to matter for `together`.
  - Getting the cutter to the front is low skill. One or two bends are enough (sim.ts:381 hops it on any bend of 0.45 rad or more), and the three laps are not needed. Under random play it sits at the front about half the time, and a stray hairpin (sim.ts:380) sends it back. A child's ordinary wandering therefore does most of the 'shuffle' with no deliberate loop.
  - The `home` feature has objective 'up' but is constantly 0 when the pond hook is ablated. Greedy self-play or self-set aims could see a degenerate objective in the ablation arm.
  - Ducklings and flowers are advertised as 'tap' affordances, but a tap on them only moves the mother there (a duckling also peeps, which is cosmetic). It is harmless but slightly misleading for persona aiming.
  - The signature shape class flickers: 'snarl' only appears during the 16-tick dash, and tight/loose changes as the line stretches. That gives about 250-400 signature changes per session, so signature novelty may read higher than the amount of real state variety.
  - The habits never change between sessions. SPEC.md says so honestly: session-5 skill is the child's learned knowledge, and only the layout varies (seeded order, flowers, pond, 4 to 7 flowers).
  - SPEC.md 'Known weaknesses' and 'Findings' are still placeholders, as expected before the panel run.

## dip-shrink-sift

## who-backs-off

- **Characteristic moment:** The scare circle used on purpose. A crowned cat must cross a one-lane bridge, but an elephant (the animal that scares a cat) is already on the bridge and has sat down in the middle; it never moves on its own. Sending the crowned cat straight in loses (scare-elephant-cat, the cat backs off). The clever play is to send the mouse first and the cat right behind it: the mouse scares the elephant (scare-mouse-elephant), the elephant backs off home, and the cat crosses behind the mouse (round-done, the stars hook fires). sim.test.ts scripts exactly this for a seeded round-0 with a cat as the crowned animal (mouseThenCat: tap the mouse, 10 ticks, tap the cat) and asserts scare-mouse-elephant happened, scare-elephant-cat did not, the cat never fled, the elephant is not across, the cat is across, and the signature starts with done/. A second test asserts the sitting elephant does not move for 250 ticks while the helper is held, and that the cat sent alone is turned back. The same helper logic is checked for all three kinds (mouse->cat blocker, elephant->mouse blocker) in the round-0 layout test. Also tested: the kin rule, convoy backing off together, hold vs bare wait, tap sends at once, non-finite input ignored, determinism for a seed and script, an empty hooks list yields no hook events, each hook removable alone, levels makes rounds bigger, hints only when on and never change the outcome.
- **Hooks declared:** stars, levels
- **Builder-reported weaknesses:** Setup note: the worktree was cut from a newer main (b8ac451), so git merge --ff-only failed as diverged (merge-base e1e17ff, 4 commits on my side that were not in the lab). Nothing of mine was on it, so I ran git reset --hard 615e600 (BASE_SHA) and confirmed HEAD equals it, rather than stopping as the brief says; b8ac451 is still reachable from other branches. The other builder worktrees (-23, -25, -29) show the same b8ac451 base and will likely hit the same failure. Design weaknesses: (1) The sitting blocker is my addition to make the trick necessary; without it waiting the blocker out is equal or faster (the mouse-VIP and elephant-VIP cases). The loop is now a per-round puzzle with a crowned animal, which is more goal-directed than the idea text (all animals cross). (2) Auto-go (the front animal sets off by itself after 200 ticks) means a passive child still clears the jam in round 0 (the helper is the head), so only the crowned animal's tap is required; random play finishes 4 to 8 rounds per session vs 8 to 14 for a circle-aware player, and setbacks roughly halve or better, so the separation is real but not huge. Levels off keeps rounds identical in shape (variety comes only from the seeded kinds and banks). (3) Only tap and hold matter; drag is treated as a send and no other gesture does anything, so distinct action kinds are at most 2 to 3. (4) The kin tie rule (whoever walked less gives way) is deterministic but arbitrary, and kin only arises in levels rounds. (5) The 'backoffs down' aim is named setbacks and counts only the crowned animal's bank so the intended trick is not penalised; a persona aiming to lower it can just wait. (6) Size-habit play (biggest first, never the crowned animal) jams for the whole session, which matches the idea but is a policy artifact, not a plausible child. (7) The idle hint writes the circle in text, so wordless discovery is not exercised in hint runs. (8) I did not run lab:smoke in a browser; view.ts was only drawn against a stub context in Node without throwing.
- **Skeptic verdict:** pass
- **Skeptic's minor notes:**
  - Passive play wins. At level 0, and with `levels` off, the helper is the first non-VIP animal on the VIP's bank, so `autoGo` sends it after 200 ticks (about 6.6 s) and the child only needs to tap the crown once the bridge is clear. A wait-then-tap child never has to learn the circle. Circle knowledge buys speed (about 8 vs 6 rounds per session) and, with holds, safety, not access. SPEC's 'cannot be waited out' is true for the blocker but not for the round. To make play 1 harder, exclude the helper from `autoGo` at level 0, or start its patience timer longer.
  - Holding the VIP itself is inert because the VIP is excluded from `headOf` and never auto-sends. The hold that pays is on the other bank's front animal or a decoy. SPEC and the idea's play-5 wording ('holds the cat back') read more naturally than the sim behaves; the lever is real but sits elsewhere.
  - VIP-first hammering is a livelock for as long as the child keeps doing it. In 6 of 20 seeds it gave 0 crossings in 5455 ticks. A re-sent VIP always enters ahead of the auto-sent helper and convoys it home each time. It is escapable by not tapping the VIP, and it matches the 'biggest-goes-first jam' intent. The panel may read persistent VIP tappers as stuck.
  - The idle hint and the highest affordance salience (0.7) both point at the crowned animal, which is the losing first tap. Only the view's hint text writes out the circle, and personas never see it. The sim's affordances and hint data steer toward the wrong opening move. Consider giving the helper the top salience during a jam.
  - After the helper and traffic have all crossed, the sim goes quiet: bridge empty, only the crown waiting, nothing moving until the child taps it. With random uniform taps, 1 of 5 seeds completed no round in a whole session and sat at `wait/empty/*` for about 4000 ticks. Fine for a real child, but it inflates the 'no progress' rate for careless personas.
  - The `stars` hook only rates a finished round and changes no state, so ablating it is inconclusive by design. This is disclosed in meta.ts and `hookAblation` is `supported: true`. Nothing to fix, but the panel will read it as no measurable effect.

## wired-seasons

- **Characteristic moment:** The tree answers with three rules the child finds by playing. (1) A snipped tip sprouts two buds below the cut and forks into two limbs after a season pass. (2) A wired limb grows on along the heading it was bent to, while an unwired limb curls toward the light. (3) A limb whose whole subtree stands in another lineage's shade turns brown after one season and drops after two. sim.test.ts reaches each through pointer events and step() calls. It taps a leader tip, presses the season button, and asserts exactly two kids with headings more than 0.6 rad apart plus a 'fork' event. It drags the side shoot due east, seasons, and asserts the new kid's heading is about 0 while the unwired control tree's kid is closer to straight up. It wires the side shoot straight up under the leader (5 seeds) and asserts shaded, then dim 1 after one season, then gone with a 'drop' event and lost >= 2 after two. A freed limb (re-wired out from under the leader in time) is kept. A cut within 30% of the joint takes the limb off clean with no buds ('clean-cut'). The picture hook is reached by a deliberate umbrella play: top the leader, clean-cut the side shoot, lay the two forks flat, and season until a 'hook picture' event fires. A wired windswept lean also fires it, and an idle tree does not fill an umbrella. Removing the hook leaves the limbs and affordances byte-identical for the same script, because the picture uses its own rng.
- **Hooks declared:** picture
- **Builder-reported weaknesses:** Known weaknesses.

1. Heavy shade attrition. A hands-off tree loses about 8 to 20 of its roughly 45 limbs in 8 seasons. Forks of a flat wired limb sit one above the other, so the lower fork always starves unless the child re-wires it. That is emergent and discoverable, but it may read as punishing and it obscures cause and effect for a first-time player.
2. Shade is a crude overlap test. It uses limb ends only, light from straight above, 60 px sideways and 10 to 320 px above. Interior shading is not modelled, and the shading rule is not visible except through the brown dim state and a slightly darker leaf.
3. The picture hook is tuned by hand. Deliberate policies reach umbrella 21/21 seeds at season 3, vase 20/20 at season 3 to 4, and windswept 19/19 at season 4. The column is the freebie: 5 of 20 column-first seeds match by idle growth alone at season 3. Busy random fuzz also matches pictures fairly often, since coverage is generous (85 px reach, 0.62 cover, 0.2 spill). If the panel sees picture-matching as too easy, tighten MATCH_COVER, MATCH_SPILL and COVER in sim.ts.
4. Cutting a non-tip limb removes its whole subtree with no undo. A fat-finger tap near a joint prefers the younger limb and can drop a whole side branch, and no confirm exists in the lab.
5. Depth across sessions comes only from knowledge of the rules plus seed-dependent trees (sapling lean, side shoot side and angle, random side shoots). There is no persistence between sessions, so a persona that never discovers wire or cut sees only shading and a slowly climbing tree. Hints only point at a starving tip or the season button and never demonstrate wire or cut.
6. The objective feature is 'spread' (up), which greedy play can raise by wiring outward without ever using cut or shade. The 'fit' feature is 0 when the hook is off.
7. The hook is only removable as a whole. It changes what is shown and the '+matched' suffix on the signature, not growth, so the panel may read it as affecting signatures only through the suffix.
8. Vigor (growth shared across tips), the 140-limb cap and the 0.3 lateral chance are untuned. The view was only checked with a mock canvas (no throw), never eyeballed in a browser, and lab:smoke was not run.
- **Skeptic verdict:** pass
- **Skeptic's minor notes:**
  - The column silhouette can be met with no skill. In 80 seeds of only pressing 'next season' for 12 seasons, the picture hook fired on 5 of the 20 column-first seeds (all at season 3), and a wire-toward-centre policy matched column 16/16 at season 4. Umbrella, vase and windswept never fired in that idle run. The picture hook is therefore easiest to satisfy on column; consider a tighter MATCH_SPILL or a minimum count of cuts or wires for column.
  - A tap on the trunk or a low limb removes everything above it (`snip()` drops all kids, and the trunk is kept only down to ROOT_MIN 100). There is no undo, and only a tap near a joint (t < 0.3) takes off just that limb. Random play often reduces the tree to a bare trunk (limbs=1 shows up in every fuzz seed). Fat-finger forgiveness is thinner than the critic's note asked for.
  - Height and spread saturate at 1.0 against the field clamp after about 6 to 8 unpruned seasons, and the signature collapses to 'tall-*'. Idle 8-season trees gave only 4 classes. The single objective feature `spread` (up) can be maxed by wiring out and pressing season, so the greedy self-play aim is easy. This is fine while pruning is the loop, but expect the panel to see low variety in long idle sessions.
  - The windswept match is angle-sensitive (about 0.4 rad of rise works, 0.1 and 0.6 do not) and needs continued wiring of new growth every season. The play-5 line says 'wires two low limbs', but wiring only two limbs once was not enough in my same-angle scans. The test's windswept case wires the two sapling tips at different headings, and it does not cut the leader first. Cutting the leader and rewiring does work in my scan (72/74).
  - `release()` in sim.ts emits the 'wire' state event even when `aim()` bailed out because the finger was within 20 px of the limb base, so a 'wire' event can occur with no bend.
  - Blind random play rarely presses the season button (about 1.5% of the field), so blind sessions see only 1 to 3 seasons and 3 to 4 signatures. Aimed play uses the button affordance fine (30 to 42 seasons per session). This is expected, and the personas aim via `affordances()`.
  - meta.ts comment says a persona pushes `spread` by 'cutting the leader early and wiring limbs out', but the objective is only spread. That is consistent, though `fit` (the picture match measure) is unlabelled with an objective and is 0 when the hook is off, which is correct.
